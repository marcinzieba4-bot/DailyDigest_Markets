🇺🇸 United States  Mixed  Rebound does not remove the earnings hurdle

Macro Stance: The American market has recovered some confidence without resolving the inflation problem that unsettled it. Reuters reports the S&P 500 and Nasdaq ending sharply higher on softer inflation data, with attention turning to earnings. [1] That reaction is relevant, but it does not erase CNBC's reported 3.5% year-on-year consumer-price increase in March, above expectations. [2] The useful stance is to distinguish a market capable of rebounding from an economy that has delivered enough disinflation to justify rapid easing. Companies still need sales, costs and investment to support earnings with the policy outlook less generous. A rebound can create room for selective participation while leaving the weakest financing and valuation assumptions exposed.

VIX stood at 14.91 index points and DXY at 105.28 index points (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2024-04-11). Those prices support a description of the session, not a guarantee of easier future financing.

New / Most Important: Reuters reports markets grappling with changing central-bank cutting expectations. [3] The ECB has now held rates and signalled a prospective reduction, while the US inflation debate continues to argue for caution. [4] The divergence matters because the dollar and global capital costs can influence company earnings even where local policy is becoming more supportive. Reuters also reports the Fed considering a slower balance-sheet runoff. [5] That is a prospective change in the pace of contraction, not an announced rate cut or renewed asset buying. Keep each policy channel attached to its actual economic mechanism. A business might benefit from improved funding conditions while still facing a high required return on new investment.

Playbook ✓: Use the rebound to review the quality of risk being held rather than assume the inflation shock has passed harmlessly. Prefer companies that can turn demand into cash without needing immediate monetary relief. Add duration or more fragile cyclicality when the sustained price and earnings evidence supports it; reduce exposure if guidance reveals that high financing or operating costs are eroding the expected benefit of demand. Reuters' account of later and fewer prospective Fed cuts remains part of the policy debate. [6] The stance becomes more constructive when profit delivery and renewed disinflation reinforce each other. Until then, a strong session is a chance to discriminate between durable businesses and positions whose main catalyst is the hope of a cheaper cost of money.

Prefer business plans that can adapt their customer mix and investment pace, rather than assume that an expanding factory base guarantees an equally accommodating external market.

New / Most Important: The weak-price report adds another reason to distinguish policy capacity from policy effectiveness. More support may be possible, but investors need to know whether it improves profitable final demand or mainly helps finance additional supply. Reuters' Fitch story describes a lower sovereign rating outlook on growth concerns. [23] An outlook change is not a downgrade of the rating itself; its relevance lies in the assessment of risks to the growth and fiscal backdrop. Companies may therefore face a combination of targeted financing opportunities and a more cautious view of the broader economic pathway. Playbook ✓: Take selective exposure where customer demand, pricing and financing can support durable cash earnings. Increase conviction when policy support is followed by evidence of profitable activity and better repayment capacity. Reduce it if weaker prices and property obligations make the expected recovery more expensive to finance or less valuable to shareholders. The better opportunity is an operating improvement whose value the stock price underestimates, supported by a balance sheet that allows the business to realise it. ⚠ Divergence: factory activity has improved, but weak inflation, property-credit pressure and trade scrutiny continue to challenge the return on capacity.
