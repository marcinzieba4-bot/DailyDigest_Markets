Credit Impulse: The important credit question is whether financing creates additional private demand or merely preserves existing borrowers. [21] [22] The transmission problem is straightforward: lending can rise while profitable investment opportunities remain scarce. In that setting, larger balance sheets do less for equity returns than better asset quality and stronger borrower cash flow. Look for financing to be followed by operating demand and healthier repayments. Until that happens, a policy-supported loan book is not enough reason to pay a growth multiple for banks.

Do not treat the announcement of looser rules as proof that the sequence has completed. Prefer businesses able to grow with modest housing activity, and require a sustained improvement in sales and pricing before adding broad developer exposure.

The Financial Times described Chinese stocks racing ahead as reforms took hold, while SCMP warned that soaring chip-stock valuations were testing traders' patience. [30] [31] Both observations fit a market in which structural hopes attract capital before near-term earnings justify every price. A renewed trade confrontation makes the distinction more expensive. Playbook ✓: Keep China exposure selective and below a broad cyclical overweight.
