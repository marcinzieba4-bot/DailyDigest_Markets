[2] When the official picture is obscured, investors should put more weight on the customer, credit and pricing commentary in company reports, but avoid treating a good quarter at a large exporter as proof that every domestic business is accelerating. Delayed inflation data and Tesla earnings are pending tests identified in Reuters' week-ahead coverage, rather than events whose outcomes investors can already trade. [6]

Playbook ✓: Keep the US allocation selective: favour companies where guidance and the balance sheet validate the price move, retain some Treasury duration against a growth disappointment, and avoid using falling yields to excuse deteriorating orders. Reuters says the Fed still appears poised to cut while concern grows about the economic-data vacuum. [7] A prospective cut is supportive only if it does not arrive alongside a more serious credit shock. Add broader cyclical exposure when operating evidence improves beyond a handful of winners; reduce it if credit concerns spread while index leadership narrows. The useful divergence is that easier sovereign yields coexist with uneven corporate results, so the next decision should be made by earnings quality rather than by a single index close.

Bloomberg's report that local-government financing-vehicle bond sales have fallen to a five-year low amid risk curbs points to a constrained part of the financing system, rather than a simple nationwide credit boom.
