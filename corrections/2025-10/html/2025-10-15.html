Playbook ✓: Own earnings visibility and keep the hedge. Add cyclicals only if subsequent company guidance confirms order resilience; reduce risk if the tariff dispute starts appearing in margins or credit costs. A bond rally alone is insufficient grounds to remove that protection.

🇨🇳 China  Bearish  export strength / domestic fragility

Macro Stance: China's investment case is split between a competitive export machine and a fragile domestic balance sheet. September exports exceeded expectations and global shipments reached a six-month high even as shipments to the US fell, according to CNBC and AP. [18] [19] That diversification is real support for production, but it does not demonstrate that households have become more confident or that developers have repaired their finances. The fresh trade confrontation makes the distinction more urgent. Reuters describes China sticking with an 'all about production' economic playbook as rivalry with the US intensifies. [20] A production-led strategy can preserve industrial scale while keeping pressure on margins if buyers at home remain cautious. For equities, the better question is who can convert output into cash after trade frictions, rather than whether an export headline looks strong.

PMIs & Activity: The September manufacturing surveys pointed to improvement, but the official measure still showed contraction; Reuters also reported slower non-manufacturing activity. [21] [22] The two observations should be read together. A better factory survey is encouraging for the direction of activity, while weak services limit the claim that recovery is spreading through domestic demand. The last August releases showed retail sales and industrial production missing expectations, as CNBC reported. [23] The September export beat offers a stronger external channel, not a substitute for those household and business indicators. Watch the next domestic releases for orders, spending and investment moving in the same direction. Until that happens, the appropriate stance is to buy identifiable earnings rather than a blanket reflation trade. Export adaptation can keep GDP production resilient while leaving companies exposed to price competition.

Credit Impulse: The most concrete financing support is the recently announced investment toolkit. Reuters reported $70 billion of financing tools to bolster investment at the end of September. [24] That gives public and project-related spending a channel of support, but an announced financing envelope is not the same thing as a broad private-credit revival. Transmission depends on projects being selected, funds being deployed and suppliers receiving usable orders. Household borrowing and developer demand have different incentives from state-supported capital expenditure. The distinction matters for banks: a larger pool of policy-linked lending can stabilize activity without necessarily improving risk-adjusted returns. Avoid converting the headline into a claim that the credit impulse has already turned decisively higher. The investable confirmation would be improving private demand alongside the project pipeline, rather than another commitment to supply credit.

Property: Property remains the main domestic obstacle. CNBC reports S&P seeing this year's property slump as much worse than expected. [25] At the company level, Reuters says most Sunac creditors approved its restructuring plan, while Vanke's former chief executive has been placed under criminal compulsory measures, according to Yicai. [26] [27] A restructuring agreement can reduce immediate uncertainty for one capital structure; it does not show that households are purchasing more homes or that cash collections have stabilized across developers. The September private survey showed new-home prices edging up while resale prices fell, another reason to distinguish segments rather than declare a bottom. [28] The equity route to a property recovery should be conditional on better sales and funding, with position sizes recognizing that a legal milestone and an operating recovery can arrive at very different speeds.

Fiscal, Policy & Trade: Rare-earth policy is now the central external risk. China expanded restrictions on rare earths and technology, AP reports, while Reuters describes tighter access to magnet export licenses. [29] [30] These measures can exert leverage over manufacturers beyond the headline tariff channel because a small but essential input may delay a much larger finished product. The US response has included an extra 100% tariff announcement and controls on critical software, according to CNBC. [31] Neither side's threat should be mistaken for a final negotiated settlement. There is also a logistics channel: Reuters reports China's retaliatory port levies, which can affect freight decisions and working capital even without a complete interruption in trade. [32] Favor suppliers with flexible sourcing and clear customer qualification; avoid assuming that a tariff exemption alone resolves access to critical components.

Markets & Flows: The Shanghai Composite finished at 3,865.23, up 1.00% over 20 local sessions, while Hang Seng closed at 25,441.35, down 3.59% over its own 20-session interval. ASHR was $32.15, down 0.22%, and KWEB $39.15, down 5.50% over 20 sessions. USD/CNY stood at 7.1000, down 0.26% over 20 observations (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2025-10-14). These are different exchange calendars and instruments, so the comparisons should not be read as a measured capital-flow series. They do show that offshore internet exposure is struggling while mainland equities have held up better. A broadly stable currency is helpful for domestic conditions, but it has not prevented substantial equity dispersion. For foreign investors, the internet basket therefore needs an earnings or demand catalyst rather than reliance on a macro rescue narrative alone.

New / Most Important: The export story is broadening geographically at the same time that the trade rules are becoming more restrictive. AP reports electric-vehicle exports doubling in September amid intense competition at home. [33] That supports the competitiveness thesis, while also warning that domestic competition is pushing companies outward. Alibaba's cloud business has launched a second Dubai data centre, Reuters reports, another example of an international expansion channel distinct from Chinese household spending. [34] The opportunities are company-specific: successful overseas capacity or service expansion can grow revenue, but political access, compliance and local returns determine whether that revenue is valuable. Treat the export beat as a reason to investigate competitive businesses, not as proof that the domestic property drag has stopped mattering.

Playbook ✓: Maintain a defensive China stance at the index level and separate domestic recovery trades from internationally competitive exporters. Consider selected cash-generative internet and industrial franchises only where operating evidence supports demand; avoid treating a lower share price as its own catalyst. A genuine upgrade would require domestic activity and property cash generation to improve alongside the external shipment data. A downgrade would follow wider export-control enforcement or persistent household weakness despite policy financing. Keep the rare-earth hedge outside companies whose own earnings depend heavily on unrestricted Chinese processing. ⚠ Divergence: exports are adapting and mainland equities are relatively resilient, yet property distress and offshore internet weakness continue. The external production engine is doing work that a domestic-demand recovery has not yet taken over.

It is therefore too simple to classify every security headline as bullish for a domestic stock basket. Prefer businesses whose funded orders or services can be identified, and assess the cash-flow cost of security and redundancy for infrastructure owners. Currency risk also matters: a regional risk shock can offset domestic fundamental gains for a foreign investor before company earnings respond.

A position can thus have strong medium-term momentum and still carry immediate reversal risk. The combination of lower inflation, a surprise rate cut and weakness in individual state-linked sectors is precisely why country-level optimism needs a stock-level hurdle. Domestic growth should be translated into company margins, funding requirements and shareholder treatment before it becomes a portfolio weight.

Playbook ✓: Remain selectively constructive on domestically supported earnings, but keep a neutral overall stance while fiscal and regional risks compete with monetary relief. Favor banks only where interest-income resilience and credit quality can absorb the effect of lower rates and prospective policy costs. For KGHM, add on an operating and price thesis rather than chasing a large recent move; for utilities, demand clarity on funding and the allocation of transition costs. Domestic consumer exposure needs evidence that income and financing relief turn into volume rather than merely covering higher costs. A firmer zloty and broader participation would justify increasing country risk; renewed dollar strength or security disruption would argue for hedging it. ⚠ Divergence: inflation and rates are moving in a friendlier direction, yet state-linked sectors and several consumer names are failing to participate. Macro relief is available, but it has not become an indiscriminate earnings tailwind.
