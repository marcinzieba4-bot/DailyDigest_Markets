Macro Stance: China's equity opportunity is increasingly about the composition of demand. The strongest investment case sits in cloud infrastructure, selected technology platforms and policy-supported capital spending; the weakest link remains the willingness of households and businesses to borrow and commit. [18] The next useful signal is breadth: improving new business should reach domestic orders and employment rather than remaining concentrated in a small group of successful producers. Credit Impulse: The latest lending warning is July's unexpected contraction in bank loans, which Reuters described as the first in 20 years. [19] That is a powerful reason to resist declaring a new credit cycle simply because equities have rallied. Reuters also reported a record $322 billion of loans for stock bets, with volatility and caution following. [20] Margin-funded equity demand can pull returns forward without repairing cash flow in the wider economy. A constructive credit impulse would involve broader willingness to invest and spend; an acceleration in speculative borrowing alone would instead make the equity advance more fragile.

Favor businesses that can benefit from household spending outside housing before taking large exposure to highly leveraged property balance sheets. A genuine turn would show up in sustained transaction demand and more dependable financing, with fewer incentives needed to induce the next buyer.
