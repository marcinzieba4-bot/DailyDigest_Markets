New / Most Important: Reuters described a record S&P 500 close as the data kept rate-cut expectations intact. [5] The important distinction is between a market rising because earnings expectations improve and one rising because the hurdle rate falls. If the employment report is weak enough to undermine spending rather than simply support easing, trim lower quality consumer and bank exposure and increase Treasury duration. If hiring is stronger and yields rise, reduce expensive long-duration equities before adding broad index leverage. Keep a small volatility hedge because calm prices leave less room for the policy narrative to be wrong.

🇨🇳 China  Bearish  selective technology / weak transmission

Macro Stance: China's equity opportunity is narrower than the excitement around its technology ambitions suggests. The official August manufacturing survey showed a fifth consecutive month of contraction, according to Reuters, while the private factory survey showed the quickest growth in five months. [15] [16] That disagreement is useful: it tells investors to examine which businesses are gaining orders rather than treating a national label as a sufficient investment thesis. An innovative exporter can improve while a property-linked supplier remains under pressure. The bearish macro stance reflects weak transmission from policy support into household balance sheets and broad private demand; it leaves room for specific technology companies to outperform. The right question is whether an earnings recovery can widen beyond the names attracting the most attention.

PMIs & Activity: The August private services PMI rose to 53, a one-year high, per Plataforma Media. [17] That is an index reading, and it provides a constructive counterweight to the official factory contraction. July factory output and retail-sales growth had missed forecasts, Reuters reported, so a stronger services survey needs to become a sustained improvement in realised activity. [18] A survey can pick up confidence, seasonal demand or a change in order timing before it becomes cash receipts. Investors should look for the next activity releases to support stronger private consumption and for companies to stop relying on discounts to generate sales. The combination would justify a broader domestic recovery position; another isolated strong survey would favour keeping the exposure concentrated.

Credit Impulse: Credit remains the clearest brake on a wholesale reflation call. Reuters reported that July bank loans unexpectedly contracted, while the Wall Street Journal described an unexpected drop in new yuan loans. [19] [20] The July vintage matters because borrowing decisions transmit more slowly than a daily equity rally. Easier access to finance helps only when a borrower sees an attractive project or a household wants to commit to a large purchase. A durable improvement would involve stronger private borrowing accompanied by orders, employment and spending. Buying financial intermediaries solely because policy can make credit cheaper confuses the supply of money with the demand to use it. Keep banks and heavily indebted domestic businesses behind companies with visible customers and internally generated cash.

Property: Housing remains a balance-sheet restraint. A Reuters poll expected home prices to fall less than previously anticipated but still described the market as weak; the July price report had shown another decline, with smaller falls in major cities. [21] [22] A slower deterioration is valuable, yet it does not automatically turn property into an engine of demand. Households need confidence in delivery, resale values and income before cheaper financing translates into a purchase. Developers need collections and financing access that survive beyond a supportive announcement. For equity positioning, give more weight to observable sales and cash conversion than to the intensity of policy language. A stabilising prime-city market could coexist with continuing stress elsewhere, so national property exposure remains a blunt expression.

Fiscal, Policy & Trade: Trade and industrial policy favour strategic capability, but they also create boundaries around commercial access. Reuters reported that Chinese companies still wanted Nvidia chips despite government pressure against buying them; the Financial Times reported that Anthropic would stop selling AI services to majority Chinese-owned groups. [23] [24] These stories make domestic substitution a credible investment theme, while reminding investors that substitution is a response to constraints rather than proof of superior near-term economics. SCMP reported that provinces were preparing business trips to the US during a shaky tariff truce. [25] A truce permits planning, but it should not be capitalised as permanent access. Prefer businesses whose demand survives under multiple trade arrangements and examine the cost of replacing imported technology.

New / Most Important: Bloomberg reported that the scale of China's stock-market rally had Beijing nervous. [26] That is the signal to watch because policy makers can welcome confidence while resisting destabilising speculation. Meanwhile, Bloomberg described a rally in Chinese solar shares on signs of industry recovery, and Yicai reported Alibaba and Tencent medical AI models moving into critical care. [27] [28] Such developments support company-specific opportunities, but they do not repair weak borrowing or housing on their own. The next useful confirmation would be an improving domestic order book and credible earnings conversion, rather than a higher valuation applied to the same policy narrative.

Playbook ✓: Keep broad China exposure below a neutral allocation and retain a small, selective technology position through liquid instruments such as KWEB. Add ASHR only if stronger borrowing and household activity accompany a sustained market recovery; use a failed rebound after the recent pullback to reduce exposure. Avoid using a commodity price as a substitute for Chinese demand data. A stronger yuan can improve the foreign investor's translation return while doing little for a weak developer's cash flow.
