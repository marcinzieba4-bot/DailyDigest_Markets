🇺🇸 United States  Mixed  Quiet consumers, stronger factories

Macro Stance: The US expansion is becoming more uneven beneath resilient equity indices. Reuters pairs tepid May retail sales with a surge in manufacturing production. That is a useful distinction for the earnings book: stronger factory activity does not automatically mean households are becoming more willing to spend, and a softer retail print does not establish that the entire economy is contracting. Favor businesses whose demand can be identified directly. Industrial suppliers may have a better activity backdrop while discretionary retailers still need to prove product relevance, inventory control and cash conversion. Avoid using either side of the release to make a uniform call across all domestic equities. [1] A manufacturer can improve output while its customers rebuild inventories, but that becomes a more durable earnings signal when final sales support repeat orders. For retailers, the counterpart is selling through existing stock profitably rather than simply increasing the volume of goods on offer.

Rates & Curve: The Treasury 2Y closed at 4.69% and the 10Y at 4.22%, leaving a 10Y–2Y spread of -47 bps. Over twenty observations, the 2Y fell 13 bps and the 10Y fell 22 bps; the spread narrowed by 9 bps. The curve remains inverted, and the move is bull flattening, with both yields lower and the 10Y down more (US Treasury, prior close 2024-06-18). Lower yields help discount rates, but an equity investor must still ask whether they reflect benign inflation or weaker demand. Those mechanisms can favor different industries even when they initially produce the same direction in bonds.

New / Most Important: The S&P 500 closed at 5,487.03 index points, up +3.37% over twenty sessions; VIX stood at 12.30 index points and DXY at 105.26 index points (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2024-06-18). Reuters reports records supported by AI momentum alongside the weak retail release. Its currency coverage says the dollar eased after retail sales missed expectations. These observations show a market willing to separate technology leadership from broader consumer softness, but they do not prove that the economic split can persist without consequences for profits. The stronger the valuation premium becomes, the more important dependable operating delivery will be. [2] [3]

Playbook ✓: Maintain quality growth exposure while keeping a measured allocation to companies that can benefit from healthier factory demand. Add consumer cyclicals only when sales quality improves without an offsetting increase in promotions or inventory. Cut broad risk if lower yields begin to coincide with weaker orders and deteriorating earnings conversion. Reuters' earlier report of unchanged consumer prices in May supports the disinflation case, while the Fed's projection of only one cut keeps the policy hurdle visible. The constructive trigger is inflation relief accompanied by enough final demand to support profits; the invalidation is a widening gap between index optimism and business delivery. Hold flexibility rather than forcing the consumer and manufacturing signals into the same conclusion. [4] [5]
