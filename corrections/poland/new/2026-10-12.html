🇵🇱 Poland  Mixed  → hold, fiscal risk rising

Macro Stance: Rates stay at 3.75% after the October RPP meeting (StockWatch [Oct 7]), but the Council is no longer unanimous in tone: Business Insider Polska [Oct 12] reports RPP members not ruling out rate changes and naming fuel and energy as the largest risks, while Strefa Inwestorów [Oct 12] asks what the RPP does to year-end with the Fed and ECB both raising. Glapiński's line remains that a November hike is not his base case but the Council is ready (PAP Biznes [Oct 8]). This week brings the scheduled detail of September CPI, quarterly GDP revisions, an MF auction and a BGK switch (Strefa Inwestorów [Oct 12]); September inflation already exceeded the reference rate (Polskie Radio 24 [Oct 7]) and the NBP target (Money.pl [Oct 8]), and Rzeczpospolita [Oct 12] sees shop prices no longer decelerating. Growth remains the counterweight, with 2025 GDP revised to 3.8% (CEO Magazyn [Oct 6]). New today is an institutional question: Business Insider Polska [Oct 12] reports the government could support the zloty in the market, which raises the question of where the currency is supposed to trade; Comparic [Oct 12] argues the worst for PLN may still be ahead. The State Tribunal question over the governor (Bloomberg [Oct 9]) is unresolved.

Markets: Monday's session showed fading momentum: Parkiet [Oct 12] describes slowing gains with a bank rebound and an Orlen sell-off, Bankier has strong banks, weak Orlen and Herkules as the top gainer, and XYZ says the market is consolidating while banks push it up; Parkiet also flags weakening demand. The zloty slipped further: USD/PLN closed 3.9093, up 0.5% on the week and 4.9% over four weeks, with Comparic [Oct 12] seeing the dollar continue its rally toward 3.93 and the euro close to 4.40 as oil rises (Magazyn MANAGER+ [Oct 12]); EUR/PLN closed 4.3781, down 0.1% on the week and up 1.4% over four weeks, and Bankier notes the zloty is stable in pairs other than the pound. Forex Club [Oct 12] has Polish bonds under pressure with yields rising but a chance of a rebound. The card still reflects last week's rotation: Orlen at 159.34 up 10.4% on the week, PGE at 16.7 up 11.1% and 19.0% over four weeks, Dino at 35.81 up 6.4%, LPP at 25040 up 4.2% and 16.1% over four weeks, CD Projekt at 263.0 up 3.7%, KGHM at 347.0 up 3.5%, Allegro at 51.75 up 15.1% over four weeks; PKO at 113.94 is down 3.6% and Pekao at 261.1 down 2.8%. EPOL closed 43.82, up 1.8% on the week and down 3.8% over four weeks.

Fiscal & Politics: Fitch revised its forecasts for Poland, with the budget hole and debt both larger (Forsal, Money.pl [Oct 12]), and INFOR [Oct 12] calculates that 115bn zł of debt service in 2026 exceeds 800+, the 13th and 14th pensions, the widow's pension and the grandparent benefit combined. The president warned of a shortage of money and the finance minister replied (Money.pl, Next Gazeta [Oct 12]). The Finance Ministry issued a statement rejecting claims that it handed supervision of the GPW and KDPW to Brussels (Gov.pl [Oct 12]), which Domański called "mindless manipulation" (Money.pl), while Business Insider Polska notes Poland secured an exception in the new EU capital-market rules. Domański is counting on a high Orlen dividend for a third straight record year (Strefa Inwestorów [Oct 12]). The Energy Minister set higher maximum fuel prices for Tuesday, with petrol 95 back above 7 zł (Forsal [Oct 12]).

Corporate & Sectors: Orlen was sold off (XTB [Oct 12]) despite supportive analysis: Investing.com argues it could gain from an oil crisis, Money.pl says undelivered American gas may work in its favour, Energy Minister Motyka wants government and Orlen to coordinate Saudi oil and Qatari gas (Rzeczpospolita [Oct 12]). Banks rebounded even as UOKiK fined four banks close to 150m zł over mortgages (PropertyNews [Oct 12]) and PKO BP cut its mortgage margin to 1.55% in an autumn promotion. CD Projekt's squeeze continues, with Comparic [Oct 12] noting shorts covering and a 19% rise, though FXMAG asks whether Tusk's attack on gaming could hurt the shares. Dino workers plan a protest tomorrow (Wiadomości Handlowe [Oct 12]) ahead of the pre-holiday strike. Housing demand is firm: mortgage applicants rose 10.1% in September versus August (Miesięcznik Finansowy BANK [Oct 12]), much of it refinancing (Rzeczpospolita) and Bankier sees a Warsaw boom with prices flat. Power and gas prices on the exchange are rising again and 2027 tariffs are under pressure (Parkiet, Business Insider Polska [Oct 12]).

Geopolitics & Security: Reuters [Oct 12] reports that Patriot interceptor deliveries to Poland will be delayed, per a top general, a day after Poland warned NATO to prepare for deadly Russian hybrid attacks (trendingtopics [Oct 12]). NATO's commander unveiled a plan for victory and said the alliance is "ready to fight tonight" (Balkan Insight, Bozeman Daily Chronicle [Oct 12]), Tusk urged the US and Europe to be more decisive on Russia (Free Malaysia Today [Oct 12]), and the economy minister warned Europe against returning to Russian energy (Anadolu [Oct 12]). The US wants Poland in the G20 as soon as possible (Notes From Poland [Oct 12]).

New / Most Important: Fitch's downgrade of Poland's fiscal path, arriving with a debt-service bill that now outweighs the main social transfers, moves the fiscal story from Sejm theatre to rating risk. Paired with talk of the government supporting the zloty directly, it tells investors that policy, not data, is driving the currency and the long end.

Playbook ✓: Reduce POLGB duration ahead of the MF auction; keep the bank rebound tactical given the UOKiK fines and crowding-out risk; stay long PGE and Dino, trim Orlen after its 10.4% week.  |  ⚠️ Divergence: Banks rising while the sovereign's outlook deteriorates is the inverse of the usual Polish correlation; either bank valuations or the fiscal headlines are wrong, and this week's auction and CPI detail will say which.
