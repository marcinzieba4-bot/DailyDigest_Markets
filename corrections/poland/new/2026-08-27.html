🇵🇱 Poland  Bearish  → hold, geopolitical risk-off

Macro Stance: The RPP's published thinking leans cautious-hawkish: the Council expects inflation to persist and worries about turbulence on global markets (Business Insider Polska, [Aug 27]), after the minutes named the members who wanted a hike (Tysol.pl, [Aug 22]) and Bankier.pl's analysts concluded wages will keep outpacing inflation so the RPP has no need to cut [Aug 20]. TradingPedia now frames the zloty outlook as shifting hawkish as import costs and the euro rise [Aug 27], which is precisely the pass-through channel BNY flagged. The real economy is not the problem: Comparic.pl reports corporate profits up 34% and investment up 19% [Aug 25] and households hold over 1.5 trillion zł of deposits with rising intent to buy cars (Money.pl, [Aug 27]); the soft spot remains July retail sales at 3.9% y/y, below forecasts (Inwestycje.pl, Strefa Biznesu, [Aug 24]). On fuel, Aramco cut wholesale diesel (Super Biznes, [Aug 27]) and Radio Kraków sees room for petrol below 7 zł [Aug 27].

Markets: Panic is Bankier.pl's word: the biggest sell-off in over two months [Aug 27], with the WIG20 the weakest index in Europe (Business Insider Polska, [Aug 27]). Parkiet's diagnosis is an aversion to Polish assets, with capital retreating from the GPW and the zloty after the CIA chief scared investors [Aug 27]; Comparic.pl calls it capital flight on geopolitics [Aug 27]. Kruk collapsed 7% and CD Projekt traded on gigantic turnover (CrypS.pl, [Aug 27]), deepening its slide while Eurocash and Mo-Bruk were hot on results (pb.pl, [Aug 27]) and Orlen is testing its first support (DNA Rynków, [Aug 27]). The card, as of Wednesday's close, shows where the damage started: CD Projekt 238.0 (-7.2% 1W, -5.9% 4W) and Orlen 149.0 (-4.1% 1W) were already the week's losers, while KGHM 364.35 (+4.7% 1W, +23.8% 4W), Dino 36.09 (+11.1% 1W, +13.5% 4W), PGE 10.83 (+3.6% 1W, +11.8% 4W), PKO 115.78 (+0.7% 1W) and Pekao 261.9 (+10.4% 4W) went into today's session at or near highs; EPOL 44.73 (+0.3% 1W, +7.6% 4W). The zloty took heavy blows (wGospodarce, [Aug 27]): a sudden weakening left the euro, dollar and pound at their dearest in a week (Bankier.pl, Forex Club, [Aug 27]), reversing the card's USD/PLN 3.6955 (flat 1W, -2.0% 4W) and EUR/PLN 4.3069 (-0.1% 1W, -0.4% 4W); FXMAG's experts expect further dollar strength [Aug 27]. The one bid was POLGBs: Parkiet reports strong demand for bonds [Aug 27], the classic flight within the market rather than out of it.

Fiscal & Politics: The 2027 budget is landing. Money.pl and WP say the 2027 deficit figure is known [Aug 27], Interia has the calculations [Aug 27], Business Insider Polska carries early speculation that the budget is in an even worse state [Aug 27], and Rzeczpospolita reports the government is to take up the draft with billions for arms and new tax rates [Aug 27]. The Fiscal Council issued its opinion on the draft and the finance minister published explanations in response (Gov.pl, [Aug 27]); Prof. Wnorowski notes July began the third year of the excessive deficit procedure (RadioMaryja.pl, [Aug 27]). The revenue side is being scraped: the internet relief is abolished while the robotisation relief stays (Business Insider Polska, [Aug 27]), with the ministry conceding it narrows budget revenue (PolsatNews.pl, [Aug 27]), the government will review PIT and CIT reliefs in 2026 and plans to repeal some (Gazeta Prawna, [Aug 27]), and property tax rises from 2027 under the published ministerial notice (Forsal.pl, [Aug 27]).

Corporate & Sectors: Banks: CIT billions weigh on results, with the sector earning only 21.4 billion zł in the half year (Bankier.pl, [Aug 27]), yet mortgages are an absolute record with over a fifth of volume refinancing (Bankier.pl, [Aug 27]), banks have lent 39.7 billion zł in mortgages (CEO Magazyn, [Aug 27]) and household mortgage debt is the highest in history (Business Insider Polska, [Aug 27]); POLSTR passed its market test as the benchmark reform enters its decisive phase (PAP, [Aug 27]) and WNP.PL calls the coming rate-setting change an earthquake for instalments [Aug 27]. Orlen signed a cooperation agreement with Veolia for industry, energy and cities (StockWatch.pl, [Aug 27]), Skarv gets a second life with 30 million barrels for Orlen (Biznes Alert, [Aug 27]), but a former Orlen director was charged by prosecutors over a report on a politician (Business Insider Polska, [Aug 27]). KGHM unions demand 6500 zł for every employee (miedziowe.pl, [Aug 27]). Tauron estimated 586 million zł of Q2 net profit and 1606 million zł EBITDA (Inwestycje.pl, [Aug 25]). CD Projekt's remaster did not rescue the share price (Comparic.pl, [Aug 27]); fans are delighted, investors are not (FXMAG, [Aug 27]).

Geopolitics & Security: The Guardian reports the CIA chief warned the Kremlin not to attack NATO, with Russia saying the UK is playing with fire [Aug 27], and Newsweek sketches what a Russian attack on NATO could look like [Aug 27]. US Patriot stocks in Europe are beyond critical because of the Iran war (PBS, France 24, [Aug 27]); Poland ruled out hosting nuclear weapons (UNN, [Aug 27]); four EU countries want to reopen the frozen Russian assets debate (Reuters, [Aug 27]); Poland received its first K2 tanks from South Korea (LIGA.net, [Aug 25]).

New / Most Important: Security risk, not the budget, broke the market today. The CIA warning turned a crowded, developed-market-upgrade-driven long into a liquidation on the day the 2027 deficit number surfaced, so foreign money sold both the index and the zloty while buying POLGBs. That ordering says investors fear an event, not insolvency, which is why the fiscal headlines barely registered.  |  Playbook ✓: Reduce beta; keep KGHM and Dino as earnings-backed cores but cut leveraged exposure, hedge PLN via the euro leg, and treat the bond bid as a parking place rather than a duration call before the budget.  |  ⚠️ Divergence: Record mortgage lending and 1.5 trillion zł of deposits sit next to the biggest equity sell-off in two months: domestic balance sheets are strong, foreign conviction is not, and the gap is where the next move comes from.
