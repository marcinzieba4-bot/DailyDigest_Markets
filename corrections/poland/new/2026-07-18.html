🇵🇱 Poland  Mixed  → on hold, oil-dependent

Macro Stance: The weekend summary from pb.pl is that Polish interest rates now "depend largely on the situation in the Middle East" ([Jul 18]), which is a fair reading of a week in which the NBP's hawks took the microphone. The bank disclosed who voted for a hike at an earlier meeting (Tysol.pl, [Jul 17]), a Council member said increases are more probable than cuts (Radio ESKA, [Jul 17]; pb.pl, [Jul 14]), and borrowers are being warned of higher instalments for many months (Direct Money, [Jul 17]). None of this is driven by current prices: June CPI was confirmed at 2.5% y/y with a 0.5% m/m fall (Miesięcznik Finansowy BANK, [Jul 15]), core inflation declined (Biznes Wprost, [Jul 16]) and food is cheapening at the fastest pace in years (Rzeczpospolita, [Jul 16]). It is driven by what comes next: Eurostat's HICP already shows 3% y/y (Do Rzeczy, [Jul 17]), prices are rising faster than the EU average (Business Insider Polska, [Jul 17]), the expiry of the CPN fuel package will lift July inflation (Gazeta Prawna, [Jul 15]; Energetyka24.com, [Jul 16]) with PKO BP seeing a transitory move above 3.5% (PAP, [Jul 15]), and pb.pl reports experts no longer believe in post-holiday cuts ([Jul 15]).

Markets: Friday's close, now on the card, confirms the rotation out of banks: PKO 106.8 is -5.4% on the week and Pekao 229.0 -6.3%, leaving them +1.4% and -2.7% over four weeks, after Parkiet reported copper and geopolitics "sank KGHM and the banks" while Orlen and Żabka closed green ([Jul 17]). KGHM 293.7 is -4.9% on the week and -19.6% over four weeks following a 3.32% Friday drop (FXMAG, [Jul 17]), and a strong tremor at the Rudna mine injured two miners on Saturday (TVP Wrocław, [Jul 18]). The winners held: Orlen 146.2 (+4.5% 1W, +15.7% 4W), Allegro 43.4 (+9.0% / +10.1%) after its preliminary results (Inwestycje.pl, [Jul 14]), LPP 19960 (+3.0% / +7.5%), Dino 29.15 (+3.3%), PGE 9.48 (+1.2%), CD Projekt 232.2 (-0.3%). Żabka's 10.87% Friday jump on the Seven & i stake talks (FXMAG, Reuters, [Jul 17]) and Bankier's view that promotional prices on Allegro, Pepco and Żabka "are over" ([Jul 17]) describe a market Masłowski calls an advanced-phase bull run ([Jul 17]); INNPoland warns Żabka investing "is getting risky" ([Jul 17]). EPOL at 40.35 is flat on the week and +0.3% over four weeks, which is the whole Polish story in one number: record local index, flat dollar return. The zloty is the reason: EUR/PLN tested 4.35, the euro's highest since November 2024 (Interia Biznes, Business Insider Polska, [Jul 17]), with FXMAG reporting the zloty and Polish bonds "strongly down" ([Jul 17]); the card has USD/PLN 3.7782 (-0.2% 1W, +1.8% 4W) and EUR/PLN 4.328 (0.0% / +1.7%). The week's POLGB auction "again did not go as it should" (wnp.pl, [Jul 16]).

Fiscal & Politics: The half-year deficit of 123.7 billion zloty (Gazeta Prawna, [Jul 16]) framed everything: GlobEnergia costs the CPN package at 4.7 billion zloty for 92 days of relief ([Jul 17]), Domański quantified what presidential vetoes cost and said they weaken the economy's potential (TVN24, Wiadomości Onet, [Jul 17]), and public debt is 2.5 trillion zloty (wPolityce.pl, [Jul 14]). The policy response is heading toward the banks: Miesięcznik Finansowy BANK pushed back that "excess liquidity is not profit" and that further CIT increases would weaken lenders' ability to finance the economy ([Jul 18]), a direct reply to the proposed CIT hike (pb.pl, [Jul 14]). Energy politics escalated on Saturday: the president filed for a referendum on energy prices (wnp.pl, [Jul 18]) a day after the Sejm rejected his VAT cut on electricity (Bankier.pl, [Jul 17]), while the government published draft tariffs giving cheaper power to industry (Bankier.pl, Rzeczpospolita, [Jul 18]). Tax rates for 2027 are settled (wnp.pl, [Jul 17]).

Corporate & Sectors: Banks end the week with two open fronts. Nawrocki signed the CHF law allowing borrowers who sue to stop repaying, which Franknews expects to trigger a new wave of lawsuits ([Jul 18]; Next Gazeta.pl, [Jul 17]); the NBP had warned of exactly that (Parkiet, [Jul 14]). The benchmark reform has stalled, and the sector now wants the NBP reference rate written into mortgage law in place of WIBOR (Gazeta Prawna, [Jul 18]). Mortgage demand is still solid, with a record average loan and some banks lending over 1.3 million zloty (Rzeczpospolita, [Jul 16]; Direct Money, [Jul 15]). Energy: Orlen adopted a biodiversity plan to 2030 (Energetyka24, [Jul 18]), the Sejm passed the Water Law amendment Orlen Termika sought (wnp.pl, [Jul 17]), and the nuclear plant on Pomerania is accelerating with a new rail link planned (Zawsze Pomorze, gwe24.pl, [Jul 18]). Retail: Dino's dispute with unions remains a scandal (INNPoland.pl, [Jul 17]) and Allegro and InPost are negotiating again (DlaHandlu.pl, [Jul 16]).

Geopolitics & Security: Fakt cautions against "overdoing the enthusiasm" about what Zelensky actually ordered regarding Poland ([Jul 18]), after a week in which support for helping Ukraine fell from 77% to 52% (Rzeczpospolita, [Jul 16]) and attacks on Ukrainians in Poland drew warnings of more to come (Fakt, [Jul 16]). Washington detailed a new sanctions package on Russia (Rzeczpospolita, [Jul 17]).

New / Most Important: The most important thing this week is a role reversal: the bank sector that carried WIG20 to records is now the policy shock absorber, facing a CIT increase, a signed CHF law and a stalled benchmark reform, while the NBP talks hikes on an oil shock the budget cannot afford to subsidise. A flat EPOL against a record WIG20 says international investors have already noticed. The referendum filing on energy prices adds a political layer to what was a fiscal problem.

Playbook ✓: Hold pattern with hawkish guidance into an oil shock is standard CEE central-bank behaviour; bank underperformance on tax and litigation risk is orthodox.  |  ⚠️ Divergence: Inflation on target, core falling, yet the Council's public voices talk hikes and the zloty is at a post-2024 low: the market is pricing fiscal dominance, not price pressure. If the government reaches for bank CIT to close a 123.7 billion zloty gap, it taxes the sector that funds the deficit, and the auction stress already visible this week would be the first symptom.
