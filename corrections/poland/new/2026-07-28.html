🇵🇱 Poland  Mixed  → hold

Macro Stance: The cut debate is moving from "when" to "whether". Citi now expects CPI to reach 3% as early as July on fuel prices (PAP Biznes, [Jul 28]) and Business Insider Polska warned inflation could breach the NBP's limit with more price rises to come [Jul 28], while FXMAG modelled diesel at 9 or 10 zł and inflation near 10% in a long Middle East conflict [Jul 24]. That lands on a Council already turning: Kotecki said the inflation picture is diverging from the NBP projection and it is too early to promise a September cut (pb.pl, [Jul 23]), a hawk called the earlier cuts premature (Gazeta Prawna, [Jul 21]) and one member put hikes ahead of cuts (Super Biznes, [Jul 21]); wnp.pl summed it up as a big change on rates from what was supposed to happen [Jul 23]. The backward-looking data remain strong: inflation fell to 3% (Obserwator Gospodarczy, [Jul 22]), yet June retail sales grew 6.2% y/y against a 5.2% consensus (Do Rzeczy, [Jul 22]), ING BSK sees Q2 GDP near 3.8% y/y (StockWatch.pl, [Jul 22]), Business Insider Polska noted no large EU economy is growing faster [Jul 28]. Unemployment fell in June (Rzeczpospolita, [Jul 23]) while wage growth slowed to its lowest since 2021 (FOCUS ON Business, [Jul 23]).

Markets: Rotation, not breakout. After Monday's Żabka collapse of around 14% on Seven & i ending stake talks (Briefs Finance, [Jul 27]), Tuesday saw the WIG and WIG20 close slightly lower with Dino sharply higher (XYZ, [Jul 28]): Orlen fell 3% while Dino gained 4.13% and CD Projekt 3.67% (FXMAG, [Jul 28]), Orlen and PGE weighed most (Inwestycje.pl, [Jul 28]) and PGE slipped back below 10 zł (DNA Rynków, [Jul 28]). Orlen paid a record dividend of 8 zł per share (Bankier.pl, [Jul 28]). The card still shows an energy-led month: Orlen at 151.0 is up 3.0% on the week and 20.8% over four weeks, PGE at 10.25 is up 8.1% and 8.2%, Allegro at 45.0 is up 21.1% in four weeks and LPP at 20600 is up 3.5% and 11.4%; banks are firmer, PKO at 108.0 (+1.2% 1W, +4.5% 4W) and Pekao at 237.6 (+2.2% 1W), and KGHM at 302.5 is still 5.8% below four weeks ago. EPOL closed at 41.07, up 1.5% on the week and 7.3% over four weeks. The zloty gave back ground: USD/PLN closed at 3.7994 (+0.1% 1W, +1.2% 4W) and EUR/PLN at 4.3195 (-0.3% 1W, +0.8% 4W), and CEO Magazyn reported the dollar breaking 3.80 PLN as markets wait for the Fed [Jul 28]; Bankier.pl sees no let-up from a strong dollar and dear euro [Jul 28]. Bonds remain the sore point after yields jumped sharply late last week (Bankier.pl, [Jul 24]); the Treasury published its August savings-bond offer (Gov.pl, [Jul 27]), which Subiektywnie o finansach reads as a test of whether banks will pay savers better ([Jul 27]).

Fiscal & Politics: The finance ministry retreated on both flanks in one day. It dropped a package of PIT and CIT anti-avoidance measures covering lump-sum taxation, company cars, the housing relief and IP Box (Rzeczpospolita, [Jul 28]; Prawo.pl, [Jul 28]) and at the same time published costings for a higher tax-free allowance, running to billions of zloty (Interia Biznes, [Jul 28]; TVN24, [Jul 28]), with Business Insider Polska putting the gain at up to 433 zł per taxpayer [Jul 28]. This comes after debt reached 61.6% of GDP, the third fastest rise in the EU (XYZ, [Jul 21]), Treasury debt passed 2.1 trillion zł (Do Rzeczy, [Jul 24]) and Rzeczpospolita wrote that Poland forgot to deleverage and will now find it hard to cut debt and deficit [Jul 28]; Arak sees more than 70% of GDP by end-2027 (Filary Biznesu, [Jul 26]). The fuel standoff continues: the windfall-profits tax sits at the Constitutional Tribunal with 4 billion zł of CPN funding blocked (Gov.pl, [Jul 27]), the Prime Minister spoke of bringing the CPN package back and Orlen responded (Money.pl, [Jul 28]), the government is considering regulated fuel prices (GlobEnergia, [Jul 28]).

Corporate & Sectors: Bank Millennium posted more than 400 million zł of profit but management is cautious on the autumn (Rzeczpospolita, [Jul 28]), set aside 61 million zł more for consumer-credit disputes (Bankier.pl, [Jul 28]) and expects a slight NIM decline with cost of risk possibly below 40-50 bp (Bankier.pl, [Jul 28]). The CJEU hardened its WIBOR case law (Rzeczpospolita, [Jul 28]), millions of loan contracts must be amended within two months (Money.pl, [Jul 28]) and banks surprised clients by raising deposit rates (Interia Biznes, [Jul 28]). Allegro is buying back 100 million zł of its shares (wirtualnemedia.pl, [Jul 28]) a day after naming Katarzyna Ostap-Tomann CFO (Parkiet, [Jul 27]). LPP Logistics opened a Romanian distribution centre sized for 35 million items (Strefa Biznesu, [Jul 28]). PGE confirmed the Rybnik coal units run to 2028 (Dziennik Zachodni, [Jul 27]).

Geopolitics & Security: Poland denied any territorial claims on Ukraine and reaffirmed support for Kyiv (logos-pres.md, [Jul 28]) and the army took delivery of new weapons as Ukrainians flagged a major threat (Forsal.pl, [Jul 28]). The backdrop is Belarusian special forces on the Ukrainian border (TVP Info, [Jul 22]) and troops reported at the Polish frontier (Kresy24.pl, [Jul 23]), Warsaw's warning that Russia may use captured Ukrainian drones against Poland and the Baltics (Defense News, [Jul 21]).

New / Most Important: The finance ministry's double retreat is the development: shelving PIT and CIT tightening while costing a higher tax-free allowance means the fiscal response to debt above 60% of GDP is looser, not tighter, on the same day Citi pulled its 3% CPI call forward to July. The NBP is being left as the only brake, which is why Kotecki's projection warning matters more than the June retail beat.  |  Playbook ✓: Hold stands; the ECB's hawkish hold narrows the NBP's room to diverge. Prefer banks and domestic retail, which absorbed Tuesday's rotation out of Orlen and PGE, and keep duration short.  |  ⚠️ Divergence: The dollar above 3.80 and yields at highs say risk-off, yet EPOL is up 7.3% in four weeks and Dino and CD Projekt rallied into a flat index; Orlen's record 8 zł dividend against a 3% drop and a revived CPN package says the market is pricing the politics, not the payout.
