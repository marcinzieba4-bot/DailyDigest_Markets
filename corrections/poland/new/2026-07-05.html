🇵🇱 Poland  Mixed  → on hold

Macro Stance: The NBP is in a holding pattern the data justify from both sides. June CPI printed 2.5% per the GUS flash, at target and below forecasts (Parkiet, [Jun 30]), with GUS crediting fruit, vegetables and cheap imports (Bankier.pl, [Jul 2]). Commerzbank noted that rate hike bets have vanished, while ING sees the market starting to play for cuts in the second half of 2026 [Jun 30]. Ahead of the RPP's July meeting, CEO Magazyn expects rates most likely unchanged [Jul 2]. The reason not to front-run a cut is fuel: Gazeta Prawna warned that the end of the CPN package could lift inflation toward 3% [Jun 30], and INFOR.PL flagged a double monthly hit to electricity bills [Jul 5]. The reason to keep a cut on the table is activity: the manufacturing PMI dropped to 46.1 in June against a 49.4 consensus (Do Rzeczy, [Jul 1]). Wages remain sticky: construction is paying up to 36 thousand zł to find workers (Money.pl, [Jul 2]) and unions called the 2027 minimum wage proposal a "mockery" (Dziennik.pl, [Jul 4]).

Markets: Warsaw enters the week at records. The WIG20 added 7.38% in the second quarter, its third consecutive positive quarter (FXMAG, [Jul 1]), Żabka, XTB and GPW itself set all-time highs (StockWatch.pl, [Jul 2]), and Friday's session delivered a 1.57% index gain with 19 of 20 members up (FXMAG, [Jul 3]). The card's weekly scoreboard: Orlen 132.92 (+7.7% 1W, -7.0% 4W) after a 4.78% jump on a record net profit (Investing.com Polska, [Jul 3]); CD Projekt 233.4 (+7.4% 1W) on Cyberpunk 2077 passing 40 mn copies (PAP Biznes, [Jul 3]) and its best Steam interest since 2023 (PPE, [Jul 5]); Allegro 38.44 (+3.3% 1W, +9.5% 4W); KGHM 335.0 (+2.2% 1W, -3.0% 4W); Pekao 233.2 (+1.9% 1W); PKO 105.24 (+1.6% 1W, +7.1% 4W); LPP 18,550 (+1.3% 1W, -14.1% 4W); PGE 9.63 (+0.3% 1W, -6.1% 4W). Dino is the lone loser at 27.7 (-4.4% 1W, -6.3% 4W), its lowest since 2022 after Barclays cut its target on slower expansion (pb.pl, [Jul 3]). EPOL's last close was 39.44 (+2.3% 1W, -1.3% 4W). The zloty is the weak link: EUR/PLN tested 4.30 on the inflation surprise (wnp.pl, [Jul 1]) before a soft US payrolls print sank the dollar and let the zloty recover (Comparic.pl, [Jul 3]); the card shows USD/PLN 3.7522 (-0.4% 1W, +2.9% 4W) and EUR/PLN 4.2859 (+0.2% 1W, +1.2% 4W). Inwestycje.pl argued the high budget deficit could weigh on the zloty in the second half [Jul 3]. POLGBs have support: investors returned to Polish debt and bought bonds worth billions in May (Rzeczpospolita, [Jul 3]).

Fiscal & Politics: The fuel shield is the live fiscal fight. The CPN package has ended with pump prices up around 60 groszy per litre (wPolityce.pl, [Jul 1]), the government intends to recoup the 4 bn zł cost through a windfall tax on fuel companies (Biznes Wprost, [Jul 2]), and PiS has tabled a bill to cut VAT and excise on fuel for the holiday season (RadioMaryja.pl, [Jul 2]). The Sejm passed the OKI personal investment account with up to 100 thousand zł sheltered from the Belka tax (Gazeta Prawna, [Jul 3]). The sovereign picture is the overhang: the EU convergence report ranked Poland's public finances the worst in the bloc (wPolityce.pl, [Jun 30]), and Do Rzeczy carried a warning that Poland could go the way of Greece [Jul 3]. The finance minister admitted there is "no chemistry" with the president (Business Insider Polska, [Jul 2]).

Corporate & Sectors: Banks have a date for the end of WIBOR: the finance ministry confirmed when the benchmark disappears for borrowers (Bankier.pl, [Jul 5]), which Money.pl called a seismic change for Poles with loans [Jul 5]. That follows the TSUE ruling of Jul 2 preserving lenders' right to recover disbursed capital on void CHF loans, which Rzeczpospolita said had banks opening champagne [Jul 2]. Energy policy was reset over the weekend: Tusk declared the end of coal and named Bełchatów and Konin as good locations for the second nuclear plant (Bankier.pl, [Jul 4]), with Bankier.pl's follow-up arguing coal guarantees security today and nuclear must secure it tomorrow [Jul 5]; Orlen switched on a farm supplying 100 thousand homes (Money.pl, [Jul 4]). KGHM's Strategy 2055+ carries over 32 bn zł of investment to 2030 (Inwestycje.pl, [Jul 3]) and a polyhalite mine near Puck on a 300 mn tonne deposit (Money.pl, [Jul 3]), though the company is in mourning after a fatal accident at the Głogów smelter (wnp.pl, [Jul 5]). Dino added nearly 150 stores in the half (Omnichannel News, [Jul 3]) yet faces OPZZ strike threats (Biznes Wprost, [Jul 3]), and housing prices beat inflation again (Obserwator Gospodarczy, [Jul 5]).

Geopolitics & Security: The US warning that Russia is discussing an armed provocation on Polish territory to test NATO (The Telegraph, [Jul 3]) is being digested: Politico reported from the eastern flank [Jul 5], EUobserver argued an attack on Poland would be Putin's last failure [Jul 5], and The Baltic Sentinel called Poland's warnings alarming but unclear [Jul 5]. Tusk said the coming months may be "critical" (BBC, [Jul 3]) and told Bloomberg Poland should be cautious in pledging aid for Ukraine [Jul 3], while The Telegraph wrote that Zelensky has a Poland problem [Jul 5]. MON will declassify all donations to Ukraine from 2022 to 2026 (Strefa Obrony, [Jul 5]).

New / Most Important: No material change: lower inflation makes a future cut more likely but timing remains unclear, and the weekend's substance was policy rather than data. The WIBOR end-date and the CHF ruling together remove two legal overhangs from the bank sector in one week, which matters more for PKO and Pekao than the rate path, and Tusk's coal exit with a two-site nuclear shortlist is a decade-long capex commitment landing on a budget the EU already rates worst in the bloc. The zloty, not equities, is where that fiscal story will be priced.  |  Playbook ✓: PLN capped by dovish repricing is consensus; banks benefit from legal clarity regardless of the RPP.  |  ⚠️ Divergence: Fuel and power bills rising into a 2.5% print mean headline CPI has likely troughed; a market playing for cuts while energy costs re-accelerate is the mismatch to watch.
