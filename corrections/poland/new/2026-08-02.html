🇵🇱 Poland  Mixed  → hold

Macro Stance: The weekend reading of Friday's GUS flash is settling into consensus: July CPI rose to 3.0% y/y (Miesięcznik Finansowy BANK, [Jul 31]) with fuel up around 14% in a month (Obserwator Gospodarczy, [Jul 31]) after the government's fuel shield expired (Magazyn MANAGER+, [Aug 1]), while food prices fell and are what keeps the index in check (xyz.pl, [Aug 2]). The policy read is uniform: Global Banking & Finance Review [Jul 31] says the print strengthens the rate-hold outlook, both the Rzeczpospolita and Parkiet economist panels [Jul 31] say the RPP should not rush to cut and Citi's above-3.5% year-end call stands as the direct read-through from the global oil spike. Growth makes patience cheap: GUS statistics show Poland growing twice as fast as the EU as a whole (Agro Profil, [Jul 31]). Politically the fuel shock is biting: 54% of Poles rate the government's handling of fuel prices badly in a new DGP poll (Gazeta Prawna, [Aug 2]). Stance: hold, with the earlier market hopes for H2 cuts now looking stale.

Markets: The week closed on a record. Business Insider Polska [Jul 31] wrote that July 31 went into the history of the Polish exchange; FXMAG [Jul 31] had WIG20 a step from the 4000-point barrier with KGHM and banks pulling it up, and Bankier [Jul 31] summed up a superb month with WIG20 one step from the record and Żabka taken over. Friday's leadership rotated away from banks: Żabka led the gains with energy names strongly higher (Inwestycje.pl, [Jul 31]), which the card confirms with PGE back above 10 at 10.11 (-3.5% 1W, +5.0% 4W) and Orlen 148.04 (-5.1% 1W, +11.4% 4W) steadying after Comparic [Jul 31] asked whether its post-rally slide is a correction or something bigger. Banks gave a little back but keep the weekly lead: PKO 111.92 (+5.0% 1W, +6.3% 4W), Pekao 245.5 (+5.0% 1W, +5.3% 4W). Consumer and growth names led the month: Dino 31.99 (+11.2% 1W, +15.5% 4W), LPP 21,720 (+7.8% 1W, +17.1% 4W), CD Projekt 249.9 (+10.5% 1W, +7.1% 4W), Allegro 44.99 (+17.0% 4W); KGHM 307.95 is +1.5% 1W but still -8.1% 4W. EPOL 42.83 is +4.7% 1W and +8.6% 4W. The zloty is firm: USD/PLN 3.7357 (-1.8% 1W, -0.4% 4W) with the dollar back near 3.75 after the Fed (Magazyn MANAGER+, [Jul 31]), EUR/PLN 4.3051 (-0.6% 1W, +0.4% 4W), and ING argues the zloty has room to rally once the dollar squeeze passes (Exchange Rates UK, [Aug 1]). Bonds: yields had risen worryingly ahead of the Fed (Subiektywnie o finansach, [Jul 29]).

Fiscal & Politics: The Sejm granted the government discharge for the 2025 budget amid a bitter row over the deficit (Strefa Biznesu, [Jul 31]), and the debt argument has turned into a blame game: President Nawrocki is pushing a deficit "hammer" with penalties for politicians who overspend (BitHub.pl, [Aug 1]), while government communicators try to pin the state of public finances on the president (wPolityce.pl, [Aug 1]). The numbers behind the fight: debt above 2 trillion zł (naTemat.pl, [Jul 27]), Arak's forecast of more than 70% of GDP by end-2027 (Filary Biznesu, [Jul 26]) and wnp.pl's [Jul 26] warning of EU sanctions. Fuel policy is stuck: Domański says the CPN package has no financing after the president's decision (Money.pl, [Jul 31]), even as Bloomberg [Jul 27] reported the government considering revived fuel-price caps. The higher tax-free allowance may be spread over three years (Business Insider Polska, [Jul 31]) and Domański revealed Trump raised tariffs on Poland over the digital tax (Do Rzeczy, [Jul 31]).

Corporate & Sectors: Couche-Tard's roughly $8.7 billion purchase of Żabka, Poland's biggest-ever deal (Reuters, [Jul 31]), dominates; Subiektywnie o finansach [Jul 31] weighs the consequences of a Circle K owner controlling the chain. Banks: Pekao's results show a machine at full speed but a new risk (Subiektywnie o finansach, [Jul 31]), four banks nudged rates up despite the RPP hold (Bankier, [Jul 31]) and a CJEU and Supreme Court turn in CHF cases gives banks a chance to gain (Interia Biznes, [Jul 31]). Consumer-credit litigation is the counterweight: Millennium has stopped charging interest on loan costs and clients may recover overpayments (Franknews.pl, [Aug 2]); borrowers get 60 days to choose a new rate formula (wnp.pl, [Jul 31]). Housing: the government offers new home loans at a 400 zł instalment over 15 years (Portal Samorządowy, [Aug 2]) alongside BGK guarantees (Bankier, [Jul 30]). Retail: Dino and its unions set new mediation dates (Farmer.pl, [Aug 1]) after the labour ministry intervened (Business Insider Polska, [Jul 30]); Allegro was ordered to change how it shows delivery options (Telepolis.pl, [Aug 1]). Energy: gas-fired generation is up 19% (GlobEnergia, [Aug 1]), the government adopted the NECP update with new duties for energy-intensive firms from 2027 (INFOR.PL, [Aug 1]), and Hungary is shutting the Paks nuclear plant as the Danube dries (XYZ, [Aug 2]), a regional supply shock.

Geopolitics & Security: The missile aftermath continues: the military explained why the Russian Kh-101 was not shot down (WNP.PL, [Aug 1]), the foreign ministry answered Moscow (Fakt, [Aug 1]), and EUobserver [Aug 1] framed the six-minute scare as proof NATO spots threats fast but decides slowly. Deputy defence minister Tomczyk said nobody will build a Patriot factory where bombs fall as Trump refuses Ukraine (Wyborcza.pl, [Aug 2]); 100 kilometres of the eastern fortification line are ready (RMF24, [Jul 31]).

New / Most Important: The fiscal blame game. With CPI back at 3% on fuel, the one thing that could blunt it, a financed CPN package, died on the president's desk, and the two branches are now arguing over who ruined the public finances rather than how to fund anything. For bondholders a president demanding a deficit hammer is marginally supportive; for equities it means no fiscal cushion if oil runs again, and the record-high index is leaning entirely on earnings and the Żabka bid.

Playbook ✓: Hold posture consistent with sticky inflation; the record close with rotation into energy and consumer names is healthy breadth, not a top.  |  ⚠️ Divergence: The oil pass-through risk makes any near-term easing impossible despite earlier market hopes for H2 cuts — and with no financed fuel shield, the next leg of pump prices goes straight into CPI.
