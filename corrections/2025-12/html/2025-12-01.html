🌍 Geographic Macro Overview

🇺🇸 United States  Mixed  hold / data dependent

Macro Stance: The US data flow is cooling while the Fed debate tilts toward a December cut. The Beige Book pointed to a cooling labour market and softer spending ahead of the December rate decision. ADP showed private payroll losses accelerating over four weeks, while weekly jobless claims fell to a seven-month low. Consumer confidence fell by the most since April as job worries grew. The October CPI release was cancelled and the BEA dropped its advance Q3 GDP estimate, so the Fed is deciding with less inflation data than usual.

Rates & Curve: The 2-year Treasury closed the prior session at 3.47% and the 10-year at 4.02%, a 10Y-2Y spread of 55 bps (US Treasury, prior close 2025-11-28). Over 20 observations the 2-year is down 12 bps and the 10-year down 6 bps, so the spread is up 6 bps; over five observations both are down 8 bps. The 3-month bill sits at 3.88%, which leaves the 10Y-3M spread at 14 bps, and the 30-year is at 4.67%, 65 bps over the 10-year (US Treasury, prior close 2025-11-28). The curve is positively sloped but still flat by historical standards (long-run average about +90 to +100 bps), and the 20-observation move is a bull steepening (both down, 2Y down more); the writer’s reading is that the market is pricing a cut rather than a growth scare. The S&P 500 closed at 6,849.09, up 4.75% over five sessions but only 0.39% over 20, and the VIX at 16.35 is down 0.56 points over 20 sessions (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2025-11-28).

New / Most Important: The most important item is who runs the Fed: Trump says he has made his choice to lead the Federal Reserve, with five finalists named and Kevin Hassett reported as the favourite. Investors see a Hassett-led Fed as a risk to the dollar, and the dollar was set for its biggest weekly fall in four months as the Fed focus sharpened. The Fed’s own split is showing up in rate options hedging. The 20-session change in the dollar index is only down 0.07% at 99.46 (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2025-11-28), so the currency has not yet re-priced the cut. Gold touched a six-week high on the same cut bets while silver hit a record.

Playbook ✓: Stay neutral on duration until the December decision and the chair pick are known. Buy the 2-year for the cut case, and hold a smaller 10-year position as the hedge against a hawkish chair signal, because the 10-year has only moved 6 bps over 20 observations. Equities need confirmation: the S&P 500 is up 4.75% over five sessions but barely changed over 20, so add exposure only if the gain holds into the decision. The consensus anchor is the 7,500 year-end 2026 call from JPMorgan and HSBC, which argues for buying dips rather than chasing. What would change the call is a Fed chair signal that puts the cut in doubt, which would push the 2-year higher and the dollar up.

European defence stocks and gas prices fell as the US and Ukraine made progress on a peace plan, and an earlier defence-stock rally was described as crumbling on the same talks. The FT asks who killed Europe’s single-market dream, which is the structural question behind the cautious equity tone.

Playbook ✓: Mixed. Add euro-dollar exposure only if the Fed chair pick points to a weaker dollar, because the ECB has flagged a dollar squeeze risk. The trigger to turn constructive is a fresh euro-zone growth print that beats the current benign reading; the trigger to turn defensive is a euro-bank funding headline.

🇨🇳 China  Bearish  targeted support / weak demand

Macro Stance: China’s domestic demand is weak and the policy response is targeted rather than broad. The factory data are soft: the AP reports China’s factory activity contracting for an eighth month in November despite the trade truce. The property and credit data are weaker still, with new bank loans falling sharply in October and missing forecasts. The government is responding with consumption measures rather than a large credit push: a 19-point plan names “hotspot” sectors to spur consumption. The Chinese equity market is down over 20 sessions, which the writer reads as the market’s verdict on that response.

PMIs & Activity: The latest factory reading in the feed is November: the AP reports China’s factory activity contracting for an eighth month in November despite the trade truce, and Bloomberg describes the factory slump as the longest stretch on record. A private survey was read two ways: CNBC reported the November factory gauge unexpectedly contracting and missing estimates, while a second CNBC headline said activity edged up but stayed in contraction as holiday demand faded. Those headlines conflict on the direction, so the clean read is that manufacturing remains in contraction, with services the weaker leg. Industrial profits fell in October after two months of strong growth, although a China Daily headline describes steady growth in industrial profits over the first 10 months.

Credit Impulse: The latest credit print in the feed is October. New bank loans fell sharply and missed the forecast, and the Bloomberg read is that China saw its worst credit growth in a year as demand dried up. The WSJ reports the worst investment decline in years as the slowdown continued. The feed carries no November credit print, so the next data release is the test. The policy signal is to let credit slow: Goldman sees a longer easing pause after the PBOC changed its mind on policy, and the central bank’s confidence messaging has raised the question of whether rate cuts are on the back burner.

Property: Property is still deteriorating, with official prices falling at the fastest pace in a year in October, and a Bloomberg headline says the home-price slump deepened as stimulus failed to halt the slide. CNBC described housing weakness intensifying alongside falling investment. The developer Vanke is the stress point: it sought a first extension on an onshore bond payment, and the bonds slumped, then the bonds plunged on a plan to delay repayment. Vanke also pledged its entire stake in its management unit to a state backer, which the writer reads as state support rather than a market solution. UBS’s property optimist retreated to a deeper slump call.

Fiscal, Policy & Trade: Fiscal policy is aimed at consumption and high-tech industries rather than housing. The 19-point plan names hotspot sectors, and China’s planning agency warned of too many robots in the economy. The trade picture is calmer: the US extended tariff exclusions on some Chinese industrial goods as part of the trade truce, and Trump says he will visit China in April after a call with Xi. The yuan is firmer, with China setting its strongest yuan fix in over a year as Fed-cut bets rise. The writer’s reading is that currency strength is a policy tool rather than a growth signal, which limits the room for the PBOC to cut rates aggressively.

Markets & Flows: The Hang Seng closed at 25,858.89, down 0.18% over 20 sessions, and the Shanghai Composite at 3,888.60 is down 1.67% over the same window (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2025-11-28). The CSI 300 ETF ASHR is down 2.83% over 20 sessions at 32.58, the FXI large-cap ETF is down 1.54% at 39.53, and the internet ETF KWEB is down 6.59% at 37.57 (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2025-11-28). USD/CNY closed at 7.0758 and is down 0.48% over 20 sessions (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2025-11-28). Flows point to state support: national-team-tied ETFs were among the most bought in the selloff, and JPMorgan upgraded China’s stocks on several positive drivers.

New / Most Important: The most important new item is the crackdown on crypto. The PBOC vowed a crackdown on virtual currency and flagged stablecoin concerns, and Hong Kong stablecoin stocks slumped on the warning. The writer’s reading is that the PBOC is tightening the perimeter around crypto rather than easing. The PBOC is also pushing yuan borrowing abroad to internationalise the currency, and IMF researchers flagged currency risks as nations swap dollar loans into yuan. This is the policy thread to watch alongside the property stress.

Playbook ✓: Bearish on domestic cyclicals and property. Avoid developers and the onshore bond market until Vanke’s repayment path is clear, and keep banks at underweight while credit growth is at a one-year low. Keep a tactical position in internet and China AI names, because the sector’s valuation is still below US peers and Goldman argues the rally has room, but size it for a drawdown, since KWEB is down 6.59% over 20 sessions. The trigger to add is a credit print that stops falling; the trigger to exit is a further property price drop in the next release. ⚠ Divergence: JPMorgan’s upgrade of China equities and the national-team buying point up, while most macro prints point down; the exception is the China Daily headline that industrial profits recorded steady growth over the first 10 months.

🇯🇵 Japan  Mixed  hike bias / fiscal expansion

Macro Stance: The Bank of Japan is leaning toward another hike. Governor Ueda sent a clear hint at a chance of a December rate hike, and Noguchi has advocated gradual increases. Tokyo core inflation has brought the BOJ closer to a hike. The fiscal side is expansionary: the government finalised a $117 billion extra budget mostly funded by debt, and Japan is issuing $75 billion in new bonds to fund the stimulus, with more short-term debt to fund Takaichi’s stimulus. The result is a central bank tightening into a looser fiscal stance, which is the tension the yen is trading.

Activity & Currency: USD/JPY closed at 156.2840 and is up 1.55% over 20 sessions (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2025-11-28). A Reuters headline says the dollar eased against the yen as the BOJ hinted at a hike, and a Morning Bid note says the rate-hike prospect arrested the yen’s decline, for now. Japan’s finance minister says FX moves are not based on fundamentals. Activity is mixed: capex growth slowed to 2.9% in Q3 but pointed to resilient demand, and the manufacturing PMI decline slowed in November. The Nikkei 225 closed at 50,253.91, down 2.05% over 20 sessions (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2025-11-28).

Playbook ✓: Mixed. Buy yen on dips only if the December hike is confirmed; a hike with the 156 level holding would argue for a short USD/JPY position with a tight stop. Keep Japanese equity exposure in AI memory and capex names, since Micron’s $9.6 billion investment in western Japan is a concrete capex signal, and hedge the currency sensitivity of the broad Nikkei. The trigger to turn bearish on the yen is a BOJ decision that is not followed by a hike; the trigger to turn bullish is a weaker dollar after the US chair decision.
