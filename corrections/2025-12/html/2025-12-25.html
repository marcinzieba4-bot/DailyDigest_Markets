🇺🇸 United States  Mixed  hold / data dependent

Macro Stance: The November payrolls print showed 64,000 jobs added after a loss of 105,000 in October, and the unemployment rate reached a four-year high. The November consumer-price read was delayed and distorted by missing data, but headline inflation still came in at a 2.7% annual rate, below forecasts. Third-quarter GDP grew 4.3%, the fastest pace in two years, so growth and labour-market weakness are pulling the Fed in opposite directions. Consumer confidence fell for a fifth straight month, yet spending held up. We stay Mixed because the data do not yet say which of those two signals will dominate the first print of 2026.

Equities confirm a risk-on tape: the S&P 500 closed at 6,932.05, up 2.46% over 20 sessions (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2025-12-24), and the VIX at 13.47 is down 27.42% over the same window (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2025-12-24). New / Most Important: The most important new item is the tone from the Fed's own ranks. Hammack said a pause in rate cuts is her base case for now and reportedly prefers holding rates steady into the spring. Against that, Fed chair candidate Hassett said the US is way behind the curve in lowering rates, and the list of frontrunners for the top Fed job is still open. Markets leaned toward cuts regardless. We read the Fed's first repo operation since 2020, of $6.8 billion, as a liquidity signal, and the S&P 500 closed at a record while traders priced rate cuts. The 10Y moved little after the GDP report, so the bond market did not buy the growth upgrade as a reason to sell duration.

Hedge the tail with a small long in TLT or IEF if the next payrolls print confirms the unemployment rise. What would change the call: a jobs print that re-tightens the labour market, which would push the 2Y up and flatten the curve, or a Fed chair pick whose stance moves the 2Y back below its 20-session reference level of 3.43% (US Treasury, prior close 2025-12-24).

🇪🇺 European Union  Bearish  steady rates / weak growth

Macro Stance: The ECB left its key rate unchanged at 2.15%, and the final meeting of 2025 was read as a hold rather than a turn. The ECB upgraded its growth outlook and closed the door to more cuts, and a Reuters poll has the ECB reported as likely to stay on hold through the end of 2026 in a Reuters poll. Euro-zone inflation was 2.2% in the flash for November, so the price picture gives no reason to cut. The growth picture is patchy: German manufacturing contracted again and French private-sector growth slowed to a crawl in the December flash PMI. Growth in the single currency is therefore less broad than the ECB's upgrade implies.

Activity & Inflation: Germany's fiscal impulse is the bright spot in the headlines: the government launched a 30 billion euro fund to mobilise private investment, and the Bundesbank expects the German economy to strengthen markedly in 2026. The underlying picture is harder. German business bankruptcies hit a decade high, households are saving at the highest rate since 2008, and VW is ending production at a German plant for the first time in company history. The EU-Mercosur deal signing has been delayed as Italy asked for more time, and German industry voiced frustration at the delay.

New / Most Important: The newest item is the EU's agreement on Ukraine financing. Leaders agreed a 90 billion euro loan without using frozen Russian assets, after pressure to use the assets. Lagarde warned against breaking EU law with the loan and said she was fully confident the reparation plan would be agreed. For markets, the loan removes a tail risk on the euro's fiscal backstop but leaves the funding question open. Note the contrast with the Bank of England, which cut key rates from 4% to 3.75% after a tight vote and signalled caution about further moves; sterling keeps rising after the BoE raised the bar on rate cuts.

Playbook ✓: Stance Bearish on the euro area's cyclical exposure. Prefer domestic-services exposure over German export industrials, since the export model is under pressure from both US and Chinese demand. Hedge the euro at the current policy rate with a long-dated payer rather than selling rates outright, because the ECB has signalled no near-term move. What would change the call: a December flash-PMI rebound in Germany, or a Bund sell-off large enough to push the ECB's hold into a hike debate.

🇨🇳 China  Mixed  targeted support / weak demand

Macro Stance: The November data set the tone: industrial output grew 4.8% year on year while retail sales rose only 1.3%, and the retail miss was sharp enough to deepen worries about consumption. The economy stalled in November as calls for reform grew, and the Euronews read was that momentum was lost amid a slump in spending and investment. Policy is leaning toward targeted fixes rather than a broad push. The benchmark lending rates were left unchanged for a seventh straight month. We stay Mixed: the stock tape is firmer than the activity data, and that gap is the central tension of the China block.

PMIs & Activity: The latest private services reading slipped to a five-month low in November, and the most recent official data show growth losing pace rather than stopping. Retail sales growth missed estimates by a wide margin, and China's economy is being described as exposed to trade jitters because consumer frailty leaves little cushion. The cleanest activity signal in the feed is weakness in demand, not in production. Until a new print shows retail sales recovering, we would not chase the cyclical rebound in Chinese equities.

Credit Impulse: Credit expansion stayed muted as demand languished, and the policy response has focused on the quality of credit rather than its quantity. China will scrub small overdue debts from credit records to help spur lending, and it will hide bad-debt records for pandemic-hit borrowers who repay small loans. Those are measures that clean the balance sheet but do little for new borrowing. The rate freeze means the central bank is not adding monetary stimulus at the margin either.

Property: Home prices slid further in November, and the slump is persisting even as the authorities vow stability. Construction activity has fallen to its lowest level in two decades. Beijing relaxed home-purchase restrictions again, a move reported on 24 December, and the government promised city-specific fixes to clear the housing glut. Developer risk is contained for now: Vanke averted a default when bondholders approved a longer grace period, though the WSJ read the episode as evidence that property easing will be limited.

Fiscal, Policy & Trade: The trade surplus is the political problem. The record trade surplus hit $1 trillion, and the IMF urged Beijing to take the brave choice of curbing exports and boosting consumption. A protectionist response to the surplus is a possibility in 2026. Separately, the dairy tariffs of up to 42.7% are China's action against the EU. Beijing vowed to retaliate against what it called unreasonable US semiconductor tariffs. On the supply side, rare-earth export general licences are being issued, which is an incremental easing of export controls.

Markets & Flows: The Shanghai Composite closed at 3,940.95, up 1.99% over 20 sessions (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2025-12-24). The Hang Seng closed at 25,818.93, down 0.42% over 20 sessions (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2025-12-24). The CSI 300 tracker ASHR rose 1.77% over 20 sessions, while the China internet ETF KWEB fell 8.56% over the same window (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2025-12-24). USD/CNY closed at 7.0280, down 0.8% over 20 sessions (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2025-12-24). The yuan is being managed higher to shield exporters and curb the carry trade, and forecasters say the move beyond the 7 level will be brief. Sell-side views diverge: Goldman sees China tech riding the AI wave further, while Citi downgraded China stocks and lifted Taiwan on AI and earnings.

New / Most Important: The most important new signal is the central bank's guidance. The PBOC signalled steady support with a focus on long-term growth, which is not a call for new stimulus. The policy package on consumer spending is the other pillar, and the property measures are a second. Investors should read the PBOC line as ruling out a sharp easing cycle in the near term, which is why the policy support is more likely to come as targeted credit than as a rate cut.

Playbook ✓: Stance Mixed. Prefer domestic-policy beneficiaries and AI-linked hardware over broad consumer and internet names, since KWEB is down 8.56% over 20 sessions (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2025-12-24) while the Shanghai Composite is up. Hedge a long CNY position against exporter-driven yuan strength, and avoid building property-linked exposure before a sustained recovery in home prices. What would change the call: a November-to-December retail rebound, or a fresh stimulus package in credit rather than in policy headlines. ⚠ Divergence: the Shanghai Composite is up 1.99% over 20 sessions and ASHR is up 1.77% (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2025-12-24), yet retail sales missed and home prices are still falling. The stock tape is pricing a recovery the activity data do not show.
