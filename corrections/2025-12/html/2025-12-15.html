🌍 Geographic Macro Overview

🇺🇸 United States  Mixed  cuts paused, bill-buying backstop

Macro Stance: The Fed cut by 25 basis points on December 10 and signalled one more cut ahead, but the vote and the tone read as a pause rather than a restart. Reuters counts two dissenters who voted against the cut on inflation risk, while Bloomberg reports three dissents, and Powell said the economy is ‘very unusual’, with tariffs keeping goods inflation high while the labour market weakens. Labour data cooled: ADP showed private payrolls falling 32,000 in November, JOLTS openings rose to 7.7 million in October, and initial claims were 236,000 in the first week of December. The ISM manufacturing index read 48.2% for November and services 52.6%, so the cycle is split rather than broken. Core inflation on the Fed’s gauge was 2.8% in the delayed September data, which keeps any further easing conditional.

Rates & Curve: The US 2Y closed at 3.52% and the 10Y at 4.19% on the December 12 prior close, a 10Y−2Y spread of 67bps; the 3M is 3.63%, so bills still sit above the 2Y (US Treasury, prior close 2025-12-12). Over 20 observations the 2Y is 6bps lower and the 10Y 8bps higher, from 3.58% and 4.11% on 13 November, so the card labels the curve ‘moderately steep, approaching the long-run average’ and the move ‘steepening (10Y up, 2Y down/flat)’ (US Treasury, prior close 2025-12-12). Five-session moves were smaller: the 2Y fell 4bps and the 10Y rose 5bps since December 5 (US Treasury, prior close 2025-12-12). Equities were softer into the weekend: the S&P 500 closed at 6,827.41, down 1.07% in the prior session and up 1.33% over 20 sessions, while the VIX rose 5.99% to 15.74 (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2025-12-12). The dollar index sat at 98.40, down 0.79% over 20 sessions (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2025-12-12).

New / Most Important: The most important new fact is the Fed’s switch to bill buying. The Fed said it would resume net asset purchases with $40 billion in securities this month, would begin Treasury bill purchases on Dec. 12, and Reuters described the operation as technical buying to manage market liquidity. BofA argues that bill buying can help lower 10-year yields, while Barron’s says the Fed is buying billions of bills and asks readers not to call it QE. The Treasury itself bought another $12.5 billion of its own debt, a second liquidity channel. The leadership question is open: Trump said Kevin Warsh is top of his Fed chair list and that the president should be consulted on rates, and Powell said the US may be overstating jobs numbers.

Playbook ✓: Keep duration near neutral, prefer the 2Y and bills to the long end, and add 10-year exposure only if the yield rise is not explained by bill buying. The card shows the 10Y 8bps higher over 20 sessions, so the long end is where the risk sits (US Treasury, prior close 2025-12-12). For equities, own quality over the Nasdaq 100 and keep a hedge into the data: the index closed at 25,196.73, down 1.91% in the prior session but up 0.81% over 20 sessions (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2025-12-12), and a 14 December week-ahead outlook lists jobs data and CPI. Change the call if the Supreme Court tariff ruling cuts tariff revenue, a risk that sits alongside the administration racing to finalise tariff payments and hamstring possible refunds, or if a chair pick ends the bill-buying backstop.

Italy’s dispute with the ECB over gold reserves was reported resolved, and the EU approved German state aid for chip factories. A Reuters headline says the EU vote on the Mercosur trade deal is slated for the coming days.

Playbook ✓: Own euro-area banks on the simplification path and the rising-rate narrative: European banks had their best year on record, and Reuters says the AI boom is supercharging their rally. Keep Bund exposure small until the hike-or-hold question resolves, since the market prices 2026 hikes while a poll keeps the ECB on hold. Add to euro risk on a Schnabel-led hike signal that lifts EUR/USD above its 20-session base, from a close of 1.1739 (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2025-12-12). Change the view if the AT1 proposal goes ahead, because it would reshape the bank capital stack the banks are being valued on.

🇨🇳 China  Bearish  stimulus promised, demand weak

Macro Stance: China’s economy stalled in November and calls for reform are getting louder. The domestic side is the weak link: November retail sales sharply missed estimates, even though industrial output grew 4.8% and retail sales 1.3% on the year. The question for the next quarter is whether promised support arrives in time, and whether it is aimed at household consumption rather than factory output.

PMIs & Activity: The official November prints come first. The non-manufacturing PMI reached 49.5, and CNBC reports that factory activity edged up but stayed in contraction. The private survey is a second read: factory activity contracted and missed estimates, and Bloomberg called the factory slump the longest stretch on record. The private services index slipped to a five-month low. A December print showing factory activity back above the contraction line, together with a recovery in retail sales, would confirm the view that policy is not yet reaching demand.

Credit Impulse: Credit is still muted. Bloomberg reports that the expansion ‘stays muted as demand languishes’, while a Reuters poll expects November bank lending to more than double from October’s pace. That rebound is off a weak base and is not a turn. The UBS estimate of 2.4 million property foreclosures shows where the credit cost lands, and China vowed a flexible rate and RRR cuts to boost the economy in 2026. Those cuts would help credit only once they are delivered.

Property: Prices are still falling. Home prices slid further in November, and a Reuters poll expects a 3.7% fall this year, with declines extending into 2026. Vanke is the pressure point. Bondholders rejected a payment extension, raising default risk, and CNBC reports an analyst warning Vanke is on the brink of default as bailout hopes fade. Bloomberg adds that Vanke lost support from a key state-owned shareholder, which narrows the rescue route.

Fiscal, Policy & Trade: Policy is leaning toward support. China promised a fiscal boost next year and acknowledged a ‘prominent’ imbalance, and the Politburo made boosting domestic demand the top goal for 2026. Against that, the trade surplus topped $1 trillion for the first time, with exports up 5.9% in November and shipments to the US down 29%. The IMF urged Beijing to curb exports and boost consumption, and Mexico slapped tariffs of up to 50% on Chinese goods, adding to the external pressure. Consumer prices rose 0.7% in November, while producer deflation was deeper than expected.

Markets & Flows: The mainland tape is weaker than the policy headlines. The Shanghai Composite closed at 3,889.35, up 0.41% in the prior session but down 2.53% over 20 sessions (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2025-12-12). The Hang Seng closed at 25,976.79, up 1.75% in the session but down 2.24% over 20 sessions (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2025-12-12). The A-share ETF ASHR closed at 32.97, down 1.05% over 20 sessions, and the China internet ETF KWEB at 36.89, down 4.95% over 20 sessions (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2025-12-12). USD/CNY closed at 7.0579, 0.76% lower over 20 sessions, so the yuan firmed (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2025-12-12). HSBC’s $13.6 billion buyout proposal for Hang Seng Bank won a board committee’s nod.

New / Most Important: Mexico’s tariff move is the new external item. Its Senate approved the tariff hikes on Chinese and other Asian imports, and China is taking the move ‘very seriously’, while urging trade partners against tariffs as its record surplus stirs tensions. The second item is Vanke, where the bondholders’ rejection puts the government’s stability pledge to a live test. Property support and the Vanke outcome are the two numbers to watch in the next data cycle.

Playbook ✓: Stay underweight mainland domestic-demand exposure, and use Hang Seng strength to trim rather than add until stimulus shows up in the numbers. Add only after an RRR cut is implemented or December retail sales show recovery; the feed carries no December data yet. Hedge KWEB exposure while its 20-session change is down 4.95% (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2025-12-12). ⚠ Divergence: the first $1 trillion trade surplus and a 1.75% Hang Seng gain in the prior session say external demand and capital still work, while November retail sales missed.

The protection pays if USD/JPY falls below its 155.57 close after a hike with a guided path; a hold or a soft guide pushes the pair up against the hedge, toward the next whole yen level above the close (writer’s reference level). Keep Nikkei exposure on exporter earnings rather than the index, since a stronger yen is a headwind for those earnings (writer’s reading). 🔥 What Retail Is Playing

Cannabis shares put a pending policy decision in focus, a familiar event trade in which the hoped-for announcement and the eventual earnings benefit can arrive on very different timetables.

Cannabis / MSOS  "“Washington might unlock the trade”"

Why Retail Is Here: Cannabis is attracting the investor who wants a visible policy catalyst rather than another distant technology promise. CNBC reported expectations, attributed to a source, that the president could sign an order to reclassify marijuana as soon as Monday, with pot stocks surging. The appeal is a memorable before-and-after story: an official action could change the constraints surrounding an industry that has spent a long time waiting for relief. That remains an expectation rather than a completed decision. Investors should not convert a source-based timetable into a completed decision or assume that every commercial consequence would arrive with the first announcement.

What Is Driving It: The share response reported by CNBC shows that some anticipation is already in the price. That matters because an event trade can be directionally correct and still disappoint a buyer who pays after expectations have expanded. The useful questions are what action is actually proposed, how the implementation process develops and which company economics would change as a result. A broader fund such as MSOS spreads some company-specific risk while retaining exposure to the same policy disappointment. Diversification within a politically sensitive industry does not remove the common catalyst on which the holdings may be trading.

The Social Signal: The public narrative is unusually portable: a change in Washington can be described in a sentence, and a rising sector makes the opportunity easy to see. CNBC's source-based report and the accompanying share reaction provide the evidence for following that narrative. They do not establish a verified ranking of retail buying or a guaranteed decision on a particular schedule. The crowd can nonetheless interpret a plausible timetable as a reason to buy before official confirmation. That is the point at which an investor should decide whether the position is a short event trade, a long operating investment or a mixture whose risks have not been clearly separated.

Historical Analog: The analogy is the earlier sequence of cannabis policy trades, in which a possible regulatory opening first attracts specialist investors and then brings in a wider audience as the political story becomes easy to repeat. The second wave often arrives when the announcement feels close, while implementation and company profitability remain less certain. An official step can be important without completing the commercial transition that an enthusiastic valuation assumes. The lesson is to identify which part of the process the stock price is rewarding and what evidence would be needed for the next stage, rather than treating political visibility itself as an earnings estimate.

Smart Money View: The supportive professional view is that reducing regulatory constraints can improve an industry's opportunity set and justify renewed analysis of its strongest operators. The cautious view asks how much relief is already capitalised, how quickly it affects cash generation and whether competitive or financing pressures absorb part of the benefit. Prefer businesses and funds whose holdings can tolerate delay. Avoid assuming that the weakest balance sheet automatically supplies the best return because its stock has more room to rebound. Policy can change the environment in which a company operates; execution, capital allocation and the purchase price still determine how much of that change reaches an ordinary shareholder. The fund's diversification should also be checked through its actual holdings and the weight of each risk. Several companies can depend on the same policy step, leaving the portfolio less diversified than the number of positions suggests.

When the Narrative Breaks: The narrative weakens if the expected action fails to arrive, if its scope is narrower than investors assumed, or if the shares cannot hold gains after a favourable announcement. That last outcome would suggest anticipation had outrun incremental buying power. Size the position before the event rather than enlarging it because a headline makes confirmation feel inevitable. Take partial profits when the expected catalyst becomes widely accepted, and retain only the exposure justified by the next commercial step. A constructive long-term view can coexist with a decision to reduce short-term event risk. The investor should be paid for uncertainty rather than borrow against the assumption that it has disappeared.
