🇺🇸 United States  Mixed  pause, chair race ahead

Macro Stance: The Federal Reserve cut rates on December 10 but signalled a pause, with one more cut penciled in for next year. November CPI rose at a 2.7% annual rate, below forecasts, in a delayed release, yet economists said the shutdown-distorted numbers were hard to trust, and the New York Fed's Williams said technical factors pushed the reading down. Hiring is cooling: the economy added just 64,000 jobs in November, the jobless rate is rising and consumer sentiment has fallen 30% since last December. The Fed is on hold in a data set few people fully trust.

Rates & Curve: At the prior close the 2Y yield was 3.48% and the 10Y 4.16%, a 10Y−2Y spread of 68bps (US Treasury, prior close 2025-12-19). Over 20 observations the 2Y is down 7bps, the 10Y is up 6bps and the spread has widened 13bps, so the curve is 'moderately steep, approaching the long-run average' and the move is 'steepening (10Y up, 2Y down/flat)' (US Treasury, prior close 2025-12-19). The 3M bill at 3.62% sits 54bps below the 10Y, and the 30Y at 4.82% is 66bps above it; the 3M moved 32bps lower over 20 observations and the 30Y 9bps higher (US Treasury, prior close 2025-12-19). Equities are calm: the S&P 500 closed at 6,834.50, up 4.52% over 20 sessions, the VIX fell to 14.91 (down 43.57% over 20 sessions) and the dollar index at 98.60 is down 1.56% over the same window (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2025-12-19).

New / Most Important: The most important new development is the Fed chair search. Trump said the next chair would believe in lower rates 'by a lot', and he interviewed governor Christopher Waller, who argued that weak jobs data supports more cuts. Fed officials on the other side are more patient: Hammack says a pause in cuts is her base case for now and prefers holding steady into the spring, while Williams sees no urgency to adjust rates further. A headline on the selection calls the process a 'no-win scenario' for whoever takes the job, as the search draws to a close.

Playbook ✓: Stay near neutral duration with a barbell. Keep a small receive in the 2Y while it sits near 3.48%, because a dovish chair pick is the one near-term event that could pull cuts back into the price, and hedge with a short in the 10Y, which has risen 6bps over 20 observations (US Treasury, prior close 2025-12-19). Keep equity beta but do not chase: with the VIX at 14.91 after a 43.57% fall (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2025-12-19), the options market is pricing little stress. The call changes if the 2Y rises above its 3.55% level from 20 observations earlier (US Treasury, prior close 2025-12-19), which would mean the market is pricing out the chair's dovish tilt.

Playbook ✓: Stay neutral duration in Bunds and gilts: the ECB has signalled no cuts and the Bank of England's Christmas cut came with a warning about the next step. 🇨🇳 China  Bearish  stimulus-lite / demand weak

Macro Stance: November data show an economy losing momentum: industrial output rose 4.8% y/y while retail sales grew 1.3%, and the retail miss was sharp enough to deepen consumption worries. The economy stalled in November as calls for reform grew, and Money.pl reports the first decline in investment in decades. Trade is the offset: the surplus topped $1 trillion for the first time, and exports rose 5.9% in November while shipments to the US fell 29%. The stance is bearish on domestic demand, with an export cushion that is doing the work.

PMIs & Activity: The latest fresh surveys are for November. Factory activity slumped for the longest stretch on record, and the private factory survey unexpectedly contracted and missed estimates. Services growth slipped to a five-month low in the private PMI, and a Reuters poll had expected factory activity to shrink for an eighth month, so the November contraction was expected. The feed carries no December PMI print, so the latest available print is November. A return to expansion in the next survey would confirm the view that the slump is cyclical rather than policy-driven.

Credit Impulse: Credit is not re-accelerating. New lending demand stays muted, and China is removing small overdue debts from credit records to help lending, a sign that officials see the credit channel as the bottleneck. Long-term debt is piling up and will deepen pricing stress, according to Bloomberg, while shadow banks are back in the spotlight. The yuan's growing role as a funding currency helps offshore borrowers, but it does not revive domestic credit demand.

Property: Property is still deteriorating. Home prices slid further in November, and a Reuters poll sees prices falling 3.7% this year and extending declines into 2026. Housing construction has hit its lowest level in two decades. Vanke's bondholders approved a grace-period extension but rejected a repayment extension, and Vanke Hong Kong sold only seven units in a weekend launch. An SCMP opinion argues the market is being reconfigured rather than collapsing, which is a distinction without much comfort for developers.

Fiscal, Policy & Trade: The policy response is incremental: a consumer-support package, a slogan of 'invest in people' that signals a pivot in policy, and the credit-record clean-up. On trade, the surplus topped $1 trillion while Beijing pushes back on partners: it lowered EU pork anti-dumping duties but kept rates of up to 18.9%, and it filed a WTO case against Indian tariffs. Mexico's 50% tariff shock has shut a trade bypass that Asian exporters used, which removes one channel of the export cushion.

Markets & Flows: Equities have lagged the macro. The Shanghai Composite closed at 3,890.45 on December 19, up 1.45% over 20 sessions (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2025-12-19), while the Hang Seng at 25,690.53 is down 1.10% over five sessions (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2025-12-19). The offshore China large-cap proxy FXI is at 38.60, up 0.05% over 20 sessions, KWEB at 36.50 is up 0.75%, and ASHR at 32.34 fell 1.91% on the prior session (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2025-12-19). USD/CNY at 7.0405 is down 0.94% over 20 sessions (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2025-12-19), and a Bloomberg forecaster says yuan strength beyond the 7 level will be brief.

New / Most Important: The most important new item is the credit-record measure: China will scrub small overdue debts from credit records to help spur lending. Together with the Vanke bondholder vote, it points to a policy focused on defusing stress rather than stimulating demand. Equity risk appetite is concentrated in technology: AI-linked IPOs in China are exploding, and MetaX soared nearly 700% in its Shanghai debut. That split is the signal the stimulus debate should watch.

Playbook ✓: Stay underweight domestic cyclicals and property developers, and prefer exporters and technology-hardware names with state support. The turn to constructive needs two things: a PMI back into expansion, and a retail-sales pace that beats the 1.3% November rate. Position for the yuan to stay firm only while exports hold. ⚠ Divergence: the Shanghai Composite is up 1.45% over 20 sessions (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2025-12-19) and the yuan is firmer, even as retail sales growth slowed to 1.3% and home prices kept falling.
