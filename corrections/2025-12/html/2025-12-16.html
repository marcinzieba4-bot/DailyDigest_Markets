🌍 Geographic Macro Overview

🇺🇸 United States  Mixed  hold / data dependent

Macro Stance: The Federal Reserve cut its policy rate by 25 basis points on 10 December, its third reduction this year, and the vote was split. The Reuters read of the decision was a pause signal, with one cut next year still pencilled in. Two dissenters voted against the cut because they saw inflation risk, and Hammack said policy should be tighter given inflation running too high. Powell said the US may be overstating its job numbers, which keeps the labour-market case for further easing alive. The Fed is therefore in a data-dependent hold bias: the next move depends on the delayed payroll and CPI prints, not on the statement.

Rates & Curve: The US Treasury par curve at the 15 December close had the 2Y at 3.51% and the 10Y at 4.18%, a 10Y-2Y spread of 67 bps (US Treasury, prior close 2025-12-15). Over 20 observations the 2Y fell 11 bps and the 10Y rose 4 bps, so the spread widened 15 bps; the curve is moderately steep, approaching the long-run average, and the 20-observation move is steepening (10Y up, 2Y down/flat). The 3M sits at 3.65% and the 30Y at 4.84%, a 10Y-3M spread of 53 bps (US Treasury, prior close 2025-12-15). Treasury yields eased into the cutoff as investors looked ahead to a busy data week. Equities were calm: the S&P 500 closed at 6,816.51 with the VIX at 16.50, and the dollar index closed at 98.15, down 1.13% over 20 sessions (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2025-12-15).

New / Most Important: The calendar matters most: the delayed US employment and CPI reports, which Reuters’ 15 December explainer says still have many gaps, are the key dates. The payroll preview calls for a softening labour market, and Investopedia’s preview of Tuesday’s jobs report puts the release on the day of this briefing. The labour data already in hand is weak: ADP showed private payrolls falling by 32,000 in November, and initial jobless claims came in at 236,000. On policy, the Fed chair search is the open question: Trump could name a chair before Christmas, with Warsh now the favourite on a prediction market. Tariff revenue is large, with Customs reporting $200 billion of collections, and a Supreme Court tariff decision may be near.

Playbook ✓: Stance is Mixed. Keep duration neutral to modestly long in the 7-10Y area, where IEF closed at 96.27, down 0.18% over 20 sessions, and avoid adding to the long end, since TLT is down 1.65% over 20 sessions at 87.40. Buy equity risk only on dips: the S&P 500 is up 1.22% over 20 sessions, but the Nasdaq 100 is up only 0.24% after the AI sell-off, so leadership is narrow. The trigger to change the call is the payroll print. A print that confirms softening labour would justify adding duration and paying for curve steepening; a firm print with the Fed split would argue for cutting duration and leaning against the front end (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2025-12-15).

Macro Stance: The ECB is on hold with a hawkish drift. The Bank’s decision falls inside the 15-19 December window that the week-ahead preview flags. 🇨🇳 China  Mixed  stimulus promised / demand weak

Macro Stance: Beijing is promising more support, but the November activity data show domestic demand still weak. Industrial output rose 4.8% year on year while retail sales rose 1.3%, and CNBC called the retail miss sharp and a deepening consumption worry. Reuters summed up the month as an economy that stalled while calls for reform grew, and Euronews put it as a loss of momentum amid a spending and investment slump. The reading is a policy floor with a demand gap: growth is not yet re-accelerating.

PMIs & Activity: The feed has no December PMI, so the latest readings are November’s. The non-manufacturing PMI reached 49.5 in November, while a private survey showed factory activity contracting unexpectedly and the private services PMI slipped to a five-month low. ABN AMRO reads the November PMIs as pointing to a further slowdown. Retail and investment both weakened in November, and the monthly print is the weak one: retail sales grew 4% over the first 11 months but rose only 1.3% year on year in November. Factory deflation worsened even as consumer inflation ticked up, and what would confirm the view is a December PMI that holds above its cycle low and a retail rebound in the next monthly print.

Credit Impulse: Credit demand is muted. Bloomberg reports that China’s credit expansion stays muted as demand languishes, and Beijing vowed a flexible rate and RRR cuts to boost the economy in 2026. Bond yields may fall to a record low in 2026, SocGen says, while the dim sum bond boom extends in a push for currency ambition. The reading is that a muted credit impulse with falling long yields is a bond-market signal of weak private demand, not of reflation. The RRR cut is the policy lever to watch.

Property: Property is the central risk. Vanke’s bondholders rejected a payment extension, raising default risk, and CNBC reported an analyst warning that Vanke is on the brink of default as bailout hopes fade. The state-owned shareholder has pulled support, and the government said it would stabilise the market while the crisis deepened. Home prices keep sliding, and a Reuters poll sees home prices falling 3.7% this year.

Fiscal, Policy & Trade: Fiscal policy is expected to be looser. China promised a fiscal boost next year and acknowledged a ‘prominent’ imbalance, and analysts expect a high fiscal deficit ratio in 2026 to buoy spending plans. Caixin reports that the 2026 priorities put demand revival at the core. On trade, the surplus topped $1 trillion for the first time on non-US growth, and the IMF urged the country to curb exports and boost consumption. Mexico’s tariffs of up to 50% on Chinese goods show the trade pushback is spreading beyond the US.

Markets & Flows: The mainland equity tape is weak while Hong Kong is supported by listings. The Hang Seng closed at 25,628.88, down 2.86% over 20 sessions, and the Shanghai Composite at 3,867.92, down 2.62% (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2025-12-15). The A-share ETF ASHR closed at 32.85, down 0.15% over 20 sessions, while the China internet ETF KWEB closed at 36.26, down 5.18%, and FXI at 38.74, down 3.44%. USD/CNY closed at 7.0540, down 0.64% over 20 sessions, so the yuan has firmed (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2025-12-15). HashKey raised $206 million in a Hong Kong IPO, and Goldman says hedge funds ditched tech stocks in Japan and Hong Kong last week.

New / Most Important: The most important new item is the mismatch between financing and consumption. Hong Kong listings are booming, with the HashKey deal and a new HKEX Tech 100 index in a pivot toward innovation-driven growth, and the dim sum bond market keeps growing, while retail sales and investment slump. The trade surplus above $1 trillion confirms that exports, not domestic demand, are carrying growth. Watch for the IMF’s call to rebalance to become policy rather than rhetoric.

Playbook ✓: Mixed: hold a small, hedged long in Hong Kong listings and the A-share proxy into any policy follow-through rather than adding mainland beta. ASHR is roughly flat over 20 sessions, down 0.15% (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2025-12-15), and the yuan firming reduces the need for a currency hedge. Trigger to turn bearish: a Vanke default after bondholders rejected an extension; trigger to turn bullish: an RRR cut with a credible property backstop, which Beijing has said it will use. ⚠ Divergence: the Hong Kong IPO and dim sum bond boom are running hot while retail sales miss, so financing is looser than consumption.
