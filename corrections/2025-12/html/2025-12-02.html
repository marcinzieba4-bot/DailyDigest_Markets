🌍 Geographic Macro Overview

🇺🇸 United States  Mixed  cut bets rising, data soft

Macro Stance: The December FOMC cut has become the base case in the feed: JP Morgan moved its Fed call to a December cut, and Reuters reported growing bets on that move. The data behind it are mixed: the November ISM manufacturing index printed at 48.2%, and the WSJ headline says manufacturing contracted for a ninth straight month. Consumer confidence fell to its lowest point since April on job worries, and the delayed September report showed 119,000 jobs and a 4.4% unemployment rate. The October CPI release was cancelled because of the shutdown, so the inflation read is thinner than usual. We take a Mixed stance: growth is slowing, but the rate path is being set in the front end.

Rates & Curve: On the prior close, the 2Y was 3.54%, the 10Y 4.09%, the 3M 3.81% and the 30Y 4.74% (US Treasury, prior close 2025-12-01). The 10Y minus 2Y spread was 55 bps, and the curve shape is positively sloped but still flat by historical standards (US Treasury, prior close 2025-12-01). Over 20 sessions the 2Y fell 7 bps and the 10Y fell 2 bps, so the spread widened 5 bps; the curve move label is bull steepening (both down, 2Y down more), but the long end did not follow: the 30Y rose 9 bps while the 10Y barely moved (US Treasury, prior close 2025-12-01). Over five sessions both yields rose 3 bps (US Treasury, prior close 2025-12-01), and on December 1 Treasury yields moved higher as markets looked to faster economic growth in 2026. The Dollar Index sat at 99.41, down 0.39% over 20 sessions, and the S&P 500 closed at 6,812.63 with the VIX at 17.24 (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2025-12-01).

New / Most Important: The biggest policy headline is the Fed chair. Trump said he had picked the next Fed chair, and he indicated that Kevin Hassett is the likely choice, with Powell's term ending in May 2026. Reuters commentary said Hassett may act as a 'shadow Fed Chair' for five months before the handover, and investors said a Hassett Fed could pressure the dollar. Fortune's profile reports Hassett saying inflation is 'way down' and seeing 'political bias' in the data; that is a stated view, not a measured fact. Consumer demand was strong at the start of the holiday season, with a record $11.8 billion in Black Friday online spending and $9.1 billion on Cyber Monday.

Playbook ✓: Mixed. Own the front end into the December meeting only at the current 2Y level of 3.54%, where the cut is largely in the price, and do not add long-end duration while the 10Y is rising on growth hopes (US Treasury, prior close 2025-12-01). Keep part of the dollar short hedged rather than short outright, because a new chair appointed in the middle of the cut debate could weigh on the dollar, as investors have argued. The dollar's worst week since July came on the cut bets, so the trade is crowded on that side. What changes the call: a 10Y close back above 4.11%, the level 20 sessions earlier, would mean the cut is being priced out, and then we would cut the front-end long.

🇨🇳 China  Bearish  domestic demand weak, markets firm

Macro Stance: Bearish on domestic demand, firmer on markets. The November factory survey is where the story starts. CNBC's headline said China's factory activity unexpectedly contracted, missing estimates, and Bloomberg called it the longest slump on record. Standard Chartered raised its 2026 China growth forecast to 4.6% on December 1, which is a forecast, not a print. The Shanghai Composite closed at 3,914.01 (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2025-12-01), down 1.57% over 20 sessions but up 2.01% over five, and USD/CNY closed at 7.0751 (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2025-12-01).

PMIs & Activity: The November survey headlines disagree on direction. One CNBC headline said activity edged up but remained in contraction, while another said it unexpectedly contracted. We give no direction and read the level as contraction, which both headlines agree on. The IIF said investment slowed more quickly than consumption in 2025, which is the structural read. Industrial profits are the other conflict: the ten-month figure was described as steady growth, while the October figure was described as a fall after two months of strong growth, and we do not extrapolate either, so the latest official picture is the October industrial data.

Credit Impulse: Credit is the weakest link. China's October new bank loans fell sharply and missed forecasts, and Bloomberg described the worst credit growth in a year as demand dried up. Earlier in November the credit cycle was described as in low gear while policymakers manage the transition. The offset is consumer lending: internet firms cautiously revived lending as Beijing pushed consumer loans. We would need a run of monthly loan growth, not one consumer-credit headline, before turning the Credit Impulse view positive.

Property: Property is still deteriorating. China's housing slide deepened as October prices fell at the fastest pace in a year, and Bloomberg said the stimulus was failing to halt the slide. Authorities told statistics providers to halt the publication of home-sales data, which lowers the quality of the signal. Vanke sought its first extension on an onshore bond payment and its bonds slumped, and it pledged its entire stake in its management unit to a state backer. Breakingviews argued a Vanke default may be a blessing in disguise, a commentary view that does not change the stress signal.

Fiscal, Policy & Trade: Policy is incremental. China named 'hotspot' sectors in a 19-point plan to spur consumption, and the central bank is expected to maintain an accommodative policy as challenges persist. The central bank also vowed a crackdown on virtual currency and flagged concerns about stablecoins. Trade is the other pressure: China's October exports fell 1.1%, hit by a 25% drop in shipments to the US, and the US extended tariff exclusions on some Chinese industrial goods as part of the trade truce. Pantheon Macro, as reported by Fortune, sees the tariffs raising $100 billion less than Trump expected, with China as the first reason.

Markets & Flows: Markets are firmer than the data. The yuan is set for its best year since 2020 despite trade strains, and USD/CNY is down 0.59% over 20 sessions (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2025-12-01). The stock rally is slow-motion but is winning investor trust, and JPMorgan upgraded China's stocks on several positive drivers. The Hang Seng closed at 26,033.26, up 1.23% over five sessions and down 0.48% over 20 (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2025-12-01). KWEB closed at 37.99, down 4.91% over 20 sessions; FXI closed at 39.64; ASHR closed at 32.83, down 0.61% over 20 (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2025-12-01).

New / Most Important: The new item for the December read is Standard Chartered's 4.6% 2026 forecast, set against a contracting factory survey, and a Beijing pledge to harness state and private sectors to build world-class firms. Analysts are also asking what comes next for the economy in 2026. In equities, foreign investors are warming to China's cheaper AI valuations despite fears of a US bubble. For the thesis, the new item matters only if it shows up in the official credit and property data.

Playbook ✓: Bearish on domestic demand. Keep China exposure to markets and the yuan rather than to domestic consumption names, since the equity and currency signals are firmer than the activity data. Size KWEB and FXI as tactical rather than core, and treat the internet platforms as the first place a consumer-lending revival would show. The trigger to change the call is two consecutive months of positive bank-loan growth together with a property price decline that stops. ⚠ Divergence: the yuan's best year since 2020 and a stock rally that is winning investor trust run against the weak PMI and credit data above.

🇯🇵 Japan  Mixed  hike signalled, yen weak

Macro Stance: Mixed. The Bank of Japan has moved toward a hike. Governor Ueda sent a clear hint of a December rate hike and said rate hikes would not put the brakes on Japan's economy. Core inflation in Tokyo has brought the BOJ closer to a hike, and national core inflation accelerated in October and stayed above the BOJ target, while the factory-activity decline slowed in November. The finance minister said there is no divergence with the BOJ on the economic assessment, and that the yen's rapid fall was not driven by fundamentals.

Activity & Currency: USD/JPY closed at 155.9280, up 1.12% over 20 sessions and down 0.49% over five (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2025-12-01). The Nikkei 225 closed at 49,303.28, down 3.94% over 20 sessions and up 1.39% over five (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2025-12-01). The rates signal is the sharper one: 2-year Japan government bond yields rose above 1% on BOJ hike speculation, and demand at the 10-year sale was stronger than the 12-month average, while the yen carry trade is back in the commentary. Fiscal policy adds supply: Japan finalised a $117 billion extra budget funded mostly by debt and will issue $75 billion of new bonds. The China dispute adds risk, since Beijing froze Japan youth exchange programmes over Takaichi's Taiwan remarks.

Playbook ✓: Mixed. Own Japanese equities through the BOJ decision only with hedged yen exposure. A hike combined with a stronger yen would hit the export-heavy Nikkei, while a yen that keeps weakening supports exporters. Avoid long-end JGB duration: the 2-year above 1% is where the market is repricing, and demand at the 10-year sale is the one signal that is holding. The trigger: a December hike with USD/JPY closing below its 155.9280 level (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2025-12-01) would confirm a carry unwind; a hike that leaves USD/JPY higher would be read as credibility for the BOJ, and we would move to Bullish on the equities.
