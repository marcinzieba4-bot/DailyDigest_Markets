The November ISM Services print was 52.6%, while the November ISM manufacturing print was 48.2%, and manufacturing has now contracted for a ninth straight month. Core PCE for September came in at 2.8%, and consumer spending stalled as inflation stayed stubborn.

Rates & Curve: The Treasury par curve at the prior close had the 2Y at 3.57% and the 10Y at 4.17%, a 10Y-2Y spread of 60 bps, with the 3M at 3.73% and the 30Y at 4.81% (US Treasury, prior close 2025-12-08). Over 20 observations the 10Y rose 6 bps while the 2Y was unchanged, so the spread widened by 6 bps and the 3M fell 20 bps; the move is labelled steepening (10Y up, 2Y down/flat) (US Treasury, prior close 2025-12-08). The shape is moderately steep, approaching the long-run average, and the 30Y sits 64 bps above the 10Y (US Treasury, prior close 2025-12-08). New / Most Important: The December FOMC decision is the dominant event: a cut is expected, but the dollar rose after the jobs data with traders expecting hawkish comments, and the Fed cut is what the market is leaning into. Economists doubled down on a December cut despite the policymaker divide, and Morningstar described a likely cut with growing divisions. The divisions are the story: Reuters framed the meeting around the Fed's internal split and the spotlight on Powell's guidance and dissents, and Fortune called it the most divided Fed in recent memory. The succession overhang is real: Bessent wants a residency rule for regional presidents, and Trump said he will name the Fed chief nominee in early 2026. Playbook ✓: Mixed. Hedge the dollar only if DXY breaks back above the 20-session reference of 99.60 (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2025-12-08).

🇨🇳 China  Bearish  domestic weak, exports strong

Macro Stance: Bearish on domestic demand, constructive on the export machine. The trade surplus topped $1 trillion for the first time, with exports growing 5.9% in November while US-bound shipments dropped 29%. Beijing set domestic demand as the top goal for 2026, and the Politburo is pledging more proactive policy to expand demand. The export strength is the counterweight to a domestic economy that is losing momentum in the factory and services PMIs and in the credit and property prints, though the November PMI direction is contested.

PMIs & Activity: The latest activity prints are weak: factory activity unexpectedly contracted in November according to the private survey, and it was described as the longest stretch of factory contraction on record. Services growth slipped to a five-month low in November on the private PMI. A CNBC reading of the same month said activity edged up but remained in contraction as fading holiday demand hit services, so the two readings conflict on the direction of the official print and the feed gives no direction on it. A Reuters poll expected factory activity to shrink for an eighth month. The confirming signal to watch is a turn in the new-orders sub-index, which the feed does not yet show.

Credit Impulse: Credit is the weakest link: China's October new bank loans fell sharply and missed forecasts, and credit growth was the worst in a year as demand dried up. Local government debt rose to $18.9 trillion as the property slump lingered. The SCMP argues the contraction reflects a financial reset rather than a collapse, and a Caixin headline shows the regulator loosening insurer capital rules to allow more long-term equity bets. Shadow banks are back in the spotlight after Xi's debt crackdown. The credit data in the feed are negative, and the visible policy response is on the capital-rule side rather than on loans.

Property: Home prices are still falling: October new-home prices fell at the fastest pace in a year, and a Reuters poll sees home prices falling 3.7% this year, extending declines into 2026. Vanke is seeking to delay a second onshore bond as its crisis grows and wants a one-year extension on a 2 billion yuan note. UBS predicts 2.4 million property foreclosures as loan defaults rise. Against that, Morgan Stanley argues mortgage aid may halt the housing slump, and officials are calling for more REITs to steady the sector. Beijing also told statistics providers to halt home-sales data publication.

Fiscal, Policy & Trade: Policy is pointing at domestic demand for 2026. Reuters reports China is likely to chase 5% GDP growth in 2026 to end deflation and pledges more proactive policy to expand demand. On trade, the surplus is at a record, and France's Macron has threatened China with tariffs in the coming months. Rare-earth licences are being streamlined, while state banks are soaking up dollars to slow yuan gains. State banks' dollar purchases show policy slowing yuan gains even as exports boom.

Markets & Flows: Hang Seng 25,765.36 is down 1.23% in the prior session, down 1.03% over five sessions and down 3.32% over 20 sessions (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2025-12-08). Shanghai Composite closed at 3,924.08, up 0.54% in the prior session and down 2.35% over 20 sessions (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2025-12-08). The China large-cap ETF FXI is down 1.18% over 20 sessions at 39.47, the China internet ETF KWEB is down 3.67% at 37.54, and the CSI 300 ETF ASHR is down 0.12% at 33.22 (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2025-12-08). USD/CNY at 7.0696 is down 0.69% over 20 sessions, a firmer yuan (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2025-12-08). The sell-side is not uniformly bearish: one headline says Wall Street bets Chinese stocks will extend a $2.4 trillion rally, and another says the market is set for earnings-driven growth after stretching valuations.

New / Most Important: The new headline is the surplus and the Politburo's 2026 aim. Bloomberg reports the Politburo made boosting domestic demand the top goal for 2026, and SCMP previewed the 2026 economic focus as leaders prepared for a major meeting. The Politburo's demand goal is the policy signal; the trade surplus is the data signal. Foreign pressure is rising too: Germany's top diplomat sees progress in China's rare-earth talks, and the EU is considering measures to reduce purchases from China.

Playbook ✓: Bearish. Underweight domestic cyclicals and property developers; the Hang Seng is down 3.32% over 20 sessions and the Shanghai Composite is down 2.35% (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2025-12-08). Prefer exporters and the policy-favoured industrial and insurance names where capital rules are loosening. Trigger to turn: monthly credit prints showing new loans re-accelerating and a Vanke resolution that removes the property tail risk; until new loans respond, size positions as if the Politburo's 2026 demand goal is aspirational. ⚠ Divergence: the record $1 trillion surplus and 5.9% export growth contradict the domestic PMIs and credit data, so if exports keep growing the weakness is a domestic policy problem, not a global demand problem.

Historical Analog: The analogy is Tesla's earlier growth-stock debate, when investors argued over whether a developing operating system should be valued through its current scale or its potential reach. The second wave often arrives after the share price has rewarded the early believers and made scepticism look costly. A different business in a different industry does not inherit that earlier company's eventual outcome. Analyst disagreement should prompt a comparison of assumptions rather than automatic obedience or automatic rejection. Prefer additions around credible operating progress when the position remains small enough to tolerate a delayed timetable. A setback should be judged against the specific reason for owning the stock, not against the need to prove an analyst wrong. Keep the holding unleveraged and avoid averaging down merely to defend membership in a bullish community. Rebuild confidence when progress improves the expected commercial return, and take partial profits if a speculative position becomes a core portfolio weight through appreciation.
