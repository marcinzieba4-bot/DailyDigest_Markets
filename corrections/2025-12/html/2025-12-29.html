🇺🇸 United States  Mixed  growth firm, jobs soft

Macro Stance: The US backdrop is a strong third quarter sitting on a weakening labour market. Real GDP grew 4.3% in the third quarter, well ahead of expectations. Payrolls rose by only 64,000 in November after falling by 105,000 in October, and the unemployment rate reached a four-year high. Inflation is the offset: the November CPI rose at a 2.7% annual rate, lower than expected, but economists flagged the report as distorted by missing data. Our stance is Mixed. Growth argues against cuts, the jobs and sentiment data argue for them, and the choice of the next Fed chair is the swing factor.

Rates & Curve: The Treasury par curve at the 2025-12-26 prior close put the 2Y at 3.46% and the 10Y at 4.14%, a 10Y-2Y spread of 68 bps; the 3M sits at 3.64%, so 10Y-3M is 50 bps, and the 30Y at 4.81% gives a 30Y-10Y spread of 67 bps (US Treasury, prior close 2025-12-26). Over 20 observations the 2Y is up 1 bp and the 10Y up 14 bps, so the 10Y-2Y spread has widened by 13 bps. The curve shape reads moderately steep, approaching the long-run average, and the move is a bear steepening with the 10Y up more than the 2Y (US Treasury, prior close 2025-12-26). The S&P 500 closed the 2025-12-26 session at 6,929.94, up 1.72% over 20 sessions (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2025-12-26). The dollar index sits at 98.02, down 1.59% over 20 sessions (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2025-12-26). The long end is cheapening while the dollar softens, which is the combination a term-premium move produces.

New / Most Important: The most important US development is the succession at the Fed. Trump has said anybody who disagrees with him will never be Fed chair. Kevin Hassett told CNBC the US is way behind the curve on lowering rates, and Fortune reports that over 80% of top CEOs think Trump would be wrong not to pick Chris Waller. Bessent sees room for a future revamp of the Fed's 2% inflation target. The market reads this as a risk to independence. This is the writer's reading: the nominee matters for the term premium as much as for the policy rate.

Playbook ✓: Stance Mixed. Stay neutral on outright duration until the nominee is named, then express the view in the curve rather than in flat duration. Own the 2Y against the 10Y while the 10Y-2Y spread keeps widening from 68 bps, since the bear steepening is led by the long end. Use equity volatility as cheap insurance: with the VIX at 13.60 and down 20.88% over 20 sessions, options on the index are inexpensive into a fiscal and Fed-politics window (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2025-12-26). Change the call if payrolls or CPI surprise against the grain: a further rise in unemployment would argue for buying the 10Y, and a re-acceleration in inflation would argue for shortening the front end. The dollar index at 98.02 is the signal to watch; a break higher would mean the dollar is catching a bid on the Fed-independence worry rather than on growth.

🇨🇳 China  Mixed  targeted easing, no bazooka

Macro Stance: China's story is a slowing activity base met by incremental policy rather than a large package. Reuters reports the economy stalled in November as calls for reform grow, and a think tank estimates growth in 2025 came in below 3%, half the official target. Beijing is responding with targeted steps. The finance ministry says fiscal policy will be more proactive in 2026, and the PBOC has signalled steady support with a focus on long-term growth. Nikkei's survey sees growth slowing to 4.5% in 2026. We read this as targeted easing, not a bazooka, and our stance is Mixed.

PMIs & Activity: The feed carries no December PMI print for China, so the latest available prints are for November. The manufacturing PMI showed factory activity shrinking for an eighth month, the non-manufacturing PMI was 49.5, and private services growth slipped to a five-month low. November industrial output grew 4.8% year on year, and retail sales grew 1.3%, sharply missing estimates and deepening consumption worries. Investment fell in November, and the SCMP describes the growth engines as sputtering as retail and investment were hit. Reuters reported that industrial profits tumbled at their fastest pace in over a year, and Money.pl reports a first investment decline in decades. What would confirm the view: the next official PMI showing new orders above the November level, and a fresh retail print that beats the consensus.

Credit Impulse: Credit remains muted. Bloomberg reports China's credit expansion stays muted as demand languishes. The benchmark lending rates were kept steady for a seventh straight month despite weak data, so the policy rate is not doing the work. Regulators are instead cleaning up the books. China will scrub small overdue debts from credit records to help spur lending, and it will hide bad debt records for pandemic-hit borrowers who repay small loans. That lowers the cost of lending on paper and improves borrower eligibility, but it does not by itself lift borrowing. We treat it as a liquidity measure rather than a demand measure.

Property: Home prices slid further in November, and Bloomberg reports the authorities vow stability while the slump persists. Beijing has eased home-purchase curbs again in late December, and China vowed city-specific fixes to clear the housing glut and stabilise the sector. The pattern is a property floor defended city by city, not a national turn. This is the writer's reading, and it is why we do not expect a sharp rebound in property-linked activity.

Fiscal, Policy & Trade: Fiscal policy is the lever Beijing is pulling. On trade, China's record surplus, which Statista puts at $1 trillion as of November, could prompt a protectionist response in 2026. Beijing sanctioned 20 US defence firms and 10 executives over the Taiwan arms sale, and it vowed to retaliate against US semiconductor tariffs. Washington has delayed new tariffs on Chinese chips until 2027. Beijing also hit EU dairy with provisional tariffs of up to 42.7%, and the EU is the second front of the trade war.

Markets & Flows: The Shanghai Composite closed at 3,963.68 on the 2025-12-26 session, up 1.93% over 20 sessions and 1.88% over five (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2025-12-26). The Hang Seng closed at 25,818.93 on the 2025-12-24 session, down 0.42% over 20 sessions (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2025-12-24). Offshore and foreign-facing exposure is weaker: KWEB fell 7.12% over 20 sessions to 34.83 and FXI is down 1.12% to 38.93, while ASHR rose 2.51% to 33.11 (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2025-12-26). USD/CNY closed at 7.0280, down 0.68% over 20 sessions (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2025-12-26). The yuan signals are two-way. The PBOC fixing signals a slow yuan gain well below estimates, and the FT reports China signals tolerance for a stronger renminbi. The Hang Seng jumped 30% in 2025.

New / Most Important: The most important policy signal is the currency and the digital yuan. The digital yuan becomes interest-bearing next year, a state broadcaster says, and the PBOC issued an action plan to strengthen the digital yuan from January 2026. That extends the state's payment and liquidity channel. For markets, the more important item is the yuan. The writer's reading: the state wants a managed, stronger yuan and a stock market that supports household wealth, and that is a different target from a growth rebound.

Playbook ✓: Stance Mixed. Own the onshore policy beneficiaries through ASHR, which is up 2.51% over 20 sessions (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2025-12-26), and stay underweight the offshore internet names while retail sales miss and property clears city by city. Add beyond a tactical position only after a fresh PMI or a credit impulse that rises rather than stays muted. Change the call if the offshore tech ETF recovers its 20-session loss and the yuan fix stops leaning against appreciation. ⚠ Divergence: the Shanghai Composite is up 1.93% over 20 sessions while KWEB is down 7.12% over the same window, so the onshore rally is not confirmed by the offshore market or by November's retail miss.

Retail is told the price can go much higher. A Kitco survey says 57% of retail investors expect silver to trade above $100 an ounce in 2026, and FXStreet calls it the great silver squeeze of 2026. Silver crossed $75 an ounce for the first time, and it is at a record high on rate-cut bets and geopolitical strife. The writer's reading is that the crowd has turned a metals rally into a supply-squeeze story, and the story is what keeps the retail buyers in.

What Is Driving It: Price is doing the work. Front-month silver closed at 76.49 on the 2025-12-26 session, up 44.54% over 20 sessions and 18.41% over five (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2025-12-26). The ETF SLV closed at 71.12, up 46.94% over 20 sessions and 19.89% over five (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2025-12-26). Gold moved too: front-month gold closed at 4,552.70, up 9.30% over 20 sessions, and GLD rose 8.78% (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2025-12-26). The news flow is fuel. Bloomberg reports gold and silver touched records as rate-cut bets rose, and Fortune reports silver at fresh record highs as debt fears and geopolitical tension mount. Musk warned about the impact of record silver prices before China limits exports, which turned the squeeze into a policy story.

The Social Signal: The crowd's attention is loud and organised. The Kitco survey is the crowd's own forecast, and a TradingKey author predicts $10,000 gold and $200 silver. The crowd reads institutional skepticism as a squeeze in disguise. SilverTrade asks whether a large bullion bank's short silver position is about to be liquidated, which the crowd takes as a squeeze trigger. The signal the crowd dismisses is the Chinese regulator: a China silver fund plunged after a string of moves to quell the frenzy. This is the crowd's view, not a reported fact, and it is the view that regulators are the last to know.

Historical Analog: The closest precedent is the Hunt brothers' silver squeeze of the late seventies, which ran on leverage and a belief in a physical shortage. First the price rose on speculative buying and borrowed money. Then the exchanges raised margin requirements, and the price collapsed as leveraged buyers were forced out. The mechanical match is the funding: silver is rising on borrowed positions and a shortage narrative, and retail is in the second wave, buying the record headlines rather than the first move. The writer's reading: this episode will break on margin, not on fundamentals.

Smart Money View: The institutional case has substance and hedges. For it: the physical market is tight, with backwardation, central banks are buying gold, as Poland's NBP buying shows, and a MarketWatch setup piece treats silver and gold as the set-up into 2026. Against it: SilverTrade asks whether a large bullion bank's short position is about to be liquidated, and Musk's warning on exports suggests policy could cap the upside. The writer's reading is that the institutions are managing margin risk on the metal rather than simply betting against it, which is the point the crowd is missing.

When the Narrative Breaks: Triggers, with the card's levels. First, a close back below the 20-session reference: front-month silver's reference is 52.92 (Yahoo Finance, ref 2025-11-26), and SLV's is 48.40. A return there would erase the rally. Second, a daily close that gives back the five-session gain of 18.41%, which would be the first sign that momentum has broken. Third, an exchange action on margin, the mechanism that ended the earlier squeezes described above, and a tightening that SilverTrade's short-position headline makes visible. Fourth, Chinese regulators continuing to quell the frenzy. The broader test is gold: a break below its 20-session reference of 4,165.20 (Yahoo Finance, ref 2025-11-26) would show the metals bid is breaking, not just silver. These are the crowd's exit signals, and the writer's reading is that they arrive before the fundamental story changes.

Copper miners / FCX  "“Copper is the new gold”"

Why Retail Is Here: Barron's headline 'Copper is the new gold' gives the crowd its hook. The AI and grid story is easy to sell, and copper's first print above $12,000 makes the mining equity the easy version of it. Freeport closed at 53.04 on the 2025-12-26 session, up 25.84% over 20 sessions (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2025-12-26). Retail reads the miner as a leveraged bet on the metal, and the copper record in Shanghai confirms the story to the crowd.

Where It Breaks: The trigger is the tariff trade. Copper's rise is tied to tariffs as much as to supply, so any change to the tariff path breaks the move, and the feed flags tariff risk as a source of 2026 volatility. The instrument risk is leverage in the miner: a close back at the 42.15 20-session reference (Yahoo Finance, ref 2025-11-26) would erase most of the gain. The writer's reading is that a US tariff delay would break this trade faster than a demand miss, which is the reverse of the consensus.

The writer's reading is that the 13.84% gain is most exposed to a fading of the hype rather than to a fundamental miss.
