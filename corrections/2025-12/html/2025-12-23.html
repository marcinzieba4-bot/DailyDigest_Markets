🌍 Geographic Macro Overview

🇺🇸 United States  Mixed  hold / data dependent

Macro Stance: The data flow is slowing without breaking, and the Fed has moved from cutting to waiting. Payrolls rose by 64,000 in November after falling by 105,000 in October, and the unemployment rate reached a four-year high in the delayed report. Reuters read the same release as job growth beating expectations while October retail sales were unchanged. The November ISM Services PMI was 52.6% and the ISM Manufacturing PMI 48.2%, so services carry the expansion while factories are still contracting. The shutdown-distorted November CPI printed at a 2.7% annual rate, below expectations.

Rates & Curve: At the 2025-12-22 close the 2Y was 3.44% and the 10Y 4.17%, a 10Y−2Y spread of 73 bps; the 3M was 3.64% and the 30Y 4.84% (US Treasury, prior close 2025-12-22). Over 20 observations the 2Y fell 7 bps and the 10Y rose 11 bps, which widened the spread by 18 bps; over five observations the 2Y fell 7 bps and the 10Y 1 bp (US Treasury, prior close 2025-12-22). The curve is “moderately steep, approaching the long-run average” and the move is “steepening (10Y up, 2Y down/flat)”. Equity volatility is low: the S&P 500 closed at 6,878.49, up 4.17% over 20 sessions, and the VIX closed at 14.08 against 23.43 twenty sessions earlier, while the dollar index at 98.29 is down 1.89% over the same window (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2025-12-22).

New / Most Important: The December cut was the story, and the message was a pause. Reuters reported that the Fed lowered rates, signalled a pause and one cut next year as growth rebounds, and Powell said the Fed sees only one cut in 2026 with no hike ahead. Hammack says a pause is her base case for now and Williams sees no urgency to adjust further. The leadership question now carries the rate path: Trump said the next chair will believe in lower rates “by a lot”, Waller interviewed well, and prediction-market odds on Kalshi put Hassett back in the lead.

Playbook ✓: Stay neutral on duration and keep the curve trade. Own the 2Y over the 10Y while the curve steepens on a rising 10Y; cut that exposure if the 2Y closes above 3.51%, its level 20 sessions ago, because that would mean the cut path is being removed (US Treasury, prior close 2025-12-22). Equity exposure can stay, and protection is cheap: a VIX at 14.08 against 23.43 twenty sessions earlier means buying downside now rather than after a shock (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2025-12-22). Wall Street’s uniform bullishness on 2026 is the flag to watch, and 200-plus fund managers have triggered a contrarian sell signal. A funding lapse in January would change the call, as set out in the Anticipatory block.

🇪🇺 European Union  Mixed  hold, growth upgraded

Macro Stance: The ECB left its key rate unchanged at 2.15% and upgraded its growth outlook, and its officials say the cutting cycle is most likely over. The Bank of England cut to 3.75% after a tight vote and signalled caution on further moves, and sterling has risen since, because the bar for further cuts is now higher. Euro-area business activity expanded at the fastest pace in 30 months in November, yet factory activity contracted and job cuts accelerated. The stance is mixed: services and growth data are firmer than manufacturing.

Activity & Inflation: Germany is the drag and the swing factor. The Bundesbank expects the German economy to strengthen markedly in 2026, but the latest German business outlook deteriorated more than expected and business bankruptcies hit a decade high. October’s German industrial output rose more than expected, so the monthly signal is mixed rather than uniformly weak. In the UK, consumer inflation fell to 3.2% in November, but the CBI describes the economy entering 2026 amid a sharp private-sector downturn and retailers reported falling sales before Christmas. Consumers are also feeling the tax squeeze.

New / Most Important: The trade-policy item is the EU-Mercosur deal. Brussels delayed the signing until January, France said it is not ready to sign, and farmers blocked roads in Brussels in protest. German industry voiced frustration at the delay. On the fiscal side, EU leaders agreed a €90bn loan for Ukraine without using frozen Russian assets, and Germany launched a €30bn fund to mobilise private investment. Estonia will nominate Müller as its candidate for ECB vice-president.

Playbook ✓: Own euro-area domestic cyclicals only where the ECB’s growth upgrade shows up in orders; the trigger is a German industrial print that matches the Bundesbank’s 2026 forecast. Stay underweight German-exposed autos until the business-outlook deterioration reverses. The Mercosur signing in January is the binary event: a signing lifts trade-exposed industrials, while a second delay keeps farm politics in charge and weighs on the export names. In the UK the FTSE 100 is up 3.47% over 20 sessions (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2025-12-22); keep the UK overweight until the Bank of England’s caution turns into cuts.

🇨🇳 China  Bearish  reflation hopes / property drag

Macro Stance: China’s 2025 growth was judged well below target: a think-tank estimate put the economy below 3% for the year, half the official goal. November activity data were uneven. Industrial output grew 4.8% and retail sales 1.3%, while retail sales growth sharply missed estimates. Reuters says the stall is prompting calls for reform. The policy answer in the feed is demand-led growth at the top of the 2026 agenda and a new package to get consumers spending. The stance is bearish on the domestic cycle, with the AI-hardware corner as the exception.

PMIs & Activity: Surveys agree that factories are shrinking. Factory activity contracted for an eighth month in November, and the private survey missed estimates on the downside. Services slipped to a five-month low in November on the private PMI. Consumer prices were up 0.7% in November while factory deflation worsened. The feed carries no December PMI print before the cutoff, so November is the latest available print; a December survey showing a turn in the factory index would be the confirmation this view needs.

Credit Impulse: Credit is not re-accelerating. Bloomberg reported that credit expansion stayed muted as demand languished, and a growing pile of long-term debt is set to deepen pricing stress. The lever being pulled is administrative: small overdue debts are being scrubbed from credit records to help spur lending, and bad-debt records for pandemic-hit borrowers who repay small loans are to be hidden. Benchmark lending rates were kept steady for a seventh straight month. That makes the impulse a policy-tool story rather than a demand story, and it has not turned.

Property: Home prices are still falling. November prices slid further, and a Reuters poll sees a 3.7% decline this year that extends into 2026. Construction slumped to its lowest level in two decades. Vanke avoided default after bondholders approved a longer grace period, but the same bondholders rejected a repayment extension, and WSJ reads the episode as a sign that property easing will be limited. Beijing pledged to step up urban renewal and stabilise the housing market in 2026.

Fiscal, Policy & Trade: Policy leans toward household spending and urban renewal rather than broad stimulus, and the ‘invest in people’ slogan is read as a pivot in economic policy. Trade is strong: the surplus topped $1 trillion, and exports grew 5.9% in November while shipments to the US dropped 29%. The friction is with Europe. China set provisional tariffs of up to 42.7% on EU dairy while lowering EU pork tariffs in a final ruling. Rare-earth licensing has eased: general export licences are being issued, and rare-earth magnet exports to the EU rose sharply as approvals took effect.

Markets & Flows: Onshore and offshore markets disagree. The Shanghai Composite closed at 3,917.36, up 2.1% over 20 sessions (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2025-12-22), while the Hang Seng closed at 25,801.77, up 0.33% (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2025-12-22). ASHR, the CSI 300 ETF, closed at 32.61, up 2.58% over 20 sessions, but KWEB, the China internet ETF, fell 4.66% over the same window to 34.76 (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2025-12-22). USD/CNY closed at 7.04, down 0.92% over 20 sessions, a firmer yuan that Bloomberg reports Beijing is pacing to shield exporters. Citi has downgraded China stocks and lifted Taiwan on AI and earnings.

New / Most Important: The new item is the chip channel. Reuters reported that Nvidia aims to begin H200 shipments to China by mid-February, and US lawmakers want disclosure of the licence reviews. Domestic substitutes are the other side of the same story: MetaX rose nearly 700% on its Shanghai debut, and traders are chasing China’s GPU makers as an alternative to Nvidia. Global investors are turning to Chinese AI as Wall Street fears a bubble, and Goldman says China tech stocks can ride the AI wave further.

Playbook ✓: Stay underweight the domestic cycle: property developers, credit-sensitive lenders and discretionary consumer names. Keep a selective AI-hardware position, sized so that the Nvidia licence news is the only thing it depends on. The view changes if a credit impulse shows up in the data, which would need more than the scrubbing of overdue records, or if lawmakers’ push for disclosure of licence reviews tightens the process. ⚠ Divergence: the Shanghai Composite is up 2.1% over 20 sessions while the November data show a slowing economy and falling home prices.

🇯🇵 Japan  Mixed  hiking / yen intervention risk

Macro Stance: The Bank of Japan raised its policy rate to the highest level since 1995 and signalled more hikes to come. A former policymaker expects the rate to reach 1.5% under Ueda, and Goldman sees a terminal rate of 1.5% by 2027. The hawkish wink suggests the next hike may come sooner than markets think. Core inflation was steady in November but stayed above the BOJ target, and Q3 GDP contracted more deeply on weak capex. The stance is mixed: the direction of policy is clear while the growth backdrop is weak.

Activity & Currency: The yen is the pressure point. USD/JPY closed at 157.68, up 0.63% over 20 sessions and 1.34% in the prior session (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2025-12-22), even as officials warn. Japan issued its sternest intervention warning, saying the yen is deviating from fundamentals, and the yen gained on intervention signals. The Nikkei 225 closed at 50,402.39, up 3.65% over 20 sessions (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2025-12-22). The 10-year JGB yield hit a 26-year high after the hike, and retail JGB sales reached their highest since 2007. Prime Minister Takaichi’s government maintains a 75% approval rating, so the fiscal agenda is not about to change.

Playbook ✓: Keep a short bias in USD/JPY into the next hike or intervention headline, and hedge yen exposure on Nikkei holdings. Prefer domestic banks, which benefit as four major banks will raise ordinary deposit rates and long-end yields rise. The yen view changes if the BOJ’s next hike is delayed, or if the yen weakens through the 157.68 close with no intervention, which would expose the warnings as empty. Fiscal risk is the second trigger: an ex-policymaker says Japan’s fiscal woes may cause more yen falls and yield rises, and the record budget has alarmed the market.

🇦🇺 Australia  Bearish  hike-bias / inflation above target

Macro Stance: The RBA held at 3.6% in December and flagged rate-hike risk as it ended its brief easing cycle. Markets have moved to price the next move up: financial markets are now certain the RBA will hike in 2026, and the Guardian says a hike could come as early as May. Consumer inflation accelerated to 3.8% in October, and the RBA’s board discussed the conditions in which a rate rise may be needed. Growth is firmer, with annual growth at a two-year high in Q3. The stance is bearish on duration, and the labour market is the swing factor: November jobs fell by the most in nine months while the unemployment rate held steady.

Demand & Financing: Households are carrying the cost. A Reuters poll expects home prices to rise about 7% in a tight market, but the cost of debt is climbing: Commonwealth Bank warned of more price pressure in 2026, and an AFR analysis warns that rising rates could cause a $7.5 billion budget blowout. Wages are going backwards as inflation rises, the AFR says. Yields reflect it: Australian yields climbed to their highest this year on rate-hike bets. In markets the S&P/ASX 200 closed at 8,699.90, up 2.05% over 20 sessions, and AUD/USD at 0.6610 is up 2.29% over the same window (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2025-12-22).

Playbook ✓: Underweight Australian long-dated bonds and rate-sensitive housing names, and prefer miners and cash-generative industrials; Macquarie sees mining stocks as the best way to play rate hikes in 2026. The Australian dollar is a second leg, up 2.29% over 20 sessions (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2025-12-22), so do not add on strength without a rate catalyst. What changes the call: a labour print weaker than November’s would take a hike out of the 2026 pricing, and a firmer inflation print would push the RBA toward action earlier than May.
