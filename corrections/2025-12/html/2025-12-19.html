What would change the call: a clearly dovish Fed chair, which would steepen the curve further, or a court loss that forces refunds and widens the deficit path.

Macro Stance: China’s domestic demand is weakening while the policy response stays incremental. November industrial output rose 4.8% year on year but retail sales only 1.3%, and the retail miss deepened worries about consumption. Reuters’ preview expects the loan prime rate to stay unchanged for a seventh month. The official cumulative retail figure looks better, at 4% growth for the first eleven months, but the monthly number is the one the market is reacting to. Reading: the easing bias is real but small, and the policy rate is not the binding constraint. Credit demand is.

PMIs & Activity: The feed carries no fresh official manufacturing PMI, so the latest private print is the reference. China’s private services PMI slipped to a five-month low in November. The growth engines are slowing together: investment and retail both weakened in November, and the New York Times reports that investment is close to its first annual decline in three decades. No activity series in the feed shows a fresh turn. The property and credit lines below point the same way.

Credit Impulse: Credit expansion stays muted as demand languishes. Bloomberg describes China’s growing pile of long-term debt as a deepening pricing stress. Steel output is set for its weakest level in seven years even as iron ore imports hit a record, which points to construction-linked demand still being weak. A shadow bank’s missed payments show growing property stress.

Property: Housing is the weakest link. Housing construction has slumped to its lowest level in two decades, and home prices slid further in November. Vanke is in a bondholder fight: holders rejected a payment extension, a second meeting with the holders of a 2 billion yuan bond has begun, and an analyst warns the developer is on the brink of default. Reading: a default would spread the stress from the developer to its suppliers and lenders, and that is the risk the policy response has to contain.
