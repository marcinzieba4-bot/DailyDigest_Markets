🌍 Geographic Macro Overview

🇺🇸 United States  Mixed  growth strong, cuts in doubt

Macro Stance: Mixed. Growth is running hot on paper while the labour market softens. The delayed third-quarter GDP estimate showed 4.3% growth, past consensus and the fastest pace in two years. November payrolls rose by 64,000 after a 105,000 fall in October, and the unemployment rate hit a four-year high even as the economy added jobs. Consumer confidence fell for a fifth straight month and reached its lowest level since the tariff rollout in April. The Fed is split on the next step. Hammack says a pause in cuts is her base case, while Fed chair candidate Hassett says the US is way behind the curve on lowering rates.

Rates & Curve: On the Treasury par curve, the 2-year closed at 3.47% and the 10-year at 4.15%, with the 3-month at 3.69% and the 30-year at 4.79% (US Treasury, prior close 2025-12-24). The 10Y-2Y spread was 68 bps and the 10Y-3M spread 46 bps. Over 20 observations the 10-year rose 14 bps and the 2-year rose 4 bps, so the spread widened by 10 bps. The card labels the shape ‘moderately steep, approaching the long-run average’ and the move ‘bear steepening (both up, 10Y up more)’. Over five observations the 2-year fell 2 bps and the 10-year fell 1 bp, so the back end is where the move has been. Equities are not signalling a growth scare: the S&P 500 closed at 6,932.05, up 2.46% over 20 sessions, the VIX fell to 13.47, down 27.42% over the same stretch, and the dollar index closed at 97.98, down 1.69% (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2025-12-24). The 10-year had slid on the softer November inflation print.

New / Most Important: The most important print is the November CPI, reported late at a 2.7% annual rate, below expectations, with core inflation at its slowest pace since 2021. The NY Fed’s Williams said technical factors distorted the reading downward, and CNBC’s economists flagged a lot of flaws in the delayed data. Treat the inflation relief as provisional. Funding conditions are firmer than the rate headlines suggest: the Fed is set to inject $6.8 billion into markets through its first repo operation since 2020. Holiday spending held up, with Visa and Mastercard reporting 4% growth in US holiday retail sales. The Dow and the S&P 500 closed at record highs as the Santa rally started.

Playbook ✓: Mixed. Keep core US equity exposure at market weight and do not add to a rally that has taken the S&P 500 to record closes. Downside protection is cheap: the VIX at 13.47 after a 27.42% fall over 20 sessions (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2025-12-24) makes index puts and put spreads a better expression than selling volatility (the writer’s reading). Own small caps only on a confirmed cut path. The Russell 2000 is up 3.33% over 20 sessions (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2025-12-24), but the Fed’s signal is still a pause. Trigger to add duration: a 10-year close back below its 4.01% level of November 25, which would reverse the 20-session bear steepening. Trigger to cut risk: a 10-year close above 4.15% with the 2-year still near 3.47%, which would mean the curve is steepening on term premium rather than on cut bets.

🇪🇺 European Union  Mixed  growth firm, Germany lagging

Macro Stance: Mixed. The euro area closed the autumn on a strong note: business activity expanded at the fastest pace in 30 months in November, even though factory activity contracted and job cuts accelerated. The December flash picture is softer. French private-sector growth is slowing to a crawl, and German manufacturing contracted again, raising the question of whether euro-area growth is at risk. The ECB held rates at its last meeting of 2025, and its Escriva expects policy to stay steady. Inflation edged up to 2.2% in the November flash.

Credit & Inflation: Policy is not adding to the squeeze. With the ECB on hold and inflation near 2.2%, the ECB has no reason to tighten in this window (the writer’s reading). The euro-area credit picture in the feed is institutional rather than cyclical: the Council of the EU backed caps on digital-euro holdings and a digital euro with both online and offline functions. The feed carries no fresh euro-area credit-growth print, so the credit read rests on the policy line. The weak spot is German industry. Business bankruptcies hit a decade high, even as the Bundesbank expects the German economy to strengthen markedly in 2026. Both can be true, and the bankruptcy count is the one that moves stocks first.

New / Most Important: European equities closed at a record high on December 19. The DAX closed at 24,340.06, up 3.73% over 20 sessions (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2025-12-23), and the STOXX 600 closed at 588.73, up 3.65% (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2025-12-23). The euro gained 1.99% against the dollar over 20 sessions to 1.1796 (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2025-12-24). The auto complex is the drag. German automakers are seeing their worst profits since 2009, and Volkswagen will end production at a German plant, a first in the company’s history.

Playbook ✓: Mixed. Favour euro-area domestic cyclicals and banks over German autos. The STOXX 600 is up 3.65% over 20 sessions, while the STOXX autos-and-parts tracker is up only 1.99% over the same period (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2025-12-23), and the sector still carries the worst-profit headline. Do not chase the DAX after a 3.73% run; buy pullbacks in the banks instead. Trigger to turn more constructive on the cycle: the next flash composite PMI confirming the November expansion despite the French slowdown. Trigger to turn defensive: French and German flash readings both contracting in the same month, which would make the factory contraction the leading signal.

🇨🇳 China  Bearish  stimulus incremental, demand weak

Macro Stance: Bearish on domestic demand, with a trade surplus doing the work. The growth story is losing momentum. Industrial output grew 4.8% in November but retail sales rose only 1.3%, and the retail number missed estimates sharply. Reuters reports the economy stalled in November as calls for reform grow. A think-tank estimate puts 2025 growth below 3%, half the official target, and the statistics bureau revised down its 2024 final GDP. The consumer is not carrying the economy (the writer’s reading), and the policy response has been incremental.

PMIs & Activity: The feed carries no fresh China PMI headline in the window, so the latest activity prints are November’s: industrial output up 4.8% year on year and retail sales up 1.3%. The Nikkei survey sees growth slowing further to 4.5% in 2026. Money.pl reports that investment has posted its first decline in decades. Consumer prices rose 0.7% in November, but factory-gate deflation worsened. That mix argues against a demand-led rebound.

Credit Impulse: The latest credit item in the feed is an expectation, not a result. A Reuters poll saw November bank lending more than doubling from the previous month. Bloomberg describes credit expansion as muted as demand languishes. Policy is incremental. The PBOC is signalling steady support with a focus on long-term growth, and benchmark lending rates have been unchanged for a seventh straight month. Authorities will scrub small overdue debts from credit records to help spur lending. Judge the next credit print against the poll, not against the rhetoric.

Property: Property is still contracting. Home prices slid further in November, and Bloomberg says the slump persists even as authorities vow stability. Beijing is easing home-buying curbs again and says it will apply city-specific fixes to clear the glut and stabilise the sector. Developer stress is still live. Vanke averted default when bondholders approved a longer grace period, after a temporary lifeline was extended. The Hong Kong Le Mont launch sold just 7 units in a muted weekend.

Fiscal, Policy & Trade: Trade is doing the work. The trade surplus passed $1 trillion for the first time, on non-US growth, while shipments to the US fell 29% in November even as total exports grew 5.9%. The protectionist response is building. China has slapped duties of up to 42.7% on EU dairy and vowed to retaliate against US semiconductor tariffs. The US has pushed its new chip tariffs out to 2027. On the domestic side, Beijing is broadening its foreign-investment list in a renewed pitch for overseas capital.

Markets & Flows: Equities diverge from the macro story. The Shanghai Composite closed at 3,959.62 on December 25, up 2.18% over 20 sessions (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2025-12-25). The Hang Seng closed at 25,818.93, down 0.42% over 20 sessions but up 1.37% over five (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2025-12-24). The CSI 300 A-share ETF (ASHR) rose 1.77% over 20 sessions to 32.82 (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2025-12-24). The China large-cap ETF (FXI) fell 2.65%, and the internet ETF (KWEB) fell 8.56% to 34.51 (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2025-12-24). USD/CNY closed at 7.0280, down 0.8% over 20 sessions (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2025-12-24). The offshore yuan has broken a major benchmark as appreciation momentum builds, and a top forecaster says strength beyond the 7 level will be brief.

New / Most Important: The most important item is the trade-policy reading. The record surplus is turning into a protectionist problem, and the SCMP says it could prompt a protectionist response in 2026. The state is steering investment toward favoured sectors, with Xi urging state giants to be pillars of national development. Foreign houses are moving within the region: Citi downgraded China stocks and lifted Taiwan on AI and earnings. The yuan is the clearest positive in the external story, and a Reuters piece says the debt boom signals its arrival as a funding currency.

Playbook ✓: Bearish on domestic cyclicals and credit beta, and prefer exporters and offshore yuan exposure. The currency is the cleaner long: USD/CNY is down 0.8% over 20 sessions. China internet beta is the short, with KWEB down 8.56% over the same stretch. Trigger to turn constructive: a credit print that beats the November bank-lending expectation, followed by stabilising home sales, and the feed shows neither yet. What would change the call: fiscal support beyond the steady-support tone of the central bank. ⚠ Divergence: the Shanghai Composite is up 2.18% over 20 sessions and the offshore yuan has broken a major benchmark, even though the November activity data and the property slump point the other way.

🇯🇵 Japan  Mixed  BOJ hiking, yen defended

Macro Stance: Mixed. The Bank of Japan is on a hiking path and says so openly. Ueda signals further hikes with more confidence over the price goal, and a hawkish signal suggests the next hike may come sooner than markets think. Policy rates have reached their highest since 1995. Core inflation held steady in November and stayed above the BOJ target, and consumer inflation has stayed above the central bank’s target for a 44th month. Tokyo inflation cooled by more than expected, which is the one soft print in the feed.

Activity & Currency: USD/JPY closed at 156.1760 (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2025-12-24), up 0.9% over five sessions and flat over 20 (-0.03%). The yen is being defended in words. Japan issued its sternest intervention warning, saying the yen was deviating from fundamentals, and the finance minister’s remarks pulled the pair back to 156. The Nikkei 225 closed at 50,407.79 on December 25 (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2025-12-25), up 0.48% over 20 sessions and 2.87% over five. Fiscal supply is the other pressure. The cabinet approved a record $785 billion budget, and the government is likely to cut super-long issuance to a 17-year low, sources say. A former BOJ policymaker warns that fiscal woes may cause more yen falls and yield rises.

Playbook ✓: Mixed. Hedge a long-yen view with options rather than spot, because the intervention warnings are explicit and the pair has been held near 156. Own the banks that benefit from the rate path, but treat the Nikkei’s 0.48% gain over 20 sessions as a fiscal-and-AI story, not a weak-yen trade. Trigger for a yen-long: a confirmed intervention, or a hawkish BOJ signal that pulls USD/JPY back under the 156.23 level of November 26 (card reference). Trigger to change the call: a super-long issuance cut that fails to hold, leaving JGB yields higher and the yen weaker, which is the ex-BOJ policymaker’s scenario.

Playbook ✓: Bearish. Underweight the ASX rate-sensitives, meaning banks, property and REITs, into the February decision that two majors expect. Express the hike view through AUD strength rather than through equities, since the currency is up 3.58% over 20 sessions and has already priced some of it. Trigger to neutralise: a February hold, or bank forecasts moving back towards cuts, which is the ‘clash’ scenario in the feed. The Social Signal: Attention is the feed’s visible tell. The Yahoo piece with an influencer headline, ‘Gold Prices Soar Past $4,400: Humphrey Yang Explains Why’, is the mainstream retail channel. Business Insider Polska’s ‘historic record on gold, prices like this have never been seen’ is the Polish version. Stocktwits and Reddit have no metals-specific chatter in the feed, so the social read is inferred from mainstream and influencer coverage, which is a weaker signal. How the crowd reads institutional skepticism is also in the headlines. Reuters’ commentary says guns and gold won in 2025 while other safety trades bombed. Retail’s crypto appetite is fading, with Bloomberg describing a brutal year for retail traders, which the writer reads as money rotating toward metals. That rotation is the crowd’s view, not a reported fact.

Historical Analog: The closest earlier episode is the 2011 gold peak. The first wave was institutional and macro: the debasement story was about quantitative easing and reserve buying, not about retail. Gold kept rising into its 2011 peak and then fell back for several years, while the record-headline and ‘buy the dip’ columns ran hardest close to the top. The mechanical similarity is that institutional hedging comes first and the crowd’s record-headline phase comes second. China cutting its US Treasury holdings to the lowest since 2008 is the reserve-diversification first wave, and today’s retail-facing headlines are the second. The 2021 meme-stock frenzy is the other reference: retail arrived late there too, and the Krispy Kreme meme headline shows the crowd still chasing elsewhere.

Smart Money View: The institutional case is real and split. For: Italy’s parliamentary panel approved a ‘people’s’ claim on the central bank’s gold, and Bloomberg’s Ziemba says gold looks the way to play a weaker dollar. Against: Reuters’ commentary warns that other safety trades bombed in 2025, Seeking Alpha says silver in 2026 is set for continued elevation but to be careful, and the Fed is not cutting fast, since Hammack says a pause is her base case. The writer’s reading is that institutions are hedging the metals through rates and currency positions rather than through the metals alone.

When the Narrative Breaks: Three observable triggers. First, a rate-cut repricing: if the 2-year rises back above its 3.47% close (US Treasury, prior close 2025-12-24), the cut bet unwinds and the metals lose their rate support. Second, the dollar: a bounce from the 97.98 DXY close (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2025-12-24) would undercut the weaker-dollar argument. Third, the price: silver back toward its 50.93 reference from November 25 would reverse the 20-session move, and gold back toward 4,140.00, the card reference from November 25, would erase the 20-session gain. A break in any two of the three turns the crowd’s narrative into a late-cycle story.

Palantir / PLTR  "they can’t get enough"

Why Retail Is Here: The crowd’s story is that Wall Street wrote Palantir off as too expensive while retail investors ‘can’t get enough’. A stock-split watch piece asks whether the stock is next, and an AI-stock list keeps it near the top. Palantir closed at 194.17, up 18.72% over 20 sessions and 9.52% over five (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2025-12-24).

Where It Breaks: The trigger is a fall back toward the 163.55 level of November 25 (card reference), which would erase the 20-session gain. The instrument risk is a short-dated call spread bought on the split story: a delay in any split would strip the premium. The writer reads the crowd as comparing names rather than pricing them, which is why the narrative can turn quickly. The five-session move of 9.52% is the part most exposed to a reversal, because it is the late chase that the valuation objection targets (the writer’s reading).

Infosys / INFY  "the short squeeze"

Why Retail Is Here: A short squeeze is the story the crowd tells after the move. Infosys’s stock suddenly surged 50% and then cooled off, MarketWatch reports, with Wall Street’s Friday mystery unexplained. Goodreturns reports the NYSE stopped trading the Infosys ADRs after a 56% jump, and ties that to a short squeeze. The crowd reads a pause in trading as proof the squeeze is real.

Where It Breaks: The trigger is the cooling itself: a second move needs the same float and short-interest dynamics, and the feed carries no price data for the ADR. The writer’s reading is that the instrument risk is a crowded short that stops being squeezed once trading resumes and liquidity returns. The crowd’s narrative breaks when the pause is lifted. Watch for a second failed push above the squeeze highs, which would show the squeeze has been used up: the crowd reads a repeatable pattern, while the writer reads a pause in trading as a sign of liquidity stress rather than proof of demand.

World Liberty USD1 / USD1  "the 20% yield"

Why Retail Is Here: The crowd’s pitch is yield. Yahoo reports the Trump-linked USD1 stablecoin crossed $3 billion in market capitalisation after Binance rolled out a 20% yield, and a Business Wire release reports the same milestone. Ranking lists of fast-moving stablecoins add to the buzz. Retail is reaching for yield in a market where bitcoin is down 2.41% over 20 sessions to 87,234.74 (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2025-12-25).

Where It Breaks: The trigger is a yield cut or a peg question. A stablecoin paying 20% depends on the platform that pays it, and the feed carries nothing on reserves. The writer’s reading is that the narrative breaks when yields are cut or the peg is questioned, and the market cap stops compounding. Watch bitcoin against its 89,387.76 reference (card) as the risk-appetite gauge: a close below it would confirm the squeeze has left crypto.
