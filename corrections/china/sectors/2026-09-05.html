💻 XLK — Technology  Mixed

Narrative: Mega-cap tech still dominates a narrow market: the Nasdaq 100 at 29,544 is down only 0.6% vs 4 weeks ago despite 10Y yields surging to 4.78%, resilience that rests on perceived earnings durability, and the AI capex cycle remains the central narrative.

Reported: Dell surged 9% after lifting its fiscal 2027 forecast on AI server strength (CNBC, [Sep 1]), with unfilled orders at a record $95 billion (Stock Titan, [Sep 1]). Snowflake spiked 22% on healthy results and AI coding momentum (CNBC, [Sep 2]). MongoDB beat every estimate and raised guidance, then lost 13.6% in a single session (24/7 Wall St., [Sep 2]).

EPS Upside Watch: Oracle — Morgan Stanley raised its target to $210 on the margin outlook (Investing.com, [Sep 4]), with results due post-market on September 10 (Moomoo, [Sep 4]). Samsara — analysts boosted their forecasts after a strong Q2 (Benzinga, [Sep 4]).

⚡ Breaking Convention: Beat-and-raise is no longer enough: Palo Alto beat but margin concerns sent its shares lower (SiliconANGLE, [Sep 1]), and MongoDB fell on a raise. When the bar keeps rising, positioning is already long, and if 10Y yields stay above 4.75% the DCF math on long-duration AI beneficiaries deteriorates.

🛢️ XLE — Energy  Bullish

Narrative: WTI at $91.48, up 17% in 4 weeks, with diesel at all-time highs of $5.85/gal, is the dominant macro signal; with China's official factory PMI still below 50 for a second month, this is an Iran supply shock, not demand pull.

Reported: Sasol's earnings rose 9% on higher oil prices and fuel sales volumes (Reuters, [Sep 1]). PetroChina's first-half profit jumped 22% despite the Iran war oil supply crunch (South China Morning Post, [Aug 31]).

EPS Upside Watch: Kosmos Energy — a Zacks upgrade and earnings revisions put it in focus (Yahoo Finance, [Sep 4]). Energy Transfer — up 29% in 2026, a run explained by two guidance raises (TIKR.com, [Aug 30]).

⚡ Breaking Convention: Firms cut rigs for the first time in six weeks even as WTI surged, the opposite of what the supply-response model predicts. Producers either doubt the spike or are physically constrained, and either way the supply-side safety valve that capped past rallies is missing: if the Iran conflict escalates, $100+ oil becomes the base case.
