🇨🇳 China  Mixed  → easing bias

Macro Stance: The PBOC's new overnight framework is now live. After debuting the overnight reverse repo at 1.25% (Reuters, [Jun 28]), it doubled the overnight cash injection while keeping the rate unchanged (Reuters, [Jun 29]); Bloomberg notes the surprise that no rate was formally released ([Jun 29]). With loan prime rates still on hold, the easing is happening at the very front end and through volume, not through a headline cut. The yuan fixing was weakened for a fourth session as the dollar advanced (Bloomberg, [Jun 23]), a managed drift against a dollar near 13-month highs, while Euronews details how the currency widens the EU's deficit ([Jun 23]); T. Rowe Price sees the yuan 1%-2% weaker by year-end (TradingView, [Jun 26]). Prices are split: May CPI rose only 1.2% (China Daily, [Jun 10]) while factory-gate inflation hit a near four-year high (Reuters, [Jun 9]).

PMIs & Activity: The growth pulse turned up in June. The official manufacturing PMI rose to 50.3 (Reuters, [Jun 29]), back into expansion from a flat 50 in May (Reuters, [May 30]) and faster than expected on tech export demand (CNBC, [Jun 29]); the non-manufacturing PMI rose to 50.2 (Reuters, [Jun 29]). The composition matters: Bloomberg frames it as a return to growth because exports are booming ([Jun 29]), and ING still expects a second-quarter slowdown ([Jun 29]). The May hard data show why: industrial output growth quickened to 4.5% (China Daily, [Jun 16]), but retail sales fell 0.6%, the first drop in over three years (Il Sole 24 Ore, [Jun 15]), and fixed-asset investment was down 4.1% in the first five months (Macau Business, [Jun 16]). Industrial profits rose 18.8% in January–May (WSJ, [Jun 26]), but profit growth slowed for the first time in six months as weak domestic demand bit (Times of India, [Jun 27]).

Credit Impulse: Total social financing grew 7.7% in May (CGTN, [Jun 12]) and topped forecasts (Bloomberg, [Jun 12]), but new loans missed as the property slump curbed demand (Reuters, [Jun 12]). The beat is government-bond issuance; private credit demand is still contracting. The PBOC is now urging banks to boost lending because demand remains weak, according to sources (Reuters, [Jun 26]). Household deleveraging continues (SCMP, [Jun 15]). Read: the headline impulse is flat-to-positive, but the household and private-firm impulse is still negative, which is why output does not convert into consumption.

Property: New home prices fell at a faster pace in May (Reuters, [Jun 15]) even as first-tier new home prices rose for a third month (SCMP, [Jun 16]) and top-city resale prices extended their rebound (Caixin, [Jun 17]). Real-estate investment fell 16.2% y/y in January–May (GMK Center, [Jun 18]). This week the stress resurfaced at the developer level: Beijing land sales plunged 66% as developers chase only "safe" prime plots (Caixin, [Jun 25]), and the Evergrande Property Services stake sale collapsed, sending its shares down 24% (Caixin, [Jun 26]). Nikkei Asia notes state developers are powering Shenzhen construction despite market woes ([Jun 29]): state capital replacing private developers.

Fiscal, Policy & Trade: Fiscal policy is tightening at the margin: Beijing narrowed its cumulative deficit for the first time since 2023 (Business Times, [Jun 23]), and the 62.5 billion yuan third batch of trade-in funds (Global Times, [Jun 26]) is small against the retail contraction. The State Council reviewed AI, trade and carbon plans (Dimsum Daily, [Jun 29]), with no new demand package. On trade, the EU and China agreed an October deadline to reset ties (Bloomberg, [Jun 29]), with three months of talks over a €360bn deficit (The Guardian, [Jun 29]) and a €3 customs charge on small parcels aimed at cheap Chinese imports (The Guardian, [Jun 29]). Beijing kept using export controls as leverage, adding 20 Japanese entities to its dual-use list (Reuters, [Jun 29]) after targeting US rare-earth firms (Mexico Business News, [Jun 24]). The NYT casts China as a relative winner from the Strait of Hormuz crisis ([Jun 29]).

Markets & Flows: Hong Kong rebounded to 23,026 on US-Iran peace-talk optimism and a tech recovery (BBN Times, [Jun 29]) after capping its worst week in over a year (SCMP, [Jun 26]), and broker stocks eye their best month since October on a trading boom (Bloomberg, [Jun 29]). But the relative picture is poor: Bloomberg says Chinese stocks are trailing the AI boom by the most since 2001 ([Jun 29]). Regulators are also clamping down on higher-yielding offshore debt issuance (Bloomberg, [Jun 29]), tightening one more outflow and funding channel.

New / Most Important: The June PMI is the first upturn in the growth pulse in months, and it is export-led. Manufacturing back above 50 on tech export demand, with services barely positive, fits the same split as May: an AI and export engine running hot while household demand and private credit stay weak. Paired with cheaper overnight money and the October EU deadline, the near-term risk for China is not recession but a trade backlash against the very sector that is carrying growth.

Playbook ✓: Property drag, weak consumption, LPR on hold and a managed yuan drift lower are all on script.  |  ⚠️ Divergence: The consensus expected a mid-year stimulus pivot; instead the June PMI upturn rests on exports while the credit impulse outside government bonds is negative and the fiscal impulse is narrowing. China is therefore exporting its excess supply rather than absorbing it at home. The transmission runs through three channels. European exporters face a €360bn deficit and a weak yuan, which is why Brussels has set an October deadline. Bulk-commodity demand tied to construction is fading with the investment slump, a drag on AUD and iron ore that an export-led PMI does not offset. And global goods disinflation is the channel Pimco expects to support EM bonds (Bloomberg, [Jun 23]). A real turn needs loan growth, not bond issuance, to re-accelerate; until it does, rallies in Hong Kong are flows, not fundamentals.
