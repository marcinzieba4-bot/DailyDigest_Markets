🇨🇳 China  Mixed  → accommodative / easing bias

Macro Stance: The easing bias runs through liquidity and the balance sheet, not the policy rate. The PBOC is conducting a 1.2 trillion yuan outright reverse repo (巴士的報, [Oct 8]) and bought a net 100 billion yuan of sovereign bonds in September (TradingView, [Oct 9]), so China's bond yields are falling as they surge everywhere else (Quartz, [Oct 8]); with US 10Y yields at 5.24%, the rate gap keeps a benchmark cut off the table. The currency is not the pressure point the dollar rally implies: the central bank says it has no need or intention to weaken the yuan for a trade edge (The Standard (HK), [Oct 8]), the yuan hit a four-year high against its basket (Bloomberg, [Oct 8]) and the fix firmed to 6.7330 from 6.7367 (www.tmgm.com, [Oct 9]). Prices no longer bind: August CPI rose 0.8% and PPI 3.8% (BigGo Finance, [Sep 8]), and September CPI is on this week's calendar (Newsquawk, [Oct 9]).

PMIs & Activity: September's official manufacturing PMI returned to growth at 50.1, exactly at consensus and up from 49.8, with non-manufacturing at 50.2 (investingLive, [Sep 29]); the private RatingDog gauges were stronger at 52.1 and 51.6 (investingLive, [Sep 30]), and Reuters ties the factory expansion to the AI boom (Reuters, [Sep 29]). The domestic half lags: August retail sales rose 0.4% against 0.6% expected (investingLive, [Sep 14]) and industrial profit growth slowed to 4.2% (Bloomberg, [Sep 27]). The holiday did not change that split: Golden Week spending per trip hit a four-year low (Gulf Times, [Oct 10]) and September passenger-vehicle retail sales dropped 24% (Bloomberg, [Oct 10]). Nikkei estimates third-quarter growth at 4.4%, below target (Nikkei Asia, [Oct 6]).

Credit Impulse: The last print is August's, weak on both measures: bank lending disappointed as credit demand stayed weak (Reuters, [Sep 14]) and total credit expansion missed forecasts (Bloomberg, [Sep 14]), with bond and equity financing exceeding loans in social financing for the first time (BigGo Finance, [Sep 14]); the government-bond leg held up while private loan demand did not. The state subsidises credit rather than igniting it: the finance ministry says 100 billion yuan of interest subsidies leveraged 17 trillion yuan of consumer credit (BigGo Finance, [Oct 9]). Meanwhile China is consolidating nearly one in four banks as strains hit smaller lenders (NTD News, [Oct 5]).

Property: Golden Week gave the clearest demand signal in months. New-home subscriptions in 50 key cities rose over 15% year on year (Moomoo, [Oct 8]), Shanghai new-home sales doubled and second-hand transactions in 11 cities jumped over 40% (BigGo Finance, [Oct 9]), and the mortgage subsidy is credited with lifting holiday homebuying (iChongqing, [Oct 10]); Bloomberg still calls it a modest recovery (Bloomberg, [Oct 8]). Top-100 developers sold 242 billion yuan in September (www.grihik.com, [Oct 8]). Balance sheets remain the weak point: Vanke reported a 16.02 billion yuan loss (ad-hoc-news.de, [Oct 7]) and PAG is in exclusive talks for a stake in an Evergrande unit (Reuters, [Oct 8]). The base is August's: prices down across 70 cities and investment down 19.9% in eight months (BigGo Finance, [Sep 14]).

Fiscal, Policy & Trade: Beijing lets local governments tap 550 billion yuan of unused debt quotas (Caixin Global, [Oct 9]), a fiscal push to meet the growth target (Reuters, [Oct 9]), and the NDRC pledges more policy tools (Bloomberg, [Oct 10]); but the growing debt burden limits space for stimulus (Financial Times, [Oct 10]) and the WSJ calls it half measures (WSJ, [Oct 8]). On trade, the EU and China struck a deal to halve Chinese hybrid exports (Reuters, [Oct 9]) within a 16-point consensus that covers export controls (Global Times, [Oct 9]). Britain is the next front, with Beijing warning of serious concern over its EV tariff plan (The Telegraph, [Oct 6]), and the US rare-earth truce was extended by just two months (Startup Fortune, [Oct 7]). The export engine behind it surged 25% in August (Nikkei Asia, [Sep 8]).

Markets & Flows: China stocks hit an over one-year low as the AI selloff deepened (The Economic Times, [Oct 9]) and the Hang Seng fell to a three-month low on the first day back (The Standard (HK), [Oct 8]) before a 1.79% rebound (Moomoo, [Oct 9]) on unconfirmed talk of regulator guidance (Bloomberg, [Oct 9]). Breakingviews argues China's slow bull market is poised to take off (Reuters, [Oct 8]), while Yahoo Finance asks why China is missing the AI rally (Yahoo Finance, [Oct 9]).

New / Most Important: The Golden Week verdict is split. Housing responded to the subsidy, with Shanghai new-home sales doubling, while spending per trip hit a four-year low and car sales fell 24%; Beijing's answer is 550 billion yuan of quota and more NDRC tools rather than a rate cut. The EU hybrid deal removes the near-term tariff tail but caps the export channel that carried August.

Playbook ✓: Property in controlled deflation with a subsidy-driven bounce, incremental quota releases, liquidity through outright repos and bond buying, and exports diverting around tariffs are all consensus.  |  ⚠️ Divergence: The structural divergence is China's absence from the global AI equity trade even as AI drives its factory PMI. If the slow-bull thesis gains traction, the re-rating catalyst will be AI re-pricing, not consumption, pulling capital from US mega-cap tech and supporting the yuan. Transmission elsewhere stays narrow: an AI-led PMI helps Asian tech supply chains; the housing bounce is the first to reach the demand iron ore and the AUD need, but with property investment down 19.9% and credit missing, the AUD, whose own factory PMI fell to 49.6 on falling new orders (investingLive, [Sep 30]), gets sentiment rather than an impulse. European autos gain from a cap on Chinese hybrids, but luxury needs a consumer whose spending per trip just hit a four-year low.
