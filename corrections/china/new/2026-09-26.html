🇨🇳 China  Bearish  → easing bias (limited data this week)

Macro Stance: Easing still runs through liquidity, not rates. The loan prime rates were held for a 16th month (Reuters, [Sep 19]) as the Fed hike narrows the room to ease (investingLive, [Sep 20]); instead the PBOC will offer banks up to 1 trillion yuan a day over the holidays (Bloomberg, [Sep 23]) and its third-quarter policy committee pledged to stay 'appropriately loose' (TradingView, [Sep 24]). The yuan hit a fresh multi-year peak as the PBOC eased its curb before the summit (Reuters, [Sep 21]), but a fix at 6.7468 against an estimate of 6.6971 (investingLive, [Sep 22]) marks the ceiling, and Societe Generale sees the PBOC keeping the yuan broadly stable (FXStreet, [Sep 25]). August CPI rose 0.8% and PPI 3.8% (BigGo Finance, [Sep 8]): energy-driven factory prices, barely positive consumer prices.

PMIs & Activity: No new monthly print landed this week, so the August surveys still set the level. The official manufacturing PMI rebounded to 49.8 with new orders back in expansion (BigGo Finance, [Aug 30]), below 50 for a second month, while the private gauge rose to 51.5 (BigGo Finance, [Aug 31]) and private services to 51.4 (BigGo Finance, [Sep 2]). Reconciled: the private surveys expand, the official one does not. Hard data split the same way: output growth quickened to 5.2% (BigGo Finance, [Sep 14]), but retail sales rose only 0.4% (investingLive, [Sep 14]) and fixed-asset investment fell 7.2% in eight months (Xinhua, [Sep 15]). The newest read is external: a private survey showed a 'surprise' jump in US orders ahead of the summit (CNBC, [Sep 25]), after August exports surged 25% (Financial Times, [Sep 7]). Domestic margins are not following, with the share of loss-making firms approaching 30% (BigGo Finance, [Sep 25]).

Credit Impulse: August bank lending disappointed (Reuters, [Sep 14]) and credit expansion missed forecasts as loan demand sagged (Bloomberg, [Sep 14]), so loans and the aggregate do not conflict this month: both missed. The composition is the story: bond and equity financing exceeded loans in social financing for the first time (BigGo Finance, [Sep 14]), so issuance, not bank lending, carries the aggregate while households and firms decline to borrow. The PBOC is relaxed, holding rates as it signals slower credit (Chosunbiz, [Sep 20]), and regulators told e-commerce apps to stop selling loans (Sixth Tone, [Sep 21]). The one forced credit flow is defensive: banks were asked to keep Vanke loans off bad-debt books (Reuters, [Sep 22]).

Property: August home prices extended their decline across 70 cities, with tier-1 cities stabilising (BigGo Finance, [Sep 14]), and property investment fell 19.9% in eight months, the steepest decline on record (BigGo Finance, [Sep 14]). Relief this week was marginal: first-tier new home sales jumped over 10% (BigGo Finance, [Sep 23]) and Beijing now requires topped-out structures for presales (China Daily, [Sep 24]), while property stocks surged on mortgage-subsidy rumours that analysts called unlikely to be implemented (BigGo Finance, [Sep 23]).

Fiscal, Policy & Trade: The summit ended with little progress on trade and AI other than two new deadlines (Yahoo Finance, [Sep 25]): the truce runs two more months while both sides work on a bigger deal (Reuters, [Sep 23]), and Greer says trade enters a 'managed' phase with the deficit set to fall to $140B (Yahoo Finance, [Sep 25]), with details due on Monday (Reuters, [Sep 25]). Beijing kept its levers visible: rare earth exports to the US dropped before the summit (Bloomberg, [Sep 21]). Europe is hardening: the ECB finds China's industrial rise hits German manufacturers hard (Reuters, [Sep 22]) and the EU urges Britain to raise tariffs on Chinese cars (Reuters, [Sep 25]). At home fiscal policy is tight: revenue growth slowed as government spending contracted (Caixin Global, [Sep 19]); the Politburo has set the Central Committee plenum for late October (U.S. News & World Report, [Sep 21]).

Markets & Flows: Mainland stocks posted their biggest one-day drop in a month on summit caution (TradingView, [Sep 24]), and Shanghai then closed for the Mid-Autumn Festival holding at 3,888.37 after a 1.22% slide (BBN Times, [Sep 25]). The Hang Seng fell to 24,510.09 as the tech sell-off deepened in holiday-thin trade (BBN Times, [Sep 25]), after Chinese AI firms fell on a report of a DeepSeek and Moonshot probe (Bloomberg, [Sep 23]). Reserves keep rotating into gold: China imported a record of over 1,000 tonnes this year (Financial Times, [Sep 22]).

New / Most Important: The summit's verdict is in: little progress beyond two new deadlines, a truce measured in months and, per Greer, a 'managed' phase in which the deficit is set to fall to $140B. The one growth-positive read is external, the surprise jump in US orders, while domestic hopes fade: October stimulus expectations are cooling (BigGo Finance, [Sep 25]) as loss-making firms near 30%. Managed trade that shrinks the US deficit, with no domestic push behind it, keeps the stance Bearish.

Playbook ✓: Higher US rates keep squeezing EM, China included, but the pressure shows in equities and in the PBOC's caution on the fix rather than in the yuan. Key data on China is expected in the week ahead per ING (ing think, [Sep 24]).  |  ⚠️ Divergence: Supply is strong and demand weak: output up 5.2% and exports up 25% against retail sales at 0.4% and property investment down 19.9%. That mix transmits abroad as competition, not demand. European exporters face it directly, as the ECB's German findings show; Australia's factory PMI fell to 49.3 (Mitrade, [Sep 22]) while China's imports miss and its property investment shrinks, leaving the AUD and iron ore without a China demand prop as all four major Australian banks forecast a hike. WTI's 10.6% run to 92.41 reflects a supply shock, not China demand, and is a terms-of-trade tax that a sub-50 PMI cannot absorb.
