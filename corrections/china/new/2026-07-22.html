🇨🇳 China  Mixed  → easing bias

Macro Stance: Loan prime rates were held for a 14th consecutive month (The Business Times, [Jul 20]) despite the second-quarter growth miss (Sharecast, [Jul 20]), and the market now eyes a rate-cut window near the end of the third quarter (BigGo Finance, [Jul 21]). Until then the PBOC's emphasis is the currency: it sees a stable yuan amid two-way fluctuations (State Council Information Office, [Jul 16]) and is moving to deepen offshore yuan liquidity (Caixin, [Jul 16]); the yuan weakened slightly on the fixing (BusinessToday Malaysia, [Jul 17]) and UOB sees an upside bias below 6.7820 (TMGM, [Jul 20]). Prices are the wrong mix: June CPI rose only 1% (chinadailyhk, [Jul 8]) and slowed more than forecast (Bloomberg, [Jul 8]), while PPI climbed 4.1% (BigGo Finance, [Jul 8]) to a near four-year high that squeezes manufacturers (Reuters, [Jul 8]).

PMIs & Activity: The surveys and the hard data point in different directions. The June official manufacturing PMI rose to 50.3 and the non-manufacturing PMI to 50.2 (Reuters, [Jun 29]); the private gauge capped the strongest factory quarter since late 2020 (Reuters, [Jun 30]). Yet second-quarter GDP growth weakened to 4.3%, below the official target range (Bloomberg, [Jul 15]). June industrial output rose 5.3% y/y and retail sales returned to growth (BusinessLine, [Jul 15]), but retail sales of consumer goods were up just 1.3% in the first half (Ecns.cn, [Jul 15]), infrastructure investment turned negative (BigGo Finance, [Jul 19]) and the car market heads for its worst year since 2021 (CNBC, [Jul 20]). Even Shanghai's 5.6% beat comes with a two-speed recovery (SCMP, [Jul 21]); Trivium reads the miss as a deepening split between tech exports and domestic demand ([Jul 21]), with June exports up 27% y/y (Reuters, [Jul 13]).

Credit Impulse: Credit is the weak link. June new loans missed forecasts (Bloomberg, [Jul 15]) and overall credit growth undershot as the PBOC flagged a "new normal" (Caixin, [Jul 16]). Household loans decreased by 366.8 billion yuan in the first half (36Kr, [Jul 17]), and record consumer defaults undermine the push to boost spending (Reuters, [Jul 15]). Read: with households repaying and private firms not borrowing, the impulse rests on state-directed financing.

Property: Prices are stabilising at the top while capital keeps leaving. June new home prices fell at a slower pace, though Reuters doubts a recovery ([Jul 14]), and first-tier prices extended a 4-month rebound (SCMP, [Jul 15]). But property investment slumped 18% y/y in January–June (The Standard, [Jul 15]) and KKR and AEW seek to sell China property assets at steep losses (Bloomberg, [Jul 20]). Developer survival still runs through state shareholders: Vanke secured a 519 million yuan loan from Shenzhen Metro at 2.29% (BigGo Finance, [Jul 21]), while Beijing curbs new bond sales by indebted LGFVs (Bloomberg, [Jul 15]).

Fiscal, Policy & Trade: Beijing vowed to achieve its annual growth target (Bloomberg, [Jul 20]) and the premier chaired a State Council executive meeting (Macau Business, [Jul 21]), but the toolkit is still plans and projects: the first five-year consumption plan (State Council Information Office, [Jul 15]) and faster state-backed projects to avoid broad stimulus (Reuters, [Jul 16]), even as the FT says leaders are zeroing in on the need for stimulus ([Jul 18]). At the margin support is being withdrawn, with a key tax break on lithium EV batteries ending (Caixin, [Jul 20]). On trade, EU lawmakers arrived for high-stakes talks (SCMP, [Jul 21]) and the foreign minister discussed the trade balance with them (Reuters, [Jul 21]) as the yuan draws scrutiny in Europe (Bloomberg, [Jul 21]) and Paris and Berlin present a united front (SCMP, [Jul 20]). A new US sanctions bill threatens a 100% tariff on buyers of Russian oil, China included (The Independent, [Jul 16]).

Markets & Flows: The rescue has broadened. After the national team bought shares worth $9bn (FT, [Jul 20]), Beijing added record inflows into a tech ETF (Bloomberg, [Jul 21]) and long-term buying by state-backed insurers (SCMP, [Jul 21]). Hong Kong semiconductor stocks surged over 11% (BigGo Finance, [Jul 21]) and Zhipu jumped 37% as it builds a giant data centre on Chinese chips (SCMP, [Jul 21]), though the Hang Seng closed down 0.04% (Moomoo, [Jul 21]). Breakingviews warns the national team faces a risky tech challenge (Reuters, [Jul 21]); Citi has upgraded China to overweight (The Standard, [Jul 20]).

New / Most Important: The market rescue has become AI industrial policy. Beijing seeks to rescue AI stocks without fueling a bubble (Bloomberg, [Jul 21]) while considering export controls on AI technologies, including barring local companies from using TSMC (Yahoo Finance, [Jul 21]). Set beside the NVIDIA-China export restriction debate in the US, which Cramer frames as national security, both sides are now fencing AI supply chains. For China that concentrates state capital in the one sector that is growing, while household credit shrinks and property investment falls; it raises valuations in tech without raising the domestic demand the Q2 miss exposed.

Playbook ✓: Rate hold, managed yuan, state support for equities and stimulus by plan rather than by cash are on script.  |  ⚠️ Divergence: Emerging-market assets rallied on China's tech support (Bloomberg, [Jul 21]), so the equity channel to EM is real, but the macro channels have not changed. China keeps importing iron ore heavily (Mining.com.au, [Jul 21]) even with property investment down 18%, because surplus steel is exported: Indian steelmakers are pivoting home as China squeezes margins (Reuters, [Jul 21]). That supports iron ore and AUD for now while pressuring steel producers from India to Europe, and an export-led 50.3 PMI keeps European exporters exposed to the curbs Paris and Berlin are aligning on. A real lift to Asian and commodity demand still needs household and private credit to turn.
