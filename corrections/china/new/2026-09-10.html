🇨🇳 China  Mixed  → accommodative

Macro Stance: Policy stays accommodative while the rest of the G4 tightens. August CPI rose 0.8% and PPI 3.8% (Xinhua, [Sep 9]) as rising energy costs lifted both (Reuters, [Sep 9]), with PPI back to monthly growth (Xinhua, [Sep 9]); but consumer prices are still below target (Free Malaysia Today, [Sep 9]), and VT Markets reads stable inflation as bolstering the view of further easing (VT Markets, [Sep 9]). The PBOC again set the fix far weaker than the Reuters estimate of 6.7074 (investingLive, [Sep 9]), leaning against a yuan whose uptrend BBH calls intact (FXStreet, [Sep 9]); forex reserves rose more than expected (Reuters, [Sep 7]) and the gold buying streak reached a 22nd month (Caixin Global, [Sep 8]).

PMIs & Activity: Every August survey improved. The official manufacturing PMI rebounded to 49.8 with new orders back in expansion (BigGo Finance, [Aug 30]), still a second month of contraction (CNBC, [Aug 30]); the private manufacturing PMI rose to 51.5 (BigGo Finance, [Aug 31]) and private services to 51.4 (BigGo Finance, [Sep 2]). The external leg is booming: exports jumped 25% as the surplus widened (AP News, [Sep 7]) and the surplus topped $800bn for the year (Nikkei Asia, [Sep 8]), but imports missed estimates (CNBC, [Sep 7]) and a tech boom props up exports amid weak domestic demand (International Finance, [Sep 9]). At home, passenger car sales slumped 24% in August (Caixin Global, [Sep 9]), after July retail sales grew only 0.6% with industrial output up 4.5% (Reuters, [Aug 17]). US firms in China are more upbeat as profits recover (Reuters, [Sep 9]).

Credit Impulse: Banks are swapping loans for bonds, snapping up bonds to seek returns as the loan slump deepens (Bloomberg.com, [Sep 8]), after July new loans posted a record contraction (Reuters, [Aug 14]) even as credit growth exceeded forecasts (Bloomberg.com, [Aug 14]); reconciled, the aggregate holds up while the loan book shrinks. The 300 billion yuan of special treasury bonds for bank capital (State Council Information Office, [Sep 7]) falls far short in The Economist's view (The Economist, [Sep 8]); the tobacco monopoly is flexing financial muscle in shoring up banks (Nikkei Asia, [Sep 9]) and S&P upgraded PICC Re's outlook after the recapitalisation (InsuranceAsia News, [Sep 9]). The 3.8 trillion yuan social security fund will tap offshore bonds via Bond Connect (The Standard (HK), [Sep 9]).

Property: Beijing is narrowing developers' exits. The securities regulator rejected mandatory convertible bond registrations from developers (BigGo Finance, [Sep 9]) and China halted a key debt-to-equity restructuring tool (The Standard (HK), [Sep 9]), while court guidance aims to smooth developer bankruptcies (South China Morning Post, [Sep 9]). Goldman sees the new presale rules cutting land sales by 30% (The Business Times, [Sep 7]), Guangdong held emergency talks with developers over off-plan sales (BigGo Finance, [Sep 7]), and Beijing pledges a new development model for real estate (Global Times, [Sep 9]). July new home prices were stagnant (Reuters, [Aug 17]) and first-tier prices flat, ending a four-month rebound (South China Morning Post, [Aug 17]).

Fiscal, Policy & Trade: The surplus is becoming geopolitical (Financial Times, [Sep 9]). The EU unveiled 'buy European' procurement rules to counter China (The Guardian, [Sep 9]) and wants initial Chinese action on the trade gap by early October (Reuters, [Sep 8]), while an Italian lobby calls for an 80% EU tariff on Chinese cars (CNBC Africa, [Sep 9]); Japan protests new Chinese measures against its chipmaking material (AP News, [Sep 8]). With Washington, China bought 1 million tons of US soybeans ahead of Xi's visit (Reuters, [Sep 9]), though Barron's warns the fragile truce could break before the summit (Barron's, [Sep 9]) and the US claims Chinese AI firms carry out 'industrial-scale' theft of trade secrets (CNN, [Sep 8]).

Markets & Flows: The Hang Seng plunged below 25,000 as US Treasury yields spiked past 4.85% and oil topped $100 (BigGo Finance, [Sep 9]), closing 1.27% lower (China Daily HK, [Sep 9]); a top fund manager says Chinese stocks face Fed and oil pressures in September (South China Morning Post, [Sep 9]). An IPO frenzy has regulators warning bankers not to flood the market with 'low-quality' companies (Financial Times, [Sep 9]). Chinese banks are buying US Treasuries after wooing dollar deposits (Reuters, [Sep 6]), while Deutsche Bank says yuan use grows as supply chains reshape trade (South China Morning Post, [Sep 9]).

New / Most Important: The US-China 10-year yield gap hit a record 317 basis points, adding pressure on the yuan (bloomingbit, [Sep 9]). It is the global tightening synchronisation (Fed, ECB, BOJ, RBA all hiking or leaning hawkish) made visible in one number, and it creates a structural capital outflow risk for China. For now the PBOC is managing appreciation rather than defending against depreciation, but if it stays accommodative while everyone else hikes, the gap is where pressure on the yuan builds.

Playbook ✓: Weak domestic demand alongside export strength, with car sales down 24% and imports missing, is consistent with an accommodative PBOC.  |  ⚠️ Divergence: China is now the only major economy easing, and this policy divergence could attract carry-trade outflows and put downward pressure on Chinese assets despite stimulus efforts. The transmission runs through demand rather than rates: improving PMIs and a 25% export surge support iron ore demand expectations and the AUD at the margin, but soft imports and falling car sales cap China's pull on EM exporters, and a record surplus meets EU procurement and tariff pushback that keeps European exporters squeezed. WTI at $101.39 is a terms-of-trade tax on an importer whose domestic demand is already weak.
