🇨🇳 China  Bullish  → easing bias maintained

Macro Stance: China remains in an accommodative posture, and the PBOC has started using the exchange rate as an easing tool. The loan prime rates were left steady for a 15th consecutive month (Reuters, [Aug 19]), the one-year at 3.0% and the five-year at 3.5% (BigGo Finance, [Aug 19]); liquidity runs through overnight reverse repos (Xinhua, [Aug 24]) and a 500 billion yuan MLF operation (BigGo Finance, [Aug 24]), and banks now use the overnight rate to price bonds as the PBOC reform widens (Bloomberg, [Aug 25]). After the yuan surged to 6.72, a 42-month high (BigGo Finance, [Aug 20]), the PBOC set a weaker fix above the Reuters estimate, signalling tolerance for depreciation to bolster growth (VT Markets, [Aug 25]), and the yuan slipped from its 3-1/2-year high (The Star, [Aug 25]). July CPI slowed to 0.5% and PPI to 3.5%, both missing expectations (BigGo Finance, [Aug 8]): there is room to ease.

PMIs & Activity: The data are the weak leg of the bull case. July's official manufacturing PMI fell to 49.2 and non-manufacturing to 49.0 (FXStreet, [Jul 31]); the private factory index eased to 50.9 (Fibre2Fashion, [Aug 3]) and private services growth hit its slowest since September 2024 (Reuters, [Aug 4]). July industrial output grew 4.5% y/y and retail sales only 0.6% (Reuters, [Aug 17]), and fixed-asset investment fell 6.7% in the first seven months (Xinhua, [Aug 17]). Exports are the offset, jumping almost a quarter in July (Financial Times, [Aug 6]) on AI-driven shipments. That is why Goldman says easing hopes revive with the economy growing 4% (Bloomberg, [Aug 23]) while warning Q3 growth could fall below 4.5% (BigGo Finance, [Aug 24]). July industrial profits are due this week, after first-half profits rose 18.7% as a tech boom masked the property slump (Caixin Global, [Jul 27]).

Credit Impulse: July bank loans posted a record contraction as credit demand faltered (Reuters, [Aug 14]), yet credit growth exceeded forecasts despite the rare loan slump (Bloomberg, [Aug 14]). Reconciled: the broad financing aggregate is holding up while the loan book itself shrank, so the impulse comes from outside bank lending. Policy is working on price rather than volume: corporate loan rates fell below 3% (BigGo Finance, [Aug 14]) and the subsidized loan program was expanded to revive demand (Caixin Global, [Aug 21]), with the SME loan cap raised to 75 million yuan. Liquidity is ample; demand for it is the missing piece.

Property: July new home prices were stagnant as demand stayed weak (Reuters, [Aug 17]); the 70-city annual decline narrowed to 3.2%, the smallest in five months, while property investment slumped nearly 20% (BigGo Finance, [Aug 17]). The monthly picture is softer, with prices falling at a faster pace (Bloomberg, [Aug 17]) and first-tier prices flat, ending a four-month rebound (SCMP, [Aug 17]). Easing continues at the margin: Beijing widened use of a $1.6 trillion fund to spur spending on homes (The Business Times, [Aug 19]) and Shanghai lowered second-home down payments (SCMP, [Aug 20]). The legacy is being closed out: Evergrande founder Hui Ka Yan was sentenced to life in prison (Reuters, [Aug 20]) and a court accepted the Evergrande liquidation a day later (SCMP, [Aug 21]).

Fiscal, Policy & Trade: Fiscal policy is the lever, but it will be targeted. China pledged timely fiscal support to bolster growth (Reuters, [Aug 20]) and optimised fiscal and financial policies to lift demand (Fibre2Fashion, [Aug 25]), yet key state media commentaries signal no "bazooka" (Caixin Global, [Aug 25]) and the $119 billion policy financing tool faces a roll-out lag (Reuters, [Aug 24]). On trade, the US eyes overcapacity tariffs of 7.5% before the Xi-Trump talks (Bloomberg, [Aug 24]), China warned the US it could retaliate over Iran sanctions (Financial Times, [Aug 25]), and EU officials will travel to China as the October deadline looms (Euronews.com, [Aug 25]), with Goldman estimating EU curbs threaten 27% of Chinese exports to the bloc (Bloomberg, [Aug 24]).

Markets & Flows: Business Insider's call to start paying attention to China's economy again captures the shift in sentiment, and capital is pivoting into physical AI, with Xpeng posting a huge funding round for its robotics unit. Near term, supply weighs: China stocks hit a near one-month low (TradingView, [Aug 25]) as Alibaba sells $10 billion of stock while major figures buy (MarketWatch, [Aug 25]), and Unitree's post-listing slump sparks bubble fears (Reuters, [Aug 25]). Dollar weakness, US Treasury buybacks reportedly redirecting capital toward Asia and Indian investors pouring record funds into overseas stocks support the flow picture.

New / Most Important: The PBOC has moved from slowing the yuan's gains to signalling tolerance for depreciation. That is the easing the bull case needed: a weaker fix loosens conditions for exporters without an LPR cut, at the moment state media rule out a bazooka. The risk has moved to trade, where Beijing's warning that it could retaliate over Iran sanctions lands weeks before the Xi-Trump talks and alongside a 7.5% overcapacity tariff threat.

Playbook ✓: Dollar weakness benefiting EM and China capital flows is textbook, and a PBOC that leans against yuan strength while pledging fiscal support fits the easing script.  |  ⚠️ Divergence: The bull case rests on flows and policy, not activity: the PMI is 49.2, the loan book shrank and property investment is down nearly 20%. A yuan held back from its high plus weak domestic demand means China exports price pressure rather than demand, which hits European exporters already facing the October deadline, while iron ore trades stimulus hopes, rising on fresh stimulus signals (TradingView, [Aug 24]) after weakening despite them (TradingView, [Aug 23]). If fiscal support stays targeted, the AUD and EM commodity exporters carry the downside, and WTI at 82.82 remains a terms-of-trade tax on China.
