🇨🇳 China  Mixed  → easing bias, but constrained

Macro Stance: China remains in a different posture from the rest of the world, easing while the West tightens: the PBOC cut the PSL rate by 0.25 percentage point to 1.5% (Forex Factory, [Sep 29]), boosted relending quotas by 700 billion yuan (BigGo Finance, [Sep 29]) and is ramping up structural tools as the State Council deploys incremental policies (BigGo Finance, [Oct 4]). The constraint is not the currency, despite a US 10Y at 5.34%: the yuan fixing was set at a 3.5-year high before the National Day holiday (The Standard (HK), [Sep 30]) and Rabobank's forecast points to a pause before further gains (Forex Factory, [Oct 3]). It is the choice of targeted over broad easing, with China's 10Y yield at an over one-year low (TradingView, [Sep 29]) and August CPI at 0.8% and PPI at 3.8% (BigGo Finance, [Sep 8]) driven by energy costs (Reuters, [Sep 9]).

PMIs & Activity: The official manufacturing PMI returned to growth at 50.1 with non-manufacturing at 50.2 (investingLive, [Sep 29]), and the private RatingDog gauges printed 52.1 and 51.6 (investingLive, [Sep 30]); 'beat' headlines (TradingView, [Sep 29]) and an official print at the 50.1 consensus (investingLive, [Sep 29]) reconcile as an in-line official survey and stronger private ones. Exports remain the bright spot: they soared 25% in August and the trade surplus tops $800bn for the year (Nikkei Asia, [Sep 8]). Consumption lags, with August retail sales up 0.4% (investingLive, [Sep 14]), peak-season auto sales growth disappointing (WSJ, [Oct 2]) and Nike's China sales down 26% (Retail Gazette, [Oct 2]); August industrial profit growth was the weakest since November (Bloomberg, [Sep 27]).

Credit Impulse: August bank lending disappointed (Reuters, [Sep 14]) and credit expansion missed forecasts (Bloomberg, [Sep 14]), with bond and equity financing exceeding loans for the first time (BigGo Finance, [Sep 14]): both measures missed, and issuance rather than bank lending now carries the aggregate. The package steers credit to state arrears and AI rather than igniting private demand: cheap loans help state firms pay overdue bills (Bloomberg, [Sep 29]) and banks are urged to finance AI and asset-light service firms (Caixin Global, [Sep 29]), while the bad consumer debt cleanup falters (The Business Times, [Sep 29]).

Property: The first central mortgage subsidy, 1 percentage point a year and up to nearly 50,000 yuan on a 1 million yuan loan (BigGo Finance, [Sep 29]), meets early turnover gains, with Beijing's September new-home sales up nearly 50% month-on-month (BigGo Finance, [Oct 1]). The fiscal cost of the bust is the binding constraint: East Asia Forum details the fiscal fallout (East Asia Forum, [Oct 4]) and a report finds deep cracks in local government finances (Social News XYZ, [Oct 4]), while Beijing shortens the leash on developers (Nikkei Asia, [Oct 2]) and August home prices fell across 70 cities with property investment down 19.9% in eight months (BigGo Finance, [Sep 14]).

Fiscal, Policy & Trade: The cabinet pledged stronger support to meet the growth target (Reuters, [Sep 28]) with fourth-quarter local bond issuance likely around 500 billion yuan (BigGo Finance, [Sep 28]); SCMP sees 'just enough' targeted support to defend GDP growth (South China Morning Post, [Oct 2]). Trade is now a two-front problem. With the US, differences persist despite the summit (India Today, [Oct 3]), though most G20 trade ministers rejected a US call for capacity curbs, China says (South China Morning Post, [Oct 4]). With Europe, Brussels pushes import quotas (Euronews.com, [Sep 28]) and fears Britain becoming a back door for Chinese cars (Financial Times, [Sep 28]), and Beijing has answered with an anti-dumping probe into an EU chemical compound (AP News, [Oct 3]).

Markets & Flows: The Hang Seng fell 2.2% for the week on a financial and tech rout (BusinessToday Malaysia, [Oct 4]) after reopening from National Day into a 3% plunge as the stimulus failed to impress (Moneycontrol.com, [Oct 2]). Mainland mutual fund shutdowns hit an eight-year high (Briefs Finance, [Oct 4]), yet NerdWallet's best-performing China stocks list for October (NerdWallet, [Oct 1]) suggests retail interest is perking up, which marks either a genuine turn or a classic retail trap.

New / Most Important: China opened a new EU anti-dumping probe days before Šefčovič's trade talks in Beijing (EU Today, [Oct 4]). The EU trade chief heads into those talks with a $800bn surplus on the table and a car dispute that targets China's highest-value export sector; Beijing is negotiating from leverage, not concession. The EU-China axis is now the primary trade friction, potentially more consequential near-term than the US truce, because it hits the export engine that is the one clear source of growth in a 50.1 PMI economy.

Playbook ✓: The export surplus stays structurally large, property is in managed decline and the policy response is gradual and targeted rather than transformative: a China stabilizing without re-accelerating.  |  ⚠️ Divergence: China now faces a two-front trade war, US tariffs and export controls plus EU escalation, and the EU front is underpriced. If both restrict Chinese exports, the surplus has to find EM destinations or compress, feeding domestic overcapacity and deflation risk in an economy where bank loans already miss and property investment is down 19.9%. The transmission to global assets runs through that growth pulse: an export-led PMI sputtering would hit Asian supply chains and EM exporters first, while weak construction keeps iron ore and the AUD without a Chinese demand anchor just as the RBA runs among the world's highest rates. A further fall in CGB yields would widen the gap with a 5.34% US 10Y and test the yuan's strength, pressuring Asian FX. European exporters with China exposure (luxury, industrials, autos) face the double hit of retaliation risk and a consumer at 0.4% retail growth.
