🇨🇳 China  Mixed  → easing bias

Macro Stance: The PBOC is easing through the plumbing, and loan prime rates are on this week's calendar (bloomingbit, [Aug 16]). Its first mid-month overnight reverse repo boosted bonds (The Edge Singapore, [Aug 14]) and liquidity management now focuses on the overnight rate (The Standard (HK), [Aug 13]); the 10-year yield hit an over 1-year low (TradingView, [Aug 13]). China Daily frames the stance as tailored easing favoured over broad stimulus (China Daily, [Aug 14]), after the Q2 report's pledge of a timely new policy rollout (Reuters, [Aug 12]). The yuan headed for a 7th weekly gain on strong fixing (The Standard (HK), [Aug 14]), and The Economist argues China's exorbitant surplus calls for a much stronger yuan (The Economist, [Aug 16]). July CPI rose 0.5% (CGTN, [Aug 9]), a six-month low (TradingView, [Aug 8]), with services supporting consumer prices (Trend News Agency, [Aug 16]) and PPI up 3.5% (China Daily, [Aug 9]).

PMIs & Activity: July's official PMIs both sit below 50, manufacturing at 49.2 and non-manufacturing at 49.0 (FXStreet, [Jul 31]). The private manufacturing PMI eased to 50.9 (Fibre2Fashion, [Aug 3]), a 4-month low (Reuters, [Aug 2]), and services growth hit its slowest since September 2024 (Reuters, [Aug 4]). Exports rose 23.9% as high-tech shipments strengthened (The Asian Banker, [Aug 14]), but the high-tech boom is seen failing to halt the slowdown (Bloomberg, [Aug 13]). Chinese car sales are booming almost everywhere except at home (The Straits Times, [Aug 14]), Goldman sees retail sales growth staying weak (Investing.com, [Aug 14]), and high-spending counties are only a rare bright spot amid weak demand (South China Morning Post, [Aug 16]). Floods and landslides add a weather drag, with Xi calling for better disaster prevention (The Straits Times, [Aug 15]).

Credit Impulse: July confirmed the demand problem and split the headline. New bank loans posted a record contraction as credit demand faltered (Reuters, [Aug 14]) and shrank as borrowing demand stays weak (Caixin Global, [Aug 15]), yet credit growth exceeded forecasts despite the rare loan slump (Bloomberg, [Aug 14]). Reconciled: aggregate financing was carried by channels outside bank lending while the loan book itself shrank; cumulative yuan loans still rose $1.53 trillion in seven months (Qatar news agency, [Aug 16]). Price is falling faster than volume: corporate loan rates fell below 3% as the PBOC signals a shift from volume to price (finance.biggo.com, [Aug 14]). Lenders are hunting new collateral, tapping AI token use to lend to asset-light startups (Global Times, [Aug 16]), while China cuts 670 rural banks as its financial sector overhaul accelerates (Caixin Global, [Aug 15]). Cheaper credit with fewer borrowers is the liquidity-trap signature.

Property: Easing continues at the city level: top cities are relaxing rules for home purchases (chinadailyhk, [Aug 12]) after Beijing eased home-buying curbs (Bloomberg, [Aug 10]), though MacroBusiness calls the new housing stimulus a bad joke (MacroBusiness, [Aug 16]). A rebound in luxury home sales is divorced from the rest of the market (South China Morning Post, [Aug 16]), and real home prices sank to their lowest level in 21 years (the deep dive, [Aug 3]). The official June data showed property investment down 18 percent y/y in January–June (The Standard (HK), [Jul 15]), and expiring land leases are a $148 billion threat (Bloomberg, [Aug 11]) that Beijing is moving to defuse (Sri Lanka Guardian, [Aug 13]). The official July price survey is still to come.

Fiscal, Policy & Trade: Fiscal stress shows up in collection: Breakingviews says China's emboldened taxman is just getting started (Reuters, [Aug 14]) and social security checks are tightening as fiscal pressures mount (ANI News, [Aug 14]), though Beijing is said to be moving to clarify tax rules that confused the ultra-wealthy (CNBC, [Aug 15]). On trade, the US accused dozens of countries of helping China dodge tariffs (BBC, [Aug 14]) and imposed 100% tariffs on some drones (The Japan Times, [Aug 14]), tariffs that hit China hardest (Table.Briefings, [Aug 16]). A US-China "Board of Investment" has stalled before the expected Trump-Xi meeting (South China Morning Post, [Aug 13]).

Markets & Flows: The Hang Seng suffered a four-day rout led by tech (BusinessToday Malaysia, [Aug 16]) before snapping the losing streak on a tech-led rally (Moomoo, [Aug 16]), and Goldman picks China stocks poised to benefit from a new wave of AI-related hardware exports (CNBC, [Aug 16]). Chinese stocks ended last week flat with attention on the Unitree debut (citynewsservice.cn, [Aug 15]). The safe-asset bid sits in bonds and gold, with the World Gold Council reporting strong official sector buying in July (World Gold Council, [Aug 14]).

New / Most Important: China revised the timing of the July economic data release and briefing (BusinessToday Malaysia, [Aug 16]), and the July activity data now land in the session ahead (investingLive, [Aug 16]), with the loan prime rates due later in the week. After a record loan contraction and sub-50 PMIs, the bar for a positive surprise is low, but a soft print is what would turn tailored easing into a rate cut.

Playbook ✓: Easing bias, falling bond yields, export strength and weak consumption are on script.  |  ⚠️ Divergence: The stance stays mixed because the external engine and the domestic one are running in opposite directions, and global assets feel the domestic one first. Fortescue's Super Special Fines iron ore stocks at China ports are at a five-week high (Reuters, [Aug 14]), so iron ore and AUD carry the downside of contracting loans and construction; EM Asia competes with exports up 23.9% that China cannot absorb at home and a yuan The Economist wants much stronger; and WTI at $82.81 remains a terms-of-trade tax on margins with PPI easing.
