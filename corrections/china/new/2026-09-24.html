🇨🇳 China  Mixed  → accommodative but constrained

Macro Stance: Accommodative through liquidity, constrained on rates and the currency. The PBOC will offer banks up to 1 trillion yuan a day over the holidays (Bloomberg.com, [Sep 23]) and inject more liquidity as Golden Week nears (Reuters, [Sep 23]), alongside an 800 billion yuan MLF (BigGo Finance, [Sep 23]), while loan prime rates stayed unchanged in September (www.tmgm.com, [Sep 21]). On the currency it has turned cautious: the yuan slipped as the PBOC signalled caution on appreciation (The Economic Times, [Sep 23]), after a midpoint of 6.7468 against an expected 6.6971 (Newsquawk, [Sep 22]). August CPI rose 0.8% and PPI 3.8% (BigGo Finance, [Sep 8]) on energy costs, and the ADB cut its inflation view as demand softens (investingLive, [Sep 23]).

PMIs & Activity: The official August manufacturing PMI rose to 49.8 with new orders back in expansion (BigGo Finance, [Aug 30]) and the private gauge reached 51.5 (BigGo Finance, [Aug 31]), but the hard data split the economy: industrial output rebounded to 5.2% (BigGo Finance, [Sep 14]) while retail sales grew 0.4% y/y against 0.6% expected (investingLive, [Sep 14]), urban unemployment hit 5.3% (TradingView, [Sep 14]) and fixed-asset investment fell 7.2% in eight months (State Council Information Office, [Sep 15]). Exports jumped 25% (AP News, [Sep 7]) and Xi arrives with the trade engine roaring (Reuters, [Sep 21]), but NEV retail sales fell 9% in the first 20 days of September (CnEVPost, [Sep 23]). The ADB holds its growth forecast at 4.6% (investingLive, [Sep 23]) and AMRO warns of weak domestic demand (BusinessToday Malaysia, [Sep 22]).

Credit Impulse: Bank loans rose less than expected after July's slump (Reuters, [Sep 14]) and credit expansion missed forecasts as loan demand sagged (Bloomberg.com, [Sep 14]); bond and equity financing exceeded loans in social financing for the first time (BigGo Finance, [Sep 14]), so securities issuance carries the aggregate while the loan book stalls. The LPR hold came as the PBOC signals slower credit (CHOSUNBIZ, [Sep 20]), regulators told e-commerce apps to stop selling loans (Sixth Tone, [Sep 21]) and regional banks are split on loan quality as property exits accelerate (Asian Banking & Finance, [Sep 22]). The holiday liquidity is plumbing, not a credit impulse.

Property: August home prices extended their decline across 70 cities while tier-1 cities stabilised (BigGo Finance, [Sep 14]), and development investment plunged 19.9% in eight months, the steepest on record (BigGo Finance, [Sep 14]). Tier-1 is the bright spot: first-tier new home sales jumped over 10% as resale homes dominate (BigGo Finance, [Sep 23]). Beyond it the support is forbearance and rumour: China asked banks to keep Vanke loans off bad-debt books (Reuters, [Sep 22]), and property stocks surged on renewed mortgage subsidy rumours that analysts call unlikely to be implemented (BigGo Finance, [Sep 23]). The real-economy read is weaker: aluminium extrusion output slumps as property weakness deepens (AL Circle, [Sep 23]).

Fiscal, Policy & Trade: The US and China agreed to extend the trade truce by two months and work on a bigger deal (Reuters, [Sep 23]) as Trump welcomed Xi with a planeside ceremony (AP News, [Sep 23]), a short window that keeps the pressure on. Open items remain: China lags on its $17B farm pledge (The Center Square, [Sep 23]) and rare earths friction has threatened the extension (South China Morning Post, [Sep 22]). Europe is hardening, calling trade rebalancing existential (Euronews.com, [Sep 23]). At home fiscal revenue growth slowed as spending contracted (Caixin Global, [Sep 19]), an IMF director argues a stronger safety net could address consumption woes (South China Morning Post, [Sep 23]) and a Central Committee plenum is set for late October (U.S. News & World Report, [Sep 21]).

Markets & Flows: Investors tempered summit hopes: China stocks edged lower (Business Standard, [Sep 23]), Chinese AI firms fell on a report of a DeepSeek and Moonshot probe (Bloomberg.com, [Sep 23]) and the Hang Seng closed below 25,000 with Alibaba down 4% (The Standard, [Sep 23]). China has spent a record amount importing gold this year (Financial Times, [Sep 22]).

New / Most Important: The truce extension landed before the meeting, so the summit opens with the floor in place and Xi trending on X alongside Trump. In a world of global tightening any US-China thaw is a marginal positive for risk assets and EM capital flows, but a two-month window plus a PBOC that is now leaning against yuan gains says Beijing is buying time, not committing to a revaluation.

Playbook ✓: China remains a monetary outlier, accommodative while DM tightens, using liquidity and the fixing rather than rates.  |  ⚠️ Divergence: The global pulse and China's have split. The US flash PMI hit a five-year high at 58.4 (Markets.com, [Sep 23]) and the euro zone's rose to 53.1, the fastest growth in over three years (IndexBox, [Sep 23]), demand that feeds China's export engine and intensifies competition for European exporters. China's domestic pulse does not transmit: Australia's flash manufacturing PMI contracted for the first time since March (TradingView, [Sep 23]), keeping the AUD under pressure (FXStreet, [Sep 23]) as property caps iron ore demand. China's fuel inventories at over decade lows raise concerns about export curbs (Reuters, [Sep 23]), a risk for product markets with WTI at 94.31.
