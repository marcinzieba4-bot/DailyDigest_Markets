🇨🇳 China  Bearish  → easing but ineffective

Macro Stance: China remains in its familiar pattern of incremental easing with limited transmission. The PBOC resumed overnight reverse repos ahead of month-end (Gulf Times, [Aug 29]) after a 500 billion yuan MLF operation (BigGo Finance, [Aug 24]); liquidity is plentiful, borrowing is not. The yuan reached a three-year high against the dollar (UA.NEWS, [Aug 27]) and stays calm in the face of US sanction threats over Iran (Livemint, [Aug 28]), with the PBOC cautious on further gains despite dollar weakness (SCMP, [Aug 27]). July CPI slowed to 0.5%, more than expected (BigGo Finance, [Aug 9]), and PPI growth to 3.5%, missing expectations (BigGo Finance, [Aug 8]), so a strong currency is importing disinflation into an economy that needs the opposite.

PMIs & Activity: July's official manufacturing PMI fell to 49.2 and non-manufacturing to 49.0 (FXStreet, [Jul 31]), and a Reuters poll sees factory activity contracting again in August (Reuters, [Aug 27]); the China PMI and US payrolls set the tone for the first week of September (BigGo Finance, [Aug 29]). July industrial output grew 4.5% y/y and retail sales only 0.6% (Reuters, [Aug 17]), while industrial profits rose 17.6% in January-July (Fibre2Fashion, [Aug 29]) at a seven-month low in growth (Anadolu Ajansı, [Aug 27]). Consumption is weakest in cars, with auto sales down 22% (GlobalChinaEV, [Aug 27]) and Toyota's sales falling for six straight months on weak China demand (The Japan Times, [Aug 28]). Exports remain the engine, up almost a quarter in July (Financial Times, [Aug 6]), and Europe's climate extremes are boosting China's sales of air conditioners and taps (SCMP, [Aug 29]).

Credit Impulse: July bank loans posted a record contraction as credit demand faltered (Reuters, [Aug 14]), yet credit growth exceeded forecasts despite the rare loan slump (Bloomberg, [Aug 14]); reconciled, the broad aggregate holds up while the loan book shrinks. Bank balance sheets are not the bottleneck: the largest five reported their strongest first-half profit since the height of the property crash (Reuters, [Aug 28]) and China Construction Bank reports accelerated growth (China Daily, [Aug 29]), though Everbright's net profit fell 24% as write-offs approached last year's full-year total (Moomoo, [Aug 28]). Beijing is now pushing credit at property directly, ramping up credit support in its overhaul of the market (The Business Times, [Aug 29]).

Property: The overhaul is moving from announcement to rules. China moved to curb housing presales to shore up confidence (Reuters, [Aug 28]), raised the threshold for new-home presales under a new national standard (Caixin Global, [Aug 29]), tightened rules on presale fund supervision to protect homebuyers (Global Times, [Aug 29]) and extended the mortgage term cap to 40 years (BigGo Finance, [Aug 28]); SCMP called it a stronger-than-expected package (SCMP, [Aug 28]). The base remains weak: July new home prices were stagnant (Reuters, [Aug 17]), a Reuters poll sees the investment slump deepening (Reuters, [Aug 27]), and Vanke's first-half loss widened as sales slumped and debt pressure built (Caixin Global, [Aug 29]).

Fiscal, Policy & Trade: Fiscal policy is extracting, not injecting. China is stepping up pressure on companies and the wealthy as local governments scramble for revenue (economy.ac, [Aug 29]), a local surtax of 11%–13% has entered legislation (BigGo Finance, [Aug 28]) and The Economist argues Beijing is tightening when it should be loosening (The Economist, [Aug 27]); the vice-premier's call for proactive fiscal policy (China Daily, [Aug 27]) has yet to translate into spending. Trade is the relief valve: the US-China truce is likely to be extended by one year, with Xi to visit Washington with a business delegation (BigGo Finance, [Aug 28]), though China opposes the 7.5% tariff plan and warns of countermeasures (Yeni Şafak English, [Aug 27]) and German industry presses Merz for a tougher line (Reuters, [Aug 28]).

Markets & Flows: The Hang Seng edged up 0.07% as Tencent and materials lifted the market (BusinessToday Malaysia, [Aug 29]) after Chinese stocks ended lower on US-China tensions (Business Standard, [Aug 28]); KE Holdings had jumped as much as 17% on the financing reform (TradingKey, [Aug 28]). China's gold stash is surging (News.com.au, [Aug 29]). Regional capital is going elsewhere: Korean individual investors purchased a net $7 billion in US stocks over the past two months, funding US mega-cap rather than regional equities including China.

New / Most Important: The property overhaul now has rules behind it: a higher presale threshold, tighter fund supervision and more credit support for the sector. That reduces delivery risk for buyers, but it does not create buyers, and Vanke's widening loss on slumping sales shows where demand stands. With fiscal policy extracting revenue and the August PMI expected to contract, this is easing that improves the plumbing without restarting the engine.

Playbook ✓: Capital continues to flee the region toward US assets, and Beijing answers with incremental, rules-based easing.  |  ⚠️ Divergence: Banks posting their best half since the property crash while the loan book contracts is the tell: credit supply is not the problem, demand is. That keeps the transmission abroad negative: property investment still deepening its slump caps iron ore despite stimulus headlines, rising on fresh signals (TradingView, [Aug 24]) after weakening despite them (TradingView, [Aug 23]), and leaves the AUD exposed; Toyota's six-month slide shows the drag on Asian and European carmakers, and WTI at 83.40 remains a terms-of-trade cost for Chinese margins.
