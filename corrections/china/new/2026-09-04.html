🇨🇳 China  Mixed  → easing bias

Macro Stance: China remains in an accommodative monetary posture, running counter to the global tightening cycle, but the currency now leads the story: the yuan broke 6.72 to a three-and-a-half-year high as Governor Pan reiterated no intention to win through depreciation (BigGo Finance, [Sep 3]), helped by yen strength weighing on the dollar (The Star, [Sep 3]), and Beijing rejected the G20 imbalance claims, saying there is no need to devalue the yuan (South China Morning Post, [Sep 3]). This reconciles with the earlier signal that China is reining in the rising yuan (Reuters, [Aug 31]): the PBOC leans against the pace, not the direction, and Goldman explains why China may prefer a stronger yuan (StreetInsider, [Sep 3]). Easing runs through the balance sheet, with net purchases of 50 billion yuan of sovereign bonds in August (TradingView, [Sep 2]). July CPI slowed to 0.5%, more than expected (BigGo Finance, [Aug 9]), and PPI growth to 3.5%, missing expectations (BigGo Finance, [Aug 8]).

PMIs & Activity: The August surveys stabilised. The official manufacturing PMI rebounded to 49.8 with new orders back in expansion (BigGo Finance, [Aug 30]) after July's 49.2 (FXStreet, [Jul 31]); the private manufacturing PMI rose to 51.5, its longest expansion streak in five years (BigGo Finance, [Aug 31]); and private services activity quickened on stronger domestic demand (KLSE Screener, [Sep 3]). ING nonetheless sees the recovery stalling as the K-shaped divergence widens (ing think, [Sep 3]). Reconciled: the surveys capture the upper arm — exports, AI hardware, private services — while the lower arm of household goods demand keeps weakening, with August NEV retail sales down 4% to 1.07 million (CnEVPost, [Sep 3]) and industrial profit growth at a seven-month low in July (Anadolu Ajansı, [Aug 27]).

Credit Impulse: July bank loans posted a record contraction (Reuters, [Aug 14]) even as credit growth exceeded forecasts (Bloomberg, [Aug 14]); reconciled, government bonds carry the aggregate while private borrowing shrinks, and that leg strengthens as China speeds up bond issuance after a national meeting on investment (South China Morning Post, [Aug 31]). Quality is now questioned too: S&P Global Ratings warns of risks from China's generous credit ratings (Reuters, [Sep 3]), and a China indicator that supports risk-taking in stocks and bitcoin is flashing red (CoinDesk, [Sep 2]). Banks are not the constraint: the largest five reported their strongest first-half profit since the height of the property crash (Reuters, [Aug 28]).

Property: China tries to look past the property slump (The Economist, [Sep 3]) with rules that shift risk away from buyers: the new home finance regime means what you see is what you get (South China Morning Post, [Sep 3]), and 40-year mortgages have landed in Shanghai with age 35 as the cutoff for new loans (BigGo Finance, [Sep 3]). The data remain weak — new home prices were stagnant in July (Reuters, [Aug 17]) while property investment slumped nearly 20% (BigGo Finance, [Aug 17]) — and developers carry the cost, with property stocks falling as the rules upend the presale funding playbook (Reuters, [Aug 31]); Longfor says it has sufficient funds to repay loans due this month (The Standard (HK), [Sep 3]).

Fiscal, Policy & Trade: China hit back at the G20 statement on its reliance on exports, accusing members of promoting protectionism (CNBC, [Sep 3]), and lashed back at the US over the row ahead of the Xi-Trump summit (Financial Times, [Sep 3]). The bilateral track is still transactional: the US and China are set to announce agriculture deals during Xi's Washington visit, Greer says (Yahoo Finance, [Sep 3]), even as US drone tariffs of up to 100pc take effect (Malay Mail, [Sep 3]). Europe is hardening: Germany's SPD backs EU tariffs on Chinese hybrid cars (Bloomberg.com, [Sep 3]) and Šefčovič says China must deliver by October or face harsher measures (Euronews.com, [Sep 2]). The Canada-US trade war and Trump's ultimatum add a third front that China is positioned to exploit. At home, a second phase of the national SME fund for tech start-ups is coming (South China Morning Post, [Sep 3]).

Markets & Flows: The Hang Seng closed flat while Shanghai gained 0.36% (24/7 Wall St., [Sep 3]), with mainland investors buying Hong Kong AI stocks and selling financials (South China Morning Post, [Sep 3]). NerdWallet's "7 Best-Performing China Stocks" piece reflects rotational interest. Bonds tell a weaker story: China escapes the global bond rout, but for the wrong reasons (Reuters, [Sep 2]), with the US-China 10-year gap at 312 basis points (bloomingbit, [Sep 2]). Central banks, led by China, kept buying gold in July (BusinessLine, [Sep 3]).

New / Most Important: A yuan at a three-and-a-half-year high, with Beijing explicitly ruling out devaluation, is China's answer to the G20 surplus charge before the Xi-Trump summit. Trump's trade war ultimatum and the Canada-US fracture could paradoxically redirect capital toward Chinese assets as a hedge against US policy chaos, and a firmer currency makes that trade easier to own.

Playbook ✓: Monetary divergence (China easy, DM tight) persists.  |  ⚠️ Divergence: Oil at $91 is a significant terms-of-trade headwind for China as a net importer — the Hormuz crisis works directly against its reflation effort by raising input costs, which limits stimulus transmission. Externally, a stronger yuan and stabilising PMIs support the AUD, which flat-lined above 0.7150 after the China data (FXStreet, [Sep 3]), and iron ore at the margin, but China's slowdown is pushing New Zealand exporters to diversify away from their top buyer (CNBC, [Sep 3]), and German firms feel growing pressure from Chinese rivals (Reuters, [Sep 3]) — the channel through which European exporters pay for China's export-led stabilisation.
