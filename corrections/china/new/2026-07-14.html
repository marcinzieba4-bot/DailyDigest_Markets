🇨🇳 China  Bearish  → easing

Macro Stance: The PBOC pledged to keep policy accommodative amid weak demand and external shocks (Reuters, [Jul 8]) and flagged "structural divergences" as the AI boom masks consumer weakness (BigGo Finance, [Jul 9]). It let the fixing go below 6.8 per dollar for the first time since 2023 (Bloomberg, [Jul 10]), although UOB now sees the yuan consolidating after a sharper drop against the dollar (Mitrade, [Jul 13]). June CPI rose 1% (Global Times, [Jul 9]) and slowed more than forecast (Bloomberg, [Jul 8]), while producer inflation hit a four-year high (Reuters, [Jul 8]): a cost squeeze, not demand-pull reflation. The premier urged an "objective" understanding of the economy (Reuters, [Jul 13]), which reads as preparation for a soft growth print.

PMIs & Activity: The external engine is running hot. June dollar exports jumped 27% y/y and imports rose 36% (Reuters, [Jul 13]), taking exports to a record $412 billion (Bloomberg, [Jul 13]), the fastest growth since 2021 on the AI boom and a tariff rush (CNBC, [Jul 13]); monthly car exports passed 1mn for the first time (Financial Times, [Jul 13]). The domestic side is not: crude oil imports plunged to the lowest in nearly a decade (Bloomberg, [Jul 13]). That matches the June PMIs, with official manufacturing at 50.3 and non-manufacturing at 50.2 (Reuters, [Jun 29]), and May's first retail-sales decline since 2022 (Il Sole 24 Ore, [Jun 15]). Second-quarter GDP is expected to slow to 4.5% (Firstpost, [Jul 13]), at the bottom of the official target range (Bloomberg, [Jul 13]).

Credit Impulse: May total social financing grew 7.7% (CGTN, [Jun 12]) and topped forecasts (Bloomberg, [Jun 12]), but new loans missed as the property slump curbed demand (Reuters, [Jun 12]) and households kept deleveraging (SCMP, [Jun 15]): the impulse is government bonds, not private borrowing. Regulators are leaning against the riskier end of the bond market, with a crackdown on inflated AAA ratings (Caixin, [Jul 7]) that leaves up to 494.6 billion yuan of bonds facing downgrade risk (BigGo Finance, [Jul 8]) and banks asked to avoid underwriting short-term LGFV bonds (Bloomberg, [Jul 8]). June credit data are next; nothing in the feed points to a private-loan revival.

Property: New home prices fell at a faster pace in May (Reuters, [Jun 15]) even as first-tier prices rose for a third month (SCMP, [Jun 16]), and real-estate investment fell 16.2% y/y in January–May (GMK Center, [Jun 18]). June resale prices fell faster in a private survey (Reuters, [Jun 30]). Developer stress is rising again: Nikkei Asia reports a fresh liquidity crunch for developers ([Jul 13]), Vanke says its first-half loss may widen to $2.2 billion (Bloomberg, [Jul 10]), and Fitch sees new home sales falling 11% to 13% this year (Mingtiandi, [Jul 9]).

Fiscal, Policy & Trade: The State Council approved China's first dedicated five-year plan to boost consumption (China Daily, [Jul 13]), set against an IMF warning that "excessive" saving is slowing growth (News24, [Jul 13]); with key national projects already listed (China Daily, [Jul 7]), support remains targeted rather than a cash transfer. The Economist notes a squeezed Beijing trying to wring more from state assets ([Jul 13]), and Sinocism flags preparation for the July Politburo meeting on the economy ([Jul 13]). Trade friction is shifting to Europe: EU duties of up to 45 per cent on Chinese tyres (Eunews, [Jul 9]), an anti-dumping probe into ducks (SCMP, [Jul 10]) and Merz urging a currency dialogue as trade worries rise (Bloomberg, [Jul 13]). Australia curbed China-linked investors in a rare-earths miner (Bloomberg, [Jul 13]) even as China opened canola imports (Reuters, [Jul 13]).

Markets & Flows: The Shanghai benchmark slumped to a three-month low on US-Iran tensions (BBN Times, [Jul 13]) as 4,600 A-shares tumbled and the AI hardware chain took the heaviest hit (BigGo Finance, [Jul 13]). Hong Kong held up, edging higher to 24,213 (BBN Times, [Jul 13]) after its strongest week in over a year (The Business Times, [Jul 10]), helped by overseas capital pouring into hard-tech giants (SCMP, [Jul 13]). Breakingviews judges the Bond Connect quota boost to lack punch and precision (Reuters, [Jul 13]).

New / Most Important: June trade is the day's print and it confirms the two-speed economy rather than easing it. A record $412 billion of exports, growing at the fastest pace since 2021, sits next to crude imports at a near-decade low, so the 36% import jump is not a broad domestic-demand signal. Growth is being exported: car shipments above 1mn a month and AI hardware are carrying a quarter in which GDP is expected to slip to the bottom of the target range. A consumption plan, not a demand package, is Beijing's answer for now.

Playbook ✓: Export strength, weak oil demand, a property drag and targeted rather than broad support are all on the deflationary script.  |  ⚠️ Divergence: The consensus reads a record export month as a growth upgrade; the growth pulse reads it as a widening imbalance. With private credit contracting, developers in a fresh liquidity crunch and the PMI barely above 50, the surplus is the release valve for domestic weakness. The transmission is to China's trading partners rather than its suppliers. European exporters absorb the car and AI-hardware surge, which is why Berlin is now asking for a currency dialogue. Oil loses a buyer as Chinese crude imports fall to a near-decade low, and construction-linked demand for iron ore and AUD stays soft while property investment falls by double digits. EM Asia shares the AI-hardware upside, but the political risk of the surplus is rising faster than the volumes.
