🇨🇳 China  Mixed  → easing bias assumed

Macro Stance: The easing bias is moving into the plumbing rather than the headline rate. China is adopting a market-driven solution to its stale loan rate benchmark (Caixin Global, [Sep 11]) after the PBOC released a five-year plan for building a financial powerhouse with nine supporting action plans (BigGo Finance, [Sep 10]). The yuan rose in volatile trading after US inflation data (Investing.com, [Sep 11]), though UOB says its bullish bias is fading (Mitrade, [Sep 11]), and the fixing was set firmer while keeping the yuan below market estimates to aid exports (VT Markets, [Sep 10]). August CPI rose 0.8% and PPI 3.8% (Xinhua, [Sep 9]); CPI excluding the food drag is estimated at 1% (Trend News Agency, [Sep 11]), and consumer prices are still below target (Free Malaysia Today, [Sep 9]).

PMIs & Activity: Every August survey improved. The official manufacturing PMI rebounded to 49.8 with new orders back in expansion (BigGo Finance, [Aug 30]), still a second month of contraction (CNBC, [Aug 30]); the private manufacturing PMI rose to 51.5 (BigGo Finance, [Aug 31]) and private services to 51.4 (BigGo Finance, [Sep 2]). The economy runs at two speeds, with record exports and a consumer who will not spend (International Finance, [Sep 10]): exports jumped 25% (AP News, [Sep 7]) and car exports in eight months surpassed the 2025 total (AP News, [Sep 10]), but imports missed estimates (CNBC, [Sep 7]) and passenger car sales slumped 24% (Caixin Global, [Sep 9]). Regulators are turning cost accounting on price wars (South China Morning Post, [Sep 11]), and HSBC says better lives for migrant workers are key to boosting consumption (South China Morning Post, [Sep 11]).

Credit Impulse: Banks are swapping loans for bonds as the loan slump deepens (Bloomberg.com, [Sep 8]), after July new loans posted a record contraction (Reuters, [Aug 14]) even as credit growth exceeded forecasts (Bloomberg.com, [Aug 14]); reconciled, the aggregate holds up while the loan book shrinks. The 300 billion yuan of special treasury bonds for bank capital (State Council Information Office, [Sep 7]) falls far short in The Economist's view (The Economist, [Sep 8]). Regulation is tightening at the edges: online lenders see profits plunge as new rules bite (Caixin Global, [Sep 10]) and the financial regulator plans to consolidate small and mid-sized institutions (BigGo Finance, [Sep 10]).

Property: Developer exits keep narrowing. Regulators are scrutinising offshore debt-swap bonds used by distressed developers (Caixin Global, [Sep 11]), after the securities regulator rejected developers' mandatory convertible bond registrations (BigGo Finance, [Sep 9]); a Guangzhou court hit R&F Properties and its chairman with high-consumption restrictions (BigGo Finance, [Sep 11]). The shift to completed-home sales is cooling the land market (Caixin Global, [Sep 10]) and Goldman sees land sales cut by 30% (The Business Times, [Sep 7]); SCMP argues Beijing can rewrite property rules but cannot undo the damage (South China Morning Post, [Sep 11]). July new home prices were stagnant (Reuters, [Aug 17]) and first-tier prices flat, ending a four-month rebound (South China Morning Post, [Aug 17]).

Fiscal, Policy & Trade: Washington is the softer front. China and the US are consulting on reciprocal tariff reductions covering $30 billion (Global Times, [Sep 10]), and Beijing says US tariff cuts could come at an early date as the summit nears (Benzinga, [Sep 11]); China bought 1 million tons of US soybeans ahead of Xi's visit (Reuters, [Sep 9]). Europe is split but hardening: the EU Parliament is ready to take a tougher line, says its lead MEP (POLITICO.eu, [Sep 11]), while the largest political group does not want confrontation (Euronews.com, [Sep 11]); DW details how China is upping pressure on German businesses (DW.com, [Sep 11]).

Markets & Flows: China stocks fell as oil prices and US yields rose (Business Standard, [Sep 11]) and the Hang Seng Tech rout nears a make-or-break zone (FOREX.com, [Sep 11]), yet the AI trade burns hot: Enflame soared 179% in its Shanghai debut (South China Morning Post, [Sep 11]) and Morningstar notes Chinese AI stocks have been on a tear while US investors have been left out (Morningstar, [Sep 11]). The yuan's steady rise is drawing foreign investors back to Chinese assets (Caixin Global, [Sep 11]) even as the US-China bond yield gap widens to a record (Hürriyet Daily News, [Sep 11]), and ISI says external-surplus recycling is moving beyond the central bank (ISI Markets, [Sep 11]).

New / Most Important: The loan-rate benchmark is being repaired. A market-driven fix lets lending rates follow funding costs without a headline cut, which matters while the Fed may hike and China's CPI and PPI are rising. It is easing through transmission rather than through the policy rate, and the test is whether it reaches borrowers when banks are buying bonds instead of lending.

Playbook ✓: Easing bias assumed: the PBOC works through benchmark reform, bank capital and the fixing rather than rate cuts, while exports carry growth.  |  ⚠️ Divergence: Chinese AI equities rallying while car sales fall 24% and the land market cools shows an economy bifurcating: tech and digital strong, old economy and property still struggling. This makes broad EM commodity longs dangerous. A cooling land market caps iron ore demand expectations and limits the support improving PMIs give the AUD, soft imports leave EM exporters little benefit, and a 25% export surge keeps European exporters under pressure. WTI at $100.05 is a terms-of-trade tax on China's weak domestic demand.
