🇨🇳 China  Mixed  → easing bias

Macro Stance: The PBOC is easing through volume, not price. Loan prime rates were left unchanged for a 14th consecutive month (The Business Times, [Jul 20]) despite the Q2 growth miss, with the market eyeing a cut window near the end of the third quarter (finance.biggo.com, [Jul 21]). Instead the central bank added the most liquidity in five months (Bloomberg, [Jul 23]) and will run overnight reverse repos alongside a 500b-yuan MLF operation, injecting a total of 2.1 trillion yuan (Reuters, [Jul 24]; China Daily, [Jul 24]). The yuan is not the release valve: UOB sees an upside bias against the dollar below 6.7820 (www.tmgm.com, [Jul 20]) as Europe scrutinises its value (Bloomberg, [Jul 21]). Prices are split: June CPI rose only 1% (Global Times, [Jul 9]) while PPI climbed 4.1% (finance.biggo.com, [Jul 8]), a near four-year high that squeezes manufacturers (Reuters, [Jul 8]).

PMIs & Activity: The last official read is June: manufacturing PMI 50.3 and non-manufacturing 50.2 (Reuters, [Jun 29]), with the private gauge capping its strongest quarter since late 2020 (Reuters, [Jun 30]); China data are on next week's calendar (ING Think, [Jul 23]). The hard data show two speeds: Q2 GDP grew 4.3%, below the official target range (Bloomberg, [Jul 15]); June industrial output rose 5.3% y/y and retail sales returned to growth (BusinessLine, [Jul 15]), but first-half retail sales were up just 1.3% (Ecns.cn, [Jul 15]) and the investment slide deepened as infrastructure joined property (Caixin Global, [Jul 15]), while exports jumped 27% y/y (Reuters, [Jul 13]). The newest consumer signals are weak: trade-in sales are dropping as the stimulus effect fades (Caixin Global, [Jul 24]) and car sales plunge 20% toward the worst year since 2021 (CNBC, [Jul 20]).

Credit Impulse: June new bank loans rose less than expected (The Star, [Jul 15]) and credit growth missed as the PBOC flagged a "new normal" (Caixin Global, [Jul 16]). Household loans fell by 366.8 billion yuan in the first half (36Kr, [Jul 17]). The finance minister's claim that fiscal-financial synergy leveraged over 17 trillion yuan in new loans (finance.biggo.com, [Jul 22]) is not a contradiction: the volume is policy-directed corporate and state-linked lending while households pay down. Banks breaking with the lending benchmark in pricing new credit (Bloomberg, [Jul 24]) is quiet price easing and a bond rally before the Politburo (Bloomberg, [Jul 22]) says the market expects more. Read: headline impulse flat, household impulse negative.

Property: June new home prices fell at a slower pace (Reuters, [Jul 14]) and first-tier prices extended a four-month rebound (South China Morning Post, [Jul 15]), but resale prices fell faster (Reuters, [Jun 30]) and property investment slumped 18 percent y/y in January–June (The Standard (HK), [Jul 15]). This week the capital side moved: KKR and AEW seek to sell China assets at steep losses (Bloomberg, [Jul 20]), and regulators curbed a loan structure used by distressed developers (Bloomberg, [Jul 22]) and offshore loans for local real estate firms (The Standard (HK), [Jul 23]). Vanke completed extensions on its 2026 public bonds (Caixin Global, [Jul 23]) with a RMB 519 million loan from Shenzhen Metro at 2.29% (BigGo Finance, [Jul 21]): state shareholders, not buyers, hold it together. Land-sales revenue plunged over 30% in the first half (finance.biggo.com, [Jul 22]).

Fiscal, Policy & Trade: Leaders are zeroing in on the need for stimulus (Financial Times, [Jul 18]) and vowed to hit the growth target (Bloomberg, [Jul 20]), yet Beijing clings to austerity despite the slowdown (Bloomberg, [Jul 22]); fiscal revenue expanded 4.7% in the first half (Reuters, [Jul 22]). The July Politburo meeting, still ahead, is the marker for stock-stimulus clues (South China Morning Post, [Jul 22]) and the growth commitment (Bloomberg, [Jul 23]). Washington will impose new tariffs of 10 to 12.5 percent on over 60 countries (China Daily Global Edition, [Jul 24]); Beijing is set to look past them before Xi's US trip (Bloomberg, [Jul 24]) and is consulting on tariff cuts on $30 billion in trade (Reuters, [Jul 23]). Toward Europe it is harder, adding 14 EU entities to its export control list (Reuters, [Jul 24]).

Markets & Flows: A Chinese AI model stoked competitive fears in global tech (Nikkei Asia, [Jul 18]); the "national team" bought shares worth $9bn (Financial Times, [Jul 20]) with record inflows into a tech ETF (Bloomberg, [Jul 21]). Hong Kong reclaimed 25,000 (BigGo Finance, [Jul 20]), Citi upgraded China to overweight (The Standard (HK), [Jul 20]) and EM assets rallied on China tech support (Bloomberg, [Jul 21]). The rescue has limits: chip stocks dived as overvaluation defies Beijing (Asia Times, [Jul 24]).

New / Most Important: The 2.1 trillion yuan injection, landing the same day banks were reported pricing loans off the benchmark and Washington rolled out new tariffs, defines Beijing's choice: liquidity and equity support now, a fiscal decision deferred to the Politburo. With austerity in the budget, shrinking household credit and fading trade-in sales, volume easing can hold up markets but cannot by itself lift domestic demand.

Playbook ✓: LPR on hold, liquidity easing, export strength, property drag and a calm tariff response before Xi's trip are on script.  |  ⚠️ Divergence: The rest of the world is tilting hawkish while China eases, and markets read the state rescue as a turn; credit and fiscal data do not. With property investment down 18 percent and infrastructure contracting, construction-linked bulk demand keeps fading, a drag on AUD and iron ore that 27% export growth does not offset. The oil spike is a terms-of-trade negative for China as a massive importer, squeezing margins already hit by a near four-year-high PPI. European exporters absorb the surplus as the yuan draws scrutiny, and EM inflows ride state buying rather than a PMI or credit upturn. The July PMIs and the Politburo meeting decide whether this becomes a demand story.
