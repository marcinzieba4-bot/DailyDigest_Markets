🇨🇳 China  Mixed  → easing / opening

Macro Stance: The PBOC held benchmark lending rates for a 16th straight month (TradingView, [Sep 20]) as the Fed hike narrows its room to ease (investingLive, [Sep 20]), so the accommodative stance now runs through liquidity, opening and the currency rather than rates. The PBOC pledged to expand financial market opening, an explicit bid for foreign capital while higher US rates pull portfolio flows away from EM. The fixing has turned from brake to accelerator: after nudging the midpoint higher for an eighth straight day (BigGo Finance, [Sep 17]) at a controlled pace, the PBOC has eased its curb and the yuan hit a fresh multi-year peak ahead of the summit (TradingView, [Sep 20]), with the Reuters estimate for the next fix at 6.6951 (investingLive, [Sep 20]). August CPI rose 0.8% and PPI 3.8% (BigGo Finance, [Sep 8]) on energy costs (Reuters, [Sep 9]), imported cost rather than demand.

PMIs & Activity: The official August manufacturing PMI rose to 49.8 with new orders back in expansion (BigGo Finance, [Aug 30]), the private gauge reached 51.5 (BigGo Finance, [Aug 31]) and private services rebounded to 51.4 (BigGo Finance, [Sep 2]). The hard data split the economy in two: industrial output rebounded to 5.2% (BigGo Finance, [Sep 14]), but retail sales grew 0.4% y/y against 0.6% expected (investingLive, [Sep 14]), urban unemployment hit 5.3% (TradingView, [Sep 14]) and fixed-asset investment fell 7.2% in eight months (State Council Information Office, [Sep 15]). Exports jumped 25% (AP News, [Sep 7]) while imports missed (CNBC, [Sep 7]). The New York Times sums it up: China's AI makes a great leap forward while its economy falls behind (The New York Times, [Sep 20]); Goldman cut its GDP forecast to 4.5% (The Standard, [Sep 15]).

Credit Impulse: Bank loans rose less than expected after July's slump (Reuters, [Sep 14]) and credit expansion missed forecasts as loan demand sagged (Bloomberg.com, [Sep 14]). The structure explains it: bond and equity financing exceeded loans in social financing for the first time (BigGo Finance, [Sep 14]), so securities issuance carries the aggregate while the loan book stalls. Pan calls slower loan growth the new normal (Reuters, [Sep 15]) and the LPR hold came as the PBOC signals slower credit (CHOSUNBIZ, [Sep 20]); new metrics to rein in banks' long-dated bond holdings (Reuters, [Sep 14]) and the $43.9b injection into major state banks (China Daily Global Edition, [Sep 14]) are balance-sheet management, not stimulus.

Property: August home prices extended their decline across 70 cities while tier-1 cities stabilised (BigGo Finance, [Sep 14]), and Shanghai recorded a 3% annual new-home price gain (Trend News Agency, [Sep 19]): a tier-1 floor inside a national decline. Development investment plunged 19.9% in eight months, the steepest on record (BigGo Finance, [Sep 14]), and land sale revenue slumped nearly 30% (BigGo Finance, [Sep 18]). The support effort is not gaining traction: a US$33 billion experiment to prop up the market is sputtering on weak demand (The Business Times, [Sep 20]), sovereign wealth funds stay away from China over lingering property woes (Nikkei Asia, [Sep 20]) and Breakingviews says the fix will hurt before it heals (Reuters, [Sep 15]).

Fiscal, Policy & Trade: Fiscal revenue growth slowed as government spending contracted (Caixin Global, [Sep 19]) and weak data have stoked calls for stimulus (Semafor, [Sep 15]). The pre-summit track delivered: Bessent says the US and China had very successful talks on trade and AI (Reuters, [Sep 20]), the two discussed a system to warn of AI national security issues (The New York Times, [Sep 20]) and Beijing named Li Chenggang its top international trade negotiator (Bloomberg.com, [Sep 20]). Tariff cuts, including on US LNG (Reuters, [Sep 18]), are on the table and Washington is holding off on new China tariffs until after the Xi meeting (Bloomberg.com, [Sep 18]). The leverage is visible too: rare-earth magnet exports to the US fell 21% (Briefs Finance, [Sep 20]), the Greenland deal has US rare earth stocks surging (BigGo Finance, [Sep 20]), Trump signed the bill targeting China's Russian energy imports (South China Morning Post, [Sep 19]) and the EU will use all tools to cut its China deficit (Reuters, [Sep 16]).

Markets & Flows: China stocks gained as the PBOC stood pat (TradingView, [Sep 20]) after logging their best day in a month (Business Recorder, [Sep 19]), and four Chinese firms seek up to $1.83 billion in Hong Kong offerings (Reuters, [Sep 20]). Bonds are bucking the global yield surge (Yahoo Finance, [Sep 17]), US Treasury holdings fell to the lowest since 2008 (Financial Times, [Sep 17]) and mainland investors are rushing into US stocks (Reuters, [Sep 15]).

New / Most Important: The Bessent-He talks came back as very successful, with an AI dialogue alongside the tariff agenda, and the PBOC answered by easing its curb on the yuan: the two sides are arriving at Xi's state visit this week (Livemint, [Sep 20]) with a currency concession and an AI channel already banked. The domestic pulse gets no such help: rates held, credit slowing, property support sputtering.

Playbook ✓: China holding and opening while the Fed tightens is the classic divergence play, and a firmer fixing into a negotiation is Beijing's standard pre-summit posture.  |  ⚠️ Divergence: A multi-year-high yuan while the Fed hikes and the domestic pulse weakens is diplomacy, not fundamentals. The transmission runs through the data: an export-led PMI and 25% export growth keep Chinese competition intense for European exporters as Brussels hardens; a soft credit impulse and a record 19.9% fall in property investment cap steel demand and iron ore; and with imports missing, the AUD and EM exporters get little from the rebound. WTI at 91.44 still taxes China's import bill, and the flash PMIs this week (Moneycontrol.com, [Sep 20]) will test whether global demand can carry the export engine.
