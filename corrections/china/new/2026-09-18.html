🇨🇳 China  Mixed  → policy direction unclear

Macro Stance: Policy signals point in different directions. The yuan hit its strongest level since 2022 after the PBOC fixing (Bloomberg.com, [Sep 17]), with the midpoint set higher for an eighth straight day and the offshore rate breaking 6.7 (BigGo Finance, [Sep 17]); the reference rate was 6.7580 against 6.7628 previously (www.tmgm.com, [Sep 17]). That is currency tightening just as the Fed hikes, while on rates a Reuters poll sees loan rates held for a 16th consecutive month in September (Reuters, [Sep 17]) and Commerzbank notes the PBOC accepts slower credit as the summit nears (www.tmgm.com, [Sep 17]). Prices give no reason to tighten: factory-gate prices outrun consumer prices by 3 points (Trend News Agency, [Sep 17]), with August CPI at 0.8% and PPI at 3.8% (BigGo Finance, [Sep 8]) on energy costs.

PMIs & Activity: The economy faces a growing divide between strong industrial output and weak domestic demand (kalkine.ca, [Sep 17]). August output grew 5.2% (State Council Information Office, [Sep 15]) while retail sales rose only 0.4% against 0.6% expected (investingLive, [Sep 14]) and fixed-asset investment fell 7.2% in the first eight months (Xinhua, [Sep 15]); Goldman cut its full-year GDP forecast to 4.5% (The Standard, [Sep 15]). The surveys match: the official manufacturing PMI was 49.8 with new orders back in expansion (BigGo Finance, [Aug 30]) and the private gauge 51.5 (BigGo Finance, [Aug 31]), while exports rose 25% (AP News, [Sep 7]). The outlet is abroad again: refined fuel exports exceeded pre-war levels, with jet fuel exports at a record (Reuters, [Sep 17]).

Credit Impulse: Caixin describes August new loans and aggregate financing as plunging (Caixin Global, [Sep 16]), after bank loans rose less than expected (Reuters, [Sep 14]) and credit expansion missed forecasts (Bloomberg.com, [Sep 14]). The central bank flags weaker credit demand (IDNFinancials, [Sep 17]) and calls slower loan growth the new normal as financing shifts beyond banks (The Asian Banker, [Sep 17]). Support is aimed at capital and cash flow, with $43.9 billion for major state banks (China Daily Global Edition, [Sep 14]) and a crackdown on big firms delaying payments to SMEs (South China Morning Post, [Sep 15]), not at loan demand.

Property: Official framing has turned brighter: China is adjusting select property policies (China Daily Global Edition, [Sep 17]) and says the domestic housing market is enjoying a gradual uptick (chinadailyhk, [Sep 17]). The data are softer: new home prices fell for a third straight month in August (Prop News Time, [Sep 16]) and property investment fell 19.9% in the first eight months (Business Recorder, [Sep 15]); reconciled, tier-1 resale prices are rising (Caixin Global, [Sep 15]) while the broad market still declines. Developer stress persists, with Vanke and Yu Liang hit with consumption restrictions (BigGo Finance, [Sep 16]), even as PE firms consider fresh bids for an Evergrande unit (Bloomberg.com, [Sep 17]).

Fiscal, Policy & Trade: The US track is two-handed. Congress approved tariffs on top buyers of Russian energy and China hit back (South China Morning Post, [Sep 17]), giving Trump a tariff weapon against China and India (CNBC, [Sep 17]), but the administration delayed a tariff round until after the summit (POLITICO Pro, [Sep 17]), Beijing says the two sides are working on a deal to reduce tariffs (China Daily, [Sep 17]) and Bessent opened talks on shared AI risks to set the agenda (CHOSUNBIZ, [Sep 17]). The US auto industry is urging Trump to keep Chinese automakers out ahead of the Xi meeting. Europe is escalating: the EU told China to limit hybrid exports or face new tariffs (EU Perspectives, [Sep 17]), Germany's Klingbeil urges measures against unfair trade with Chinese carmakers (Reuters, [Sep 17]) and the EU trade chief will visit Beijing in October (Free Malaysia Today, [Sep 17]).

Markets & Flows: Hong Kong stocks dropped after the Fed raised rates and signalled more (South China Morning Post, [Sep 17]), the Hang Seng closing at 24,604.29 after the Fed hike and an HKMA rate rise (BBN Times, [Sep 17]), while mainland stocks headed for their best day in a month ahead of the Trump-Xi meeting (TradingView, [Sep 17]). China is bucking the global surge in bond yields (Yahoo Finance, [Sep 17]) and its US Treasury holdings fell to the lowest since 2008 (Financial Times, [Sep 17]); a CKGSB survey finds cautious investor sentiment for Q3 2026 (Yahoo Finance, [Sep 17]).

New / Most Important: The yuan at its strongest since 2022 is the week's surprise. With the Fed hiking and Chinese yields falling, textbook divergence says CNY should weaken; instead the PBOC has guided it firmer for eight straight days ahead of the summit, a political signal that trades growth support for diplomatic goodwill while credit is weak.

Playbook ✓: US-China decoupling continues as expected in autos, now with Europe following on hybrids.  |  ⚠️ Divergence: A stronger yuan on top of 0.4% retail growth and plunging credit squeezes exporters at home, yet the volume keeps flowing out, so European OEMs face both Chinese competition and a tariff fight over it. For EM and the AUD the growth pulse is the problem: record-weak property investment and a steel sector told by CISA to cut production (GMK Center, [Sep 16]) keep iron ore demand soft. Oil back at 96.01 (↑9.3% vs 4W) eases the terms-of-trade tax at the margin.
