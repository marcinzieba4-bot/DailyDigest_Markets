🇨🇳 China  Bearish  ↓ easing cycle

Macro Stance: The PBOC is easing funding while refusing to chase the bond rally. Overnight reverse repos debuted at 1.25% (Reuters, [Jun 28]) and the injection was doubled (Reuters, [Jun 29]); it then signalled easing with a trillion-yuan outright reverse repo, ending a three-month tapering streak (BigGo Finance, [Jul 3]), while cutting bond purchases on wariness over falling yields (Bloomberg, [Jul 2]), so net bond operations injected only 10 billion yuan in June (Macau Business, [Jul 3]). Liquidity now comes through repo, not outright bond buying. The fix leans against weakness: USD/CNY was set at 6.8047 against 6.8088 (TMGM, [Jul 3]), firmer than estimates, which VT Markets reads as managed strength amid softer growth [Jul 3], and HSBC says structural forces still support the RMB (Exchange Rates UK, [Jul 4]). May CPI rose only 1.2% (China Daily, [Jun 10]) while factory-gate inflation hit a near four-year high (Reuters, [Jun 9]); Caixin warns deflation risks are squeezing profits [Jun 30].

PMIs & Activity: June's upturn is narrow. The official manufacturing PMI rose to 50.3 and non-manufacturing to 50.2 (Reuters, [Jun 29]); the private factory gauge grew for a seventh month (China Daily, [Jul 2]) but less than expected (Bloomberg, [Jun 30]). Services slowed slightly (Reuters, [Jul 2]), though by less than forecast (The Business Times, [Jul 3]). Consumption is rolling over: NEV retail sales fell 7% in June (CnEVPost, [Jul 3]) as the subsidy pullback hits auto and appliance sales (Nikkei Asia, [Jul 2]). May's split stands: industrial output growth quickened while retail sales and investment contracted (Reuters, [Jun 15]); exports jumped 19.4% (AP News, [Jun 9]), but retail sales fell 0.6% (Il Sole 24 Ore, [Jun 15]), fixed-asset investment fell 4.1% in five months (Macau Business, [Jun 16]) and profit growth slowed for the first time in six months (Times of India, [Jun 27]).

Credit Impulse: Total social financing grew 7.7% in May (CGTN, [Jun 12]) and topped forecasts (Bloomberg, [Jun 12]), but new loans missed as the property slump curbed demand (Reuters, [Jun 12]): government bonds, not private demand. Stress is visible at the edges: China took over private lender Zhongbang Bank, citing credit risks (The Standard, [Jul 3]); Beijing is capping LGFV bond yields (IFR, [Jul 2]); and Le Monde describes Guizhou's bridge and highway boom running up against a wall of debt [Jul 4]. With debt above 300% of GDP (UPI, [Jun 29]), the private impulse is negative and the local-government channel is spent.

Property: Asia Times says the housing market is in free fall as buyers wait for floor prices [Jul 3], and Briefs Finance has home prices slipping again [Jul 3], consistent with resale prices falling faster in June (Reuters, [Jun 30]). Sales are concentrating, not recovering: top-tier cities overtook regional hubs (Caixin, [Jul 3]), and the top 100 developers' June sales rose 11.8% month on month on a half-year push (Moomoo, [Jul 1]). Real-estate investment fell 16.2% y/y in January–May (GMK Center, [Jun 18]). Resolution is state-led: Vanke named a former Shenzhen financial regulator as president (Caixin, [Jul 3]) and Country Garden cleared its first offshore coupon (Caixin, [Jul 2]).

Fiscal, Policy & Trade: Fiscal support is receding: vehicle and vessel tax breaks for NEVs and energy-saving cars are ending (China Daily HK, [Jul 4]) and a tax crackdown is hitting listed firms (SCMP, [Jul 2]). Control is rising, with curbs on foreign investments (Times of India, [Jul 1]) and a landmark law over offshore tech transfers (SCMP, [Jul 1]). On trade, Beijing signals openness to cut the EU deficit as Brussels toughens (SCMP, [Jul 3]); von der Leyen says the EU will retaliate if China misses October (Euronews, [Jul 3]), while the chief trade MEP calls that deadline "not realistic at all" (Euronews, [Jul 3]). The state iron ore buyer extended restrictions on Fortescue (Bloomberg, [Jul 3]), and China and the US target tariff cuts on farm goods (Bloomberg, [Jul 2]).

Markets & Flows: China shares ended higher as technology stocks rebounded (Business Standard, [Jul 3]) and the Hang Seng rose 0.8% on tech and financials (Dimsum Daily, [Jul 3]), after a three-week mainland low (The Star, [Jul 2]). Wall Street banks are recovering in China amid a trading boom (FT, [Jul 4]), and mainland investors are buying Hong Kong shares, favouring SMIC and Zhipu AI (SCMP, [Jul 3]): the flow is into AI and brokers, not the domestic cycle. The capital wall keeps rising, with curbs on higher-yielding offshore debt (Bloomberg, [Jun 29]) and a shutdown of retail gold trading (TipRanks, [Jul 1]).

New / Most Important: The newest China signal is geopolitical: Iran's envoy says Beijing will get Hormuz concessions (Bloomberg, [Jul 4]). For an importer with weak domestic demand, secured and cheaper crude is a terms-of-trade gift and helps explain why the PBOC can lean on the fix. It does nothing for the domestic problem that Guizhou illustrates: the local governments that built the last cycle cannot fund the next one, and households are not borrowing to replace them.

Playbook ✓: Continued easing at the front end, falling home prices, receding subsidies and a managed yuan are on script.  |  ⚠️ Divergence: The oil collapse is not a China-demand signal on its own; it is supply (the Strait of Hormuz reopening, Beijing's concessions, some fuel export curbs lifted on ample supplies per Bloomberg [Jun 30]) meeting a Chinese economy whose PMI strength is all exports. Transmission runs three ways. Bulk commodities: the state iron ore buyer's extended Fortescue restrictions and real-estate investment down 16.2% hit iron ore and the AUD directly. Europe: the WSJ says China is devastating the last stronghold of German industry [Jul 3], a share loss that hurts European exporters whether or not October brings retaliation. EM: cheaper crude flatters importers, while excess Chinese supply exports disinflation through goods prices even with a firmer fix. Hong Kong rallies on AI and broker flows are flows, not fundamentals.
