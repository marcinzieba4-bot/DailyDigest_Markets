🇨🇳 China  Bullish  ↓ easing bias

Macro Stance: The PBOC is easing through volume while wary of the bond rally. It held the 7-day reverse repo rate while adding liquidity (TradingView, [Jun 30]) and ran a trillion-yuan outright reverse repo, ending a three-month tapering streak (BigGo Finance, [Jul 3]), but cut bond purchases on concern over falling yields (Bloomberg, [Jul 2]). The fixing leans against weakness: USD/CNY was set at 6.8047 versus 6.8088 (tmgm.com, [Jul 3]), a firmer-than-estimated fix that VT Markets reads as managed yuan strength amid softer growth ([Jul 3]); JPMorgan Asset Management bets the strong-yuan trend will flip (Bloomberg, [Jul 6]). Prices stay split: May CPI rose only 1.2% (China Daily, [Jun 10]) while factory-gate inflation hit a near four-year high (Reuters, [Jun 9]).

PMIs & Activity: June confirms an export- and tech-led upturn. The official manufacturing PMI rose to 50.3 and non-manufacturing to 50.2 (Reuters, [Jun 29]); the private factory gauge capped its strongest quarter since late 2020 (Reuters, [Jun 30]) though it grew less than expected (Bloomberg, [Jun 30]), and private services slowed slightly (Reuters, [Jul 2]). May showed the split: exports jumped 19.4% (AP News, [Jun 9]) while retail sales fell 0.6% (Il Sole 24 Ore, [Jun 15]) and fixed-asset investment was down 4.1% in the first five months (Macau Business, [Jun 16]). The domestic leg is still softening: NEV retail sales fell 7% in June on preliminary CPCA data (CnEVPost, [Jul 3]) as the subsidy pullback hits autos and appliances (Nikkei Asia, [Jul 2]), and a Nikkei survey sees second-quarter growth slowing on weak domestic demand ([Jul 6]).

Credit Impulse: The last print is two-track. Total social financing grew 7.7% in May (CGTN, [Jun 12]) and topped forecasts (Bloomberg, [Jun 12]), but new loans missed as the property slump curbed demand (Reuters, [Jun 12]). The beat is government bonds; Goldman's shorthand is that households are lying flat and no longer borrowing (Binance, [Jul 5]). Stress is surfacing in small lenders: Beijing took over Zhongbang Bank citing credit risks (The Standard, [Jul 3]), and the SCMP warns the rescue "might not be the last" ([Jul 6]) as the new top regulator steps up its risk cleanup (Bloomberg, [Jul 6]). Capping LGFV bond yields (IFR, [Jul 2]) keeps local funding cheap but does nothing for loan demand.

Property: Resale home prices fell faster in June, a private survey shows (Reuters, [Jun 30]), after new home prices fell at a faster pace in May (Reuters, [Jun 15]) and real-estate investment dropped 16.2% y/y in January–May (GMK Center, [Jun 18]). Sales are concentrating, not recovering: the top 100 developers sold nearly RMB 1.6 trillion in the first half with an 11.8% month-on-month rise in June (Moomoo, [Jul 1]), led by top-tier cities (Caixin, [Jul 3]), while Asia Times describes a market in free fall as buyers wait for floor prices ([Jul 3]). Restructuring runs under state direction: Country Garden cleared its first offshore coupon after its overhaul (Caixin, [Jul 2]) and Vanke named a former Shenzhen financial regulator as president (Caixin, [Jul 3]).

Fiscal, Policy & Trade: Xi's growth pledge lifted stocks after the PMI (Modern Diplomacy, [Jul 1]), but consumer support is being withdrawn: tax breaks for energy-saving cars and NEVs are ending (chinadailyhk, [Jul 4]). Trade is thawing with Washington and hardening elsewhere. Beijing pledged $17 billion in annual US farm purchases (BigGo Finance, [Jul 2]) and will drop its 10% tariff on US agricultural goods (AgWeb, [Jul 6]); von der Leyen says the EU will retaliate if China misses the October deadline (Euronews, [Jul 3]), even as China signals openness to cutting the gap (SCMP, [Jul 3]). Beijing put export controls on 40 Japanese entities (AP News, [Jul 1]) and its state iron-ore buyer extended curbs on Fortescue (Bloomberg, [Jul 3]).

Markets & Flows: China is breaking step with global markets as investors buy in (Reuters, [Jul 6]), and the SCMP reads the divergent market as a microcosm of the economy ([Jul 6]). After an 11% first-half fall in Hong Kong (SCMP, [Jun 30]), mainland investors are buying SMIC and Zhipu AI (SCMP, [Jul 3]), foreign A-share holdings hit a record (Global Times, [Jul 1]) and China Resources New Energy tripled on debut (Business Times, [Jul 2]). The counter-signal is households: China's biggest ETF is now a US$13 billion gold fund as state stock-market support fades (Business Times, [Jul 6]).

New / Most Important: The "China breaks step" story is the key signal. While global equities face semiconductor headwinds and rising yields, Chinese equities are attracting inflows on a relative-value basis, and DeepSeek's role in the US chip sell-off reinforces China's AI-independence narrative. But the bid is narrow — AI, chips and new listings — and the PMI upturn behind it rests on tech exports, while property, private credit and car sales still deteriorate. This is a capital-flow rotation, not a reflation trade.

Playbook ✓: Easing through liquidity operations, a managed firm fixing and an AI-led equity bid are on script.  |  ⚠️ Divergence: The flows are running ahead of the growth pulse. An export-driven PMI at 50.3, a loan book households will not use and resale prices falling faster in June mean the rally prices an AI premium, not a domestic recovery, and the transmission follows that split. Construction demand stays weak, so iron ore is flat near $98 (Kalkine, [Jul 6]) and the AUD bounce lacks conviction (FOREX.com, [Jul 5]). European exporters take the other side: Goldman says Europe's growth is hit more by Chinese exports than by the bigger trade gap (Reuters, [Jul 2]). EM Asia gets AI flows but no demand impulse. Until loan growth and property sales outside the top tier turn, this is selective positioning, not macro confidence.
