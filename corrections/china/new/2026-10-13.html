🇨🇳 China  Mixed  → easing bias maintained

Macro Stance: China remains in a different monetary cycle from the rest of the world: the PBOC keeps easing through a 1.2 trillion yuan outright reverse repo (巴士的報, [Oct 8]) and a net 100 billion yuan of sovereign bond purchases in September (TradingView, [Oct 9]) while the Fed, ECB and RBA hold restrictive, and with US 10Y yields at 5.23% the gap caps a benchmark cut. The divergence is not producing the yuan weakness the old playbook predicts: the PBOC set the fix firmer at 6.7313 from 6.7330 (FXStreet, [Oct 12]), the yuan holds near a 3-1/2-year high against the dollar as rate bets ease (Forex Factory, [Oct 12]), and the central bank rebuffs undervaluation claims (VT Markets, [Oct 12]). Prices are not the problem: August CPI rose 0.8% and PPI 3.8% (BigGo Finance, [Sep 8]), with September inflation due this week (BigGo Finance, [Oct 11]).

PMIs & Activity: September's official manufacturing PMI returned to growth at 50.1, exactly at consensus and up from 49.8, with non-manufacturing at 50.2 (investingLive, [Sep 29]); the private RatingDog gauges printed 52.1 and 51.6 (investingLive, [Sep 30]), and the IMF says the AI boom bolstered global trade growth, benefiting China (Reuters, [Oct 12]). The domestic half is weak: August retail sales rose 0.4% against 0.6% expected (investingLive, [Sep 14]) and industrial profit growth slowed to 4.2% (Bloomberg, [Sep 27]). September car data sharpen the split: exports rose 64% while domestic sales fell 27% (Fortune, [Oct 12]), and China's auto sales fell despite the traditional peak season (WSJ, [Oct 12]). Nikkei estimates third-quarter growth at 4.4%, below target (Nikkei Asia, [Oct 6]).

Credit Impulse: August is still the latest print, and both measures missed: bank lending disappointed as credit demand stayed weak (Reuters, [Sep 14]) and total credit expansion missed forecasts (Bloomberg, [Sep 14]), with bond and equity financing exceeding loans in social financing for the first time (BigGo Finance, [Sep 14]), the government leg holding while private demand did not. Beijing subsidises credit rather than igniting it: 100 billion yuan of interest subsidies leveraged 17 trillion yuan of consumer credit (BigGo Finance, [Oct 9]). The system is still cleaning up, with China accelerating its small-bank purge to contain systemic risks (CHOSUNBIZ, [Oct 6]) and a Yiren Digital chairman detained as investment defaults spread (South China Morning Post, [Oct 12]).

Property: The mortgage subsidy reached transactions over the holiday: new-home subscriptions in 50 key cities rose over 15% year on year (Moomoo, [Oct 8]) and Shanghai new-home sales doubled while second-hand transactions in 11 cities jumped over 40% (BigGo Finance, [Oct 9]). The quality of the bounce is disputed: one report calls the resale-led rebound a hollow narrative (IANS LIVE, [Oct 12]) and the SCMP argues cheap debt is not enough to get homebuyers spending again (South China Morning Post, [Oct 12]). Developer stress persists, with Vanke reporting a 16.02 billion yuan loss (ad-hoc-news.de, [Oct 7]) and PAG in exclusive talks with liquidators for an Evergrande unit (Bloomberg, [Oct 9]). August's base was weak: prices down across 70 cities, investment down 19.9% in eight months (BigGo Finance, [Sep 14]).

Fiscal, Policy & Trade: Beijing is not in a holding pattern: local governments may tap 550 billion yuan of unused debt quotas (Caixin Global, [Oct 9]) and the NDRC pledges more policy tools (Bloomberg, [Oct 10]). The constraint is cost, with debt interest surging to 1.4 trillion yuan (BigGo Finance, [Oct 12]); Global Times counters that the debt level won't limit policy space (Global Times, [Oct 12]). The Financial Street Forum opens next week with financial reform policies due (BigGo Finance, [Oct 12]). On trade, the EU will cap Chinese hybrid imports at 350,000 a year (The Guardian, [Oct 12]), putting a $7.5 billion hybrid boom under new curbs (Reuters, [Oct 12]), and Brussels eyes December trade initiatives (Reuters, [Oct 12]). Exports are in focus this week amid weak domestic demand (The Economic Times, [Oct 12]).

Markets & Flows: Beijing moved to steady the A-share market after a sharp tech selloff (South China Morning Post, [Oct 12]); the CSRC held a symposium after the CSI 300 fell 12% in the third quarter (IndexBox, [Oct 11]). Stocks fell again as higher global yields and geopolitical tensions weighed (Business Recorder, [Oct 12]) while big bank shares hit record highs (BigGo Finance, [Oct 12]). The Hang Seng closed 0.3% higher (chinadailyhk, [Oct 12]). The value call has a following: the South China Morning Post highlights a Munger disciple calling Chinese stocks an undervalued opportunity.

New / Most Important: September's car data are the cleanest read on the economy: exports up 64%, domestic sales down 27%. The same weakness reached Europe overnight, as China gloom hit LVMH and dimmed hopes of a luxury revival (Reuters, [Oct 12]), while Brussels, having capped hybrids, eyes December trade measures, so the export valve is narrowing just as it carries more of the load.

Playbook ✓: Foreign investors remain underweight, property stress weighs on sentiment without a systemic credit event, and policy releases quotas and steadies markets rather than cutting rates.  |  ⚠️ Divergence: The tell is the gap between an AI- and export-led factory PMI above 50 and a domestic consumer that stopped buying cars. Credit is directed into manufacturing and technology while consumption support stays thin, so transmission to global assets runs through factories, not households. Asian tech supply chains gain; the AUD and iron ore need a property recovery that has reached sales but not the 19.9% investment slump; European luxury and autos lose twice, to a weak Chinese consumer and to Chinese cars redirected abroad; EM exporters face more Chinese supply. The undervalued-equity thesis is right on valuation but premature until credit or property confirms.
