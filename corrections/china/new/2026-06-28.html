🇨🇳 China  Mixed  → easing bias

Macro Stance: The PBOC is rebuilding its operating framework rather than cutting. Loan prime rates were held for a 13th month in June (Reuters, [Jun 21]), the 1-year LPR at 3.0% (Mitrade, [Jun 21]), and the bank now plans an overnight reverse repo as the next stage of its policy shift (Bloomberg, [Jun 25]), with a survey pointing to a new overnight rate of 1.35% ([Jun 25]): the bias is easing, but the tool is liquidity, not price. The yuan fixing was weakened for a fourth session as the dollar advanced (Bloomberg, [Jun 23]): a managed drift lower against a dollar at 13-month highs, while the ECB openly calls the yuan undervalued (Reuters, [Jun 22]); T. Rowe Price sees it falling 1%-2% by year-end (TradingView, [Jun 26]). Prices are split. May CPI rose only 1.2% and missed expectations (China Daily, [Jun 10]), while factory-gate inflation hit a near four-year high on Iran-war input costs and the AI boom (Reuters, [Jun 9]).

PMIs & Activity: The official manufacturing PMI stalled at 50 in May (Reuters, [May 30]) with factory orders contracting (Wood Central, [Jun 1]), and the non-manufacturing gauge was barely positive at 50.1 (Macau Business, [May 31]). Private surveys were firmer, with services at 54.4 (TradingView, [Jun 2]). Industrial output growth quickened to 4.5% in May (China Daily, [Jun 16]), but retail sales fell 0.6%, the first drop in over three years (Il Sole 24 Ore, [Jun 15]), and fixed-asset investment was down 4.1% in the first five months (Macau Business, [Jun 16]). Industrial profits rose 18.8% in January–May (WSJ, [Jun 26]) and but profit growth slowed for the first time in six months as weak domestic demand bit (Times of India, [Jun 27]).

Credit Impulse: Total social financing grew 7.7% in May (CGTN, [Jun 12]) and topped forecasts (Bloomberg, [Jun 12]), but new loans missed as the property slump curbed demand (Reuters, [Jun 12]). The beat is government-bond issuance; private credit demand is still contracting. The PBOC is now urging banks to boost lending because demand remains weak, according to sources (Reuters, [Jun 26]). Household deleveraging continues (SCMP, [Jun 15]) against a $300 billion pile of bad consumer debt (Bloomberg, [Jun 17]). Read: the headline impulse is flat-to-positive, but the household and private-firm impulse is still negative, which is why output does not convert into consumption.

Property: New home prices fell at a faster pace in May (Reuters, [Jun 15]) even as first-tier new home prices rose for a third month (SCMP, [Jun 16]) and top-city resale prices extended their rebound (Caixin, [Jun 17]). Real-estate investment fell 16.2% y/y in January–May (GMK Center, [Jun 18]). This week the stress resurfaced at the developer level: Beijing land sales plunged 66% as developers chase only "safe" prime plots (Caixin, [Jun 25]), and the Evergrande Property Services stake sale collapsed, sending its shares down 24% (Caixin, [Jun 26]). Bloomberg describes steel entering a long plateau after the property crash ([Jun 21]).

Fiscal, Policy & Trade: The fiscal signal is the surprise. Beijing narrowed its cumulative deficit for the first time since 2023 (Bloomberg, [Jun 22]). A third batch of 62.5 billion yuan for consumer-goods trade-ins was allocated (Global Times, [Jun 26]): targeted support, small relative to the retail contraction. Exports jumped 19.4% in May (AP, [Jun 9]) and shipments to the US grew 35% (CNBC, [Jun 8]). The EU's deficit with China reached a record €1bn a day (The Guardian, [Jun 15]), and German carmakers are embarking on historic job cuts as Chinese rivals flood the market (Financial Times, [Jun 27]). Beijing hit 10 US firms, including MP Materials, with export controls (Reuters, [Jun 22]), and the 55% tariff on Australian beef is now in force (Australian Rural & Regional News, [Jun 22]).

Markets & Flows: Onshore and offshore have decoupled. Mainland stocks hit a 4-1/2-year high (TradingView, [Jun 22]), led by AI names on demand optimism and policy support (Bloomberg, [Jun 21]). Hong Kong is the opposite: the HSCEI entered a technical bear market (finance.biggo.com, [Jun 23]), Bloomberg ties the slide to spending woes ([Jun 23]), and the Hang Seng capped its worst week in over a year amid a tech sell-off (SCMP, [Jun 26]). Citi says beaten-down China internet stocks are worth buying on the dip (Investing.com, [Jun 27]) and PDD Holdings was flagged as a top non-tech pick, but with the Fed and ECB tightening together and Asia FX weak, the capital-account backdrop is hostile.

New / Most Important: Pressure for a policy response is building from inside. Government advisers are calling for a fix to the two-speed economy (Bloomberg, [Jun 27]), the same week the PBOC urged banks to lend into weak demand. With new loans missing and the first fiscal-gap cutback since 2023, the credit impulse that matters for households is not turning. The open question for the second half is whether Beijing reverses the fiscal tightening; until it does, growth leans on exports just as Europe prepares trade defences.

Playbook ✓: Property drag, weak consumption, LPR on hold and a managed yuan drift lower are all on script.  |  ⚠️ Divergence: The consensus expected a mid-year stimulus pivot; instead the credit impulse outside government bonds is negative and the fiscal impulse is narrowing. China is therefore exporting its excess supply rather than absorbing it at home. The transmission runs through three channels. European exporters face record bilateral deficits and a weak yuan, and the auto job cuts show the damage is already in payrolls. Bulk-commodity demand tied to construction is fading, with the steel plateau and an investment slump that hit AUD alongside the beef tariff. And global goods disinflation is the channel Pimco expects to support EM bonds (Bloomberg, [Jun 23]). A real turn needs loan growth, not bond issuance, to re-accelerate; until it does, rallies in Hong Kong are flows, not fundamentals.
