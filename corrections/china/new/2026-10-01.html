🇨🇳 China  Bearish  → Easing but ineffective

Macro Stance: Easing arrived before the holiday, through structural channels rather than the benchmark rate. The PBOC cut the PSL rate by 0.25 percentage point to 1.5% (Forex Factory, [Sep 29]) and boosted relending quotas by 700 billion yuan (BigGo Finance, [Sep 29]), after pledging to keep policy 'appropriately loose' (TradingView, [Sep 24]). The currency is not the binding constraint a 5.23% US 10Y would imply: the fix was set at 6.7351 vs 6.7411 (www.tmgm.com, [Sep 30]), a 3.5-year high before the National Day holiday (The Standard (HK), [Sep 30]), while China's 10Y yield sits at an over one-year low (TradingView, [Sep 29]). The constraint is transmission: August CPI rose 0.8% and PPI 3.8% (BigGo Finance, [Sep 8]), on rising energy costs rather than demand (Reuters, [Sep 9]).

PMIs & Activity: September brought the first official expansion in three months: manufacturing at 50.1 and non-manufacturing at 50.2 (investingLive, [Sep 29]), with the private RatingDog gauges stronger at 52.1 for manufacturing and 51.6 for services (investingLive, [Sep 30]). The 'beat' headlines (TradingView, [Sep 29]) and an official factory print exactly at the 50.1 consensus (investingLive, [Sep 29]) reconcile: the official survey met forecasts, the upside came from services and the export-leaning private surveys. The engine is AI (Nikkei Asia, [Sep 30]) and August exports that jumped 25% (AP News, [Sep 7]); the domestic side lags, with August industrial profits up only 4.2%, the weakest since November (Bloomberg, [Sep 27]), retail sales up just 0.4% (investingLive, [Sep 14]) and NEV retail sales down 20% in the first 27 days of September (CnEVPost, [Sep 30]).

Credit Impulse: August bank lending disappointed (Reuters, [Sep 14]) and credit expansion missed forecasts (Bloomberg, [Sep 14]), with bond and equity financing exceeding loans for the first time (BigGo Finance, [Sep 14]): no conflict, both measures show weak private demand. The package redirects credit rather than igniting it: cheap loans help state firms pay overdue bills (Bloomberg, [Sep 29]) and banks are urged to finance AI and asset-light service firms (Caixin Global, [Sep 29]). Household credit stays impaired, with the bad consumer debt cleanup faltering (The Business Times, [Sep 29]).

Property: The central government will subsidise mortgages for the first time, a 1 percentage point annual rate subsidy worth up to nearly 50,000 yuan on a 1 million yuan loan (BigGo Finance, [Sep 29]) for first-time buyers (bloomingbit, [Sep 30]), and Finance Minister Lan Fo'an wants the subsidy scope expanded (BigGo Finance, [Sep 30]). Developers got a bid, Vanke A hitting a six-month high (Moomoo, [Sep 30]), but the base is weak: August home prices fell across 70 cities and property investment dropped 19.9% in eight months (BigGo Finance, [Sep 14]), and Bloomberg asks why China can't sort out its property market mess (Bloomberg, [Sep 30]).

Fiscal, Policy & Trade: The package delivers on the cabinet's pledge of stronger support to meet the growth target (Reuters, [Sep 28]), alongside fourth-quarter local bond issuance likely around 500 billion yuan (BigGo Finance, [Sep 28]). The market's read is enough for the target and no more: a 'mini stimulus' seen securing the GDP target (Bloomberg, [Sep 29]) that underwhelms in a sign the two-speed economy will persist (Bloomberg, [Sep 30]). On trade, the US and China pledge tariff cuts on $60 billion of goods (Reuters, [Sep 28]), but Beijing extended two trade barrier probes into the US (Reuters, [Sep 30]) and vows a response if the EU targets Chinese trade (Reuters, [Sep 29]), with ties to Brussels on the brink ahead of a make-or-break October (South China Morning Post, [Sep 30]).

Markets & Flows: Chinese stocks sank to a 13-month low (South China Morning Post, [Sep 28]) and limped to a quarterly drop as the stimulus fell short (TradingView, [Sep 30]); the SSE Composite rose 0.31% to 3,842.19 ahead of Golden Week as the stimulus failed to ignite buyers (BBN Times, [Sep 30]). The yuan is the counterpoint, set for a seventh straight quarterly gain as exporters sell dollars before the holiday (Business Recorder, [Sep 30]), the gap between stocks and the yuan Bloomberg ties to a two-speed economy (Bloomberg, [Sep 29]).

New / Most Important: The verdict on the package came in on the eve of the holiday as a gap between data and markets. Private PMIs at 52.1 and 51.6 confirm the official return above 50, yet equities closed the quarter lower and Bloomberg calls the stimulus underwhelming: investors read a PSL cut, 700 billion yuan of relending and a first-home mortgage subsidy as a floor under the growth target, not a reflation. The firm yuan fix shows Beijing is not buying growth with a weaker currency, so the burden falls on fiscal follow-through that is being drip-fed (Bloomberg, [Sep 30]).

Playbook ✓: Property structurally impaired, households not re-levering, and policy working through structural tools, quota-tapping and subsidies rather than a benchmark cut or a fiscal bazooka: the well-known muddle-through.  |  ⚠️ Divergence: The data are improving faster than the transmission to global assets. An export- and AI-led PMI above 50 helps Asian tech supply chains and EM currencies at the margin (the rupiah gained on the Chinese PMI, per FXStreet [Sep 30]), though Bank Indonesia is still preparing strategies to safeguard it and Vietnam flags foreign capital outflows. The AUD struggled to gain traction despite the solid PMIs (FXStreet, [Sep 30]) because the property demand behind iron ore is still contracting, with investment down 19.9% and credit missing; it stays hostage to the RBA. Oil at 92.18 is a supply story, not a Chinese demand pull. European luxury and auto exporters need a Chinese consumer still at 0.4% retail growth, and face retaliation over EU curbs. A China that eases selectively, keeps the yuan firm and exports its way to a 50.1 PMI leaves EM Asia competing with its factories rather than selling to its demand.
