🇨🇳 China  Mixed  → Easing bias

Macro Stance: The PBOC is easing through volume, not price. It set the overnight reverse repo rate steady at 1.25% (Reuters, [Jul 28]) and is maintaining liquidity support before month-end (TradingView, [Jul 28]), after adding the most liquidity in five months (Bloomberg, [Jul 23]) and injecting a total of 2.1 trillion yuan via overnight repos and MLF (Reuters, [Jul 24]). The fixing is being used to lean against weakness: the PBOC marginally lowered the daily yuan fix versus the dollar (TradingPedia, [Jul 27]), Nomura reads the fixing as pointing to more yuan gains (Exchange Rates UK, [Jul 28]), and the yuan eased from a one-week high as the Fed and Politburo meetings loom (KLSE Screener, [Jul 28]). Prices are split: June CPI rose only 1% (Global Times, [Jul 9]) while PPI climbed 4.1% (finance.biggo.com, [Jul 8]), a near four-year high (Reuters, [Jul 8]).

PMIs & Activity: The last official read is June: manufacturing PMI 50.3 and non-manufacturing 50.2 (Reuters, [Jun 29]); July's numbers are the next test (ING Think, [Jul 23]). Q2 GDP grew 4.3%, below the official target range (Bloomberg, [Jul 15]); June industrial output rose 5.3% y/y (BusinessLine, [Jul 15]), first-half retail sales just 1.3% (Ecns.cn, [Jul 15]), while June exports surged 27% from a year earlier (AP News, [Jul 14]). The profit data sharpen the split: first-half industrial profits rose 18.7% as a tech boom masks the property slump (Caixin Global, [Jul 27]), yet growth is the slowest this year (Financial Times, [Jul 26]) and arrears and "involution" still slam traditional industries (South China Morning Post, [Jul 27]); Reuters finds high-tech manufacturing hubs pulling ahead in uneven growth ([Jul 28]). Consumption is soft: July car sales are set to fall 16.8% (GlobalChinaEV, [Jul 27]) and Mercedes cut its outlook as China's luxury slump deepens (Bloomberg, [Jul 28]).

Credit Impulse: June new bank loans rose less than expected (The Star, [Jul 15]) and credit growth missed as the PBOC flagged a "new normal" (Caixin Global, [Jul 16]); household loans fell by 366.8 billion yuan in the first half (36Kr, [Jul 17]). The finance minister's 17 trillion yuan of first-half new loans (finance.biggo.com, [Jul 22]) is policy-directed lending, not private demand, and the cleanest tell is that cash at Chinese banks has swelled to a record as borrowing slows (Nikkei Asia, [Jul 28]). Pricing is loosening quietly, with banks breaking with the lending benchmark (Bloomberg, [Jul 24]) and launching the first repo-linked loans (Yahoo Finance, [Jul 27]). Read: liquidity is abundant, the impulse is not.

Property: June new home prices fell at a slower pace (Reuters, [Jul 14]) and first-tier prices extended a four-month rebound (South China Morning Post, [Jul 15]), but resale prices fell faster (Reuters, [Jun 30]) and property investment slumped 18 percent y/y in January–June (The Standard (HK), [Jul 15]). State-linked developers are still buying prime land: Poly Developments and China Resources Land spent 23.2 billion yuan in a day in Shanghai's inner ring (finance.biggo.com, [Jul 28]), and property stocks led gains on hopes for supportive policies (Moomoo, [Jul 28]). Elsewhere capital exits, with KKR and AEW joining the sell-off wave (The Real Deal, [Jul 22]), and regulators curbed loan structures used by distressed developers (Bloomberg, [Jul 22]). Vanke completed its bond extensions with a Shenzhen Metro loan (China Economic Review, [Jul 23]).

Fiscal, Policy & Trade: Beijing clings to austerity despite the slowdown (Bloomberg, [Jul 22]); tax revenue topped 10 trln yuan in the first half (Xinhua, [Jul 28]), and Caixin's analysis is that China will likely forgo major stimulus as the growth target remains in reach ([Jul 28]) ahead of the July Politburo meeting. On trade, China says the US pledged to cap replacement tariffs at 20% (Bloomberg, [Jul 27]) and it held off on retaliation over Section 301 (South China Morning Post, [Jul 27]), but it is gearing up to fight overcapacity claims as a trade clash looms (Bloomberg, [Jul 28]) with a new document rejecting them (South China Morning Post, [Jul 28]), while Germany maps China's weaknesses for a trade war (Bloomberg, [Jul 28]). Beijing scrapped panda bond fees to bolster yuan debt (South China Morning Post, [Jul 28]).

Markets & Flows: China is throwing a 1-2-3 punch at the global AI trade (Bloomberg, [Jul 28]): ASML slid on a report of China starting DUV tool production (Bloomberg, [Jul 27]), CXMT is now worth more than Intel (Yahoo Finance, [Jul 28]) and the KOSPI plunged 11% on China chip competition fears (Nikkei Asia, [Jul 28]), though CNBC notes big caveats to the breakthrough ([Jul 28]). Hong Kong hit an 8-week high (TradingView, [Jul 28]) even as mainland markets tumbled (The Standard (HK), [Jul 28]) on AI circular-financing fears (Moomoo, [Jul 28]).

New / Most Important: The pre-Politburo consensus has hardened against a package: with the target in reach, Caixin expects Beijing to forgo major stimulus, and the PBOC is holding its new policy rate at 1.25%. Record cash in banks as borrowing slows shows why that matters: the system is liquid, households and private firms simply are not borrowing, so more volume easing adds little without fiscal support for demand.

Playbook ✓: Steady policy rate, ample liquidity, export- and tech-led profits, weak property and a measured tariff response are on script.  |  ⚠️ Divergence: China's tech wins are now a shock to other markets: Korean memory names sold off on its chip advances, Europe's exporters feel its weak luxury demand as Mercedes cuts guidance, and Fortune notes Chinese exports are lowering inflation abroad ([Jul 28]). On commodities, iron ore eased as faltering China demand offset stimulus hopes (Business Recorder, [Jul 28]), a direct drag on AUD that an 18 percent property-investment slump will not reverse. Oil at $84.61 remains a terms-of-trade hit for a major importer. EM flows follow AI, not a PMI or credit upturn.
