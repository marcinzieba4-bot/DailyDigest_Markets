🇨🇳 China  Mixed  → easing bias / stimulus posture

Macro Stance: The PBOC keeps its easing bias through quantities, not price: a 700 billion yuan outright reverse repo left an October net injection of 400 billion yuan (BigGo Finance, [Oct 14]), Governor Pan says policy stays moderately loose (BigGo Finance, [Oct 15]), and Deputy Governor Xuan pledges to firmly expand domestic demand (BigGo Finance, [Oct 18]). The LPR is on this week's calendar (www.christophe-barraud.com, [Oct 18]). The constraint is not the currency: the yuan was poised for a third weekly gain on strong exports (Reuters, [Oct 16]), and the fix, nudged stronger again (TradingPedia, [Oct 16]), stays on the weak side of the 6.6947 estimate (Newsquawk, [Oct 16]); the PBOC says reforms, not exchange rates, must address global imbalances (CGTN, [Oct 18]). Prices are the limit: September CPI rose 0.9% against 1% expected (investingLive, [Oct 14]) while factory inflation hit 5%, the fastest in over four years (investingLive, [Oct 14]), jolted by oil (Bloomberg, [Oct 14]). With US 10Y yields at 5.15%, China is the one major market where bond yields are not rising (The Indian Express, [Oct 16]).

PMIs & Activity: September's official manufacturing PMI returned to growth at 50.1 with non-manufacturing at 50.2 (investingLive, [Sep 29]), and RatingDog manufacturing leapt to 52.1 (FXStreet, [Sep 30]). Trade shows where that comes from: exports surged 36.3% y/y and imports rose 31% (The Daily News, [Oct 14]), with the January-September surplus at $938 billion (The Journal, [Oct 14]). Q3 GDP came in above expectations, though downside risks remain (Westpac IQ, [Oct 18]), at the SCIO briefing on the first three quarters (CGTN, [Oct 18]); Reuters' poll had seen growth picking up as exports power ahead (Reuters, [Oct 18]), and a consensus that expected growth to miss the official target (Yahoo Finance, [Oct 18]) sets a low bar. The preview split still holds: industry holds up, consumers and property lag (investingLive, [Oct 18]), with passenger-car retail sales down 24% in the first 11 days of October (Gasgoo, [Oct 16]).

Credit Impulse: September new bank loans jumped to 1.08 trillion yuan, above forecast (TradingView, [Oct 14]), but household credit contracted by over 1 trillion yuan in the first three quarters (BigGo Finance, [Oct 14]). The two reconcile: the headline beat is corporate and seasonal, while household credit keeps shrinking despite years of stimulus spending (Startup Fortune, [Oct 18]) and The Asian Banker sees export growth strengthening as household borrowing remains weak (The Asian Banker, [Oct 16]). The impulse is not reaching households.

Property: State media say the property recovery gains traction as new policies take effect (CGTN, [Oct 18]), and Shanghai's market broke records (China Daily, [Oct 14]); against that, the SCMP argues cheap debt is not enough to get buyers spending (South China Morning Post, [Oct 12]) and the GDP preview has property lagging. Supply is being re-engineered: Hangzhou and Shenzhen fleshed out rules for the housing sales overhaul (Caixin Global, [Oct 16]) and first-tier land auctions cooled sharply in October (BigGo Finance, [Oct 13]). The base is August: home prices stayed weak (The Standard (HK), [Sep 15]) and property investment dropped 19.9% in eight months (Business Recorder, [Sep 15]).

Fiscal, Policy & Trade: Beijing completed this year's 1.3 trillion yuan of ultra-long special government bonds (BigGo Finance, [Oct 17]) and the State Council mapped out a deeper AI+ push (BigGo Finance, [Oct 17]), but the debt stock is the ceiling: interest costs have surged to CNY 1.4 trillion (BigGo Finance, [Oct 12]), and the IMF urges bigger fiscal stimulus (Reuters, [Oct 15]). On trade, Trump waived the Russia-related sanctions that threatened 100% tariffs on India and China (Hindustan Times, [Oct 17]), while Beijing moved to curb tech exports, including AI (AP News, [Oct 16]), and proposed export controls on solar-manufacturing technologies (Reuters, [Oct 18]) as solar panel exports extend their slump (Reuters, [Oct 18]).

Markets & Flows: Beijing moved to prop up stocks with selling curbs and buybacks (Bloomberg, [Oct 16]), but Chinese stocks turned the biggest losers as the global AI trade stumbled (Bloomberg, [Oct 17]), and a Friday bounce failed to rescue Hong Kong's weekly loss (BusinessToday Malaysia, [Oct 18]); Asian markets are churning on tech and China's outlook. Bonds price the weak domestic side: long-dated yields hit a 15-month low as the PBOC keeps cash flowing (TradingView, [Oct 15]).

New / Most Important: Q3 GDP beat expectations, but the beat confirms the two-speed economy rather than ending it: exports and AI hardware carry the headline while household credit shrinks and property lags. A growth surprise lowers the urgency of a benchmark cut at this week's LPR and argues for more of the same: liquidity, quota-based fiscal support and state help for equities, with the Xuan pledge on domestic demand still to be matched by spending.

Playbook ✓: Booming exports beside weak households, a PBOC easing quantities rather than rates, a state floor under stocks and low CGB yields all follow the script.  |  ⚠️ Divergence: Transmission to global assets is splitting by channel. Asian tech supply chains get the export boom, South Korea's factory growth at a 4-month high on export orders (Reuters, [Sep 30]), even as Korea's stock losses hurt domestic consumption. Iron ore and the AUD need property and construction, and that channel is not turning: investment is down 19.9% and land auctions are cooling, consistent with the Australian dollar at its lowest since July (Economies.com, [Oct 15]) and with Australia's own home prices tumbling (Nikkei Asia, [Oct 17]). European exporters face both sides of the surplus: a weak Chinese consumer, with VW flagging enormous pressure, and Chinese competition the EU is trying to cap. Oil's bid is an energy shock showing up in China's PPI, not Chinese reflation, and EM Asia competes with Chinese exports rather than selling into Chinese demand.
