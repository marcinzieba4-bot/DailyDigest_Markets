🇨🇳 China  Mixed  → easing bias maintained

Macro Stance: Inflation has turned up, which narrows the room for rate cuts. August CPI rose 0.8% y/y and producer prices surged 3.8%, beating expectations (BigGo Finance, [Sep 8]), with the core gauge above forecast as energy costs jumped (BigGo Finance, [Sep 8]); CNBC says wholesale inflation topped estimates while consumer prices met forecast (CNBC, [Sep 8]). Reconciled: headline CPI was in line, PPI and core beat, against July's 0.5% and 3.5% (BigGo Finance, [Aug 8]). The PBOC keeps leaning on the currency instead: it set a weaker fix than the Reuters view, stoking depreciation expectations (VT Markets, [Sep 8]), OCBC says the fix slows the downside pace (Mitrade, [Sep 8]), forex reserves rose more than expected (Reuters, [Sep 7]) and the gold buying streak reached a 22nd month (Caixin Global, [Sep 8]).

PMIs & Activity: Every August survey improved. The official manufacturing PMI rebounded to 49.8 with new orders back in expansion (BigGo Finance, [Aug 30]), still a second month of contraction (CNBC, [Aug 30]); the private manufacturing PMI rose to 51.5 (BigGo Finance, [Aug 31]) and private services to 51.4 (BigGo Finance, [Sep 2]). Trade confirms the external leg: exports jumped 25% as the surplus widened (AP News, [Sep 7]), the surplus topped $800bn for the year (Nikkei Asia, [Sep 8]) and foreign trade rose 17.6% in eight months (China Daily, [Sep 8]), but imports missed estimates (CNBC, [Sep 7]). Domestic demand lags: car exports stayed strong while domestic sales declined more (Reuters, [Sep 8]), and July retail sales grew only 0.6% with industrial output up 4.5% (Reuters, [Aug 17]).

Credit Impulse: Banks are swapping loans for bonds: they are snapping up bonds to seek returns as the loan slump deepens (Bloomberg.com, [Sep 8]), after July new loans posted a record contraction (Reuters, [Aug 14]) even as credit growth exceeded forecasts (Bloomberg.com, [Aug 14]); reconciled, the aggregate holds up through bonds while the loan book shrinks. The 300 billion yuan of special treasury bonds to boost bank capital (State Council Information Office, [Sep 7]) is judged by The Economist to fall far short (The Economist, [Sep 8]) and by Breakingviews to achieve its least important aim (Reuters, [Sep 7]). China sold 50yr special sovereign bonds at an average yield of 2.2778% (Newsquawk, [Sep 8]). Capital adds lending capacity; it does not create borrowers.

Property: China is souring on a tool that helped property firms overhaul debt (Bloomberg.com, [Sep 8]), and Goldman sees the new presale rules cutting land sales by 30% (The Business Times, [Sep 7]); Guangdong held emergency talks with developers over off-plan sales rules (BigGo Finance, [Sep 7]) and SCMP argues the presale rules are still a source of risk (South China Morning Post, [Sep 7]). For buyers, 40-year mortgages have landed in Shanghai (BigGo Finance, [Sep 3]). The data are weak: July new home prices were stagnant (Reuters, [Aug 17]) and first-tier prices flat, ending a four-month rebound (South China Morning Post, [Aug 17]).

Fiscal, Policy & Trade: The export boom sharpens every front. The EU wants initial Chinese action on the trade gap by early October (Reuters, [Sep 8]) after 19 G20 finance ministers agreed 'cheap exports' are unsustainable, with China the dissenter (AP News, [Sep 2]); the NYT says surging exports loom over Trump's talks with Xi (The New York Times, [Sep 8]), and Japan protests new Chinese measures against its exports of a key chipmaking material (AP News, [Sep 8]). The US claims Chinese AI firms carry out 'industrial-scale' theft of trade secrets (CNN, [Sep 8]), rare-earth firms halted US shipments ahead of the summit (International Finance, [Sep 8]), yet Xi is set to bring top business leaders to Washington (Yahoo Finance, [Sep 7]). At home, Beijing deployed its first 2026 policy financing funds (Reuters, [Sep 3]).

Markets & Flows: The Hang Seng slipped 95 points as tech and insurers weighed while mining and oil stocks rallied (The Standard (HK), [Sep 8]), after bank and insurer shares slid on the injection plan (Nikkei Asia, [Sep 7]). Bonds are the haven: the Iran war and $40 trillion of debt made Chinese bonds look safe (Washington Examiner, [Sep 8]) and the US-China yield gap is at a record (Investing.com, [Sep 8]). Chinese banks are buying US Treasuries after wooing dollar deposits (Reuters, [Sep 6]), and ETFs are in focus as corporate profits jumped 26% in Q2 (Yahoo Finance, [Sep 8]).

New / Most Important: August inflation re-accelerated: PPI at 3.8% and core above forecast, with ING attributing the rebound to higher tech and energy prices (ing think, [Sep 8]) while China Daily reads it as sustained recovery in domestic demand (China Daily HK, [Sep 8]). The car data side with ING: domestic sales are falling, so this is cost and tech pricing, not household demand. Combined with a 25% export surge, it removes urgency for rate cuts and leaves easing to the fixing, bank capital and liquidity.

Playbook ✓: Easing bias maintained through an incremental stimulus drip rather than rates.  |  ⚠️ Divergence: China now combines rising factory-gate prices, a 25% export surge and soft imports, with loans contracting. The transmission is uneven: better new orders and an S&P Global reading of the fastest global growth in over two years (S&P Global, [Sep 8]) support iron ore demand expectations and the AUD at the margin, but higher Chinese export prices into a record surplus push both competition and imported inflation onto European exporters and the ECB, while weak domestic demand keeps China's imports soft for EM. WTI at $95.58 is a terms-of-trade tax that eats into the gains the PMIs imply.
