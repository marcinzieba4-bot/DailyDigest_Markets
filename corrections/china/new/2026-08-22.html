🇨🇳 China  Mixed  → easing bias

Macro Stance: Rates are on hold and the burden has shifted to the budget. China kept loan prime rates steady for a 15th month (Reuters, [Aug 19]), the 1-year at 3.0% and the 5-year at 3.5% (BigGo Finance, [Aug 19]), while expanding consumer and small-business loan subsidies (The Asian Banker, [Aug 21]); liquidity runs through the overnight window, with 7-day reverse repos at zero for seven straight sessions (BigGo Finance, [Aug 19]). The yuan surged to 6.72 per dollar, a 42-month high (BigGo Finance, [Aug 20]), even as the PBOC fixed at 6.7808, weaker than the previous fix (CryptoRank, [Aug 20]) and well above the 6.7262 Reuters estimate (investingLive, [Aug 20]), a deliberate brake after its move to slow yuan gains (Bloomberg, [Aug 19]). Prices leave room to ease: July CPI rose 0.5% and PPI growth slowed to 3.5%, both missing expectations (BigGo Finance, [Aug 8]).

PMIs & Activity: July's official manufacturing PMI fell to 49.2 and non-manufacturing to 49.0 (FXStreet, [Jul 31]); the private factory gauge eased to 50.9 (Fibre2Fashion, [Aug 3]) and private services growth was the slowest since September 2024 (Reuters, [Aug 4]). The hard data confirmed the slide: industrial output grew 4.5% y/y and retail sales just 0.6% (Reuters, [Aug 17]), and fixed-asset investment fell 6.7% in the first seven months (Xinhua, [Aug 17]); services spending is at least outpacing goods (Caixin Global, [Aug 21]). Exports remain the engine, jumping almost a quarter in July (Financial Times, [Aug 6]). The August flash PMIs abroad went the other way: euro-zone activity grew at the fastest pace since November (Reuters, [Aug 21]), the US composite improved to 56 (Kitco, [Aug 21]) and Japan's manufacturing PMI jumped to 55.1 on chip and AI orders (BigGo Finance, [Aug 20]), a cycle China supplies but does not share.

Credit Impulse: July new bank loans posted a record contraction as credit demand faltered (Reuters, [Aug 14]), only the second contraction this year (The Standard (HK), [Aug 14]), yet Bloomberg reports credit growth exceeded forecasts despite the rare loan slump ([Aug 14]). Reconciled: headline financing, which includes bond issuance, beat; bank lending to households and firms shrank, leaving cumulative yuan loans up 10.38 trillion yuan in January–July (Xinhua, [Aug 14]). The expanded subsidised-loan programme (Caixin Global, [Aug 21]) targets that gap by cutting the effective cost of SME and consumer borrowing without touching the LPR; whether it revives demand is the August credit print's question.

Property: July new home prices were stagnant as demand stayed weak (Reuters, [Aug 17]) and first-tier prices were flat, ending a four-month rebound (South China Morning Post, [Aug 17]); the 70-city index fell 3.2% y/y, the smallest drop in five months, while property investment slumped nearly 20% (BigGo Finance, [Aug 17]). A flood of discounted auction homes is deepening the slump (Asia Times, [Aug 21]). Policy is granular: Shanghai started a new round of easing (Bloomberg, [Aug 19]) and is lowering second-home down payments (South China Morning Post, [Aug 20]), and housing provident fund rules are being reshaped (China.org.cn, [Aug 21]). A court accepted Evergrande's liquidation a day after its founder's life sentence (South China Morning Post, [Aug 21]), with creditors still owed $300 billion (Fortune, [Aug 21]).

Fiscal, Policy & Trade: China plans new fiscal support policies amid the growth slowdown (Bloomberg, [Aug 21]) and new measures to boost domestic demand in the second half (Global Times, [Aug 21]), and unveiled steps to boost weak consumption (South China Morning Post, [Aug 21]) after pledging timely fiscal support (Reuters, [Aug 20]) and raising the SME interest-subsidy loan cap to 75 million yuan (BigGo Finance, [Aug 20]). Revenue gives some room, with public budget revenue up 5.8% in the first seven months (China Daily, [Aug 21]). Trade is fragmenting: Beijing rejected a US tariff report as Mexico plans new China curbs (South China Morning Post, [Aug 21]), slammed EU foreign-subsidy rules and linked them to trade talks (POLITICO.eu, [Aug 21]), and struck a Swiss deal scrapping most levies (Bloomberg, [Aug 20]); India is leaving the door ajar for Chinese investors.

Markets & Flows: The Hang Seng rose 1.21% to reclaim 26,000 (Moomoo, [Aug 21]), its fifth straight gain (Dimsum Daily, [Aug 21]), and A-shares closed higher as precious metals led (BigGo Finance, [Aug 21]) while investors await fiscal support (The Economic Times, [Aug 21]). Flows are mixed: mainland capital pulled a net HK$10.4 billion from Hong Kong stocks (BigGo Finance, [Aug 20]), materials names are emerging as market leaders (Bloomberg, [Aug 20]), and China trimmed US Treasury holdings as the global reserve shift gathers pace (Firstpost, [Aug 18]).

New / Most Important: Fiscal policy is now explicitly the lead instrument. New support policies, a second-half demand package and an expanded subsidised-loan scheme follow an LPR hold, so the mix is budget-funded and aimed at consumers and SMEs, with no rate cut and a yuan held back by weaker fixings. Evergrande's liquidation closes the legal chapter of the bust, not the economic one.

Playbook ✓: Rates on hold, targeted fiscal support, city-level property easing and a managed yuan are on script.  |  ⚠️ Divergence: The August flash PMIs show the US and euro-area cycle accelerating without China, whose own July data missed and whose loans contracted. That gap decides transmission: iron ore rose only as China demand concerns met stimulus hopes (TradingView, [Aug 18]), so AUD, at a two-month high on US Treasury buybacks (Moomoo, [Aug 19]) rather than Chinese demand, is exposed if the fiscal package underwhelms. European industry feels the "China shock" (Axios, [Aug 21]), with a German state politician calling for EU tariffs on Chinese hybrids (Reuters, [Aug 21]), and Iranian oil offers to Chinese buyers fell as the US blockade bites (Reuters, [Aug 21]), raising China's import bill with WTI at 87.06.
