🇨🇳 China  Mixed  → easing bias, but incremental

Macro Stance: China remains in a different monetary regime from the rest of the world, easing while the West tightens: it rolls out cash for Golden Week as other economies tighten (bloomingbit, [Oct 1]), after the PBOC cut the PSL rate by 0.25 percentage point to 1.5% (Forex Factory, [Sep 29]) and boosted relending quotas by 700 billion yuan (BigGo Finance, [Sep 29]). The easing is incremental and structural, not a benchmark cut, and it is not weakening the currency despite a 5.28% US 10Y: the yuan fixing was set at a 3.5-year high before the National Day holiday (The Standard (HK), [Sep 30]), with China's 10Y yield at an over one-year low (TradingView, [Sep 29]). August CPI rose 0.8% and PPI 3.8% (BigGo Finance, [Sep 8]), lifted by rising energy costs (Reuters, [Sep 9]).

PMIs & Activity: The official manufacturing PMI returned to growth at 50.1 with non-manufacturing at 50.2 (investingLive, [Sep 29]), and the private RatingDog gauges printed 52.1 and 51.6 (investingLive, [Sep 30]); the 'beat' headlines (TradingView, [Sep 29]) and an official print exactly at the 50.1 consensus (investingLive, [Sep 29]) reconcile as an in-line official survey and stronger private ones. China Daily says policy support buoys the full-year growth outlook (China Daily Global Edition, [Oct 2]), but the driver is the AI boom (Nikkei Asia, [Sep 30]) and August exports up 25% (AP News, [Sep 7]). Consumption is the weak leg: August retail sales rose 0.4% (investingLive, [Sep 14]), peak-season auto sales growth disappointed (WSJ, [Oct 2]), Nike's China sales plunged 26% (Retail Gazette, [Oct 2]) and August industrial profit growth was the weakest since November (Bloomberg, [Sep 27]).

Credit Impulse: August bank lending disappointed (Reuters, [Sep 14]) and credit expansion missed forecasts (Bloomberg, [Sep 14]), with bond and equity financing exceeding loans for the first time (BigGo Finance, [Sep 14]): headline financing and loans agree that private demand is weak and government issuance carries the aggregate. The package targets credit rather than igniting it: cheap loans help state firms pay overdue bills (Bloomberg, [Sep 29]) and banks are urged to finance AI and asset-light service firms (Caixin Global, [Sep 29]), while households keep deleveraging as the bad consumer debt cleanup falters (The Business Times, [Sep 29]).

Property: The first central mortgage subsidy, 1 percentage point a year and up to nearly 50,000 yuan on a 1 million yuan loan (BigGo Finance, [Sep 29]), arrives with Beijing's September new-home sales up nearly 50% month-on-month (BigGo Finance, [Oct 1]) and the top 100 developers at 2.26 trillion yuan of January-September sales (BigGo Finance, [Oct 1]). But Beijing is also shortening the leash on developers (Nikkei Asia, [Oct 2]), a Vanke loss underscores the stress (Traders Union, [Oct 1]), and August home prices fell across 70 cities with property investment down 19.9% in eight months (BigGo Finance, [Sep 14]). ThinkChina's summary fits: to stabilise, not stimulate (ThinkChina, [Oct 2]).

Fiscal, Policy & Trade: The cabinet pledged stronger support to meet the growth target (Reuters, [Sep 28]), with fourth-quarter local bond issuance likely around 500 billion yuan (BigGo Finance, [Sep 28]); SCMP sees Beijing doing 'just enough' with targeted fiscal support to defend GDP growth (South China Morning Post, [Oct 2]) and BofA sees more aggressive stimulus as unlikely for now (Yahoo Finance, [Oct 1]). The tariff narrative stays live: Greer says no specific timeline is set for enforcing the US-China Board of Trade (South China Morning Post, [Oct 2]), PIIE finds diverging trade data pointing to tariff evasion (Peterson Institute for International Economics, [Oct 2]), and a Commission official says China drives a worrying rise in EU imports (Reuters, [Oct 1]).

Markets & Flows: Hong Kong, closed for National Day on Oct 1 (BBN Times, [Oct 1]), reopened into a 3% plunge as the stimulus failed to impress (Moneycontrol.com, [Oct 2]), Asia's worst (NewsBytes, [Oct 2]), with surging bond yields and higher oil weighing (Free Malaysia Today, [Oct 2]). Mainland stocks had already limped to a quarterly drop (TradingView, [Sep 30]) after a 13-month low (South China Morning Post, [Sep 28]). The yuan is the exception, set for a seventh straight quarterly gain as exporters sell dollars (Business Recorder, [Sep 30]).

New / Most Important: Hong Kong's reopening delivered the first full market verdict on the package, and it was a 3% plunge, Asia's worst, after Beijing's stimulus disappointed. The commentary has converged on the same reading: 'just enough' to defend GDP growth, to stabilise, not stimulate. A PMI back at 50.1 and a first mortgage subsidy keep the downside contained, but with developers on a shorter leash and consumption soft, incremental easing is a floor, not a re-acceleration.

Playbook ✓: Stimulus announced but insufficient, property still declining, consumption soft: nothing this week disrupts that view, and surging US and European yields still pull portfolio money out of Chinese equities, as Hong Kong's slump shows.  |  ⚠️ Divergence: The traditional "China stimulus → commodity reflation → EM/AUD rally" chain is broken at the property link. In prior cycles PBOC easing flowed through credit into property, infrastructure and commodity imports, lifting iron ore and the AUD; this cycle bank loans miss, property investment is down 19.9% and households will not re-lever, so the impulse is an export- and AI-led PMI that helps Asian tech supply chains rather than resource exporters. That is why the AUD is not benefiting despite the RBA hiking aggressively: Australia imports tighter financial conditions while its main customer stabilises rather than reflates. European exporters face a consumer at 0.4% retail growth and an EU trade front that is hardening. Commodity-linked assets in Chile, Peru, Brazil and Australia cannot rely on the China put that backstopped them in 2009, 2016 and 2020 until property stabilises, which requires household confidence, a social variable, not a monetary one.
