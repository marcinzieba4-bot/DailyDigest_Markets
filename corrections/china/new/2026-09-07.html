🇨🇳 China  Mixed  → easing bias

Macro Stance: The PBOC is leaning against yuan strength rather than cutting rates. China reins in a rising yuan as weak domestic demand clouds the outlook (Reuters, [Aug 31]), yet the currency broke 6.72 to a three-and-a-half-year high (BigGo Finance, [Sep 3]) before the fixing was set at 6.7787 vs 6.7807 previous (TMGM, [Sep 4]); Chinese banks are buying US Treasuries after wooing dollar deposits (Reuters, [Sep 6]), a quiet brake on appreciation. The central bank bought a net 50 billion yuan of sovereign bonds in August (TradingView, [Sep 2]). July CPI slowed to 0.5%, more than expected (BigGo Finance, [Aug 9]), and PPI growth to 3.5%, missing expectations (BigGo Finance, [Aug 8]): price pressure is too weak to justify a strong currency.

PMIs & Activity: Every August survey improved. The official manufacturing PMI rebounded to 49.8 with new orders back in expansion (BigGo Finance, [Aug 30]), still a second month of contraction (CNBC, [Aug 30]); the private manufacturing PMI rose to 51.5 (BigGo Finance, [Aug 31]) and private services to 51.4 (BigGo Finance, [Sep 2]). Reconciled: the official gauge is below 50, the private surveys well above it, and Natixis calls it stabilisation with an uneven recovery (CNBC, [Sep 1]). Hard data lag: July industrial output grew 4.5% y/y and retail sales only 0.6% (Reuters, [Aug 17]), fixed-asset investment fell 6.7% in seven months (Xinhua, [Aug 17]) and profit growth was the slowest in seven months (CNBC, [Aug 26]). A Reuters poll sees August export growth accelerating (Reuters, [Sep 6]), with China data on this week's calendar (Forex Factory, [Sep 6]).

Credit Impulse: Credit is being repaired from the top. China will pump $54 billion into state banks and insurers (Reuters, [Sep 6]), with ICBC and ABC raising a combined CNY 260 billion (BigGo Finance, [Sep 6]). Demand is the problem: July new loans posted a record contraction (Reuters, [Aug 14]) even as credit growth exceeded forecasts (Bloomberg.com, [Aug 14]); reconciled, the aggregate holds up while the loan book shrinks. S&P Global Ratings warns of risks from China's generous credit ratings (Reuters, [Sep 3]). Capital adds lending capacity; it does not create borrowers.

Property: China is calling time on the home-sales system that worsened its meltdown (Bloomberg.com, [Sep 1]); property stocks fell as the rules upended the presale funding playbook (Reuters, [Aug 31]) and Goldman sees a 30% hit to land sales (Bloomberg.com, [Sep 6]). For buyers, 40-year mortgages have landed in Shanghai (BigGo Finance, [Sep 3]). The data are weak: July new home prices were stagnant (Reuters, [Aug 17]) and first-tier prices flat, ending a four-month rebound (South China Morning Post, [Aug 17]). Longfor says it can repay loans due this month (The Standard (HK), [Sep 3]).

Fiscal, Policy & Trade: The G20 left China isolated: Bessent said 19 finance ministers agreed 'cheap exports' are unsustainable, with China the dissenter (AP News, [Sep 2]), and Beijing accused the group of promoting protectionism (CNBC, [Sep 3]). Šefčovič says China must deliver by October or face harsher measures (Euronews.com, [Sep 2]) and Germany's SPD backs EU tariffs on Chinese hybrids (Bloomberg.com, [Sep 3]). Washington mixes pressure and deals: some Chinese rare-earth firms halted US shipments (Reuters, [Sep 4]), yet the two sides debate 'nonsensitive' tariff cuts ahead of the Trump-Xi summit (Nikkei Asia, [Sep 2]). At home, Beijing will speed up bond issuance (South China Morning Post, [Aug 31]) and deployed its first 2026 policy financing funds (Reuters, [Sep 3]).

Markets & Flows: China escaped the global bond rout for the wrong reasons (Reuters, [Sep 2]); the China-US yield disparity hit a record (Nikkei Asia, [Sep 4]). Equities ignore earnings: profits rose 25.7% while the CSI 300 fell 9% (The Next Web, [Sep 5]) and Morgan Stanley cut its index targets (BigGo Finance, [Sep 4]), though investors are turning to Chinese stocks as an alternative to crowded AI trades (Yahoo Finance, [Sep 5]). Alibaba declined on macro and competitive headwinds; the central bank extended its gold-buying spree (FXStreet, [Sep 6]).

New / Most Important: The $54 billion recapitalisation, framed by The Guardian as a £40bn stimulus amid fears over sluggish growth (The Guardian, [Sep 6]), is the week's clearest policy move. Beijing is pivoting from broad demand-side measures toward directly strengthening financial intermediaries, closer to the 2008-09 Western playbook than to infrastructure-led stimulus, which suggests balance-sheet stress at lenders is more acute than official data imply. It lands after a month of better surveys: the supply of credit is being repaired as demand shows its first signs of life, and the test is whether lending, not just capital ratios, responds.

Playbook ✓: Sluggish growth → stimulus is playing out, through bank capital, liquidity and the fixing rather than rates; Alibaba declining on macro and competitive headwinds is consensus.  |  ⚠️ Divergence: Rebounding oil imports and private PMIs above 50 point to a stabilising real economy while Beijing recapitalises banks into a record loan contraction. Better new orders support iron ore demand expectations and the AUD at the margin, but the improvement is export-led and meets a G20 line-up against cheap exports, so European exporters face more Chinese competition and tougher EU lines, and weak loan demand keeps China's import demand soft for EM. WTI at $91.48 is a terms-of-trade tax that eats into the gains the PMIs imply.
