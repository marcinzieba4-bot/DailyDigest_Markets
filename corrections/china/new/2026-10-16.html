🇨🇳 China  Mixed  → easing bias, but constrained

Macro Stance: The PBOC is easing through quantities, not price: a 700 billion yuan outright reverse repo left an October net injection of 400 billion yuan (BigGo Finance, [Oct 14]), and Governor Pan says policy stays moderately loose and imbalances cannot be fixed by exchange rates (BigGo Finance, [Oct 15]). The constraint is no longer the currency: the yuan holds near a 3-1/2-year high (Business Recorder, [Oct 12]), and the fix, firmer through the week at 6.7253 (FXStreet, [Oct 15]), stays well on the weak side of the 6.6977 estimate (investingLive, [Oct 15]), managed appreciation. Prices are the limit: September CPI rose 0.9% against 1% expected (investingLive, [Oct 14]) while factory inflation hit 5%, the fastest in over four years (investingLive, [Oct 14]), jolted by oil (Bloomberg, [Oct 14]). That is cost-push, and with US 10Y yields at 5.13% it keeps benchmark rates on hold.

PMIs & Activity: September's official manufacturing PMI returned to growth at 50.1 with non-manufacturing at 50.2 (investingLive, [Sep 29]), and RatingDog manufacturing leapt to 52.1 (FXStreet, [Sep 30]). Trade shows where that comes from: exports surged 36.3% y/y and imports rose 31% (Investing.com, [Oct 14]) on AI-related shipments (Financial Times, [Oct 14]), with the January-September surplus at $938 billion (ABC News, [Oct 14]). Domestic demand runs at the other speed: August retail sales grew just 0.4% (investingLive, [Sep 14]) and September passenger-car retail sales dropped 24% (Bloomberg, [Oct 10]). Reuters sees Q3 growth set to pick up on stimulus (Reuters, [Oct 15]), while an AFP poll sees the target missed (Digital Journal, [Oct 15]) and the IMF has cut its 2026 projection (South China Morning Post, [Oct 13]).

Credit Impulse: September new bank loans jumped to 1.08 trillion yuan, above forecast (TradingView, [Oct 14]), on stimulus and a seasonal boost (Reuters, [Oct 14]). The composition reconciles that beat with the deeper slump in credit demand investingLive sees beneath it (investingLive, [Oct 14]): household credit contracted by over 1 trillion yuan in the first three quarters (BigGo Finance, [Oct 14]), and The Economist calls falling consumer credit another warning sign (The Economist, [Oct 14]). After August's miss (Reuters, [Sep 14]), this is a month carried by stimulus and the quarter-end; the impulse is not reaching households.

Property: Golden Week gave the mortgage subsidy its first test: Shanghai new-home sales doubled and secondhand transactions in 11 cities jumped over 40% (BigGo Finance, [Oct 9]). The quality is contested: a report calls the resale-led rebound a hollow narrative (IANS LIVE, [Oct 12]) and the SCMP argues cheap debt is not enough to get buyers spending (South China Morning Post, [Oct 12]). Supply is being re-engineered: Shenzhen raised the pre-sale bar to structural topping-out (BigGo Finance, [Oct 15]) and first-tier land auctions cooled sharply in October (BigGo Finance, [Oct 13]). The base is still August: home prices fell across 70 cities (BigGo Finance, [Sep 14]) and property investment dropped 19.9% in eight months (Business Recorder, [Sep 15]).

Fiscal, Policy & Trade: Fiscal support is quota-based: local governments may tap 550 billion yuan in unused debt quotas (Caixin Global, [Oct 9]). The debt stock is the ceiling: the FT says the burden limits stimulus space (Financial Times, [Oct 10]), and the IMF urges bigger fiscal stimulus (Reuters, [Oct 15]), warning the effort lacks ambition and leaves the yuan undervalued (Bloomberg, [Oct 15]). The Financial Street Forum opens next week, with regulators set to unveil reform policies (BigGo Finance, [Oct 12]). On trade, the EU caps Chinese hybrid imports at 350,000 a year (The Guardian, [Oct 12]) but is assessing more sectors for quotas (Bloomberg, [Oct 15]); the US and China released $60 billion product lists for tariff cuts (Agri News, [Oct 11]).

Markets & Flows: Equities are not buying the data. China stocks closed at a 14-month low as US inflation stoked Fed fears (marketscreener.com, [Oct 15]), after a one-year low as the AI hardware rout deepened (The Economic Times, [Oct 14]), and selling spread to Hong Kong (24/7 Wall St., [Oct 15]). The CSRC held a symposium after the CSI 300 fell 12% in Q3 (IndexBox, [Oct 12]). Bonds price the weak domestic side: long-dated yields hit a 15-month low as the PBOC keeps cash flowing (TradingView, [Oct 15]).

New / Most Important: The September trade, price and credit data land on one verdict. Exports up 36.3%, loans of 1.08 trillion yuan and PPI at 5% read like reflation, but each is narrow: AI hardware exports, a stimulus and seasonal loan beat with households deleveraging, and an oil-driven PPI while CPI undershot. The IMF's call for more fiscal stimulus and stocks at a 14-month low show the market reading it the same way: a two-speed economy managed with liquidity and quotas, not demand support.

Playbook ✓: Booming exports beside weak households, a PBOC easing quantities rather than rates, incremental quotas and EU pushback on the surplus all follow the script; Indonesia's index-exclusion slump shows how fragile Asia-Pacific flows remain.  |  ⚠️ Divergence: Transmission to global assets is splitting by channel. Asian tech supply chains get the export boom, South Korea's factory growth at a 4-month high on export orders (Reuters, [Sep 30]). Iron ore and the AUD need property and construction, and that channel is not turning: investment is down 19.9%, land auctions are cooling and Pilbara miners face China pressure and job cuts (Proactive Investors Australia, [Oct 15]), consistent with the Aussie at its lowest since July. European exporters face both sides of the surplus: a weak Chinese consumer, with VW deliveries posting their steepest drop in more than four years (Automotive News, [Oct 12]), and Chinese competition the EU is trying to cap. Oil's bid is an energy shock showing up in China's PPI, not Chinese reflation, and EM Asia competes with Chinese exports rather than selling into Chinese demand.
