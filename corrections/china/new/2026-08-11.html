🇨🇳 China  Mixed  → easing bias

Macro Stance: The PBOC set out its long game. Its five-year reform plan vows to expand the yuan's international use (Reuters, [Aug 10]), and the central bank ruled out yuan devaluation while pledging FX transparency (FXStreet, [Aug 10]); Deutsche Bank was named clearing bank for the renminbi (Reuters, [Aug 10]), the yuan clearing stamp that makes Frankfurt part of the offshore plumbing. Analysts warn the yuan's recent calm may end on seasonal factors (Bloomberg, [Aug 10]). Short-term liquidity was left alone, with zero volume in the 7-day reverse repo (Newsquawk, [Aug 10]). Prices argue for easing: July CPI rose 0.5% (CGTN, [Aug 9]) and PPI 3.5% (China Daily, [Aug 9]), with Caixin noting inflation cooled faster than expected on weak demand ([Aug 10]), and China Daily writes that interest rate and RRR cuts may be on the table ([Aug 4]). The PBOC also made its largest gold purchase since 2023 (Caixin Global, [Aug 8]).

PMIs & Activity: The official manufacturing PMI fell to 49.2 and the non-manufacturing PMI to 49.0 (FXStreet, [Jul 31]); the private factory index eased to 50.9 (Fibre2Fashion, [Aug 3]), a 4-month low (Reuters, [Aug 2]). Exports are the exception: they surged 23.9% and the surplus widened to $112.5B (Seeking Alpha, [Aug 7]), growth easing from June's 27% (Yahoo Finance UK, [Jul 13]) while the merchandise surplus remains elevated (FocusEconomics, [Aug 10]). Domestic demand is the problem. Pou Sheng's China retail business dug a deeper hole in July (SGB Media, [Aug 10]), Chevrolet is exiting China's domestic retail market (BigGo Finance, [Aug 10]) and Q2 GDP growth of 4.3% fell below the target range (Bloomberg, [Jul 15]). Seeking Alpha says the slowdown is getting harder to ignore ([Aug 10]).

Credit Impulse: July credit is still to come and the previews conflict. A Reuters poll sees bank lending set to plummet on subdued demand and a seasonal slowdown ([Aug 7]), while Caixin expects loans to rebound on a low base ([Aug 7]): a sharp drop from June's seasonal peak that still compares well with a weak July last year. VT Markets reads July credit demand as weak ([Aug 8]), and International Banker frames China's banking problem as one of unwanted credit ([Aug 10]). June set the tone, with new loans rising less than expected (The Star, [Jul 15]) and household loans down 366.8 billion yuan in the first half (36Kr, [Jul 17]). Supply is not the constraint: the panda bond market is surging even as Goldman Sachs flags limits to yuan expansion (South China Morning Post, [Aug 10]).

Property: Beijing eased home-buying curbs to boost the market (Bloomberg, [Aug 10]), following Shanghai (Bloomberg, [Aug 7]). The data explain why: housing prices fell 0.5% in July as sales volume weakened (Investing.com, [Aug 3]), the top-100 developer sales slump deepened (Trivium China, [Aug 5]) and a key steel price hit a near-decade low on the construction slump (Bloomberg, [Aug 6]), after property investment slumped 18 percent y/y in January–June (The Standard, [Jul 15]). China Cinda warns of a first-half profit drop as the slump weighs (Caixin Global, [Aug 5]), and Hong Kong's retail property market is weak (South China Morning Post, [Aug 10]).

Fiscal, Policy & Trade: Fiscal capacity exists, with provinces reporting H1 revenue growth above expectations (BigGo Finance, [Aug 6]) and the finance ministry issuing 6 billion yuan of sovereign bonds in Macao (Global Times, [Aug 10]). The 20% offshore tax on the super-rich (South China Morning Post, [Aug 10]) is already having an impact on flows (Family Wealth Report, [Aug 10]). Trade is two-way. China imposed anti-dumping tariffs of up to 54.3% on US and Mexican pecans (Global Times, [Aug 10]) while accelerating US soybean buying ahead of the Trump-Xi summit (AgroLatam, [Aug 10]); US tariff refunds are flowing back to Chinese firms (South China Morning Post, [Aug 10]), but Washington suspended de minimis treatment for Chinese exports (Legis1, [Aug 10]) and the Senate bill puts China at risk of 100% tariffs (Fibre2Fashion, [Aug 9]).

Markets & Flows: Beijing is leaning against the AI frenzy. China clamped down on margin debt after the AI stock frenzy (Bloomberg, [Aug 10]), brokers are tightening client scrutiny (The Edge Malaysia, [Aug 10]) and the "national team" is battling the AI stock rollercoaster (Nikkei Asia, [Aug 10]). Shanghai and Hong Kong stocks rose as consumer gains offset tech weakness (The Economic Times, [Aug 10]), after the HSI ended last week 0.84% lower (BusinessToday Malaysia, [Aug 9]); the Hang Seng Tech index is set for an overhaul with more firms and an AI focus (Bloomberg, [Aug 10]). Korea's market missing "long money", per the Korea Times, says regional flows remain selective.

New / Most Important: The PBOC's five-year plan, the devaluation pledge and Deutsche Bank's clearing role in Frankfurt all point one way: the yuan is a strategic asset, not an export-support lever. With the yuan near its strongest in years, July inflation cooling on weak demand and loans expected to plummet, the easing has to come through rates, RRR and fiscal, while Beijing reins in margin debt so that it does not inflate an equity bubble instead.

Playbook ✓: A stimulus drip, managed property decline, yuan internationalisation and calibrated market cooling are on script.  |  ⚠️ Divergence: A yuan held firm by policy while domestic demand weakens tightens conditions for exporters and means less import demand for the rest of Asia. Iron ore at a year low (Tech Times, [Aug 4]) and steel at a near-decade low say Australia's China channel is shifting from bulk to critical minerals, where the US is now backing local miners (Nikkei Asia, [Aug 10]), so AUD strength rests on the RBA. Germany's trade deficit with China keeps growing (Reuters, [Aug 9]), a drag for European exporters, and oil at $83.13 is a terms-of-trade tax on factories already below 50.
