🇨🇳 China  Mixed  → easing bias, constrained execution

Macro Stance: The PBOC's easing runs through liquidity rather than rates: a 1.2 trillion yuan outright reverse repo (巴士的報, [Oct 8]) and a net 100 billion yuan of sovereign bond purchases in September (TradingView, [Oct 9]). With US 10Y yields at 5.24%, a benchmark cut stays off the table, but capital flight is not the constraint the old playbook implies: the fix firmed again to 6.7298 (www.tmgm.com, [Oct 13]), the yuan holds near a three-year high despite a strong dollar as PBOC fixing supports it (The Economic Times, [Oct 13]), and Goldman says it is undervalued by up to 25% (investingLive, [Oct 13]). The PBOC also approved Visa to prepare a domestic card-clearing business (Reuters, [Oct 13]). Prices are firm: August CPI rose 0.8% (Xinhua, [Sep 9]) and PPI 3.8% (Xinhua, [Sep 9]), and September inflation is on today's schedule (BigGo Finance, [Oct 13]).

PMIs & Activity: September's official manufacturing PMI returned to growth at 50.1, exactly at consensus and up from 49.8 (investingLive, [Sep 29]), with non-manufacturing at 50.2 (investingLive, [Sep 29]); the private RatingDog gauges printed 52.1 and 51.6 (investingLive, [Sep 30]). Industrial profit growth slowed to 4.2% in August (Briefs Finance, [Sep 27]) and retail sales rose 0.4% against 0.6% expected (investingLive, [Sep 14]). September autos show the two-speed economy, with passenger-vehicle exports up 64% as retail sales drop (TOKENPOST, [Oct 12]). The IMF sees growth at 4.5% in 2026 (Breakingthenews.net, [Oct 13]) after revising its projection down and cautioning on export reliance (South China Morning Post, [Oct 13]); Nikkei puts third-quarter growth at 4.4% (Nikkei Asia, [Oct 6]), and GDP is in view this week (Moomoo, [Oct 13]).

Credit Impulse: August is still the latest print, and it missed on both measures: bank lending disappointed as credit demand stayed weak (Reuters, [Sep 14]) and total credit expansion missed forecasts (Bloomberg, [Sep 14]), with bond and equity financing exceeding loans in social financing for the first time (BigGo Finance, [Sep 14]); government issuance held up while private loan demand sagged. Credit is being subsidised rather than ignited, with 100 billion yuan of interest subsidies leveraging 17 trillion yuan of consumer credit (BigGo Finance, [Oct 9]). Until a September print shows private loans turning, the impulse reads as state-led and narrow.

Property: Holiday transactions rebounded on the mortgage subsidy, with new-home subscriptions in 50 key cities up over 15% year on year (Moomoo, [Oct 8]) and Shanghai new-home sales doubling (BigGo Finance, [Oct 9]). Supply is adjusting the other way: first-tier land auctions cooled sharply in October as ready-home sales rules force developers to rework their land math (BigGo Finance, [Oct 13]). China Jinmao posted 84.6 billion yuan of contracted sales for nine months (TipRanks, [Oct 13]), while Vanke reported a 16.02 billion yuan loss (ad-hoc-news.de, [Oct 7]) and PAG is in exclusive talks with liquidators for an Evergrande unit (Bloomberg, [Oct 9]). The August base: prices down across 70 cities (BigGo Finance, [Sep 14]) and investment down 19.9% in eight months (Business Recorder, [Sep 15]).

Fiscal, Policy & Trade: Execution is constrained by cost: local governments may tap 550 billion yuan of unused quotas (Caixin Global, [Oct 9]) and the NDRC pledges more tools (Bloomberg, [Oct 10]), but debt interest has surged to 1.4 trillion yuan, squeezing stimulus space (BigGo Finance, [Oct 12]). Trade is the swing factor this week: exports are seen growing at a faster pace in September (Reuters, [Oct 13]), projected at 25.5% (BigGo Finance, [Oct 13]), with the surplus nearing a record as pressure mounts from partners (Chronicle-Tribune, [Oct 13]). Europe is already acting, capping hybrid imports at 350,000 a year (The Guardian, [Oct 12]) and eyeing December trade initiatives (Reuters, [Oct 12]).

Markets & Flows: Blue chips fell to a 14-month low on the tech selloff (Business Recorder, [Oct 13]) after Beijing moved to steady the A-share market (South China Morning Post, [Oct 12]), and regulators will curb futures quant trading to contain risks amid the slump (South China Morning Post, [Oct 13]). Offshore investors stay sidelined, while mainland traders keep piling into Hong Kong stocks (South China Morning Post, [Oct 13]).

New / Most Important: The policy signal is not silence but triage: Beijing is defending the equity market with trading curbs and stability operations while the IMF tells it to shift away from exports, just as consensus expects September exports to accelerate to 25.5%. Today's inflation print and this week's GDP decide whether the PMI rebound is spreading into prices and incomes or staying inside the export sector.

Playbook ✓: An easing bias capped by the US rate gap, property as a drag on household confidence and local balance sheets, and Chinese EV competition pressuring European automakers are consensus.  |  ⚠️ Divergence: China is shifting from a property-driven growth model to an export- and tech-led one, and the transmission map shifts with it. A factory PMI above 50 built on AI hardware lifts Asian tech supply chains, while the housing bounce reaches transactions but not land purchases or the 19.9% investment slump, so construction-linked demand for iron ore, and the AUD that rides it, gets little. European exporters face Chinese autos redirected toward them and a Chinese consumer that hit LVMH (Reuters, [Oct 12]). EM manufacturers compete with a surplus nearing a record. Investable China is in purgatory until credit or property gives it a floor.
