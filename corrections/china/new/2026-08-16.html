🇨🇳 China  Bearish  → easing bias

Macro Stance: The PBOC is easing through the plumbing while signalling no broad stimulus. Its first mid-month overnight reverse repo boosted bonds (The Edge Singapore, [Aug 14]) and liquidity management now focuses on the overnight rate (The Standard (HK), [Aug 13]); the 10-year yield hit an over 1-year low (TradingView, [Aug 13]). China Daily frames the stance as tailored easing favoured over broad stimulus (China Daily, [Aug 14]), in line with the Q2 report's pledge of a timely new policy rollout (Reuters, [Aug 12]). The yuan headed for a 7th weekly gain on strong fixing and waning US rate hike hopes (The Standard (HK), [Aug 14]), and the five-year plan rules out devaluation (FXStreet, [Aug 10]). July CPI rose 0.5% (CGTN, [Aug 9]), a six-month low (TradingView, [Aug 8]), with PPI up 3.5% (China Daily, [Aug 9]).

PMIs & Activity: July's official PMIs both sit below 50, manufacturing at 49.2 and non-manufacturing at 49.0 (FXStreet, [Jul 31]). The private manufacturing PMI eased to 50.9 (Fibre2Fashion, [Aug 3]), a 4-month low (Reuters, [Aug 2]), and services growth hit its slowest since September 2024 (Reuters, [Aug 4]). Exports rose 23.9% as high-tech shipments strengthened (The Asian Banker, [Aug 14]), but the high-tech boom is seen failing to halt the slowdown (Bloomberg, [Aug 13]). Chinese car sales are booming almost everywhere except at home (Reuters, [Aug 14]), with NEV retail down 17% in the first week of August (CnEVPost, [Aug 13]), and Goldman sees retail sales growth staying weak (Investing.com, [Aug 14]). Weather is now a drag too: Xi called for better disaster prevention as floods and landslides batter China (The Straits Times, [Aug 15]).

Credit Impulse: July confirms the demand problem and splits the headline. New bank loans posted a record contraction as credit demand faltered (Reuters, [Aug 14]) and shrank as borrowing demand stays weak (Caixin Global, [Aug 15]), yet credit growth exceeded forecasts despite the rare loan slump (Bloomberg, [Aug 14]). Reconciled: aggregate financing was carried by channels outside bank lending while the loan book itself shrank; cumulative yuan loans still rose 10.38 trln yuan in January–July (Xinhua, [Aug 14]). Price is falling faster than volume: corporate loan rates fell below 3% as the PBOC signals a shift from volume to price (finance.biggo.com, [Aug 14]), and banks are testing repo-linked corporate loans (South China Morning Post, [Aug 14]). Cheaper credit with fewer borrowers is the textbook liquidity-trap signature, and the supply side is consolidating as China cuts 670 rural banks in an accelerating financial sector overhaul (Caixin Global, [Aug 15]).

Property: Easing continues at the city level: top cities are relaxing rules for home purchases (chinadailyhk, [Aug 12]). Prices have not responded: a private survey shows housing prices fell 0.5% in July as sales volume weakened (Investing.com, [Aug 3]), and real home prices sank to their lowest level in 21 years (the deep dive, [Aug 3]). The official June data showed new home prices falling at a slower pace (Reuters, [Jul 14]) but property investment down 18 percent y/y in January–June (The Standard (HK), [Jul 15]). Expiring land leases are a $148 billion property threat that China is racing to solve (Bloomberg, [Aug 11]). The official July price survey is still to come.

Fiscal, Policy & Trade: Fiscal stress shows up in collection: Breakingviews says China's emboldened taxman is just getting started (Reuters, [Aug 14]) and social security checks are tightening as fiscal pressures mount (ANI News, [Aug 14]), though Beijing is said to be moving to clarify tax rules that confused the ultra-wealthy (CNBC, [Aug 15]). On trade, Beijing rejected Washington's "tariff evasion" accusations (China Daily, [Aug 15]) after the US accused dozens of countries of helping China dodge tariffs (BBC, [Aug 14]) and imposed 100% tariffs on some drones (The Japan Times, [Aug 14]); an ECB blog warns China's aid for key sectors can feed trade tensions (Bloomberg, [Aug 14]), and a US-China "Board of Investment" has stalled before the expected Trump-Xi meeting (South China Morning Post, [Aug 13]). China's US deal is gaining soybean traction (World Grain, [Aug 14]).

Markets & Flows: Chinese stocks ended the week flat with attention on the Unitree debut (citynewsservice.cn, [Aug 15]), an IPO that drew 8,000x demand while internet names underwhelmed (Growth Dragons, [Aug 15]); liquidity tightened ahead of the listing (The Economic Times, [Aug 14]) and JD.com slid after a rare revenue plunge (Financial Times, [Aug 13]). The safe-asset bid sits in bonds and gold, with the World Gold Council reporting strong official sector buying in July (World Gold Council, [Aug 14]).

New / Most Important: The policy response to the July loan contraction is structural, not cyclical. Cutting 670 rural banks and clarifying tax rules for the wealthy address balance-sheet hygiene and capital-flight risk, while the PBOC keeps cheapening overnight money; none of it creates borrowers. Beijing's rejection of the tariff-evasion charge keeps the summit track tense just as exports are the only engine running, which keeps the stance bearish into the July activity and home-price data.

Playbook ✓: Weak domestic demand, an easing bias and targeted rather than broad support are on script.  |  ⚠️ Divergence: A 10-year yield at an over 1-year low and a strong yuan look like confidence but are the signature of savers chasing safe assets while loans shrink. For global assets the transmission runs through credit and construction: Fortescue's Super Special Fines iron ore stocks at China ports are at a five-week high (Reuters, [Aug 14]), so iron ore and AUD carry the downside; EM Asia competes with exports up 23.9% that China cannot absorb at home; and WTI at $82.40 is a terms-of-trade tax on margins with PPI easing.
