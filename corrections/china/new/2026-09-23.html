🇨🇳 China  Mixed  → easing bias

Macro Stance: The posture stays structurally accommodative against the global tightening impulse, but the bias is expressed through liquidity and opening rather than rates. The PBOC left loan prime rates unchanged in September (www.tmgm.com, [Sep 21]) as the Fed hike narrows its room to ease (investingLive, [Sep 20]), pledged further opening-up to foreign financial institutions (South China Morning Post, [Sep 22]) and will issue RMB bills in Hong Kong (BusinessToday Malaysia, [Sep 22]). The fixing headlines conflict: the yuan hit a 3-1/2 year high on a strong PBOC fixing (Investing.com, [Sep 22]), yet the midpoint was set at 6.7468 against an expected 6.6971, a touch weaker than the prior 6.7459 (Newsquawk, [Sep 22]). Reconciled: spot is at the high, but the fix is now leaning against further gains on the eve of the summit, consistent with Nikkei's view that China slow-walks appreciation (Nikkei Asia, [Sep 21]). August CPI rose 0.8% and PPI 3.8% (BigGo Finance, [Sep 8]) on energy costs (Reuters, [Sep 9]).

PMIs & Activity: The official August manufacturing PMI rose to 49.8 with new orders back in expansion (BigGo Finance, [Aug 30]) and the private gauge reached 51.5 (BigGo Finance, [Aug 31]), but the hard data split the economy: industrial output rebounded to 5.2% (BigGo Finance, [Sep 14]) while retail sales grew 0.4% y/y against 0.6% expected (investingLive, [Sep 14]), urban unemployment hit 5.3% (TradingView, [Sep 14]) and fixed-asset investment fell 7.2% in eight months (State Council Information Office, [Sep 15]). Exports jumped 25% (AP News, [Sep 7]), Xi rolls into the summit with the trade engine roaring (Reuters, [Sep 21]) and China's share of global container exports has soared to 40% (Financial Times, [Sep 21]). AMRO forecasts growth at 4.5% in 2026 and 2027 and warns of weak domestic demand (BusinessToday Malaysia, [Sep 22]).

Credit Impulse: Bank loans rose less than expected after July's slump (Reuters, [Sep 14]) and credit expansion missed forecasts as loan demand sagged (Bloomberg.com, [Sep 14]); bond and equity financing exceeded loans in social financing for the first time (BigGo Finance, [Sep 14]), so securities issuance carries the aggregate while the loan book stalls. The LPR hold came as the PBOC signals slower credit (CHOSUNBIZ, [Sep 20]), regulators told e-commerce apps to stop selling loans (Sixth Tone, [Sep 21]) and regional banks are split on loan quality as property exits accelerate (Asian Banking & Finance, [Sep 22]).

Property: August home prices extended their decline across 70 cities while tier-1 cities stabilised (BigGo Finance, [Sep 14]); development investment plunged 19.9% in eight months, the steepest on record (BigGo Finance, [Sep 14]), and second-hand sales now exceed 50% of the market (State Council Information Office, [Sep 21]). The policy response has moved to developer credit: China asked banks to keep Vanke loans off bad-debt books (Reuters, [Sep 22]) and Vanke shares surged on the report (Investing.com, [Sep 22]), after the provident fund overhaul sparked a property stock rally (BigGo Finance, [Sep 21]). Demand has not followed: a US$33 billion support experiment is sputtering (The Business Times, [Sep 20]) and sovereign wealth funds stay away over lingering property woes (Nikkei Asia, [Sep 20]).

Fiscal, Policy & Trade: Fiscal revenue growth slowed as spending contracted (Caixin Global, [Sep 19]); Xi chaired the Politburo, which set a Central Committee plenum for late October (U.S. News & World Report, [Sep 21]). The New York round produced candid exchanges (China Daily, [Sep 22]) and Greer says talks on a truce extension continue (Bloomberg.com, [Sep 21]), but rare earths friction threatens to stall the extension (South China Morning Post, [Sep 22]) and tariffs and export controls are expected to persist (CNBC, [Sep 22]). Beijing's goodwill gesture was adding drug precursor chemicals to its export control list (Reuters, [Sep 22]). The trending hashtags #请叠纸停止抹黑中国政府 and #PPGruinsChineseCulturalExport show cultural friction running alongside the trade track.

Markets & Flows: The pre-summit tape is flat: China stocks fell as the summit loomed (TradingView, [Sep 22]) and closed mostly flat as investors watched the talks (Business Standard, [Sep 22]), while Hong Kong eyes fresh inflows from mainland insurers (South China Morning Post, [Sep 22]). China has spent a record amount importing over 1,000 tonnes of gold this year (Financial Times, [Sep 22]), and the summit is driving silver to settle and equities to weaken preemptively.

New / Most Important: Asking banks to keep Vanke loans off bad-debt books is regulatory forbearance for the largest stressed developer: it buys time for the balance sheets, not demand for the apartments. With this week's summit the key near-term catalyst (Business Insider, [Sep 22]), Beijing is stabilising developer credit and the fixing at home while negotiating on rare earths abroad.

Playbook ✓: Chinese cultural export tensions and trade friction are well-flagged risks; policy support by forbearance rather than stimulus is Beijing's established property script.  |  ⚠️ Divergence: China easing while the Fed hikes should widen the rate differential and pressure CNY, yet the yuan sits at a multi-year high, and the summit adds binary optionality. Transmission runs through the data: the ECB finds China's industrial rise hits German manufacturers hard (Reuters, [Sep 22]), the channel for European exporters; Australia's flash manufacturing PMI shrank in September (Investing.com, [Sep 22]) as a weak Chinese property and credit pulse caps iron ore demand and the AUD; and WTI at 91.49 adds an import-cost squeeze that weak domestic demand cannot absorb.
