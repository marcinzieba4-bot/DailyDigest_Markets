🇨🇳 China  Mixed  → easing bias but constrained

Macro Stance: The PBOC is easing while most major central banks hike or hold, but through structural tools: it cut the PSL rate to 1.5% and expanded relending quotas (Fibre2Fashion, [Sep 30]), a 25 bp cut to a targeted rate rather than to the LPR. The constraint is the rate gap, with US 10Y yields at 5.32% against Chinese lending at 1.7% (Modern Diplomacy, [Oct 6]). So far the yuan absorbs it: the fixing was set at a 3.5-year high before the National Day holiday (The Standard (HK), [Sep 30]), the currency was set for a seventh straight quarterly gain as exporters sold dollars (Business Recorder, [Sep 30]), and UOB sees gradual upside within defined bands (www.tmgm.com, [Oct 6]). Reserves tell the other half: FX reserves shrank by $38.1 billion while gold holdings rose for a record 23rd month (BigGo Finance, [Oct 6]). August CPI rose 0.8% and PPI 3.8% (BigGo Finance, [Sep 8]), so deflation no longer forces a broad cut.

PMIs & Activity: September brought the first official expansion in three months: manufacturing at 50.1, exactly the consensus, and non-manufacturing at 50.2 (investingLive, [Sep 29]), with the private RatingDog gauges stronger at 52.1 and 51.6 (investingLive, [Sep 30]). The rebound is AI-led (Reuters, [Sep 29]) and export-led after a 25% August jump (AP News, [Sep 7]); the domestic side lags, with August retail sales up 0.4% against 0.6% expected (investingLive, [Sep 14]) and industrial profits up just 4.2%, the weakest since November (Bloomberg, [Sep 27]). The first holiday reads are firmer: National Day retail sales rose as travel volumes surged (www.citynewsservice.cn, [Oct 6]). The quarter still looks soft, with Q3 growth estimated below target at 4.4% despite the AI boom (Nikkei Asia, [Oct 6]).

Credit Impulse: August bank loans rose less than expected (Reuters, [Sep 14]) and credit expansion missed forecasts (Bloomberg, [Sep 14]), with bond and equity financing exceeding loans for the first time (BigGo Finance, [Sep 14]): both measures missed, and government bonds carried what growth there was. The banking system is being shrunk, not levered: China closed hundreds of banks to bolster the financial system (Financial Times, [Oct 3]), 670 in 2025 (Yahoo Finance, [Oct 5]), and is accelerating the small-bank purge to contain systemic risks (CHOSUNBIZ - Chosunbiz, [Oct 6]), a clean-up that adds no lending capacity in the near term.

Property: The mortgage subsidy of 1 percentage point for first-time buyers (bloomingbit, [Sep 30]) is meant to unlock housing demand (China.org.cn, [Sep 30]), and the holiday gives the first test: Shenzhen and Guangzhou property markets picked up steam during National Day (chinadailyhk, [Oct 6]), after Beijing's September new-home sales surged nearly 50% month-on-month (BigGo Finance, [Oct 1]). The base is weak: August home prices fell across 70 cities (BigGo Finance, [Sep 14]) and property investment fell 19.9% in eight months (Business Recorder, [Sep 15]). Developers did not rally: property shares slid after the stimulus (Prop News Time, [Oct 2]), and the growth target is under scrutiny as stimulus struggles to lift housing (Traders Union, [Oct 6]).

Fiscal, Policy & Trade: Bloomberg judges the stimulus underwhelming, a sign the two-speed economy will persist (Bloomberg, [Sep 30]), and Beijing is seen doing 'just enough' to defend GDP growth (South China Morning Post, [Oct 2]). The EU-China talks are the live wire: they zero in on cars (POLITICO.eu, [Oct 6]), Franco-German unity paves the way for a tougher EU line (Bloomberg, [Oct 6]), and China opened an anti-dumping probe into European chemical exports beforehand (Financial Times, [Oct 3]). The UK weighs following the EU with EV tariffs (Bloomberg, [Oct 5]), and Washington has no timeline for the cuts on $60 billion of goods (Yahoo Finance, [Oct 1]).

Markets & Flows: The holiday thinned the tape: the SSE Composite stayed shut for Golden Week (BBN Times, [Oct 5]) and Hong Kong reopened without Stock Connect (BBN Times, [Oct 1]). The Hang Seng dropped 3%, Asia's worst, after the stimulus disappointed (NewsBytes, [Oct 2]) and the Hang Seng Tech index fell into a bear market on tax fears (Gotrade, [Oct 5]), before the Hang Seng closed 1 percent higher on Tuesday (The Standard (HK), [Oct 6]).

New / Most Important: The first holiday data and the first quarterly estimate point in different directions. Holiday retail and travel rose and Shenzhen and Guangzhou housing picked up, the first sign the mortgage subsidy reaches buyers; yet Q3 growth is estimated at 4.4%, below target, with exports and AI hardware doing the work. A holiday bounce in services and showrooms is not yet a credit impulse, and the reserve data show the yuan's strength is being managed rather than bought.

Playbook ✓: The deflationary domestic impulse, with weak consumption, property stress and cautious lending, continues on script, as does the drip-feed of targeted stimulus; trade partners push back on an export machine that keeps growing.  |  ⚠️ Divergence: The Seeking Alpha 'global liquidity regime shift' has a China leg: with the yuan firm and reserves shrinking, China is not recycling surpluses into Treasuries, while its growth stays export-led. Transmission to global assets therefore runs through exports, not demand. An AI-led PMI above 50 helps Asian tech supply chains, but with credit missing, property investment falling and August imports below estimates (CNBC, [Sep 7]), iron ore and the AUD get no property-led bid; the Australian dollar struggled despite the solid PMIs (FXStreet, [Sep 30]) and ASX stocks most exposed to the Chinese consumer are sinking fast (AFR, [Oct 6]). European luxury and auto exporters face a weak Chinese consumer and now a trade fight centred on cars, so the reflation trade in EM and European exporters stays impaired until credit and housing turn.
