🇨🇳 China  Bearish  → easing bias

Macro Stance: The PBOC is easing through volume, not price. Loan prime rates were left unchanged for a 14th consecutive month (The Business Times, [Jul 20]), even with growth below target. Instead the central bank added the most liquidity in five months (Bloomberg, [Jul 23]) and is running overnight reverse repos alongside a 500b-yuan MLF operation, injecting a total of 2.1 trillion yuan (Reuters, [Jul 24]; China Daily, [Jul 24]). The yuan is not the release valve: UOB sees an upside bias against the dollar below 6.7820 (www.tmgm.com, [Jul 20]) as Europe scrutinises its value (Bloomberg, [Jul 21]). Prices are split: June CPI rose only 1% (Global Times, [Jul 9]) while PPI climbed 4.1% (finance.biggo.com, [Jul 8]), a near four-year high that squeezes manufacturers (Reuters, [Jul 8]).

PMIs & Activity: The last official read is June: manufacturing PMI 50.3 and non-manufacturing 50.2 (Reuters, [Jun 29]); July's numbers are the next test (ING Think, [Jul 23]). The hard data show two speeds: Q2 GDP grew 4.3%, below the official target range (Bloomberg, [Jul 15]); June industrial output rose 5.3% y/y and retail sales returned to growth (BusinessLine, [Jul 15]), but first-half retail sales were up just 1.3% (Ecns.cn, [Jul 15]) and the investment slide deepened as infrastructure joined property (Caixin Global, [Jul 15]), while June exports surged 27% from a year earlier (Yahoo Finance UK, [Jul 13]). Now the factory engine is slowing too: industrial profit growth slowed again in June as retreating oil prices sapped the earnings lift (CNBC, [Jul 26]), to the slowest pace this year (Financial Times, [Jul 26]). Trade-in sales are dropping as the stimulus effect fades (Caixin Global, [Jul 24]).

Credit Impulse: June new bank loans rose less than expected (The Star, [Jul 15]) and credit growth missed as the PBOC flagged a "new normal" (Caixin Global, [Jul 16]). Household loans fell by 366.8 billion yuan in the first half (36Kr, [Jul 17]). The finance minister's claim that fiscal-financial synergy leveraged over 17 trillion yuan in new loans (finance.biggo.com, [Jul 22]) is not a contradiction: the volume is policy-directed corporate and state-linked lending while households pay down, and NST Online argues the real challenge is household confidence rather than property ([Jul 26]). Banks breaking with the lending benchmark in pricing new credit (Bloomberg, [Jul 24]) is quiet price easing. Read: headline impulse flat, household impulse negative.

Property: June new home prices fell at a slower pace (Reuters, [Jul 14]) and first-tier prices extended a four-month rebound (South China Morning Post, [Jul 15]), but resale prices fell faster (Reuters, [Jun 30]) and property investment slumped 18 percent y/y in January–June (The Standard (HK), [Jul 15]). The capital side is in retreat: KKR and AEW seek to sell China assets at steep losses (Bloomberg, [Jul 20]), and regulators curbed a loan structure used by distressed developers (Bloomberg, [Jul 22]) and offshore loans for local real estate firms (The Standard (HK), [Jul 23]). Vanke completed extensions on its 2026 public bonds (Caixin Global, [Jul 23]) with a RMB 519 million loan from Shenzhen Metro at 2.29% (BigGo Finance, [Jul 21]): state shareholders, not buyers, hold it together.

Fiscal, Policy & Trade: Beijing pledged stronger policy support (TradingView, [Jul 21]) and vowed to hit the growth target (Bloomberg, [Jul 20]), yet clings to austerity despite the slowdown (Bloomberg, [Jul 22]), and growth is set to slow in the second half as it avoids broad stimulus (Yahoo Finance UK, [Jul 25]). The July Politburo meeting, still ahead, is the marker for the growth commitment (Bloomberg, [Jul 23]). Regulation is tightening at the edges: Trip.com was fined US$765 million in an antitrust action (PYMNTS.com, [Jul 26]). On trade, Washington will impose new tariffs of 10 to 12.5 percent on over 60 countries (China Daily Global Edition, [Jul 24]); Beijing is set to look past them before Xi's US trip (Bloomberg, [Jul 24]) and is consulting on tariff cuts on $30 billion in trade (Reuters, [Jul 23]). Toward Europe it keeps adding EU companies to its dual-use export control list (Table.Briefings, [Jul 26]).

Markets & Flows: Capital is chasing AI, not consumption. CXMT's 466% debut surge made it the most valuable China-listed company (CNBC, [Jul 26]) and revived interest in battered shares (TradingView, [Jul 26]), after the "national team" bought shares worth $9bn (Financial Times, [Jul 20]). Hong Kong is weaker: it sank back below 25,000 on oil and tariff fears (BusinessToday Malaysia, [Jul 25]) as Middle East tensions weighed on the week (BusinessToday Malaysia, [Jul 26]). BCA tips a three-month reversion trade into China away from South Korea (Yahoo Finance UK, [Jul 25]). ASML's slide on China-related news shows how fast export-control risk now hits Western equipment makers.

New / Most Important: June industrial profits remove the last cushion in the domestic story. First-half profits rose 18.7% (TradingView, [Jul 26]), but growth is now the slowest this year, and Reuters frames exports as what cushions an uneven recovery ([Jul 26]). With retail demand soft, household credit shrinking and the budget on austerity, the oil-price lift to margins was the bridge; it is fading ahead of a July Politburo meeting that must decide whether fiscal policy finally steps in.

Playbook ✓: Stimulus drip-feed, weak property, LPR on hold and export-led profits are all on script.  |  ⚠️ Divergence: Markets are bidding China's AI champions while the growth pulse weakens underneath: slower profits, an 18 percent property-investment slump and contracting infrastructure keep construction-linked bulk demand fading, a drag on iron ore and on the AUD, whose bounce as Mideast tensions ease rests on oil, not China. European exporters absorb the surplus as the yuan draws scrutiny, and EM inflows ride state buying and IPO euphoria rather than a PMI or credit upturn. A turn needs household credit and fiscal transfers, not liquidity alone.
