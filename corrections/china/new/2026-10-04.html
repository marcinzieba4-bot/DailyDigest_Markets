🇨🇳 China  Mixed  → easing bias / accommodative

Macro Stance: The PBOC keeps an accommodative posture through structural tools rather than the benchmark rate: it cut the PSL rate by 0.25 percentage point to 1.5% (Forex Factory, [Sep 29]) and boosted relending quotas by 700 billion yuan (BigGo Finance, [Sep 29]), and China rolls out cash for Golden Week as other economies tighten (bloomingbit, [Oct 1]). The yuan is not under the pressure the 5.28% US 10Y would suggest: the fixing was set at a 3.5-year high before the National Day holiday (The Standard (HK), [Sep 30]) and Rabobank's forecast points to a pause before further gains (Forex Factory, [Oct 3]), while China's 10Y yield sits at an over one-year low (TradingView, [Sep 29]). August CPI rose 0.8% and PPI 3.8% (BigGo Finance, [Sep 8]), lifted by energy costs (Reuters, [Sep 9]).

PMIs & Activity: The official manufacturing PMI returned to growth at 50.1 with non-manufacturing at 50.2 (investingLive, [Sep 29]), and the private RatingDog gauges printed 52.1 and 51.6 (investingLive, [Sep 30]); the 'beat' headlines (TradingView, [Sep 29]) and an official print at the 50.1 consensus (investingLive, [Sep 29]) reconcile as an in-line official survey and stronger private ones. The impulse is AI- and export-led (Nikkei Asia, [Sep 30]), after August exports jumped 25% (AP News, [Sep 7]). Consumption is the weak leg: August retail sales rose 0.4% (investingLive, [Sep 14]), peak-season auto sales growth disappointed (WSJ, [Oct 2]), Nike's China sales plunged 26% (Retail Gazette, [Oct 2]) and August industrial profit growth was the weakest since November (Bloomberg, [Sep 27]).

Credit Impulse: August bank lending disappointed (Reuters, [Sep 14]) and credit expansion missed forecasts (Bloomberg, [Sep 14]), while bond and equity financing exceeded loans for the first time (BigGo Finance, [Sep 14]): both measures missed, and the shift toward capital-market financing means the aggregate rests on issuance rather than bank lending. The package steers credit: cheap loans help state firms pay overdue bills (Bloomberg, [Sep 29]) and banks are urged to finance AI and asset-light service firms (Caixin Global, [Sep 29]), while household credit stays impaired as the bad consumer debt cleanup falters (The Business Times, [Sep 29]).

Property: The first central mortgage subsidy, 1 percentage point a year and up to nearly 50,000 yuan on a 1 million yuan loan (BigGo Finance, [Sep 29]), meets early signs of turnover, Beijing's September new-home sales up nearly 50% month-on-month (BigGo Finance, [Oct 1]) and the top 100 developers at 2.26 trillion yuan of January-September sales (BigGo Finance, [Oct 1]). Policy tilts to supply discipline: Beijing is shortening the leash on developers (Nikkei Asia, [Oct 2]) and Wuhan's overhaul focuses on supply, not demand (South China Morning Post, [Oct 3]). The base stays weak, with a Vanke loss underscoring the stress (Traders Union, [Oct 1]) and August home prices down across 70 cities as property investment fell 19.9% in eight months (BigGo Finance, [Sep 14]).

Fiscal, Policy & Trade: The cabinet pledged stronger support to meet the growth target (Reuters, [Sep 28]), with fourth-quarter local bond issuance likely around 500 billion yuan (BigGo Finance, [Sep 28]); SCMP sees 'just enough' targeted fiscal support to defend GDP growth (South China Morning Post, [Oct 2]) and calls for a bigger role for private firms (South China Morning Post, [Oct 3]). With the US, differences persist despite the summit (India Today, [Oct 3]) and Greer sets no timeline for the Board of Trade (South China Morning Post, [Oct 2]). Europe is escalating: after a Commission official flagged a worrying rise in EU imports from China (Reuters, [Oct 1]), Beijing launched an anti-dumping probe into European chemical exports (Financial Times, [Oct 3]).

Markets & Flows: Hong Kong, closed for National Day on Oct 1 (BBN Times, [Oct 1]), reopened into a 3% plunge as the stimulus failed to impress (Moneycontrol.com, [Oct 2]), the Hang Seng sinking nearly 641 points as banks and tech slid (BusinessToday Malaysia, [Oct 3]); mainland stocks had limped to a quarterly drop (TradingView, [Sep 30]). The primary market is the bright spot: Hong Kong IPO proceeds more than doubled in the first nine months to HK$388 billion, per Financial Secretary Paul Chan, and a best-performing China stocks list for October (NerdWallet, [Oct 1]) shows retail attention returning. The yuan, set for a seventh straight quarterly gain (Business Recorder, [Sep 30]), contrasts with weak equities.

New / Most Important: Beijing opened a new front with Europe: an anti-dumping probe into EU chemical imports ahead of trade talks (Free Malaysia Today, [Oct 3]). It answers the Commission's import alarm with leverage rather than concessions, days after Beijing vowed a response to any EU move (Reuters, [Sep 29]). With the US truce holding but unresolved and the domestic package sized to defend the growth target, external demand is China's main growth engine, and the EU is where that engine now meets resistance.

Playbook ✓: Hong Kong as the conduit for offshore capital fits the playbook, as does targeted easing with property in managed decline and consumption soft.  |  ⚠️ Divergence: The HK IPO doubling is significant for global allocators still anchored to the "China uninvestable" narrative, but it is a supply-side phenomenon (companies listing while the window is open) that sits beside a 3% index plunge, not evidence of demand-side recovery. The transmission to global assets runs through the growth pulse: an AI- and export-led PMI at 50.1 helps Asian tech supply chains, while bank loans missing and property investment down 19.9% leave the construction demand behind iron ore and the AUD absent. European exporters face a double hit, a Chinese consumer at 0.4% retail growth and a chemicals probe that signals retaliation risk across industrials, autos and luxury. Watch whether new listings hold up in secondary trading; if post-IPO returns turn negative, the window closes fast.
