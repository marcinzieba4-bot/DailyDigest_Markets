🇨🇳 China  Mixed  → easing bias but constrained

Macro Stance: The PBOC is easing through quantities, not price: a 700 billion yuan outright reverse repo left an October net injection of 400 billion yuan (BigGo Finance, [Oct 14]), Governor Pan says policy stays moderately loose (BigGo Finance, [Oct 15]), and he backs closer IMF surveillance of fiscal and spillover risks (Reuters, [Oct 17]). The constraint is not the currency: the yuan was poised for a third weekly gain on strong exports (Reuters, [Oct 16]), and the fix, nudged stronger again (TradingPedia, [Oct 16]), stays on the weak side of the 6.6947 estimate (Newsquawk, [Oct 16]), managed appreciation. Prices are the limit: September CPI rose 0.9% against 1% expected (investingLive, [Oct 14]) while factory inflation hit 5%, the fastest in over four years (investingLive, [Oct 14]), jolted by oil (Bloomberg, [Oct 14]). That is cost-push; with US 10Y yields at 5.10%, China is the one major market where bond yields are not rising (The Indian Express, [Oct 16]), and benchmark rates stay on hold.

PMIs & Activity: September's official manufacturing PMI returned to growth at 50.1 with non-manufacturing at 50.2 (investingLive, [Sep 29]), and RatingDog manufacturing leapt to 52.1 (FXStreet, [Sep 30]). Trade shows where that comes from: exports surged 36.3% y/y and imports rose 31% (Investing.com, [Oct 14]) on AI-related shipments (Financial Times, [Oct 14]), with the January-September surplus at $938 billion (ABC News, [Oct 14]). Domestic demand runs at the other speed: August retail sales grew just 0.4% (investingLive, [Sep 14]) and passenger-car retail sales fell 24% in the first 11 days of October (Gasgoo, [Oct 16]). Q3 GDP is in focus this week (Seoul Economic Daily, [Oct 17]): Reuters' poll sees growth picking up as exports power ahead (The Lufkin Daily News, [Oct 17]), while Bloomberg says tardy stimulus sets up a second quarter of sub-target growth (Bloomberg, [Oct 16]).

Credit Impulse: September new bank loans jumped to 1.08 trillion yuan, above forecast (TradingView, [Oct 14]), on stimulus and a seasonal boost (Reuters, [Oct 14]). The composition reconciles that beat with the deeper slump in demand investingLive sees beneath it (investingLive, [Oct 14]): household credit contracted by over 1 trillion yuan in the first three quarters (BigGo Finance, [Oct 14]), and The Asian Banker sees export growth strengthening as household borrowing remains weak (The Asian Banker, [Oct 16]). The impulse is corporate and seasonal; it is not reaching households.

Property: New home prices fell again as recovery remains elusive (The Lufkin Daily News, [Oct 17]), which outweighs Shanghai's record-breaking market (China Daily, [Oct 14]): a report calls the resale-led rebound a hollow narrative (IANS LIVE, [Oct 12]) and the SCMP argues cheap debt is not enough to get buyers spending (South China Morning Post, [Oct 12]). Supply is being re-engineered: Hangzhou and Shenzhen fleshed out rules for the housing sales overhaul (Caixin Global, [Oct 16]) and first-tier land auctions cooled sharply in October (BigGo Finance, [Oct 13]). The base is still August: home prices fell across 70 cities (BigGo Finance, [Sep 14]) and property investment dropped 19.9% in eight months (Business Recorder, [Sep 15]).

Fiscal, Policy & Trade: Beijing completed this year's 1.3 trillion yuan of ultra-long special government bonds (BigGo Finance, [Oct 17]), but the debt stock is the ceiling: interest costs have surged to CNY 1.4 trillion (BigGo Finance, [Oct 12]), and the IMF urges bigger fiscal stimulus (Reuters, [Oct 15]), warning the effort lacks ambition (Bloomberg, [Oct 15]). On trade, Trump waived the Russia-related sanctions that threatened 100% tariffs on India and China (Hindustan Times, [Oct 17]), while Beijing moved to curb tech exports, including AI (AP News, [Oct 16]); the EU will propose import curbs on other Chinese sectors (Reuters, [Oct 16]), and China warns it should not underestimate its ability to fight (Bloomberg, [Oct 16]).

Markets & Flows: Beijing moved to prop up stocks with selling curbs and buybacks (Bloomberg, [Oct 16]) and shares rebounded from 14-month lows (Devdiscourse, [Oct 16]), but Chinese stocks still turned the biggest losers as the global AI trade stumbled (Bloomberg, [Oct 17]). Bonds price the weak domestic side: long-dated yields hit a 15-month low as the PBOC keeps cash flowing (TradingView, [Oct 15]).

New / Most Important: The tail risk on the export engine eased just as the domestic data softened again. Trump's waiver removes the 100% secondary-tariff threat, and Reuters' poll has exports powering Q3 growth; at the same time new home prices fell again and household credit is contracting. The GDP print will likely confirm the two-speed split rather than resolve it: external demand and AI hardware carry the headline while property and consumers lag, and policy is supporting asset prices and quotas, not household demand.

Playbook ✓: Booming exports beside weak households, a PBOC easing quantities rather than rates, a state floor under stocks and EU pushback on the surplus all follow the script; Indonesia's index-exclusion threat shows how fragile Asia-Pacific flows remain.  |  ⚠️ Divergence: Transmission to global assets is splitting by channel. Asian tech supply chains get the export boom, South Korea's factory growth at a 4-month high on export orders (Reuters, [Sep 30]). Iron ore and the AUD need property and construction, and that channel is not turning: investment is down 19.9%, home prices are still falling and land auctions are cooling, consistent with the Australian dollar at its lowest since July (Economies.com, [Oct 15]) and Australia's manufacturing PMI at 49.6 as new orders fell (investingLive, [Sep 30]). European exporters face both sides of the surplus: a weak Chinese consumer, with VW deliveries posting their steepest drop in more than four years (Automotive News, [Oct 12]), and Chinese competition the EU is trying to cap. Oil's bid is an energy shock showing up in China's PPI, not Chinese reflation, and EM Asia competes with Chinese exports rather than selling into Chinese demand.
