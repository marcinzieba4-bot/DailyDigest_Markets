🇨🇳 China  Mixed  → accommodative but constrained

Macro Stance: The PBOC is accommodating through quantities, not price: a 700 billion yuan outright reverse repo left an October net injection of 400 billion yuan (BigGo Finance, [Oct 14]), Governor Pan says policy stays moderately loose (BigGo Finance, [Oct 15]), and he now pledges to further open financial markets to foreigners (Reuters, [Oct 16]). The constraint is not the currency: the yuan is set for a third weekly gain on strong exports (Reuters, [Oct 16]), and the fix, nudged stronger again (TradingPedia, [Oct 16]), stays on the weak side of the 6.6947 estimate (Newsquawk, [Oct 16]), managed appreciation. Prices are the limit: September CPI rose 0.9% against 1% expected (investingLive, [Oct 14]) while factory inflation hit 5%, the fastest in over four years (investingLive, [Oct 14]), jolted by oil (Bloomberg, [Oct 14]). That is cost-push; with US 10Y yields at 5.10%, China is the one major market where bond yields are not rising (The Indian Express, [Oct 16]), and benchmark rates stay on hold.

PMIs & Activity: September's official manufacturing PMI returned to growth at 50.1 with non-manufacturing at 50.2 (investingLive, [Sep 29]), and RatingDog manufacturing leapt to 52.1 (FXStreet, [Sep 30]). Trade shows where that comes from: exports surged 36.3% y/y and imports rose 31% (Investing.com, [Oct 14]) on AI-related shipments (Financial Times, [Oct 14]), with the January-September surplus at $938 billion (ABC News, [Oct 14]). Domestic demand runs at the other speed: August retail sales grew just 0.4% (investingLive, [Sep 14]) and passenger-car retail sales fell 24% in the first 11 days of October (Gasgoo, [Oct 16]). Bloomberg says tardy stimulus sets up a second quarter of sub-target growth (Bloomberg, [Oct 16]), and the IMF has cut its 2026 projection (South China Morning Post, [Oct 13]).

Credit Impulse: September new bank loans jumped to 1.08 trillion yuan, above forecast (TradingView, [Oct 14]), on stimulus and a seasonal boost (Reuters, [Oct 14]), and the strong credit data buoyed shares at the open (www.marketscreener.com, [Oct 16]). The composition reconciles that beat with the deeper slump in demand investingLive sees beneath it (investingLive, [Oct 14]): household credit contracted by over 1 trillion yuan in the first three quarters (BigGo Finance, [Oct 14]), and The Asian Banker sees export growth strengthening as household borrowing remains weak (The Asian Banker, [Oct 16]). The impulse is corporate and seasonal; it is not reaching households.

Property: The mortgage subsidy boosted holiday homebuying (iChongqing, [Oct 10]) and Shanghai's market broke records (China Daily, [Oct 14]), but the quality is contested: a report calls the resale-led rebound a hollow narrative (IANS LIVE, [Oct 12]) and the SCMP argues cheap debt is not enough to get buyers spending (South China Morning Post, [Oct 12]). Supply is being re-engineered: Hangzhou and Shenzhen fleshed out rules for the housing sales overhaul (Caixin Global, [Oct 16]) and first-tier land auctions cooled sharply in October (BigGo Finance, [Oct 13]). The base is still August: home prices fell across 70 cities (BigGo Finance, [Sep 14]) and property investment dropped 19.9% in eight months (Business Recorder, [Sep 15]).

Fiscal, Policy & Trade: The Finance Ministry vows a more proactive fiscal policy (CGTN, [Oct 10]), but the debt stock is the ceiling: the FT says the burden limits stimulus space (Financial Times, [Oct 10]), and the IMF urges bigger fiscal stimulus (Reuters, [Oct 15]), warning the effort lacks ambition (Bloomberg, [Oct 15]). The Financial Street Forum is expected to bring more opening up by regulators (South China Morning Post, [Oct 16]). On trade, Beijing moved to curb tech exports, including AI (AP News, [Oct 16]); the EU will propose import curbs on other Chinese sectors (Reuters, [Oct 16]) after capping hybrids at 350,000 a year (The Guardian, [Oct 12]), and China warns it should not underestimate its ability to fight (Bloomberg, [Oct 16]).

Markets & Flows: Beijing stepped in: China moved to prop up stocks with selling curbs and buybacks (Bloomberg, [Oct 16]), and shares rebounded from 14-month lows as AI firms unveiled buybacks and strong forecasts (Devdiscourse, [Oct 16]), though they remain set for a weekly decline (TradingView, [Oct 16]). Bonds price the weak domestic side: long-dated yields hit a 15-month low as the PBOC keeps cash flowing (TradingView, [Oct 15]).

New / Most Important: The state is now managing the market as well as the macro. Selling curbs and buybacks put a floor under equities a day after a 14-month low, and AI export controls show Beijing guarding the one sector driving its 36.3% export surge. Neither changes the domestic picture: loans of 1.08 trillion yuan came with household credit contracting, CPI undershot at 0.9%, and Bloomberg sees sub-target growth into the next print. Policy is supporting prices of assets, not demand.

Playbook ✓: Booming exports beside weak households, a PBOC easing quantities rather than rates, a national-team floor under stocks and EU pushback on the surplus all follow the script; Indonesia's MSCI-driven slump (per NYT) shows how fragile Asia-Pacific flows remain.  |  ⚠️ Divergence: Transmission to global assets is splitting by channel. Asian tech supply chains get the export boom, South Korea's factory growth at a 4-month high on export orders (Reuters, [Sep 30]). Iron ore and the AUD need property and construction, and that channel is not turning: investment is down 19.9% and land auctions are cooling, consistent with the Australian dollar at its lowest since July (Economies.com, [Oct 15]) and Australia's manufacturing PMI at 49.6 as new orders fell (investingLive, [Sep 30]). European exporters face both sides of the surplus: a weak Chinese consumer, with VW deliveries posting their steepest drop in more than four years (Automotive News, [Oct 12]), and Chinese competition the EU is trying to cap. Oil's bid is an energy shock showing up in China's PPI, not Chinese reflation, and EM Asia competes with Chinese exports rather than selling into Chinese demand.
