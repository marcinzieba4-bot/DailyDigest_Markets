🇨🇳 China  Mixed  → accommodative

Macro Stance: The PBOC is easing through the plumbing rather than the headline rates. It ran its first mid-month overnight reverse repo, which boosted bonds (Bloomberg, [Aug 13]), alongside a 1 trillion yuan reverse repo (BigGo Finance, [Aug 13]), and is fine-tuning liquidity management with a focus on the overnight rate (The Standard (HK), [Aug 13]); Caixin reads it as a signal of diversified loan pricing benchmarks (Caixin Global, [Aug 13]). The 10-year yield hit an over 1-year low (TradingView, [Aug 13]). This follows the Q2 monetary policy report's pledge of a timely new policy rollout (Reuters, [Aug 12]) and a 1 trillion yuan relending facility for private enterprises (BigGo Finance, [Aug 12]). The yuan holds a 3-1/2-year high as US inflation dims Fed hike prospects (KLSE Screener, [Aug 13]), but Beijing is steadying it as exporters feel the squeeze (Nikkei Asia, [Aug 13]). July CPI rose 0.5% (CGTN, [Aug 9]), a six-month low (TradingView, [Aug 8]), with PPI up 3.5% (China Daily, [Aug 9]).

PMIs & Activity: July's official PMIs both sit below 50, manufacturing at 49.2 and non-manufacturing at 49.0 (FXStreet, [Jul 31]). The private manufacturing PMI eased to 50.9 (Fibre2Fashion, [Aug 3]), a 4-month low (Reuters, [Aug 2]), and services growth hit its slowest since September 2024 (Reuters, [Aug 4]). Exports beat estimates in July on AI-driven shipments (CNBC, [Aug 6]) and DHL Express is tripling its Shenzhen air cargo capacity, but the high-tech boom is seen failing to halt the slowdown (Bloomberg, [Aug 13]). Consumption is the weak leg: July new car sales slumped 21%, a 10th straight decline (finance.biggo.com, [Aug 11]), and NEV retail fell 17% in the first week of August (CnEVPost, [Aug 13]).

Credit Impulse: The July print is still pending and the previews conflict. A Reuters poll sees July bank lending set to plummet amid subdued demand and a seasonal slowdown (Reuters, [Aug 7]), while Caixin expects July loans to rebound on a low base (Caixin Global, [Aug 7]). Reconciled: the base effect can flatter the year-on-year comparison even as the monthly flow collapses, and low loan growth is already fuelling easing bets (VT Markets, [Aug 8]). June set the tone, with new bank loans rising less than expected (The Star, [Jul 15]) and household loans down 366.8 billion yuan in the first half (36Kr, [Jul 17]). Banks look to unload US$8.2b in bad credit-card loans (South China Morning Post, [Aug 11]), and The Economist asks whether the debt-bomb squad is about to blow up (The Economist, [Aug 13]). Cheaper overnight funding addresses supply; the binding constraint is demand.

Property: City-level easing is running ahead of price stabilisation. Beijing further relaxed home-buying curbs (Reuters, [Aug 7]), following Shanghai (Bloomberg, [Aug 7]). A private survey shows housing prices fell 0.5% in July as sales volume weakened (Investing.com, [Aug 3]), and real home prices have sunk to their lowest level in 21 years (the deep dive, [Aug 3]). The official June data showed new home prices falling at a slower pace (Reuters, [Jul 14]) but property investment down 18 percent y/y in January–June (The Standard (HK), [Jul 15]). Expiring land leases are a $148 billion threat Beijing is racing to solve (Bloomberg, [Aug 11]), and a key steel price at a near-decade low confirms the construction slump (The Edge Malaysia, [Aug 7]).

Fiscal, Policy & Trade: Fiscal policy is leaning on collection: authorities are mounting a tax-collection crackdown in a sign of fiscal worry (Bloomberg, [Aug 12]) and stepping up offshore trust tax enforcement on wealthy clients in Singapore and Hong Kong (BigGo Finance, [Aug 13]). Former premier Zhu Rongji, architect of the market reforms, died at 97 (Reuters, [Aug 12]). Trade frictions are building into the summit: the White House says more than 40 nations help China dodge tariffs (The Washington Post, [Aug 13]), Trump launched tariffs targeting Chinese drone technology (Financial Times, [Aug 13]) after a 15% tariff on polysilicon (Nikkei Asia, [Aug 7]), and a US-China "Board of Investment" has stalled before the expected Trump-Xi meeting (South China Morning Post, [Aug 13]). Beijing answered with anti-dumping tariffs of up to 54.3% on US and Mexican pecans (Global Times, [Aug 10]) while accelerating US soybean buying (AgroLatam, [Aug 10]).

Markets & Flows: Bonds are the clean expression of the easing bias; equities are not. The Hang Seng closed 1.1% lower (chinadailyhk, [Aug 13]), JD.com slid after a rare revenue plunge (Financial Times, [Aug 13]), and the AI surge pushed the A-H premium to a 1-year high (South China Morning Post, [Aug 13]). Regulators are clamping down on margin debt after the AI stock frenzy (Bloomberg, [Aug 10]), and the PBOC made its largest monthly gold purchase since 2023 (Kitco, [Aug 7]).

New / Most Important: The first mid-month overnight reverse repo is the operational follow-through to the Q2 report. By shifting focus to the overnight rate and diversifying loan pricing benchmarks, the PBOC is lowering funding costs without a headline cut, and the bond market has taken the hint with the 10-year at an over 1-year low. It is accommodation aimed at banks' costs; whether it reaches borrowers is the question the July credit data will answer.

Playbook ✓: Accommodative liquidity, falling bond yields, export resilience and weak consumption are on script.  |  ⚠️ Divergence: Easier money is meeting PMIs of 49.2 and 49.0, falling car sales and a steel price at a near-decade low, so the transmission to global assets runs through weak construction rather than a reflation trade. Chinese port stocks are rising as steel demand softens (Hellenic Shipping News, [Aug 11]), which leaves iron ore and AUD exposed while the RBA leans hawkish; European exporters face a German trade deficit with China at record levels (Centre for European Reform (CER), [Aug 9]); and WTI at $81.96 remains a terms-of-trade tax on margins as PPI eases.
