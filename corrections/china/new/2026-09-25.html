🇨🇳 China  Bearish  → easing bias but constrained

Macro Stance: Easing runs through liquidity, not rates. The loan prime rates were held for a 16th month (Reuters, [Sep 19]) as the Fed hike narrows the room to ease (investingLive, [Sep 20]); instead the PBOC will offer banks up to 1 trillion yuan a day over the holidays (Bloomberg, [Sep 23]) and its third-quarter policy committee pledged to stay 'appropriately loose' (TradingView, [Sep 24]). The yuan hit a fresh multi-year peak as the PBOC eased its curb before the summit (Reuters, [Sep 21]), but a fix at 6.7468 against an estimate of 6.6971 (investingLive, [Sep 22]) marks the ceiling. August CPI rose 0.8% and PPI 3.8% (BigGo Finance, [Sep 8]): energy-driven factory prices, barely positive consumer prices.

PMIs & Activity: The August official manufacturing PMI rebounded to 49.8 with new orders back in expansion (BigGo Finance, [Aug 30]), below 50 for a second month, while the private gauge rose to 51.5 (BigGo Finance, [Aug 31]) and private services to 51.4 (BigGo Finance, [Sep 2]). Reconciled: the private surveys expand, the official one does not, and weak official services mark an uneven recovery (Reuters, [Aug 30]). Hard data split the same way: output growth quickened to 5.2% (BigGo Finance, [Sep 14]), but retail sales rose only 0.4% against 0.6% expected (investingLive, [Sep 14]) and fixed-asset investment fell 7.2% in eight months (Xinhua, [Sep 15]). Exports surged 25% in August (Financial Times, [Sep 7]) while imports missed (CNBC, [Sep 7]).

Credit Impulse: August bank lending disappointed (Reuters, [Sep 14]) and credit expansion missed forecasts as loan demand sagged (Bloomberg, [Sep 14]), so loans and the aggregate do not conflict this month: both missed. The composition is the story: bond and equity financing exceeded loans in social financing for the first time (BigGo Finance, [Sep 14]), so issuance, not bank lending, carries the aggregate while households and firms decline to borrow. The PBOC is relaxed, holding rates as it signals slower credit (Chosunbiz, [Sep 20]), and regulators told e-commerce apps to stop selling loans (Sixth Tone, [Sep 21]). The one forced credit flow is defensive: banks were asked to keep Vanke loans off bad-debt books (Reuters, [Sep 22]).

Property: August home prices extended their decline across 70 cities, with tier-1 cities stabilising (BigGo Finance, [Sep 14]), and property investment fell 19.9% in eight months, the steepest decline on record (BigGo Finance, [Sep 14]); land sales revenue was down 28.6% (Devdiscourse, [Sep 18]). Relief this week was marginal: first-tier new home sales jumped over 10% (BigGo Finance, [Sep 23]) and Beijing now requires topped-out structures for presales (China Daily, [Sep 24]), while property stocks surged on mortgage-subsidy rumours that analysts called unlikely to be implemented (BigGo Finance, [Sep 23]).

Fiscal, Policy & Trade: Xi arrived with China's trade engine roaring (Reuters, [Sep 21]) and a 40% share of global container exports (Financial Times, [Sep 21]), and got a two-month truce extension while both sides work on a bigger deal (Reuters, [Sep 23]), but no breakthroughs (Reuters, [Sep 24]). Beijing kept its levers visible: rare earth exports to the US fell 20% before the summit (TradingView, [Sep 21]) and drug precursors joined the export control list (Reuters, [Sep 22]). Europe is hardening: the ECB finds China's industrial rise hits German manufacturers hard (Reuters, [Sep 22]). At home fiscal policy is tight, with January-August revenue up 5.7% (Reuters, [Sep 18]) as spending slumps (Bloomberg, [Sep 18]); the Politburo has set the Central Committee plenum for late October (U.S. News & World Report, [Sep 21]).

Markets & Flows: Mainland stocks posted their biggest one-day drop in a month on summit caution (TradingView, [Sep 24]) and the Hang Seng fell 1.69% in the morning as tech and property tumbled (BigGo Finance, [Sep 24]), after Chinese AI firms fell on a report of a DeepSeek and Moonshot probe (Bloomberg, [Sep 23]). Reserves keep rotating: China imported a record of over 1,000 tonnes of gold this year (Financial Times, [Sep 22]) as its Treasury holdings hit an 18-year low (Businesskorea, [Sep 18]).

New / Most Important: The summit delivered a two-month truce and no breakthroughs, and the market answered with the biggest one-day mainland drop in a month. Beijing pairs that diplomatic holding pattern with one at home: no rate cut, holiday liquidity of up to 1 trillion yuan a day and a pledge to step up counter-cyclical adjustments (Newsquawk, [Sep 24]) without saying how. The pulse it manages is a factory PMI below 50, retail sales at 0.4% and credit that missed on loans and the aggregate.

Playbook ✓: China remains in a low-growth loop with limited stimulus firepower, and rising US rates and a stronger dollar keep tightening its room to ease: the PBOC manages the fix and supplies liquidity rather than cutting. Key data on China is due next week per ING (ing think, [Sep 24]).  |  ⚠️ Divergence: Supply is strong and demand weak: output up 5.2% and exports up 25% against retail sales at 0.4% and property investment down 19.9%. That mix transmits abroad as competition, not demand. European exporters face it directly, as the ECB's German findings show; Australia's factory PMI fell to 49.3 (Mitrade, [Sep 22]) while China's imports miss and its property investment shrinks, leaving the AUD and iron ore without a China demand prop just as the RBA is set to hike. WTI at 92.20 is a terms-of-trade tax that a sub-50 PMI cannot absorb.
