🇨🇳 China  Mixed  → easing bias maintained

Macro Stance: Rates are on hold and the budget is doing the easing. China kept loan prime rates steady for a 15th month (Reuters, [Aug 19]), the 1-year at 3.0% and the 5-year at 3.5% (BigGo Finance, [Aug 19]), while expanding consumer and small-business loan subsidies (The Asian Banker, [Aug 21]); liquidity runs through the overnight window, with 7-day reverse repos at zero for seven straight sessions (BigGo Finance, [Aug 19]). The PBOC keeps leaning against the currency: after the yuan surged to 6.72 per dollar, a 42-month high (BigGo Finance, [Aug 20]), it set the fixing weaker than the previous fix (CryptoRank, [Aug 20]) and far weaker than the Reuters estimate (investingLive, [Aug 20]), and the offshore yuan has now slipped on another weak fixing (TradingView, [Aug 23]). Prices leave room to ease: July CPI rose 0.5% and PPI growth slowed to 3.5%, both missing expectations (BigGo Finance, [Aug 8]).

PMIs & Activity: July's official manufacturing PMI fell to 49.2 and non-manufacturing to 49.0 (FXStreet, [Jul 31]); the private factory gauge eased to 50.9 (Fibre2Fashion, [Aug 3]) and private services growth was the slowest since September 2024 (Reuters, [Aug 4]). The hard data confirmed the slide: industrial output grew 4.5% y/y and retail sales just 0.6% (Reuters, [Aug 17]), and fixed-asset investment fell 6.7% in the first seven months (Xinhua, [Aug 17]). Goldman puts growth at about 4%, which has revived easing hopes (Bloomberg, [Aug 23]), while the People's Daily insists annual targets are on track (South China Morning Post, [Aug 23]). The top end of consumption is split: luxury sales plunge as the tax push hits rich shoppers (Livemint, [Aug 22]), yet Europe's luxury giants discern green shoots (Bloomberg, [Aug 23]), a stabilising brand channel against a shrinking wealthy customer base. Abroad, euro-zone activity grew at the fastest pace since November (Reuters, [Aug 21]) and the US composite improved to 56 (Kitco, [Aug 21]).

Credit Impulse: July new bank loans posted a record contraction as credit demand faltered (Reuters, [Aug 14]), only the second contraction this year (The Standard (HK), [Aug 14]), yet Bloomberg reports credit growth exceeded forecasts despite the rare loan slump ([Aug 14]). Reconciled: headline financing, which includes bond issuance, beat; bank lending to households and firms shrank, leaving cumulative yuan loans up 10.38 trillion yuan in January–July (Xinhua, [Aug 14]). The expanded subsidised-loan programme (Caixin Global, [Aug 21]) targets that gap without touching the LPR; whether it revives borrowing is the August credit print's question.

Property: July new home prices were stagnant as demand stayed weak (Reuters, [Aug 17]); the 70-city index fell 3.2% y/y, the smallest drop in five months, while property investment slumped nearly 20% (BigGo Finance, [Aug 17]). A flood of discounted auction homes is deepening the slump (Asia Times, [Aug 21]), and Reuters finds the crisis grinding on after the Evergrande sentencing ([Aug 23]), with creditors still owed $300 billion (Fortune, [Aug 21]). Restructuring inches forward as Logan won court approval for its debt plan (Yahoo Finance Singapore, [Aug 23]). Policy remains granular: Shanghai is lowering second-home down payments (South China Morning Post, [Aug 20]) and housing provident fund rules are being reshaped (China.org.cn, [Aug 21]).

Fiscal, Policy & Trade: China pledges greater fiscal support in the second half (China Daily, [Aug 22]) and is stepping it up as growth weakens (The Economic Times, [Aug 22]), but Bloomberg calls the $119 billion answer to sagging investment late ([Aug 23]); the SME interest-subsidy loan cap was raised to 75 million yuan (BigGo Finance, [Aug 20]). Iran is the external risk: Trump's plan to choke Iran's economy risks Chinese blowback (The Japan Times, [Aug 22]), and China and Russia could hobble the plan to isolate Tehran (Al Jazeera, [Aug 20]). On trade, Beijing rejected a US tariff report as Mexico plans new China curbs (South China Morning Post, [Aug 21]), slammed EU foreign-subsidy rules (POLITICO.eu, [Aug 21]), and state media hailed Canada's "Chinese-style" tariff retaliation (Bloomberg, [Aug 23]).

Markets & Flows: Hong Kong reclaimed 26,000 as the Hang Seng jumped 310.97 points (BusinessToday Malaysia, [Aug 22]), but stocks then fell on a tech sell-off (TradingView, [Aug 23]) as Alibaba and YMTC fundraising hit China tech on supply woes (Bloomberg, [Aug 23]). Mainland capital pulled a net HK$10.4 billion from Hong Kong stocks (BigGo Finance, [Aug 20]), and China trimmed US Treasury holdings as the global reserve shift gathers pace (Firstpost, [Aug 18]). Xiaomi's Xring chip partnership with TSMC keeps the hardware story alive.

New / Most Important: The debate has moved from whether Beijing eases to whether it is enough. Goldman's 4% growth estimate has revived easing hopes, the fiscal pledge is official, yet Bloomberg judges the $119 billion investment answer late, iron ore is weakening and equity supply from Alibaba and YMTC is weighing on tech. Markets are pricing the support as too small and too slow to lift August.

Playbook ✓: Rates on hold, incremental fiscal support, city-level property easing and a managed yuan are on script.  |  ⚠️ Divergence: Euro-area and US activity are accelerating without China, whose July loans contracted. The transmission is negative for China-exposed assets: iron ore weakens despite China stimulus (TradingView, [Aug 23]), leaving AUD, already softening on weak jobs, without its usual China cushion. European exporters see mixed demand, wine producers seeking new markets as China sales sour (South China Morning Post, [Aug 23]) while luxury houses spot green shoots. Iranian oil offers to Chinese buyers fell as the US blockade bites (Reuters, [Aug 21]), and WTI at $85.31, up 3.3% in four weeks, is a growing terms-of-trade tax on China.
