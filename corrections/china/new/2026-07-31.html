🇨🇳 China  Mixed  → easing bias

Macro Stance: Policy rates are on hold but the easing bias is now explicit. The PBOC set the overnight reverse repo rate steady at 1.25% (Reuters, [Jul 28]) and maintained liquidity support before month-end (TradingView, [Jul 28]), after injecting a total of 2.1 trillion yuan (Reuters, [Jul 24]); one read of the Politburo is that RRR and rate cuts are likely in Q3 (BigGo Finance, [Jul 30]). The bond market agrees: the 10-year yield hit a one-month low (TradingView, [Jul 30]) and the 30-year a year low (finance.biggo.com, [Jul 30]). The yuan rose to its highest since early 2023 as the dollar dropped after the Fed (Bloomberg, [Jul 30]), after a fix at a 3-1/2-year high (Business Recorder, [Jul 29]); a weaker fixing is now expected to temper the gains (Bloomberg, [Jul 30]). June CPI rose only 1% (Global Times, [Jul 9]) while PPI climbed 4.1% (finance.biggo.com, [Jul 8]).

PMIs & Activity: The growth pulse turned down. July factory activity unexpectedly contracted on a demand slump and typhoons (CNBC, [Jul 30]), ending a four-month expansion streak (Forex Factory, [Jul 30]) and falling for the first time in five months (Financial Times, [Jul 30]); Reuters ties it to faltering demand and slowdown concerns ([Jul 30]), against a poll that had expected a stall (Reuters, [Jul 29]) and June's print of 50.3 (Reuters, [Jun 29]). ING calls the drop sharp, with markets awaiting policy support ([Jul 30]). Exports are no longer a clean offset: US-bound shipments fell in July after a brief recovery (CNBC, [Jul 30]). Q2 GDP grew 4.3%, below the target range (Bloomberg, [Jul 15]), and first-half profits rose 18.7% as a tech boom masks the property slump (Caixin Global, [Jul 27]), at the slowest growth this year (Financial Times, [Jul 26]). Consumer demand is soft: Apple's China sales slowed (Yahoo Finance, [Jul 30]) and BMW's profit fell as the China slump deepened (Investing.com, [Jul 30]).

Credit Impulse: June new bank loans rose less than expected (The Star, [Jul 15]) and credit growth missed as the PBOC flagged a "new normal" (Caixin Global, [Jul 16]); household loans fell by 366.8 billion yuan in the first half (36Kr, [Jul 17]). Cash at Chinese banks has swelled to a record as borrowing slows (Nikkei Asia, [Jul 28]): liquidity is ample, credit demand is not. Pricing is loosening quietly, with banks breaking with the lending benchmark (Bloomberg, [Jul 24]) and launching repo-linked loans (Yahoo Finance, [Jul 27]). Falling long-bond yields are the market's verdict on the impulse: flat, with households negative.

Property: June new home prices fell at a slower pace (Reuters, [Jul 14]) and first-tier prices extended a four-month rebound (South China Morning Post, [Jul 15]), but resale prices fell faster (Reuters, [Jun 30]) and property investment slumped 18 percent y/y in January–June (The Standard (HK), [Jul 15]). State-linked names still buy prime land, with Poly Developments and China Resources Land spending 23.2 billion yuan in a day in Shanghai (finance.biggo.com, [Jul 28]), and property stocks rose on hopes for supportive policies (Moomoo, [Jul 28]). Stress is surfacing in Hong Kong, where builder Parkview missed an interest payment on a loan (The Business Times, [Jul 30]).

Fiscal, Policy & Trade: The Politburo has spoken, and the headlines diverge in tone more than substance. Leaders pledged to support the economy without big new stimulus plans (Reuters, [Jul 30]); SCMP stresses pledges of policy support and spending ([Jul 30]), ING a supportive tone with few tangible measures ([Jul 30]), the WSJ little appetite for major stimulus ([Jul 30]), while Bloomberg reports top officials will consider new stimulus after the slowdown ([Jul 30]). Reconciled: incremental measures now, as The Straits Times puts it ([Jul 30]), a larger package only if data worsen; BigGo expects fiscal policy to accelerate ([Jul 30]). On trade, China says the US pledged to cap replacement tariffs at 20% (Bloomberg, [Jul 27]), Bessent says US officials pressed China on rare earths and farm goods commitments (Reuters, [Jul 30]), and Beijing vows to "expand" trade ties as leaders turn cautious (Nikkei Asia, [Jul 30]).

Markets & Flows: Chinese stocks are on track for their worst month in a decade (Financial Times, [Jul 30]) as chip stocks slump on a rotation away from tech (Bloomberg, [Jul 30]), despite firms readying the most buybacks since the trade war (The Edge Malaysia, [Jul 30]). ASML rebounded 6.8% as the China threat looks less immediate (Yahoo Finance, [Jul 30]), and Zhongji slipped on its $6.8 billion Hong Kong debut (Reuters, [Jul 30]). Central banks led by China drove record Q2 gold demand (TipRanks, [Jul 30]).

New / Most Important: The data and the Politburo landed together, and they do not match. July factory activity contracted for the first time in five months, yet leaders chose support without a big new package. That leaves the burden on monetary policy, where RRR and rate cuts are now the market's base case, and on falling bond yields, while a yuan at its highest since early 2023 tightens conditions for exporters.

Playbook ✓: Incremental Politburo support, liquidity easing, weak property and a managed trade truce are on script.  |  ⚠️ Divergence: The consensus waited for the Politburo to turn the cycle; instead the PMI slipped into contraction and the response was incremental. With property investment down 18 percent, iron ore easing as faltering China demand offset stimulus hopes (Business Recorder, [Jul 28]) leaves AUD exposed alongside the RBA's softer turn. European carmakers absorb a deepening China slump, EM equity flows suffer as Chinese stocks head for their worst month in a decade, and oil at $84.69 stays a terms-of-trade tax on China's margins.
