🇨🇳 China  Mixed  → Policy easing bias

Macro Stance: The fixing has turned. After a reference rate of 6.7698 against an estimate of 6.7083 (investingLive, [Sep 13]), the PBOC strengthened the yuan fix ahead of the Trump-Xi summit (Bloomberg.com, [Sep 14]), setting the midpoint at its strongest in over three and a half years (BigGo Finance, [Sep 14]), while Beijing urges more FX hedging as the strong yuan hits exporters (Reuters, [Sep 14]). Rates are not the tool: the central bank plans new metrics to rein in banks' long-dated bond holdings (Reuters, [Sep 14]) with the 10-year yield near its 2025 low (TradingView, [Sep 14]). August CPI rose 0.8% and PPI 3.8% (BigGo Finance, [Sep 8]) on energy costs (Reuters, [Sep 9]), imported cost-push rather than domestic reflation, so the easing bias holds.

PMIs & Activity: The August activity data confirm a two-speed economy. Industrial output rebounded to 5.2% as the fixed-asset investment decline widened (BigGo Finance, [Sep 14]), beating estimates (TradingView, [Sep 14]) on a tech boom that powers the factories while consumption slows (Yahoo Finance, [Sep 14]); retail sales rose only 0.4% against 0.6% expected (investingLive, [Sep 14]) and urban unemployment hit 5.3% (TradingView, [Sep 14]). The surveys said the same: the official manufacturing PMI was 49.8 with new orders back in expansion (BigGo Finance, [Aug 30]) and the private gauge 51.5 (BigGo Finance, [Aug 31]). Exports jumped 25% (AP News, [Sep 7]) while imports missed estimates (CNBC, [Sep 7]), and refiners lifted throughput as fuel exports rebounded (Reuters, [Sep 14]).

Credit Impulse: August credit disappointed on both measures. Bank loans rose less than expected after July's slump (Reuters, [Sep 14]) and total credit expansion missed forecasts as loan demand sagged (Bloomberg.com, [Sep 14]); unlike July, when the aggregate beat while loans contracted, there is nothing to reconcile this time. Bond and equity financing exceeded loans in social financing for the first time (BigGo Finance, [Sep 14]), and banks keep shifting into bonds as loan demand slumps (Briefs Finance, [Sep 8]), which the PBOC's duration metrics now target. Beijing is recapitalising instead, injecting $43.9 billion into major state banks (China Daily Global Edition, [Sep 14]), a boost The Economist says falls far short (The Economist, [Sep 8]), and is pushing large firms to pay SMEs faster (BigGo Finance, [Sep 14]).

Property: August home prices extended their decline across 70 cities, with the secondhand market weakening while tier-1 cities show stabilisation (BigGo Finance, [Sep 14]); Reuters says prices remain weak, keeping pressure on the economy (Reuters, [Sep 14]). Real estate development investment plunged 19.9% in the first eight months, the steepest decline on record (BigGo Finance, [Sep 14]). The presale overhaul is cooling the land market (Caixin Global, [Sep 10]) and straining local government income (The Straits Times, [Sep 14]), while developers plan pre-emptive price rises before the August 28 policy takes effect (36Kr, [Sep 13]) and Seazen turns to a REIT spinoff for funds (Benzinga, [Sep 14]).

Fiscal, Policy & Trade: Bloomberg says the economy has entered the phase that determines 2026 stimulus (Bloomberg.com, [Sep 14]), and Briefs Finance argued the August data may force stronger support (Briefs Finance, [Sep 13]). The US track is live but fragile: the two sides are consulting on tariff cuts covering $30 billion (Global Times, [Sep 10]), soybean buying has passed the halfway mark of the US pledge (The Edge Singapore, [Sep 14]) and China Gas signed a 20-year LNG deal with Venture Global (Reuters, [Sep 14]), yet a Chinese threat to scrap the summit hit US rare-earth stocks (Yahoo Finance, [Sep 14]). Europe nears crunch time: EU and Chinese trade chiefs hold a call on Thursday (POLITICO.eu, [Sep 14]) as Germany prepares to lobby for more tariffs (Bloomberg.com, [Sep 14]).

Markets & Flows: China stocks fell on the AI selloff (TradingView, [Sep 14]) while the Hang Seng closed higher despite Z.AI slumping 9% (The Standard, [Sep 14]), and the yuan slipped as weak data weighed (TradingView, [Sep 14]). Mainland retail money is rushing into US tech, draining fresh offshore quotas (Caixin Global, [Sep 14]), and China's funding costs have fallen below the US (IDNFinancials, [Sep 14]), with the 10-year yield gap at a record 317 basis points (bloomingbit, [Sep 9]). Alibaba trades at a 30% valuation discount on capex drag.

New / Most Important: The August data deliver the split in one release: factories at 5.2% on tech and exports, retail sales at 0.4%, unemployment at 5.3%, property investment at a record decline and loans below forecast. That is the case for stimulus Bloomberg and Briefs Finance flag, and the stronger fix ahead of the summit shows Beijing will spend currency goodwill before it spends on households.

Playbook ✓: The policy easing bias is consensus and the data justify it, but delivery stays targeted: bank capital, SME payment rules and the fixing rather than rate cuts.  |  ⚠️ Divergence: The growth pulse is being exported. Output beating on tech while retail sales and imports miss means more Chinese supply for European exporters, which is why Germany wants tariffs. A record property investment slump weighs on steel and iron ore demand and the AUD, and EM exporters get little from 0.4% retail growth. Oil at 106.20 is a terms-of-trade tax on a large crude importer. The partial offset is the yuan: a stronger fix supports Asian FX at the margin.
