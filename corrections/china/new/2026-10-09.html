🇨🇳 China  Bullish  ↓ easing bias / accommodative

Macro Stance: The PBOC stays accommodative while the Fed and ECB lean hawkish, and it now pairs easing with an explicit currency anchor. After cutting the PSL rate to 1.5% (IndexBox, [Oct 2]) and ramping up structural tools (BigGo Finance, [Oct 4]), it issued a rare statement that China has no need or intention to weaken the yuan for a trade edge (The Standard (HK), [Oct 8]); the yuan hit a four-year high against its basket (Bloomberg, [Oct 8]), with the fix at 6.7367 (Mitrade, [Oct 8]). Easing is passing into the bond market rather than the currency: China's bond yields are falling as they surge everywhere else (Quartz, [Oct 8]), even with CNY 225 billion of special bonds auctioned in one day (BigGo Finance, [Oct 7]). August CPI rose 0.8% and PPI 3.8% (BigGo Finance, [Sep 8]); the September CPI is next (Admirals, [Oct 8]).

PMIs & Activity: September brought the first official expansion in three months: manufacturing at 50.1, exactly the consensus, and non-manufacturing at 50.2 (investingLive, [Sep 29]), with the private RatingDog gauges stronger at 52.1 and 51.6 (investingLive, [Sep 30]), an AI-led rebound (Reuters, [Sep 29]) on top of a 25% August export jump (AP News, [Sep 7]). The domestic side is the test: August retail sales rose only 0.4% (investingLive, [Sep 14]) and Q3 growth is estimated below target at 4.4% (Nikkei Asia, [Oct 6]). The holiday reads conflict and reconcile on volume versus value: retail sales rose as travel volumes surged (www.citynewsservice.cn, [Oct 6]), but Bloomberg finds weak holiday spending casting a shadow as markets reopen (Bloomberg, [Oct 8]), so more trips at lower spend per head.

Credit Impulse: August bank loans rose less than expected (Reuters, [Sep 14]) and credit expansion missed forecasts (Bloomberg, [Sep 14]), with bond and equity financing exceeding loans for the first time (BigGo Finance, [Sep 14]): both measures missed, and government bonds carried what growth there was. The small-bank purge is accelerating (CHOSUNBIZ - Chosunbiz, [Oct 6]). The impulse is fiscal: the public budget has climbed to a record high (Table.Briefings, [Oct 6]).

Property: This is where the bullish case is building. China home sales posted a modest recovery after the latest stimulus (Bloomberg, [Oct 8]), new-home subscriptions in 50 key cities rose by over 15% year-on-year during the National Day holiday (Moomoo, [Oct 8]), and CNBC sees the market possibly set for a turnaround (CNBC, [Oct 8]), with S&P seeing a top-city recovery next year (Traders Union, [Oct 8]). The base is still weak: August home prices fell across 70 cities (BigGo Finance, [Sep 14]), property investment fell 19.9% in eight months (Business Recorder, [Sep 15]) and Vanke reported a RMB 16.02 billion loss (ad-hoc-news.de, [Oct 7]); PAG is in exclusive talks for a stake in an Evergrande unit (Reuters, [Oct 8]).

Fiscal, Policy & Trade: Critics call the response half measures (WSJ, [Oct 8]) that may shore up GDP while reinforcing imbalances (Bloomberg, [Oct 8]); Shanghai trades ahead of a Politburo meeting (BBN Times, [Oct 7]). Europe's trade tensions with China are coming to a head (The New York Times, [Oct 7]): the EU took a harder line as talks kicked off (Bloomberg, [Oct 8]), its envoy called the deficit 'unsustainable' (AFP.com, [Oct 8]) and the sides clashed over PHEV curbs and rare earths (CHOSUNBIZ - Chosunbiz, [Oct 8]), while Beijing pushes for equal dialogue (Reuters, [Oct 8]).

Markets & Flows: Reuters Breakingviews argues China's slow bull market is poised to take off (Reuters, [Oct 8]) and more global investors eye Chinese stocks after 4 years underweight (South China Morning Post, [Oct 8]). The tape is split: the Big Six banks hit record highs while optical stocks plunged (finance.biggo.com, [Oct 8]), and the Hang Seng dropped to a three-month low on financial and tech selling as A shares fell after the holiday (The Standard (HK), [Oct 8]).

New / Most Important: The PBOC's statement ruling out a weaker yuan, delivered as the EU talks opened, is the day's key signal. It answers Brussels on the currency, anchors foreign investors' FX risk just as underweight funds look again, and lets easing run through falling bond yields instead of depreciation. Paired with the first holiday lift in home subscriptions, it gives the slow-bull thesis its two ingredients: a stable currency and a housing floor.

Playbook ✓: The consensus script says China is a value trap, cheap but uninvestable on property, local debt and geopolitics, with EU friction following Washington's line, and weak holiday spending and missed credit still fit it.  |  ⚠️ Divergence: Global allocators sit at multi-year underweights, so a shift from 'uninvestable' to 'tactical' could force a sharp flow reversal. Transmission runs through the yuan and housing, not through industrial metals: a firm yuan reduces the PBOC's intervention burden and EM Asia's competitive-devaluation risk, and if subscriptions turn into sales and starts, iron ore demand and the AUD gain a property channel they have lacked. Any de-escalation on hybrid curbs would lift European autos and Chinese exporters together; failure leaves the slow bull purely domestic and services-led.
