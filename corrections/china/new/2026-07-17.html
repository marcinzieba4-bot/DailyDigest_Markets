🇨🇳 China  Mixed  → easing bias

Macro Stance: The PBOC is managing, not cutting. It sees a stable renminbi amid two-way fluctuations (China Daily, [Jul 16]), is moving to deepen offshore yuan liquidity and broaden asset supply (Caixin, [Jul 16]), and flagged slower credit growth as a "new normal" (Caixin, [Jul 16]). Front-end support came through tax-week liquidity (Bloomberg, [Jul 14]) with a floor that bills not be re-discounted below 0.5% (Reuters, [Jul 14]). Prices: June CPI rose 1% (Global Times, [Jul 9]) and producer inflation hit a four-year high (Reuters, [Jul 8]), which Bloomberg calls "bad inflation" ([Jul 15]).

PMIs & Activity: Second-quarter GDP grew 4.3%, below the official target range (Bloomberg, [Jul 15]), even as June industrial output rose 5.3% y/y and retail sales returned to growth (BusinessLine, [Jul 15]) and the urban unemployment rate fell further (China Daily, [Jul 16]). The engine is narrow: Capital Economics says AI is now the main driver of growth ([Jul 16]) and the WSJ describes a two-speed economy pairing high-tech success with domestic gloom ([Jul 16]). Investment is sliding as infrastructure joins property (Caixin, [Jul 15]), exports jumped 27% y/y in June (Reuters, [Jul 13]) and the official manufacturing PMI was 50.3 (Reuters, [Jun 29]). July has started soft, with NEV retail sales down 8% in the first two weeks (CnEVPost, [Jul 15]).

Credit Impulse: June credit growth missed forecasts (Caixin, [Jul 16]) and new bank loans rose less than expected (The Star, [Jul 15]). In May the headline beat because government bonds carried total social financing to 7.7% growth (CGTN, [Jun 12]) while loans missed (Reuters, [Jun 12]); in June neither leg delivered. Record consumer defaults undermine the push to boost spending (Reuters, [Jul 15]), LGFVs that have added debt face curbs on new bonds (Bloomberg, [Jul 15]), and one of China's top investors says finance, not AI, is the country's biggest bottleneck (CNBC, [Jul 16]). With the PBOC calling slower credit the new normal, the impulse is flat at best.

Property: Property investment slumped 18% y/y in January–June (The Standard, [Jul 15]). Prices are stabilising only at the top: first-tier home prices extended a four-month rebound (SCMP, [Jul 15]), while nationally new home prices declined at a slower pace (The Standard, [Jul 15]). Developers face a fresh liquidity crunch (Nikkei Asia, [Jul 13]), and Morgan Stanley is screening for property stocks that can weather near-term weakness (Investing.com, [Jul 16]).

Fiscal, Policy & Trade: Beijing is betting on faster state-backed projects to shore up growth and avoid broad stimulus (Reuters, [Jul 16]), while Breakingviews warns the local-debt cleanup leaves a growth mess (Reuters, [Jul 16]) and Westpac argues for urgent stimulus ([Jul 15]). The July Politburo meeting is being prepared (Sinocism, [Jul 13]). Europe is hardening: Merz is inching Germany toward backing an EU push against China (Bloomberg, [Jul 16]), and Brussels is preparing an emergency response to renewed rare-earth controls (EU Today, [Jul 16]) after the IEA put $6.5 trillion of Western industry at risk (Reuters, [Jul 15]). With Washington the mood is calmer: the first US LNG cargo in a year docked in China (Bloomberg, [Jul 16]).

Markets & Flows: The AI trade cracked on the mainland. ChiNext plunged over 7% as nearly 5,000 stocks fell in the global tech sell-off (BigGo Finance, [Jul 16]), the Shanghai benchmark lost 1.85% on weak GDP data and the chip sell-off (BBN Times, [Jul 16]), and the chip pullback drove investor fear to a record high (Bloomberg, [Jul 16]). CXMT's $8.6 billion IPO drew less frenzied demand (Reuters, [Jul 16]). Hong Kong diverged, reclaiming 25,000 (Moomoo, [Jul 16]) as Alibaba jumped 7.9% on the Apple AI approval (TradingView, [Jul 16]), though its discount to China widened on liquidity pressure (Bloomberg, [Jul 16]).

New / Most Important: Beijing has chosen its response to the growth miss: faster state-backed projects, not broad stimulus, alongside a central bank that calls slower credit the new normal. That puts the burden on the state balance sheet just as the local-debt cleanup constrains it, and leaves the private side, where loans missed and defaults are at a record, without a direct lift. The ChiNext rout matters for the same reason: if the AI engine that carried the quarter wobbles, there is no domestic-demand engine ready to take over before the Politburo meets.

Playbook ✓: Targeted, investment-led support, a managed yuan, weak private credit and a property drag are on script.  |  ⚠️ Divergence: The consensus wanted a demand stimulus after a 4.3% quarter; Beijing is offering projects and a stable currency instead. The transmission splits by asset. Faster state-backed projects are a modest positive for bulk demand, and BHP has already won higher quarterly iron ore prices despite China's buying curbs (Reuters, [Jul 16]), a near-term support for AUD that property investment down 18% will limit. European exporters face the opposite: no Chinese demand push, a record surplus and a German government drifting toward EU action. Oil gains nothing from a project-led plan while Chinese crude imports sit near a decade low, and EM Asia's AI-hardware exporters now carry China's chip-sector volatility.
