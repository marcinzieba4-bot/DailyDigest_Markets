🇨🇳 China  Bearish  → easing bias

Macro Stance: The PBOC is shifting its easing tools, not its bias. Overnight reverse repos debuted at 1.25% (Reuters, [Jun 28]) and the injection was doubled (Reuters, [Jun 29]). It then signalled easing with a trillion-yuan outright reverse repo, ending a three-month tapering streak (BigGo Finance, [Jul 3]), while cutting bond purchases on wariness over falling yields (Bloomberg, [Jul 2]); net bond operations injected only 10 billion yuan in June (Macau Business, [Jul 3]). The reconciliation: liquidity now comes through repo, not outright bond buying, so the PBOC eases funding without validating the deflationary bond rally. The fix went the other way: USD/CNY was set at 6.8047 against 6.8088 previously (TMGM, [Jul 3]), firmer than estimates, which VT Markets reads as managed yuan strength amid softer growth [Jul 3]. May CPI rose only 1.2% (China Daily, [Jun 10]) while factory-gate inflation hit a near four-year high (Reuters, [Jun 9]).

PMIs & Activity: June's upturn is narrow. The official manufacturing PMI rose to 50.3 and non-manufacturing to 50.2 (Reuters, [Jun 29]); the private factory gauge grew for a seventh month (China Daily, [Jul 2]) but less than expected (Bloomberg, [Jun 30]). On services the headlines conflict: Reuters reports a slight slowdown [Jul 2] and The Business Times says the gauge fared better than forecast despite the slip [Jul 3], so it slowed, but by less than feared. Consumption is weakening: preliminary data show NEV retail sales fell 7% in June (CnEVPost, [Jul 3]) as the subsidy pullback hits auto and appliance sales (Nikkei Asia, [Jul 2]). May's split stands: output growth quickened to 4.5% (China Daily, [Jun 16]) and exports jumped 19.4% (AP News, [Jun 9]), but retail sales fell 0.6% (Il Sole 24 Ore, [Jun 15]) and fixed-asset investment fell 4.1% in five months (Macau Business, [Jun 16]).

Credit Impulse: Total social financing grew 7.7% in May (CGTN, [Jun 12]) and topped forecasts (Bloomberg, [Jun 12]), but new loans missed as the property slump curbed demand (Reuters, [Jun 12]): government bonds, not private demand. Credit stress is surfacing in small lenders: China took over private lender Zhongbang Bank, citing credit risks (The Standard, [Jul 3]), and Beijing is capping LGFV bond yields (IFR, [Jul 2]). With debt above 300% of GDP (UPI, [Jun 29]), the private impulse is negative.

Property: Asia Times says the housing market is in free fall as buyers wait for floor prices [Jul 3], and Briefs Finance has home prices slipping again [Jul 3], consistent with resale prices falling faster in June (Reuters, [Jun 30]). Sales are concentrating, not recovering: top-tier cities overtook regional hubs to drive property sales (Caixin, [Jul 3]), and the top 100 developers' June sales rose 11.8% month on month on a half-year push (Moomoo, [Jul 1]). Real-estate investment fell 16.2% y/y in January–May (GMK Center, [Jun 18]). Resolution is state-led: Vanke named a former Shenzhen financial regulator as president (Caixin, [Jul 3]) and Country Garden cleared its first offshore coupon after its overhaul (Caixin, [Jul 2]).

Fiscal, Policy & Trade: Fiscal support is receding: tax breaks for green vehicles end in 2027 (The Standard, [Jul 3]) and a tax crackdown is hitting listed firms (SCMP, [Jul 2]). Control is rising, with curbs on foreign investments (Times of India, [Jul 1]) and a landmark law over offshore tech transfers (SCMP, [Jul 1]). On trade, Beijing signals openness to cut the EU deficit as Brussels toughens (SCMP, [Jul 3]); von der Leyen says the EU will retaliate if China misses October (Euronews, [Jul 3]), while the chief trade MEP calls that deadline "not realistic at all" (Euronews, [Jul 3]). With Washington, the two sides target tariff cuts on farm goods (Bloomberg, [Jul 2]).

Markets & Flows: China shares ended higher as technology stocks rebounded (Business Standard, [Jul 3]) and the Hang Seng rose 0.8% on tech and financials (Dimsum Daily, [Jul 3]), after a three-week mainland low (The Star, [Jul 2]). Southbound demand is selective: mainland investors are buying Hong Kong shares, favouring SMIC and Zhipu AI (SCMP, [Jul 3]), and booming AI chip trade is sealing Hong Kong's role as a gateway (Bloomberg, [Jul 3]). The capital wall keeps rising, with curbs on higher-yielding offshore debt (Bloomberg, [Jun 29]) and a shutdown of retail gold trading (TipRanks, [Jul 1]); Adam Tooze calls it China's "walled-in wealth" [Jul 1].

New / Most Important: The takeover of Zhongbang Bank on credit risks is the week's bearish marker: the property and consumer squeeze is now reaching small private lenders. The PBOC's answer is a trillion-yuan outright reverse repo rather than bond buying, and a firmer fix that leans against yuan weakness. That is a defensive easing bias, protecting funding and the currency while NEV sales fall and home prices slip again.

Playbook ✓: Stimulus-lite, falling home prices, receding consumer subsidies and a managed yuan are on script.  |  ⚠️ Divergence: The consensus reads June's PMI as a turn; a bank seizure, weaker NEV sales and the PBOC's resistance to its own bond rally say the domestic cycle is still deteriorating. Transmission runs three ways. Europe: the WSJ describes China devastating the last stronghold of German industry [Jul 3], and Berlin is pledging to defend trade (SCMP, [Jul 3]); European exporters lose share whether or not October brings retaliation. Bulk commodities: with real-estate investment down 16.2%, construction demand for iron ore keeps fading, a drag on AUD that export PMIs do not offset. Oil: some fuel export curbs are being lifted on ample supplies (Bloomberg, [Jun 30]), more barrels into a market where WTI is already down 26.1% in four weeks. Hong Kong rallies on southbound AI flows are flows, not fundamentals.
