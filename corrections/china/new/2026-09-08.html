🇨🇳 China  Mixed  → unclear

Macro Stance: The PBOC is leaning against yuan strength rather than cutting rates. It set a weaker fixing (VT Markets, [Sep 7]) against a Reuters estimate of 6.7104 (Moomoo, [Sep 7]), yet the yuan hit a fresh three-and-a-half-year high despite the weak guidance (The Edge Malaysia, [Sep 7]); Chinese banks are buying US Treasuries after wooing dollar deposits (Reuters, [Sep 6]), and forex reserves rose more than expected in August (Reuters, [Sep 7]). The central bank bought a net 50 billion yuan of sovereign bonds in August (TradingView, [Sep 2]) and added the most gold since 2023 even as prices jumped (Bloomberg.com, [Sep 7]). July CPI slowed to 0.5% and PPI to 3.5%, both missing expectations (BigGo Finance, [Aug 8]): price pressure is too weak to justify a strong currency.

PMIs & Activity: Every August survey improved. The official manufacturing PMI rebounded to 49.8 with new orders back in expansion (BigGo Finance, [Aug 30]), still a second month of contraction (CNBC, [Aug 30]); the private manufacturing PMI rose to 51.5 (BigGo Finance, [Aug 31]) and private services to 51.4 (BigGo Finance, [Sep 2]). Reconciled: the official gauge is below 50, the private surveys well above it, and Natixis calls it stabilisation with an uneven recovery (CNBC, [Sep 1]). Trade now confirms the external leg: exports jumped 25% in August as the trade surplus widened (AP News, [Sep 7]), exports to the US surged 34% (TradingView, [Sep 7]), but imports missed estimates (CNBC, [Sep 7]). Domestic hard data lag: July industrial output grew 4.5% y/y and retail sales only 0.6% (Reuters, [Aug 17]), and fixed-asset investment fell 6.7% in seven months (Xinhua, [Aug 17]).

Credit Impulse: Credit is being repaired from the top. China will issue 300 billion yuan in special treasury bonds to boost the capital of financial firms (State Council Information Office, [Sep 7]), part of a $54 billion injection into state banks and insurers (Reuters, [Sep 6]). Demand is the problem: July new loans posted a record contraction (Reuters, [Aug 14]) even as credit growth exceeded forecasts (Bloomberg.com, [Aug 14]); reconciled, the aggregate holds up while the loan book shrinks. Breakingviews argues the recap may achieve its least important aim (Reuters, [Sep 7]), while analysts call it a first step to easing financial strains (South China Morning Post, [Sep 7]). Capital adds lending capacity; it does not create borrowers.

Property: China is calling time on the home-sales system that worsened its meltdown (Bloomberg.com, [Sep 1]), and Goldman sees the new presale rules cutting land sales by 30% (The Business Times, [Sep 7]). Guangdong held emergency talks with developers as the policy shift sparked uncertainty over off-plan sales rules (BigGo Finance, [Sep 7]), and SCMP argues the presale rules are still a source of risk (South China Morning Post, [Sep 7]). For buyers, 40-year mortgages have landed in Shanghai (BigGo Finance, [Sep 3]). The data are weak: July new home prices were stagnant (Reuters, [Aug 17]) and first-tier prices flat, ending a four-month rebound (South China Morning Post, [Aug 17]).

Fiscal, Policy & Trade: The G20 left China isolated: Bessent said 19 finance ministers agreed 'cheap exports' are unsustainable, with China the dissenter (AP News, [Sep 2]), and Beijing accused the group of promoting protectionism (CNBC, [Sep 3]). Šefčovič says China must deliver by October or face harsher measures (Euronews.com, [Sep 2]) and the EU trade chief will meet CEOs over China strategy (POLITICO.eu, [Sep 7]). Washington mixes pressure and deals: some Chinese rare-earth firms halted US shipments (Reuters, [Sep 4]), yet the two sides debate 'nonsensitive' tariff cuts ahead of the Trump-Xi summit (Nikkei Asia, [Sep 2]) and Xi is set to bring top business leaders to Washington (Yahoo Finance, [Sep 7]). At home, Beijing deployed its first 2026 policy financing funds (Reuters, [Sep 3]).

Markets & Flows: The recap did not lift lenders: bank and insurer shares slid after the injection plan (Nikkei Asia, [Sep 7]) and the Hang Seng dipped 237 points as insurers and banks weighed (The Standard (HK), [Sep 7]), while tech climbed (Finimize, [Sep 7]). China escaped the global bond rout for the wrong reasons (Reuters, [Sep 2]) and the China-US yield disparity hit a record (Nikkei Asia, [Sep 4]); offshore renminbi borrowing hit a record high of nearly $150bn (Financial Times, [Sep 7]). Equities ignore earnings: profits rose 25.7% while the CSI 300 fell 9% (The Next Web, [Sep 5]), though investors are turning to Chinese stocks as an alternative to crowded AI trades (Yahoo Finance, [Sep 5]).

New / Most Important: The August trade print is the week's defining data point: a 25% export surge that the FT says sets the stage for a record annual trade surplus (Financial Times, [Sep 7]), paired with imports that missed estimates. It confirms the private PMIs but in the worst possible shape for diplomacy, landing days after 19 G20 finance ministers lined up against cheap exports. CNBC calls China's weak consumer the world's problem (CNBC, [Sep 7]) and ABN AMRO sees domestic imbalances worsening as trade tensions brew (ABN AMRO, [Sep 7]).

Playbook ✓: China remains a muddle-through story where stimulus, now through bank capital, liquidity and the fixing, prevents collapse but does not reignite domestic demand.  |  ⚠️ Divergence: The external and domestic engines are pulling apart: exports up 25% with imports missing, while banks are recapitalised into a record loan contraction. Better new orders support iron ore demand expectations and the AUD at the margin, but an export-led upswing with soft imports means China exports its overcapacity, so European exporters face more Chinese competition and tougher EU lines while EM exporters to China see little benefit. WTI at $92.32 is a terms-of-trade tax that eats into the gains the PMIs imply.
