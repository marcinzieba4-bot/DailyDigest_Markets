🇨🇳 China  Mixed  → easing bias maintained

Macro Stance: China remains in a counter-cyclical easing posture, still dealing with structural headwinds from the trade war and the property sector, but the currency is being managed through the banks rather than the fixing alone. The PBOC's reference rate came in firmer, 6.7787 vs. 6.7807 previous (www.tmgm.com, [Sep 4]), after the yuan broke 6.72 to a three-and-a-half-year high (BigGo Finance, [Sep 3]), while Chinese banks are buying US Treasuries as Beijing tries to slow yuan gains (Firstpost, [Sep 4]). Commerzbank argues the services PMI rebound keeps the PBOC cautious (Mitrade, [Sep 4]), so easing runs through the balance sheet — net purchases of 50 billion yuan of sovereign bonds in August (TradingView, [Sep 2]) — not rate cuts. July CPI slowed to 0.5% and PPI to 3.5%, both missing expectations (BigGo Finance, [Aug 8]).

PMIs & Activity: The August surveys stabilised. The official manufacturing PMI rebounded to 49.8 with new orders back in expansion (BigGo Finance, [Aug 30]); the private manufacturing PMI rose to 51.5, its longest expansion streak in five years (BigGo Finance, [Aug 31]); and private services activity quickened on stronger domestic demand (KLSE Screener, [Sep 3]). ING nonetheless sees the recovery stalling as the K-shaped divergence widens (ing think, [Sep 3]). Reconciled: the surveys capture exporters, AI hardware and private services, while household goods demand and investment keep weakening — August NEV retail sales fell 4% to 1.07 million (CnEVPost, [Sep 3]), China signals urgency over construction timelines amid an investment decline (South China Morning Post, [Sep 4]), and industrial profit growth hit a seven-month low in July (Anadolu Ajansı, [Aug 27]).

Credit Impulse: July bank loans posted a record contraction (Reuters, [Aug 14]) even as credit growth exceeded forecasts (Bloomberg, [Aug 14]); reconciled, government bonds carry the aggregate while private borrowing shrinks, and the bond leg strengthens as China speeds up issuance after a national meeting on investment (South China Morning Post, [Aug 31]). Credit quality is under scrutiny: S&P Global Ratings warns of risks from China's generous credit ratings (Reuters, [Sep 3]), and a China indicator that supports risk-taking in stocks and bitcoin is flashing red (CoinDesk, [Sep 2]). Beijing is reforming property project financing with greater lead-bank oversight (The Asian Banker, [Sep 4]), part of ramping up credit support in the property overhaul (The Business Times, [Aug 29]).

Property: China tries to look past the property slump (The Economist, [Sep 3]) with rules that move risk away from buyers: the new home finance regime means what you see is what you get (South China Morning Post, [Sep 3]), and 40-year mortgages have landed in Shanghai with age 35 as the cutoff for new loans (BigGo Finance, [Sep 3]). The data remain weak — new home prices were stagnant in July (Reuters, [Aug 17]) — and developers bear the cost: property stocks fell as the rules upend the presale funding playbook (Reuters, [Aug 31]), and Vanke's first-half loss widened as sales slumped (Caixin Global, [Aug 29]).

Fiscal, Policy & Trade: The bilateral track is warming while the multilateral one hardens. Xi seeks to bring a large CEO delegation on his US visit (Reuters, [Sep 4]) and Greer is optimistic the US and China can stabilise ties (South China Morning Post, [Sep 4]), yet Trump and Bessent slam China for a never-ending stream of cheap exports (Yahoo Finance, [Sep 4]), and Chinese rare earth firms have halted some US shipments over geopolitical worries (Reuters, [Sep 4]). Europe is the sharper front: Volkswagen will cut 50,000 more jobs to counter tariffs and Chinese competition (France 24, [Sep 4]), German companies want a tougher EU-China policy (South China Morning Post, [Sep 4]), and Šefčovič says China must deliver by October or face harsher measures (Euronews.com, [Sep 2]). At home Beijing announced a $1 trillion investment push in the sports industry (China Economic Review, [Sep 4]).

Markets & Flows: China's 26% earnings boom landed with a thud in the stock market (Bloomberg.com, [Sep 4]); profits soar but stocks keep sliding (Briefs Finance, [Sep 4]). The Hang Seng closed up 1.75% while Shanghai slipped into the red (24/7 Wall St., [Sep 4]), Chinese stocks head for a weekly loss as the AI rally loses steam (Free Malaysia Today, [Sep 4]), margin debt continues to unwind (South China Morning Post, [Sep 4]) and Morgan Stanley cut its targets for China and Hong Kong indexes (BigGo Finance, [Sep 4]). The China-US yield disparity hit a record amid the global bond rout (Nikkei Asia, [Sep 4]).

New / Most Important: The 26% earnings boom that failed to lift prices is a striking disconnect, and it signals deep structural scepticism from allocators: the capital-flow problem is not an earnings problem but a governance and geopolitical risk premium problem, now compounded by a record yield gap that pulls domestic savings into Treasuries. NerdWallet's "7 Best China Stocks for September" coverage reflects retail interest trying to bottom-fish what institutions are selling.

Playbook ✓: Earnings recovery without equity re-rating is the classic EM value trap pattern.  |  ⚠️ Divergence: The 26% earnings growth is materially above what most bears expected — if the geopolitical risk premium compresses (a successful Xi visit, agriculture deals, trade progress), the snap-back in Chinese equities would be violent. Global risk appetite is not there yet to fund it: credit is contracting outside the state, the official factory PMI is still below 50, and US yields keep drawing capital to the dollar. The transmission runs through growth and flows — stabilising PMIs support iron ore and the AUD at the margin, but an export-led China with a managed yuan is costing European manufacturers jobs, and with WTI at $91.48 the terms-of-trade squeeze limits how much of the profit boom reaches domestic demand and EM exporters.
