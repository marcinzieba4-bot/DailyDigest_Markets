🇨🇳 China  Mixed  → easing bias / liquidity expansion

Macro Stance: China is easing while the Fed, fresh from hawkish minutes, hikes. The PBOC cut the PSL rate to 1.5% (IndexBox, [Oct 2]) and is ramping up structural tools alongside the State Council's incremental policies (BigGo Finance, [Oct 4]); the liquidity expansion now runs through the bond market, with CNY 225 billion of special government bonds auctioned in one day (BigGo Finance, [Oct 7]) and the 30-year at 2.1078% (Newsquawk, [Oct 7]). Bond yields are surging around the world but not in China (WSJ, [Oct 7]). The yuan held steady despite dollar strength during the Golden Week holiday (TradingView, [Oct 7]), UOB sees tight ranges (Mitrade, [Oct 7]), and Beijing defends its yuan policy as Europe presses on the surplus (Gulf Times, [Oct 7]). The PBOC bought 21 tonnes of gold in September, its largest monthly purchase in 3 years (Kitco, [Oct 7]), while forex reserves fell (The Economic Times, [Oct 7]). August CPI rose 0.8% and PPI 3.8% (BigGo Finance, [Sep 8]).

PMIs & Activity: September brought the first official expansion in three months: manufacturing at 50.1, exactly the consensus, and non-manufacturing at 50.2 (investingLive, [Sep 29]), with the private RatingDog gauges stronger at 52.1 and 51.6 (investingLive, [Sep 30]). The rebound is AI-led (Reuters, [Sep 29]) and export-led after a 25% August jump (AP News, [Sep 7]), while August retail sales rose only 0.4% (investingLive, [Sep 14]) and industrial profits 4.2%, the weakest since November (Bloomberg, [Sep 27]). National Day retail sales rose as travel volumes surged (www.citynewsservice.cn, [Oct 6]), but Q3 growth is estimated below target at 4.4% (Nikkei Asia, [Oct 6]) and a US think tank sees China slowing as global growth stays solid (South China Morning Post, [Oct 7]). Nissan is on pace for its worst global sales in 23 years as its China collapse bites (Automotive News, [Oct 7]).

Credit Impulse: August bank loans rose less than expected (Reuters, [Sep 14]) and credit expansion missed forecasts (Bloomberg, [Sep 14]), with bond and equity financing exceeding loans for the first time (BigGo Finance, [Sep 14]): both measures missed, and government bonds carried what growth there was. That mix is intensifying, as this week's special-bond supply shows. The system is being shrunk: China closed 670 banks in 2025 (Yahoo Finance, [Oct 5]) and is accelerating the small-bank purge (CHOSUNBIZ - Chosunbiz, [Oct 6]). Low rates also strain balance sheets, with insurers forced to raise capital (BigGo Finance, [Oct 7]).

Property: The mortgage subsidy is now in force, with Beijing helping homebuyers with interest payments (Table.Briefings, [Oct 7]), and Shenzhen and Guangzhou markets picked up during the holiday (chinadailyhk, [Oct 6]) after Beijing's September new-home sales surged nearly 50% month-on-month (BigGo Finance, [Oct 1]). The subsidy has also hardened a standoff, with second-hand sellers turning defiant on price cuts (BigGo Finance, [Oct 7]). The base is weak: August home prices fell across 70 cities (BigGo Finance, [Sep 14]), property investment fell 19.9% in eight months (Business Recorder, [Sep 15]), and Vanke reported a RMB 16.02 billion loss (ad-hoc-news.de, [Oct 7]).

Fiscal, Policy & Trade: The public budget has climbed to a record high (Table.Briefings, [Oct 6]) and Societe Generale says targeted stimulus supports growth (www.tmgm.com, [Oct 7]); Shanghai trades ahead of a Politburo meeting (BBN Times, [Oct 7]). The EU-China talks began in Beijing amid escalating pressure (Al Jazeera, [Oct 7]), with Brussels seeking to cut its deficit (Reuters, [Oct 7]); China slapped down a request for voluntary curbs on hybrid car exports (Financial Times, [Oct 7]) and warned it has tools ready if tensions escalate (Bloomberg, [Oct 7]), while Germany blocked a logistics sale to Cosco (Reuters, [Oct 7]).

Markets & Flows: Chinese stocks gained on the return from Golden Week (TradingView, [Oct 7]) but lost steam as trade resumed (Investing.com, [Oct 7]); Hong Kong fell over 2% during the holiday (BigGo Finance, [Oct 7]). The RMB 4.66 trillion foreign-inflow figure reported this week has to be read against that: equity flows are not visible in the tape, so the bid is more plausibly in bonds, where yields are not rising.

New / Most Important: The EU talks opened with Beijing holding the line: no voluntary hybrid curbs and a warning that tools are ready. With domestic demand weak and Q3 growth estimated at 4.4%, exports are the one engine running, so China cannot concede volume without a domestic offset; the record budget and the special-bond wave are that offset, and the coming Politburo meeting is where it would be scaled.

Playbook ✓: EU-China trade tension escalating with the trade chief in Beijing is on script, a slow-burn dispute over EVs, hybrids and green tech, as is a drip-feed of targeted easing and bond-funded fiscal support.  |  ⚠️ Divergence: The inflow figure contradicts consensus underweights, but its transmission runs through bonds and the yuan, not through demand. A firm yuan eases the PBOC's constraint and lets fiscal supply expand cheaply; yet with credit missing, property investment falling and retail sales up 0.4%, iron ore and the AUD still lack a property-led bid, and ASX stocks most exposed to the Chinese consumer are sinking fast (AFR, [Oct 6]). European autos face both a collapsing Chinese market and a hybrid fight. The PBOC's gold buying as forex reserves fall is the quiet global signal: official diversification away from dollar assets that persists even with gold weak.
