🇨🇳 China  Mixed  → easing bias, truce stability

Macro Stance: The easing bias turned into action before the holiday, but through structural tools. The PBOC cut the PSL rate by 0.25 percentage point to 1.5% (Forex Factory, [Sep 29]), widened its scope and boosted relending quotas by 700 billion yuan (BigGo Finance, [Sep 29]), which Caixin calls a targeted growth push (Caixin Global, [Sep 29]): a cut to a structural lending rate, not a benchmark one, with US 10Y yields at 5.29% keeping the room for a broad cut narrow. The yuan firmed as exporters converted dollars before the week-long holiday (Business Recorder, [Sep 29]), with the fix at 6.7411 (www.tmgm.com, [Sep 29]), while China's 10Y yield remains at an over one-year low (TradingView, [Sep 29]). August CPI rose 0.8% and PPI 3.8% (BigGo Finance, [Sep 8]).

PMIs & Activity: September brought the first official expansion in three months: manufacturing returned to growth at 50.1 and non-manufacturing printed 50.2 (investingLive, [Sep 29]), with the official composite at a nine-month high (TradingView, [Sep 29]). The private gauges were stronger, factory activity growing at the fastest pace in five months (Investing.com, [Sep 29]) and services at a three-month high (Reuters, [Sep 29]). The 'beat' headlines (TradingView, [Sep 29]) and an official factory print exactly at the 50.1 consensus (investingLive, [Sep 29]) reconcile: manufacturing met forecasts, the upside came from services and the private surveys. Reuters ties the factory expansion to the AI boom (Reuters, [Sep 29]), consistent with August industrial profits rising only 4.2%, the weakest since November (Bloomberg, [Sep 27]), while electronics led January-August gains (CGTN, [Sep 28]); August retail sales rose just 0.4% (investingLive, [Sep 14]).

Credit Impulse: August bank lending disappointed (Reuters, [Sep 14]) and credit expansion missed forecasts (Bloomberg, [Sep 14]), with bond and equity financing exceeding loans for the first time (BigGo Finance, [Sep 14]): no conflict, both measures missed. The new package targets credit rather than igniting it: cheap PBOC loans to clear local government arrears (Briefs Finance, [Sep 29]) recycle money to firms the state owes, and banks are urged to expand financing for AI and asset-light service firms (Caixin Global, [Sep 29]). Household credit stays impaired, with the bad consumer debt cleanup faltering (The Business Times, [Sep 29]).

Property: The central government will subsidise mortgages for the first time, with a 1 percentage point annual rate subsidy worth up to nearly 50,000 yuan on a 1 million yuan loan (BigGo Finance, [Sep 29]), as China turns to mortgage subsidies to boost an ailing economy (Bloomberg, [Sep 29]) in a 'mini stimulus' targeting affordable homes and infrastructure (Nikkei Asia, [Sep 29]). It follows the State Council's move to tap local debt quotas for property and jobs (BigGo Finance, [Sep 28]) and Shanghai's presale rules requiring structural topping-out (BigGo Finance, [Sep 28]). The base it works on is weak: August home prices fell across 70 cities and property investment dropped 19.9% in eight months (BigGo Finance, [Sep 14]).

Fiscal, Policy & Trade: The package delivers on the cabinet's pledge of stronger support to meet the growth target (Reuters, [Sep 28]), alongside fourth-quarter local bond issuance likely around 500 billion yuan (BigGo Finance, [Sep 28]); local governments are told to 'intensify' policy tweaks to boost consumption (South China Morning Post, [Sep 29]). Bloomberg sees it securing the GDP target, not much more (Bloomberg, [Sep 29]). On trade, the US and China pledge tariff cuts on $60 billion of goods (Reuters, [Sep 28]) and Beijing expects trade to grow despite external challenges (Reuters, [Sep 29]), but it vows a response if the EU targets Chinese trade (Reuters, [Sep 29]) after Brussels pushed for import quotas (Euronews, [Sep 28]).

Markets & Flows: China stocks ended higher on the policy support pledge (Reuters, [Sep 29]) and upbeat PMIs (TradingView, [Sep 29]) after sinking to a 13-month low (South China Morning Post, [Sep 28]), but the Hang Seng closed down 0.4% as offshore sellers trimmed China exposure (24/7 Wall St., [Sep 29]). Bloomberg flags a yawning gap between stocks and the yuan in a two-speed economy (Bloomberg, [Sep 29]).

New / Most Important: Both halves of the growth pulse moved on the same day: the official factory PMI back above 50 at 50.1 with non-manufacturing at 50.2, and a targeted package of a PSL cut to 1.5%, 700 billion yuan of relending and the first central mortgage subsidy. The PMI rebound is AI- and export-led; the subsidy is the first policy aimed directly at household housing demand. Neither is broad: the cut is to a targeted lending rate and the 'mini stimulus' is sized to secure the target, not to reflate.

Playbook ✓: The truce extension is consensus, and so is the policy style: structural tools, quota-tapping and subsidies instead of a benchmark rate cut or a fiscal bazooka, with property weakness still weighing on domestic demand and government bond yields suppressed.  |  ⚠️ Divergence: Transmission to global assets is improving at the margin but stays narrow. An AI- and export-led PMI above 50 helps Asian tech supply chains. The mortgage subsidy is the first policy that reaches the property demand iron ore and the AUD need, but with property investment down 19.9% and credit missing, the AUD, under an RBA that just hiked to a 15-year high, gets a sentiment lift rather than a demand impulse. European luxury and auto exporters need a Chinese consumer still held back by 0.4% retail growth, and face retaliation threats over EU curbs; a China that eases selectively but does not reflate leaves EM Asia competing with its exports rather than selling to its demand.
