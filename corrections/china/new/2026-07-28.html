🇨🇳 China  Mixed  → easing bias

Macro Stance: The PBOC is easing through volume, not price: rather than cut benchmark rates, the central bank added the most liquidity in five months (Bloomberg, [Jul 23]) and is running overnight reverse repos alongside a 500b-yuan MLF operation, injecting a total of 2.1 trillion yuan (Reuters, [Jul 24]; China Daily, [Jul 24]). The currency is being managed tightly: the PBOC marginally lowered the daily yuan fix versus the dollar (TradingPedia, [Jul 27]) while Europe scrutinises the yuan's value (Bloomberg, [Jul 21]). Prices are split: June CPI rose only 1% (Global Times, [Jul 9]) while PPI climbed 4.1% (finance.biggo.com, [Jul 8]), a near four-year high that squeezes manufacturers (Reuters, [Jul 8]).

PMIs & Activity: The last official read is June: manufacturing PMI 50.3 and non-manufacturing 50.2 (Reuters, [Jun 29]); July's numbers are the next test (ING Think, [Jul 23]). Q2 GDP grew 4.3%, below the official target range (Bloomberg, [Jul 15]); June industrial output rose 5.3% y/y and retail sales returned to growth (BusinessLine, [Jul 15]), but first-half retail sales were up just 1.3% (Ecns.cn, [Jul 15]) and investment slid as infrastructure joined property (Caixin Global, [Jul 15]), while June exports surged 27% from a year earlier (AP News, [Jul 14]). The June profit data sharpen the split: first-half industrial profits rose 18.7% as a tech boom masks the property slump (Caixin Global, [Jul 27]), chipmaking profits soared 2,500% (South China Morning Post, [Jul 27]), yet growth is the slowest this year (Financial Times, [Jul 26]) and payment arrears and "involution" still slam traditional industries (South China Morning Post, [Jul 27]). July car sales are set to fall 16.8% (GlobalChinaEV, [Jul 27]).

Credit Impulse: June new bank loans rose less than expected (The Star, [Jul 15]) and credit growth missed as the PBOC flagged a "new normal" (Caixin Global, [Jul 16]); household loans fell by 366.8 billion yuan in the first half (36Kr, [Jul 17]). The finance minister's claim that fiscal-financial synergy leveraged over 17 trillion yuan in new loans (finance.biggo.com, [Jul 22]) is not a contradiction: the volume is policy-directed corporate and state-linked lending while households pay down. Pricing is loosening quietly: banks are breaking with the lending benchmark (Bloomberg, [Jul 24]) and the biggest lenders launched the first repo-linked loans (Yahoo Finance, [Jul 27]). Read: headline impulse flat, household impulse negative, price easing by the back door.

Property: June new home prices fell at a slower pace (Reuters, [Jul 14]) and first-tier prices extended a four-month rebound (South China Morning Post, [Jul 15]), but resale prices fell faster (Reuters, [Jun 30]) and property investment slumped 18 percent y/y in January–June (The Standard (HK), [Jul 15]). Foreign capital is exiting, with KKR and AEW looking to join the sell-off wave (The Real Deal, [Jul 22]), and regulators curbed a loan structure used by distressed developers (Bloomberg, [Jul 22]) and offshore loans for local real estate firms (The Standard (HK), [Jul 23]). Vanke completed extensions on its 2026 public bonds (Caixin Global, [Jul 23]) with a RMB 519 million loan from Shenzhen Metro at 2.29% (BigGo Finance, [Jul 21]): state shareholders, not buyers, hold it together.

Fiscal, Policy & Trade: Beijing pledged stronger policy support (TradingView, [Jul 21]) yet clings to austerity despite the slowdown (Bloomberg, [Jul 22]), and growth is set to slow in the second half as it avoids broad stimulus (Yahoo Finance Australia, [Jul 25]); the July Politburo meeting, still ahead, is the marker for the growth commitment (Bloomberg, [Jul 23]). On trade, China says the US pledged to cap replacement tariffs at 20% (Bloomberg, [Jul 27]); it condemned the new Section 301 tariffs but held off on retaliation (South China Morning Post, [Jul 27]), opposed the "forced labour" tariffs (Reuters, [Jul 27]) and is drawing lines on tariffs and AI ahead of the Xi-Trump summit (Bloomberg, [Jul 27]). Europe is the harder front: 14 EU entities went onto its export control list (Reuters, [Jul 24]) and Italy's machine tool sector wants tougher EU trade rules (Reuters, [Jul 27]).

Markets & Flows: China's tech self-reliance is now moving global prices. CXMT vaulted to the top of China's valuations with a 466% Shanghai debut (Reuters, [Jul 27]), ASML slid on a report of China starting DUV tool production (Bloomberg, [Jul 27]) and Asia stocks tumbled as China chip advances rattled sentiment (Investing.com, [Jul 27]). Chinese tech firms have raised $27.13 bln in Hong Kong (Reuters, [Jul 27]), but the Hang Seng opened shy of the 25,000 mark (The Standard (HK), [Jul 27]), and a Big Short trader's bearish AI bets are testing China's stock support (South China Morning Post, [Jul 27]).

New / Most Important: A 20% cap on replacement tariffs, if Washington confirms it, would remove the tail risk on China's export engine, and Beijing's choice not to retaliate shows it wants that deal before the summit. That matters because exports and AI-linked profits are all that is carrying growth; the domestic side still needs the Politburo to move.

Playbook ✓: LPR on hold, liquidity easing, export-led profits, weak property and a calm tariff response are on script.  |  ⚠️ Divergence: Markets read China as an AI supply-chain winner while its old economy weakens. The transmission is uneven: China's chip advances hit Korea's memory names, part of why the Kospi is down 10.8%, while an 18 percent property-investment slump keeps construction-linked bulk demand fading, a drag on AUD and iron ore. Breakingviews argues China can keep dousing world oil prices ([Jul 27]), yet the oil spike remains a terms-of-trade negative for a major importer. European exporters face the surplus, and EM inflows ride IPO euphoria rather than a PMI or credit upturn.
