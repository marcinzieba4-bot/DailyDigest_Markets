🇨🇳 China  Mixed  → easing bias

Macro Stance: The PBOC is now openly leaning against yuan strength: China is reining in the rising yuan as weak domestic demand clouds the outlook (Reuters, [Aug 31]), with the reference rate set at 6.7828 against an estimate of 6.7344 (investingLive, [Aug 30]) after the currency reached a three-year high (UA.NEWS, [Aug 27]). Liquidity stays ample, with overnight reverse repos resumed ahead of month-end (Gulf Times, [Aug 29]). July CPI slowed to 0.5% and PPI to 3.5%, both missing expectations (BigGo Finance, [Aug 8]): price pressure is too weak to justify a strong currency, which is exactly the PBOC's argument.

PMIs & Activity: The two August factory surveys disagree on the level but agree on direction. The official manufacturing PMI rebounded to 49.8 with the new orders index back in expansion (BigGo Finance, [Aug 30]), contracting less than expected (CNBC, [Aug 30]) after July's 49.2 (FXStreet, [Jul 31]); the private manufacturing PMI rose to 51.5, its longest expansion streak in five years (BigGo Finance, [Aug 31]). Reconciled: the private survey leans toward export-oriented firms and is in expansion, while the official gauge stays below 50 because domestic activity is still contracting — the factory rebound remains dependent on exports (EU Today, [Aug 31]) and weak services signal an uneven recovery (Reuters, [Aug 30]). Industrial profits rose 17.6% in January-July (Fibre2Fashion, [Aug 29]) with growth at a seven-month low (Anadolu Ajansı, [Aug 27]), and auto sales slumped 22% (GlobalChinaEV, [Aug 27]).

Credit Impulse: July bank loans posted a record contraction as credit demand faltered (Reuters, [Aug 14]), yet credit growth exceeded forecasts despite the rare loan slump (Bloomberg, [Aug 14]); reconciled, government bonds carry the aggregate while the loan book shrinks, and that leg is about to strengthen as China speeds up bond issuance after a national meeting on investment (South China Morning Post, [Aug 31]). Lenders are thriving regardless: the largest five banks reported their strongest first-half profit since the height of the property crash (Reuters, [Aug 28]) and bank stocks surged on strong earnings (BigGo Finance, [Aug 30]). Beijing is ramping up credit support in its overhaul of the property market (The Business Times, [Aug 29]), the most direct attempt yet to restart household borrowing.

Property: New home prices were stagnant in July as demand stayed weak (Reuters, [Aug 17]). The policy response is structural: China moved to curb housing presales to shore up confidence (Reuters, [Aug 28]), raised the presale threshold under a new national standard (Caixin Global, [Aug 29]) and extended mortgage terms to 40 years (www.briefasia.com, [Aug 30]). The equity verdict has hardened: after a limit-up surge on the mortgage change, property stocks fell as the new rules upend the presale funding playbook (Reuters, [Aug 31]) — a double-edged sword, in the SCMP's phrase (South China Morning Post, [Aug 31]), positive for buyers and negative for developer cash flow. Vanke's first-half loss widened as sales slumped (Caixin Global, [Aug 29]) and a Reuters poll sees the investment slump deepening (Reuters, [Aug 27]).

Fiscal, Policy & Trade: The G20 the US is hosting has turned into a forum on China's surplus: Bessent says G20 countries should consider more trade barriers on China to cut imbalances (Reuters, [Aug 31]) and presses them to confront its $1.2 trillion trade surplus (investingLive, [Aug 30]); Beijing replies that it has never deliberately pursued a trade surplus and opposes unilateral tariffs (Global Times, [Aug 31]), while the EU wants to convince China to curb exports to the bloc (POLITICO.eu, [Aug 31]). Bilaterally the truce is likely to be extended by one year, with Xi to visit Washington (BigGo Finance, [Aug 28]), even as China opposes the 7.5% tariff plan (Yeni Şafak English, [Aug 27]); the Canada-US trade war is a secondary drag on global trade flows that China aims to win (The Washington Post, [Aug 25]). At home fiscal policy is extracting: a local surtax of 11%–13% has entered legislation (BigGo Finance, [Aug 28]) and The Economist argues Beijing is tightening when it should loosen (The Economist, [Aug 27]).

Markets & Flows: The Hang Seng ended flat at 25,566.99 (BBN Times, [Aug 31]) as China and Hong Kong stocks slipped on weak economic data and the property overhaul (The Economic Times, [Aug 31]). Leadership is defensive: dividend stocks reached an 11-year high at the expense of weak tech (Bloomberg.com, [Aug 31]) and bank stocks are bid, while stock ETFs saw a net outflow of more than 5 billion yuan (36Kr, [Aug 31]).

New / Most Important: The private manufacturing PMI at 51.5 confirms what the official 49.8 only hinted at: the factory pulse turned in August, and it turned on export orders. That is why the PBOC is capping the yuan — a stronger currency would tax the only engine running, with domestic demand still contracting and July loans negative. The policy mix is therefore weak yuan, front-loaded bond issuance and property reform, not rate cuts.

Playbook ✓: China remains a policy-easing outlier in a global hiking environment, and the deflationary-domestic / inflationary-global split it faces is stark: CPI at 0.5% at home against WTI at $89.56 abroad.  |  ⚠️ Divergence: Both August PMIs improved just as the trade climate hardened, so the transmission is two-sided. Better new orders and a property package support iron ore demand expectations and the AUD at the margin, but a recovery led by exports, defended by a capped yuan, means more Chinese competition for European exporters facing tougher EU lines, while sub-50 official readings and weak services keep China's import demand soft for EM. The global bond selloff, with the US 10Y at 4.79%, widens the rate differential against the yuan anyway, and war-premium oil is a terms-of-trade tax that eats into the margin gains the PMIs imply.
