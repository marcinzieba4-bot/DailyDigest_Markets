🇨🇳 China  Bearish  → easing bias

Macro Stance: The PBOC is rebuilding its operating framework rather than cutting. Loan prime rates were held for a 13th month in June (Business Times, [Jun 22]), and over the weekend the bank debuted its overnight reverse repo at 1.25%, according to sources (Reuters, [Jun 28]), below the 1.35% a survey had pointed to (Bloomberg, [Jun 25]). That is easing at the very front end by framework, not by headline cut. The yuan fixing was weakened for a fourth session as the dollar advanced (Bloomberg, [Jun 23]): a managed drift lower against a dollar at 13-month highs, while the ECB openly calls the yuan undervalued (Reuters, [Jun 22]); T. Rowe Price sees it falling 1%-2% by year-end (TradingView, [Jun 26]). Prices are split: May CPI rose only 1.2% (China Daily, [Jun 10]) while factory-gate inflation hit a near four-year high (Reuters, [Jun 9]).

PMIs & Activity: The official manufacturing PMI stalled at 50 in May (Reuters, [May 30]) with factory orders contracting (Wood Central, [Jun 1]), and the non-manufacturing gauge was barely positive at 50.1 (Macau Business, [May 31]). Private surveys were firmer, with services at 54.4 (TradingView, [Jun 2]), and a Reuters poll sees June factory activity returning only to meagre growth ([Jun 28]). Industrial output growth quickened to 4.5% in May (China Daily, [Jun 16]), but retail sales fell 0.6%, the first drop in over three years (Il Sole 24 Ore, [Jun 15]), and fixed-asset investment was down 4.1% in the first five months (Macau Business, [Jun 16]). Industrial profits rose 18.8% in January–May (WSJ, [Jun 26]) and but profit growth slowed for the first time in six months as weak domestic demand bit (Times of India, [Jun 27]).

Credit Impulse: Total social financing grew 7.7% in May (CGTN, [Jun 12]) and topped forecasts (Bloomberg, [Jun 12]), but new loans missed as the property slump curbed demand (Reuters, [Jun 12]). The beat is government-bond issuance; private credit demand is still contracting. The PBOC is now urging banks to boost lending because demand remains weak, according to sources (Reuters, [Jun 26]). Household deleveraging continues (SCMP, [Jun 15]). Read: the headline impulse is flat-to-positive, but the household and private-firm impulse is still negative, which is why output does not convert into consumption.

Property: New home prices fell at a faster pace in May (Reuters, [Jun 15]) even as first-tier new home prices rose for a third month (SCMP, [Jun 16]) and top-city resale prices extended their rebound (Caixin, [Jun 17]). Real-estate investment fell 16.2% y/y in January–May (GMK Center, [Jun 18]). This week the stress resurfaced at the developer level: Beijing land sales plunged 66% as developers chase only "safe" prime plots (Caixin, [Jun 25]), and the Evergrande Property Services stake sale collapsed, sending its shares down 24% (Caixin, [Jun 26]). The Straits Times describes steel entering a long plateau after the property crash ([Jun 22]).

Fiscal, Policy & Trade: The fiscal signal is the surprise. Beijing narrowed its cumulative deficit for the first time since 2023 (Bloomberg, [Jun 22]). A third batch of 62.5 billion yuan for consumer-goods trade-ins was allocated (Global Times, [Jun 26]): targeted support, small relative to the retail contraction. Exports jumped 19.4% in May (AP, [Jun 9]) and shipments to the US grew 35% (CNBC, [Jun 8]). The EU's deficit with China reached a record €1bn a day (The Guardian, [Jun 15]), and German carmakers are embarking on historic job cuts as Chinese rivals flood the market (Financial Times, [Jun 27]). Beijing hit 10 US firms, including MP Materials, with export controls (Reuters, [Jun 22]), and the 55% tariff on Australian beef is now in force (Australian Rural & Regional News, [Jun 22]).

Markets & Flows: Onshore and offshore have decoupled. Mainland stocks hit a 4-1/2-year high (TradingView, [Jun 22]), led by AI names on demand optimism and policy support (Business Times, [Jun 22]). Hong Kong is the opposite: the HSCEI entered a technical bear market (finance.biggo.com, [Jun 23]), Bloomberg ties the slide to spending woes ([Jun 23]), and the Hang Seng capped its worst week in over a year amid a tech sell-off (SCMP, [Jun 26]). The Hang Seng Tech index rebounded nearly 4% as Baidu's Kunlun chip unit eyed a $50 billion Hong Kong IPO (finance.biggo.com, [Jun 28]),. CITIC Securities' acknowledgement that Hong Kong has significantly underperformed other Asian markets this year is a capital-flow red flag: the rebound is AI issuance, not a consumer re-rating.

New / Most Important: The 1.25% overnight rate is the first concrete easing step of the new framework, and it came in below expectations. It lands as government advisers call for a fix to the two-speed economy (Bloomberg, [Jun 27]) and the PBOC urges banks to lend into weak demand. Cheaper overnight money helps bond and equity financing, the channels the AI sector uses, but not households. With new loans missing and the first fiscal-gap cutback since 2023, the credit impulse that matters for households is not turning. Until Beijing reverses the fiscal tightening, growth leans on exports, and Europe is closing the gates (economy.ac, [Jun 28]).

Playbook ✓: Property drag, weak consumption, LPR on hold and a managed yuan drift lower are all on script.  |  ⚠️ Divergence: The consensus expected a mid-year stimulus pivot; instead the credit impulse outside government bonds is negative and the fiscal impulse is narrowing. China is therefore exporting its excess supply rather than absorbing it at home. The transmission runs through three channels. European exporters face record bilateral deficits and a weak yuan, and the auto job cuts show the damage is already in payrolls. Bulk-commodity demand tied to construction is fading, with the steel plateau and an investment slump that hit AUD alongside the beef tariff. And global goods disinflation is the channel Pimco expects to support EM bonds (Bloomberg, [Jun 23]). A real turn needs loan growth, not bond issuance, to re-accelerate; until it does, rallies in Hong Kong are flows, not fundamentals.
