🇨🇳 China  Mixed  → easing bias but constrained

Macro Stance: China remains in a structural deceleration overlaid with cyclical easing that runs through liquidity, not rates. The PBOC left the loan prime rates unchanged in September (www.tmgm.com, [Sep 21]), with the five-year rate at 3.5% (IDNFinancials, [Sep 21]), and with US 10Y yields at 5.25% the rate differential constrains how far it can cut. Instead it will offer banks up to 1 trillion yuan a day over the holidays (Bloomberg, [Sep 23]) and pledged to stay 'appropriately loose' (TradingView, [Sep 24]). The yuan is not the pressure point: it hit a fresh multi-year peak as the PBOC eased its curb (Reuters, [Sep 21]), the fix at 6.7468 against an estimate of 6.6971 (investingLive, [Sep 22]) marks the ceiling, and Societe Generale sees volatility rising as the truce runs to January (VT Markets, [Sep 26]). August CPI rose 0.8% and PPI 3.8% (BigGo Finance, [Sep 8]).

PMIs & Activity: The August official manufacturing PMI rebounded to 49.8 with new orders back in expansion (BigGo Finance, [Aug 30]), below 50 for a second month, while the private gauge rose to 51.5 (BigGo Finance, [Aug 31]). Reconciled: the private survey expands, the official one does not. The newest hard data confirm a narrow expansion: August industrial profits rose 4.2% (Briefs Finance, [Sep 27]), the weakest since the November slump (Bloomberg, [Sep 27]), leaving January-August up 15.7% with electronics contributing over 60% (BigGo Finance, [Sep 27]). Reuters says profit growth slowed further as economic imbalances deepen (Reuters, [Sep 27]). August output growth quickened to 5.2% (BigGo Finance, [Sep 14]), but retail sales rose only 0.4% (investingLive, [Sep 14]) and fixed-asset investment fell 7.2% in eight months (Xinhua, [Sep 15]); externally, a private survey showed a 'surprise' jump in US orders before the summit (CNBC, [Sep 25]).

Credit Impulse: August bank lending disappointed (Reuters, [Sep 14]) and credit expansion missed forecasts as loan demand sagged (Bloomberg, [Sep 14]), so loans and the aggregate do not conflict this month: both missed. The composition is the story: bond and equity financing exceeded loans in social financing for the first time (BigGo Finance, [Sep 14]), so issuance, not bank lending, carries the aggregate while households and firms decline to borrow. Regulators are narrowing consumer credit further, telling e-commerce apps to stop selling loans (Sixth Tone, [Sep 21]), and the one forced credit flow is defensive: banks were asked to keep Vanke loans off bad-debt books (Reuters, [Sep 22]).

Property: August home prices extended their decline across 70 cities, with tier-1 cities stabilising (BigGo Finance, [Sep 14]), and property investment fell 19.9% in eight months, the steepest decline on record (BigGo Finance, [Sep 14]). The marginal signs are better: first-tier new home sales jumped over 10% (BigGo Finance, [Sep 23]), Beijing now requires topped-out structures for presales (China Daily, [Sep 24]) and global investors see signs of recovery in China per JLL (South China Morning Post, [Sep 27]), while Hong Kong home prices were largely flat in August (Reuters, [Sep 27]). Property stocks surged on mortgage-subsidy rumours that analysts called unlikely to be implemented (BigGo Finance, [Sep 23]).

Fiscal, Policy & Trade: The summit's tangible outcome is a low-tariff regime on $60bn of goods (Financial Times, [Sep 27]), built from tariff cuts on $30 billion each and an AI dialogue (Reuters, [Sep 26]), with China to buy 10 million tons of US coal in 2027 and 2028 (Yahoo Finance, [Sep 26]). It sits on a truce extended by only two months (Reuters, [Sep 23]), Trump and Xi will meet twice more after the summit failed to resolve tensions (Financial Times, [Sep 26]), and China's continued control of rare earths loomed over the meeting (The New York Times, [Sep 22]). At home, policy is extracting rather than spending: the tax grab squeezes the rich elite harder than ever (Bloomberg, [Sep 27]) and October stimulus expectations are cooling (BigGo Finance, [Sep 25]) ahead of the Central Committee plenum set for late October (ThePrint, [Sep 21]).

Markets & Flows: Chinese stocks hit a one-year low as chip and optical firms slid (Yahoo Finance, [Sep 27]) and the Hang Seng slid 1% as tech lost steam (BusinessToday Malaysia, [Sep 27]); consumer stocks face a lost decade as AI steals the spotlight (Bloomberg, [Sep 26]). Reserves keep moving into gold, with a record of over 1,000 tonnes imported this year (Financial Times, [Sep 22]).

New / Most Important: August industrial profits slowed to 4.2%, the weakest since November, and electronics supplied over 60% of the year's gains. That is the growth pulse in one number: an AI and export engine on a sub-50 PMI, with consumer-facing industry left behind, and it arrived with Chinese stocks at a one-year low. The $60bn tariff regime helps the export engine at the margin; it does nothing for the domestic demand that profits, retail sales and credit all say is missing.

Playbook ✓: The 'managed deceleration' narrative holds: Beijing drip-feeds liquidity without delivering the bazooka, keeping growth above its floor while tolerating slower credit creation, and the US yield advantage keeps foreign money underweight. Key data on China is due this week per ING (ing think, [Sep 24]).  |  ⚠️ Divergence: The data now split along the AI line: electronics profits and US orders up, consumer stocks and retail sales down, property investment still falling 19.9%. That transmits abroad unevenly. Asian tech supply chains share the AI leg; the AUD and iron ore do not, because the demand they need sits in property and credit, both still contracting, as the RBA hikes. European autos face Chinese competition rather than Chinese demand, and rare-earth leverage over their supply chains stayed off the summit table. WTI's 11.5% four-week run is a supply shock that squeezes the consumer-facing margins China is already losing.
