🇨🇳 China  Mixed  ↓ easing stance

Macro Stance: Beijing left loan prime rates unchanged for a 14th consecutive month in July (The Business Times, [Jul 20]) despite the second-quarter growth miss (Sharecast, [Jul 20]), so easing remains a liquidity story. The PBOC boosted liquidity to smooth tax payments and debt issuance (Bloomberg, [Jul 14]) and told some banks not to re-discount bills at rates below 0.5% (Reuters, [Jul 14]), a floor under a bill market that banks use to pad loan books when genuine demand is missing. It signalled comfort with yuan levels and expects two-way moves (Bloomberg, [Jul 15]); after a slightly weaker fixing (BusinessToday Malaysia, [Jul 17]), UOB sees an upside bias for the yuan below 6.7820 (TMGM, [Jul 20]). Prices are the wrong mix: June CPI rose only 1% (chinadailyhk, [Jul 8]) and slowed more than forecast (Bloomberg, [Jul 8]), while PPI climbed 4.1% (BigGo Finance, [Jul 8]) to a near four-year high that squeezes manufacturers (Reuters, [Jul 8]).

PMIs & Activity: The surveys and the hard data point in different directions. The June official manufacturing PMI rose to 50.3 and the non-manufacturing PMI to 50.2 (Reuters, [Jun 29]); the private gauge capped the strongest factory quarter since late 2020 (Reuters, [Jun 30]). Yet second-quarter GDP growth weakened to 4.3%, below the official target range (Bloomberg, [Jul 15]). June industrial output rose 5.3% y/y and retail sales returned to growth (BusinessLine, [Jul 15]), but retail sales of consumer goods were up just 1.3% in the first half (Ecns.cn, [Jul 15]), infrastructure investment turned negative (BigGo Finance, [Jul 19]) and the car market heads for its worst year since 2021 as sales plunge 20% (CNBC, [Jul 20]). The engine is external: June exports surged 27% y/y (Reuters, [Jul 13]).

Credit Impulse: Credit is the weak link. June new loans missed forecasts (Bloomberg, [Jul 15]) and overall credit growth undershot as the PBOC flagged a "new normal" (Caixin, [Jul 16]). Household loans decreased by 366.8 billion yuan in the first half (36Kr, [Jul 17]), and record consumer defaults undermine the push to boost spending (Reuters, [Jul 15]). Read: with households repaying and private firms not borrowing, the impulse rests on state-directed financing, and the bill-rate floor shows banks scrambling for assets.

Property: Prices are stabilising at the top while capital keeps leaving. June new home prices fell at a slower pace, though Reuters doubts a recovery ([Jul 14]), and first-tier prices extended a 4-month rebound (SCMP, [Jul 15]). But property investment slumped 18% y/y in January–June (The Standard, [Jul 15]), KKR and AEW seek to sell China property assets at steep losses (Bloomberg, [Jul 20]) and Beijing is curbing new bond sales by indebted LGFVs (Bloomberg, [Jul 15]), so the land-finance drag on local governments persists.

Fiscal, Policy & Trade: Beijing vowed to achieve its annual growth target after the economy weakened (Bloomberg, [Jul 20]), but the toolkit is still plans and state projects: a five-year consumption plan targeting 60 trillion yuan of retail sales by 2030 (Caixin, [Jul 14]) and faster state-backed projects to avoid broad stimulus (Reuters, [Jul 16]), even as the FT says leaders are zeroing in on the need for stimulus ([Jul 18]). At the margin fiscal support is being withdrawn, with a key tax break on lithium EV batteries ending (Caixin, [Jul 20]). Trade friction is building: the surplus with the EU hit a record (Bloomberg, [Jul 14]), Brussels signalled emergency import curbs (SCMP, [Jul 14]) and France and Germany put up a united front against China's economic "challenges" (SCMP, [Jul 20]); a new US sanctions bill threatens a 100% tariff on buyers of Russian oil, China included (The Independent, [Jul 16]); and China weighs tighter export controls on AI models and chips (FT, [Jul 20]).

Markets & Flows: The rescue is working for now. The national team injected 60 billion yuan into A-shares as regulators moved to counter the global market shock (Global Times, [Jul 20]), the securities regulator vowed stability after the rout (Reuters, [Jul 20]) and Chinese stocks rebounded once the buying was revealed (Bloomberg, [Jul 20]); the Hang Seng surged nearly 600 points to reclaim 25,000 (BigGo Finance, [Jul 20]), Hong Kong stocks are beating Korean shares by the most in 40 years (Bloomberg, [Jul 20]) and Citi upgraded China to overweight (The Standard, [Jul 20]). Alibaba, flagged as possibly the best Chinese AI play after Apple's endorsement, jumped 7.9% on the Apple Intelligence approval (TradingView, [Jul 16]).

New / Most Important: The policy mix after a 4.3% quarter is the signal. Beijing froze the price of credit for a 14th month, promised to hit the growth target and let the national team buy shares worth $9bn (FT, [Jul 20]). That is support for asset prices, not for borrowers. With June loans missing and households repaying debt, the gap between a rescued equity market and a still-contracting private credit impulse is widening, and the end of the battery tax break shows fiscal policy is not yet loosening at the margin. Equity flows can follow the state; the real economy needs credit.

Playbook ✓: Stimulus-driven stabilisation without breakout growth, an LPR on hold and a managed yuan continue.  |  ⚠️ Divergence: The backstop is drawing global money back, but the channels through which China reaches the rest of the world are unchanged. An export-led 50.3 PMI and 27% export growth push volume into Europe, where Paris and Berlin are aligning on curbs, a margin risk for European exporters and autos. Construction demand is still falling with property investment down 18%, a headwind for iron ore and AUD, and crude imports at the lowest in nearly a decade (Bloomberg, [Jul 13]) cap China's support for oil. For EM, a stable yuan and an equity floor help at the margin, but only a turn in household and private credit would lift China's import demand.
