🇨🇳 China  Mixed  → easing bias assumed

Macro Stance: The PBOC is leaning against yuan strength rather than cutting rates: China is reining in the rising yuan as weak domestic demand clouds the outlook (Reuters, [Aug 31]), with the reference rate set at 6.7828 against an estimate of 6.7344 (investingLive, [Aug 30]) after the currency reached a three-year high (UA.NEWS, [Aug 27]). The next test is the bond calendar: government bond sales are set to accelerate, putting the focus on the PBOC (Bloomberg.com, [Sep 1]), after the central bank began overnight reverse repos from Aug 27 (Fibre2Fashion, [Aug 26]). July CPI slowed to 0.5%, more than expected (BigGo Finance, [Aug 9]), and PPI growth to 3.5%, missing expectations (BigGo Finance, [Aug 8]): price pressure is too weak to justify a strong currency.

PMIs & Activity: Both August factory surveys improved, but they disagree on the level. The official manufacturing PMI rebounded to 49.8 with the new orders index back in expansion (BigGo Finance, [Aug 30]), contracting less than expected (CNBC, [Aug 30]) after July's 49.2 (FXStreet, [Jul 31]); the private manufacturing PMI rose to 51.5, its longest expansion streak in five years (BigGo Finance, [Aug 31]), as factory activity picked up on improving demand (Reuters, [Sep 1]). Reconciled: the private survey leans toward export-oriented firms and is in expansion, while the official gauge stays below 50 because domestic activity is still contracting — the rebound remains dependent on exports (EU Today, [Aug 31]), and Natixis sees stabilisation with an uneven recovery (CNBC, [Sep 1]). Regionally, factory activity bounced in August as AI fuelled Asian expansion (Reuters, [Sep 1]). Industrial profits rose 17.6% in January-July (Fibre2Fashion, [Aug 29]) with growth at a seven-month low (Anadolu Ajansı, [Aug 27]), and auto sales slumped 22% (GlobalChinaEV, [Aug 27]).

Credit Impulse: July bank loans posted a record contraction as credit demand faltered (Reuters, [Aug 14]), yet credit growth exceeded forecasts despite the rare loan slump (Bloomberg, [Aug 14]); reconciled, government bonds carry the aggregate while the loan book shrinks, and that leg strengthens as China speeds up bond issuance after a national meeting on investment (South China Morning Post, [Aug 31]). Lenders are thriving regardless: the largest five banks reported their strongest first-half profit since the height of the property crash (Reuters, [Aug 28]). Beijing is ramping up credit support in its overhaul of the property market (The Business Times, [Aug 29]), the most direct attempt yet to restart household borrowing, while hidden local government debt has been cut by half in two years (Caixin Global, [Sep 1]).

Property: New home prices were stagnant in July as demand stayed weak (Reuters, [Aug 17]). The response is structural: China is calling time on the home-sales system that worsened its meltdown (Bloomberg.com, [Sep 1]), having moved to curb presales (Reuters, [Aug 28]), raised the presale threshold under a new national standard (Caixin Global, [Aug 29]) and extended mortgage terms to 40 years (www.briefasia.com, [Aug 30]). The equity verdict is split: property stocks fell as the new rules upend the presale funding playbook (Reuters, [Aug 31]), positive for buyers and negative for developer cash flow.

Fiscal, Policy & Trade: The G20 ended without a joint communiqué: China derailed the consensus after the US-hosted meeting (Financial Times, [Sep 1]), dissenting as Bessent said 19 finance ministers agreed to address cheap exports (PBS, [Sep 1]), after he urged more trade barriers on China (Reuters, [Aug 31]) over its $1.2 trillion surplus (investingLive, [Aug 30]). Beijing says it has never deliberately pursued a trade surplus (Global Times, [Aug 31]), and Germany aims for a tougher China trade package in coming weeks (Bloomberg.com, [Sep 1]). Bilaterally the tone is softer: Premier Li welcomed US firms to expand in China while urging Washington to address Beijing's concerns (Reuters, [Sep 1]), and the truce is likely to be extended by one year, with Xi to visit Washington (BigGo Finance, [Aug 28]). At home, China unveiled measures to expand consumer goods consumption (State Council Information Office, [Sep 1]), but fiscal policy is extracting: a local surtax of 11%–13% has entered legislation (BigGo Finance, [Aug 28]).

Markets & Flows: The Hang Seng closed nearly 1% lower as China growth fears hit Hong Kong harder (24/7 Wall St., [Sep 1]), with China and Hong Kong stocks hit by the global bond selloff (NST Online, [Sep 1]). Leadership is defensive: dividend stocks reached an 11-year high at the expense of weak tech (Bloomberg.com, [Aug 31]), and foreign inflows into A-shares could slow after a record surge (South China Morning Post, [Sep 1]). The China-US yield gap nears a record as the Treasury selloff deepens (Bloomberg.com, [Sep 1]).

New / Most Important: China's veto of the G20 communiqué turns the surplus into an open multilateral dispute just as both August PMIs confirm that exports are the engine of the factory rebound. Beijing is splitting the front: defiant at the G20, courting US business at home, with the Xi visit and truce extension as the prize. The trade risk is now concentrated in Europe, where Germany is preparing a tougher package.

Playbook ✓: China remains a policy-easing outlier in a global hiking environment, managing the yuan, reforming property and easing via bond supply rather than rates.  |  ⚠️ Divergence: Oil at $90.93 benefits Chinese strategic buyers at the margin but squeezes downstream margins, and with the official PMI below 50 and services weak, the commodity complex is being driven by Middle East supply disruption, not Asian growth. The transmission to global assets is two-sided: better new orders and the property package support iron ore expectations and the AUD at the margin, but an export-led recovery defended by a capped yuan means more competition for European exporters, and a near-record China-US yield gap keeps EM Asia currencies under pressure from the dollar side.
