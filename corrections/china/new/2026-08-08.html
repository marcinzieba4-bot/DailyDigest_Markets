🇨🇳 China  Mixed  → easing bias

Macro Stance: The PBOC is signalling more easing without delivering it yet. It pledged timely adjustment of its policy tools (Reuters, [Aug 2]), China Daily writes that interest rate and RRR cuts may be on the table ([Aug 4]), and liquidity is ample after a CNY 500 billion injection (BigGo Finance, [Aug 4]). The reserve data add a twist: gold holdings rose by the most since October 2023 as the buying pace quickened (Reuters, [Aug 7]), a 21st straight month (China Daily, [Aug 7]), while July FX reserves rose less than expected (The Standard, [Aug 7]). The yuan stayed firm near a 3-1/2-year high on robust exports (The Star, [Aug 7]) despite a weaker fixing (Business Recorder, [Aug 3]), and swap lines were renewed with Malaysia (The Star, [Aug 7]) and Argentina (South China Morning Post, [Aug 7]). June CPI rose 1.0% while PPI climbed 4.1% (finance.biggo.com, [Jul 8]); July prices are still to come.

PMIs & Activity: The official manufacturing PMI fell to 49.2 and the non-manufacturing PMI to 49.0 (FXStreet, [Jul 31]); the private factory index eased to 50.9 (Fibre2Fashion, [Aug 3]), a 4-month low (Reuters, [Aug 2]), and Standard Chartered sees growth momentum softening again (www.tmgm.com, [Aug 3]). Trade is the counterweight, and the headlines need reconciling. Exports surged 24% in July (Firstpost, [Aug 7]) and beat estimates (CNBC, [Aug 6]), yet AP reports they slowed slightly ([Aug 7]): both are true, as growth eased from June's 27% (Reuters, [Jul 13]) but beat forecasts. Likewise the trade surplus narrowed (South China Morning Post, [Aug 7]) but still exceeded forecasts (TradingView, [Aug 6]). Caixin says AI demand lifted export prices ([Aug 7]), and trade grew 17.3% in the first seven months (China Daily, [Aug 7]). Domestic demand remains the gap after Q2 GDP growth of 4.3% fell below the target range (Bloomberg, [Jul 15]).

Credit Impulse: The July previews conflict. A Reuters poll sees bank lending set to plummet on subdued demand and a seasonal slowdown ([Aug 7]), while Caixin expects July loans to rebound on a low base ([Aug 7]). Reconciled: a sharp drop from June's seasonal peak that still looks better than a weak July last year, which is not a turn in the impulse. June set the tone: new loans rose less than expected (The Star, [Jul 15]), credit growth missed as the PBOC flagged a "new normal" (Caixin Global, [Jul 16]) and household loans fell 366.8 billion yuan in the first half (36Kr, [Jul 17]). China selling long-dated offshore yuan bonds at record low yields (Bloomberg, [Aug 4]) is the market's verdict on demand.

Property: Policy is finally moving in the top tier. Beijing further relaxed home-buying curbs in a bid to boost the market (Reuters, [Aug 7]), following Shanghai (Bloomberg, [Aug 7]). The data explain the urgency: housing prices fell 0.5% in July as sales volume weakened (Investing.com, [Aug 3]), the top-100 developer sales slump deepened (Trivium China, [Aug 5]) and property investment slumped 18 percent y/y in January–June (The Standard, [Jul 15]), even as June new home prices fell at a slower pace (Reuters, [Jul 14]). China Cinda warns of a first-half profit drop as the slump weighs (Caixin Global, [Aug 5]), and PIIE argues the property bust is spilling across China's borders ([Aug 7]).

Fiscal, Policy & Trade: SCMP reads the Politburo as cautious confidence ([Aug 3]), and Oxford Economics says an unspent fiscal arsenal targets a Q3 recovery (CNBC, [Aug 3]). The new lever is a surprise tax on offshore trusts (CNBC, [Aug 4]) that now reaches offshore insurance income (Reuters, [Aug 6]). On trade, the US imposed a 15% tariff on polysilicon (Nikkei Asia, [Aug 7]), which Chinese experts slam as protectionism (Global Times, [Aug 7]), after Beijing answered earlier US moves with sanctions and tighter drone export rules (Financial Times, [Aug 5]). A Chinese diplomat calls the tariffs "tortuous" for the soybean trade (Bloomberg, [Aug 7]), and SCMP asks whether this "speed bump" could stop the Xi-Trump summit ([Aug 6]).

Markets & Flows: China and Hong Kong stocks rose as strong trade data and AI demand lifted sentiment (The Economic Times, [Aug 7]), and SCMP sees a global AI trade revival brightening the outlook for Chinese tech ([Aug 7]). That reverses part of the week's damage, when the Hang Seng slumped nearly 500 points as insurers sank (The Standard, [Aug 6]). Flows stay supportive: mainland investors extended their Hong Kong buying spree (South China Morning Post, [Aug 5]), Morgan Stanley lifted its Hang Seng target to 28,400 (BigGo Finance, [Aug 5]) and Chinese investors poured $1.2bn into gold ETFs (TradingView, [Aug 6]).

New / Most Important: Beijing easing home-buying curbs right after Shanghai puts both top-tier cities in play, and it lands with July home prices still falling 0.5% and developer sales sliding. Exports are carrying growth and the PBOC is accumulating gold at the fastest pace since 2023, but a city-level relaxation is a test of demand, not a fix: watch July home prices and credit next.

Playbook ✓: Incremental easing, top-tier property relaxation, strong AI-led exports and a firm yuan are on script.  |  ⚠️ Divergence: Equities cheer the export beat while the PMIs and the credit previews say domestic demand is still contracting. Iron ore at a year low (Tech Times, [Aug 4]) gives AUD little support into a live RBA decision, even as SCMP asks whether China can boost the Australian dollar ([Aug 6]). European exporters take the other side of the trade boom, with China exporting more to Germany as the deficit grows (All-About-Industries, [Aug 7]), and the tax crackdown hit British bank shares (The Times, [Aug 5]). With oil at $78.18, China's margin squeeze is an input-cost story, not a demand one.
