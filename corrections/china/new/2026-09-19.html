Macro Stance: Rates are on hold and the currency is doing the work. A Reuters poll has loan prime rates steady for a 16th consecutive month in September (Reuters, [Sep 17]) and Governor Pan says slower loan growth is the new normal (Reuters, [Sep 15]), so there is no easing default. The fixing carries the signal: the midpoint was raised for an eighth straight day (BigGo Finance, [Sep 17]) and the yuan is at its strongest since 2022 (Bloomberg.com, [Sep 17]), yet the PBOC set 6.7580 (www.tmgm.com, [Sep 17]) against a Reuters estimate of 6.7065 (investingLive, [Sep 17]), guiding appreciation into the summit while capping its pace. August CPI rose 0.8% and PPI 3.8% (BigGo Finance, [Sep 8]) on energy costs (Reuters, [Sep 9]): imported cost, not demand. The rupee's slide after the Fed hike shows the Asian FX pressure Beijing is leaning through.

PMIs & Activity: The official August manufacturing PMI rose to 49.8 with new orders back in expansion (BigGo Finance, [Aug 30]), the private gauge reached 51.5 (BigGo Finance, [Aug 31]) and private services rebounded to 51.4 (BigGo Finance, [Sep 2]). The hard data split the economy in two: industrial output rebounded to 5.2% (BigGo Finance, [Sep 14]), but retail sales grew 0.4% y/y against 0.6% expected (investingLive, [Sep 14]), urban unemployment hit 5.3% (TradingView, [Sep 14]) and fixed-asset investment fell 7.2% in eight months (State Council Information Office, [Sep 15]). Exports jumped 25% (AP News, [Sep 7]) while imports missed (CNBC, [Sep 7]). Reconciled: the PMI rebound is real but export- and AI-led on top of contracting domestic demand, hence Goldman's cut of its GDP forecast to 4.5% (The Standard, [Sep 15]).

Credit Impulse: Bank loans rose less than expected after July's slump (Reuters, [Sep 14]) and credit expansion missed forecasts as loan demand sagged (Bloomberg.com, [Sep 14]). The structure explains it: bond and equity financing exceeded loans in social financing for the first time (BigGo Finance, [Sep 14]), so securities issuance carries the aggregate while the loan book stalls. Pan's de-emphasis of loan volume targets (BigGo Finance, [Sep 15]) and new metrics to rein in banks' long-dated bond holdings (Reuters, [Sep 14]) show Beijing accepts a weaker impulse; the $43.9b injection into major state banks (China Daily Global Edition, [Sep 14]) is balance-sheet repair, not stimulus.

Property: August home prices extended their decline across 70 cities while tier-1 cities stabilised (BigGo Finance, [Sep 14]), and pre-owned prices gained in top-tier cities (Caixin Global, [Sep 15]): a tier-1 floor inside a national decline. Development investment plunged 19.9% in eight months, the steepest on record (BigGo Finance, [Sep 14]), and land sale revenue slumped nearly 30% (BigGo Finance, [Sep 18]), straining local government income (Bloomberg.com, [Sep 13]). Breakingviews says the fix will hurt before it heals (Reuters, [Sep 15]) as China adjusts select property policies (China Daily, [Sep 18]); Vanke and Yu Liang were hit with consumption restrictions over unpaid debt (BigGo Finance, [Sep 16]).

Fiscal, Policy & Trade: The fiscal pullback stretched into August as spending slumped (Bloomberg.com, [Sep 18]) even with January-August revenue up 5.7% (Reuters, [Sep 18]); weak data have stoked calls for stimulus (Semafor, [Sep 15]). Trade is the active front: the US and China are discussing tariff cuts (Bloomberg, [Sep 15]), including on US LNG (Reuters, [Sep 18]), Washington is holding off on new China tariffs until after the Xi meeting (Bloomberg.com, [Sep 18]) and Bessent meets He Lifeng this weekend (Reuters, [Sep 15]). The counter-current: Trump signed the Russia sanctions bill exposing China to possible 100% tariffs (TRT World, [Sep 18]), the EU will use all tools to cut its China deficit (Reuters, [Sep 16]) and Beijing opposes a voluntary limit on hybrid exports (Reuters, [Sep 18]).

Markets & Flows: Equities trade the summit, not the data. China stocks were on track for their best day in a month (TradingView, [Sep 17]) and the Hang Seng closed up 0.6% as the mainland led Asia (24/7 Wall St., [Sep 18]), after Hong Kong dropped on the Fed hike (South China Morning Post, [Sep 17]). Bonds are bucking the global yield surge (Yahoo Finance, [Sep 17]), US Treasury holdings fell to the lowest since 2008 (Financial Times, [Sep 17]) and mainland investors are rushing into US stocks (Reuters, [Sep 15]).

New / Most Important: The White House has scheduled the Trump-Xi summit for next week (NTD News, [Sep 18]) and both sides are clearing the ground: tariffs delayed, LNG and farm cuts on the table, BYD, CATL and Xiaomi executives possibly joining Xi's visit (Reuters, [Sep 18]). Beijing's contribution is the yuan, walked to a four-year high (Moneycontrol.com, [Sep 18]), a gesture that costs exporters margin just as domestic demand is too weak to replace them.

Playbook ✓: Rates on hold, credit allowed to slow and a firmer fixing into a negotiation is Beijing's standard pre-summit posture; dollar strength pressuring Asian FX is textbook.  |  ⚠️ Divergence: A four-year-high yuan while the Fed hikes and the domestic pulse weakens is diplomacy, not fundamentals. The transmission runs through the data: an export-led PMI and 25% export growth keep Chinese competition intense for European exporters as Brussels hardens; a soft credit impulse and a record 19.9% fall in property investment cap steel demand, so iron ore rose only slightly as stimulus hopes outweighed demand concern (TradingView, [Sep 16]); and with imports missing, the AUD and EM exporters get little from the rebound.
