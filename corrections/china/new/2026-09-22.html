🇨🇳 China  Bearish  → accommodative but constrained

Macro Stance: Accommodative but constrained, and the constraint now binds. The PBOC left loan prime rates unchanged in September (www.tmgm.com, [Sep 21]), the five-year at 3.5% (IDNFinancials, [Sep 21]), as the Fed hike narrows its room to ease (investingLive, [Sep 20]); Pan calls slower loan growth the new normal (Reuters, [Sep 15]). Diverging from DM tightening keeps portfolio outflow pressure alive, with mainland investors rushing into US stocks (Reuters, [Sep 15]). The currency headlines conflict: the PBOC eased its curb and the yuan hit a fresh multi-year peak (Reuters, [Sep 21]), yet Nikkei says China slow-walks appreciation as the US and Europe demand rebalancing (Nikkei Asia, [Sep 21]). Reconciled: the yuan is allowed to rise at Beijing's pace, a summit concession rather than a policy turn. The central bank pledged to expand financial market opening (Investing.com UK, [Sep 21]). August CPI rose 0.8% and PPI 3.8% (BigGo Finance, [Sep 8]) on energy costs (Reuters, [Sep 9]).

PMIs & Activity: The official August manufacturing PMI rose to 49.8 with new orders back in expansion (BigGo Finance, [Aug 30]) and the private gauge reached 51.5 (BigGo Finance, [Aug 31]), but the hard data split the economy: industrial output rebounded to 5.2% (BigGo Finance, [Sep 14]) while retail sales grew 0.4% y/y against 0.6% expected (investingLive, [Sep 14]), urban unemployment hit 5.3% (TradingView, [Sep 14]) and fixed-asset investment fell 7.2% in eight months (State Council Information Office, [Sep 15]). The export engine is the exception: exports jumped 25% (AP News, [Sep 7]), Xi rolls into the summit with China's trade engine roaring (Reuters, [Sep 21]) and its share of global container exports has soared to 40% (Financial Times, [Sep 21]). Goldman cut its GDP forecast to 4.5% (The Standard, [Sep 15]).

Credit Impulse: Bank loans rose less than expected after July's slump (Reuters, [Sep 14]) and credit expansion missed forecasts as loan demand sagged (Bloomberg.com, [Sep 14]). Bond and equity financing exceeded loans in social financing for the first time (BigGo Finance, [Sep 14]), so securities issuance carries the aggregate while the loan book stalls. The LPR hold came as the PBOC signals slower credit (CHOSUNBIZ, [Sep 20]), and regulators told e-commerce apps to stop selling loans (Sixth Tone, [Sep 21]): household credit is being policed, not encouraged.

Property: August home prices extended their decline across 70 cities while tier-1 cities stabilised (BigGo Finance, [Sep 14]); development investment plunged 19.9% in eight months, the steepest on record (BigGo Finance, [Sep 14]). Second-hand sales now exceed 50% of the market (State Council Information Office, [Sep 21]). Equities found a catalyst: the provident fund overhaul and bullish foreign calls sparked a property stock rally, with Vanke limit-up for a second day (BigGo Finance, [Sep 21]). Fundamentals have not followed: a US$33 billion support experiment is sputtering on weak demand (The Business Times, [Sep 20]), sovereign wealth funds stay away over lingering property woes (Nikkei Asia, [Sep 20]) and economists urge Beijing to act fast on local-level debt (South China Morning Post, [Sep 21]).

Fiscal, Policy & Trade: Fiscal revenue growth slowed as government spending contracted (Caixin Global, [Sep 19]). Xi chaired the Politburo, which set a Central Committee plenum for late October (U.S. News & World Report, [Sep 21]), with no stimulus signal attached; the Papergames hashtags trending on X are a reminder of nationalist regulatory risk in gaming. The summit track is progressing: the US proposed extending the trade truce by six months, per the NYT (Forex Factory, [Sep 21]), Greer says talks on an extension continue (Bloomberg.com, [Sep 21]), Bessent says officials are considering lower tariffs on noncritical items (The Hill, [Sep 21]) and the AI safety talks reconvene in Shenzhen in two months (Reuters, [Sep 21]). The friction is intact: rare earth exports to the US fell 20% (TradingView, [Sep 21]) and China has not delivered its promised US farm purchases (The New York Times, [Sep 21]).

Markets & Flows: Chinese shares climbed ahead of the summit (Free Malaysia Today, [Sep 21]) and Hong Kong and the mainland moved higher in sync (24/7 Wall St., [Sep 21]) as global stocks rallied on lower oil and US-China hopes (Yahoo Finance Singapore, [Sep 21]). BofA warns an offshore trust tax crackdown could hit single stocks (Reuters, [Sep 21]), Saudi Arabia's exit from Beijing's payment scheme is a setback to dedollarisation (Fortune, [Sep 21]) and the Economic Times' EM capital flight commentary shows higher US rates pulling money out of China and other EMs.

New / Most Important: A six-month truce extension is now on the table, so the summit has a floor. That is the bearish point: the floor is external. Rates are held, the yuan is rising, credit is being policed and the property rally rests on provident-fund tweaks, so the domestic economy enters the summit with less support than the equity tape implies.

Playbook ✓: Continued policy divergence with DM central banks is consensus; a firmer yuan into a negotiation is Beijing's standard pre-summit posture.  |  ⚠️ Divergence: Equities are pricing the summit while the growth pulse says contraction at home. Transmission runs through the data: a 40% share of global container exports and 25% export growth mean more Chinese competition for European exporters; a record 19.9% fall in property investment and a stalling loan book cap steel demand and iron ore; and AUD/USD edged higher on the PBoC hold (VT Markets, [Sep 21]), a policy pause rather than a demand impulse. WTI at 91.41 adds an import-cost squeeze that weak domestic demand cannot pass on.
