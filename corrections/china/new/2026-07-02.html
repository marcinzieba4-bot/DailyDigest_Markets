🇨🇳 China  Mixed  → easing bias

Macro Stance: Easing is happening at the very front end. The PBOC debuted overnight reverse repos at 1.25% (Reuters, [Jun 28]), below the 1.35% a Bloomberg survey expected ([Jun 25]), then doubled the injection at an unchanged rate (Reuters, [Jun 29]). Quantity signals conflict: TradingView reports liquidity added [Jun 30] while BigGo Finance tallies a drain of over 1.16 trillion yuan [Jun 30] — daily top-ups against larger maturities, so overnight money is cheaper but the liquidity stock is not expanding. The yuan steadied after the PMI (Yahoo Finance Australia, [Jun 29]), but options traders have turned bullish on the dollar (Bloomberg, [Jun 30]) and T. Rowe Price sees the yuan 1%–2% weaker by year-end (TradingView, [Jun 26]). Prices stay split: May CPI rose only 1.2% (China Daily, [Jun 10]) while factory-gate inflation hit a near four-year high (Reuters, [Jun 9]), and Caixin warns deflation risks are squeezing profits [Jun 30].

PMIs & Activity: The June pulse is up but narrow. The official manufacturing PMI rose to 50.3 (Reuters, [Jun 29]) from a flat 50 in May; non-manufacturing rose to 50.2 (Reuters, [Jun 29]). The private gauge capped its best quarter since 2020 (The Business Times, [Jul 1]), yet June grew less than expected (Bloomberg, [Jun 30]): a high average with momentum fading. China Daily now says the economy is on track to meet its growth target [Jul 1]. May's hard data show the split: output growth quickened to 4.5% (China Daily, [Jun 16]) and exports jumped 19.4% (AP News, [Jun 9]), but retail sales fell 0.6% (Il Sole 24 Ore, [Jun 15]), fixed-asset investment fell 4.1% in five months (Macau Business, [Jun 16]), and profit growth slowed for the first time in six months (Times of India, [Jun 27]).

Credit Impulse: Total social financing grew 7.7% in May (CGTN, [Jun 12]) and topped forecasts (Bloomberg, [Jun 12]), but new loans missed as the property slump curbed demand (Reuters, [Jun 12]): the beat is government bonds, the miss is private demand. The PBOC is urging banks to lend because demand remains weak (Reuters, [Jun 26]). With debt above 300% of GDP (UPI, [Jun 29]), the household and private-firm impulse is still negative.

Property: June's two datapoints need reconciling. The top 100 developers sold nearly RMB 1.6 trillion in the first half, with June up 11.8% month on month (Moomoo, [Jul 1]), while resale home prices fell faster, a private survey shows (Reuters, [Jun 30]). The likelier reading is a half-year-end sales push, not a recovery in pricing power. May new home prices fell at a faster pace (Reuters, [Jun 15]) and real-estate investment fell 16.2% y/y in January–May (GMK Center, [Jun 18]). State capital is replacing private balance sheets: Shenzhen Metro's chairman now also chairs Vanke (BigGo Finance, [Jun 30]), while Beijing land sales plunged 66% (Caixin, [Jun 25]).

Fiscal, Policy & Trade: The 62.5 billion yuan third batch of trade-in funds (Global Times, [Jun 26]) is small against a retail contraction, and SCMP argues that as China tightens the fiscal net it must strike a balance [Jul 1]. Policy energy is going into control: Beijing imposed "national security" rules on overseas investments (The Straits Times, [Jul 1]) and a landmark law asserting control over offshore tech transfers (SCMP, [Jul 1]). On trade, the EU and China set an October deadline (Bloomberg, [Jun 29]) and Brussels issued new steel and e-commerce rules to cut the imbalance (AP News, [Jul 1]); the EPP's chief warns of conflict without an autumn deal (Euronews, [Jul 1]). AP counts 40 Japanese entities under new export controls [Jul 1].

Markets & Flows: Mainland stocks rose on robust factory data and Xi's speech (The Business Times, [Jul 1]), and foreign holdings of A-shares hit a record high in the first half (Global Times, [Jul 1]), but financial stocks slumped despite a massive dividend payout (Caixin, [Jul 1]) and Hong Kong shares fell 11% in the half (SCMP, [Jun 30]). The capital wall is rising: curbs on higher-yielding offshore debt (Bloomberg, [Jun 29]) and cross-border swaps (The Business Times, [Jun 26]), and TipRanks reports China shutting down retail gold trading [Jul 1]. Adam Tooze's Chartbook calls this China's "walled-in wealth", cut off from the AI boom [Jul 1].

New / Most Important: On the day Xi pledged growth and stocks rallied on the factory data, Beijing's actual policy move was to raise the wall: national-security screening of overseas investment and statutory control over offshore tech transfers. That protects the export and AI engine behind the 50.3 PMI, but releases nothing to households; with resale prices falling and private loans missing, savings stay trapped in deposits, bonds and, until now, gold.

Playbook ✓: Property drag, weak consumption, no headline rate cut and a managed yuan drift are on script.  |  ⚠️ Divergence: The consensus wanted a mid-year stimulus pivot; instead an export-led PMI sits on a negative private credit impulse while the global factory cycle peaks — S&P Global's June survey shows sustained growth but fading optimism [Jul 1]. China therefore exports its excess supply, and the transmission runs three ways. European exporters face new EU trade defences and an October deadline, a direct risk to European industrials. Construction-linked bulk demand fades with real-estate investment down 16.2%, a drag on AUD and iron ore that export PMIs do not offset. And Bloomberg sees fuel export curbs lifted on ample supplies [Jun 30], more barrels into an oil market where WTI is already down 29% in four weeks. Until loan growth, not bond issuance, re-accelerates, Hong Kong rallies are flows, not fundamentals.
