🇨🇳 China  Mixed  ↓ easing bias

Macro Stance: The PBOC is easing at the margin without moving headline rates. Its Q2 report pledged timely new policy rollout (Reuters, [Aug 12]) and created a 1 trillion yuan private-enterprise relending facility (BigGo Finance, [Aug 12]); the first mid-month overnight reverse repo boosted bonds (Bloomberg, [Aug 13]), and July corporate loan rates fell below 3% as the bank signals a shift from volume to price (finance.biggo.com, [Aug 14]). This week's loan prime rate decision (bloomingbit, [Aug 16]) is the test: Reuters sees rates held despite economic weakness ([Aug 18]), while analysts say a surprise cut cannot be ruled out (investingLive, [Aug 17]). The yuan broke through 6.74 per dollar, its highest in about three and a half years (BigGo Finance, [Aug 17]), then eased on soft data and a weaker fixing (Business Recorder, [Aug 18]) even as TradingView saw it firmer on a strong fixing ([Aug 18]): the PBOC is managing the pace, not the direction. Prices argue for easing: July CPI rose 0.5% and PPI growth slowed to 3.5%, both missing expectations (BigGo Finance, [Aug 8]).

PMIs & Activity: July's official manufacturing PMI fell to 49.2 and non-manufacturing to 49.0 (FXStreet, [Jul 31]); the private factory gauge eased to 50.9 (Fibre2Fashion, [Aug 3]), a four-month low (Reuters, [Aug 2]), and private services growth was the slowest since September 2024 (Reuters, [Aug 4]). The hard data confirmed the slide: industrial output grew 4.5% y/y and retail sales just 0.6% (Reuters, [Aug 17]), output, retail sales and investment all missed forecasts (The Business Times, [Aug 17]), and fixed-asset investment fell 6.7% in the first seven months (Xinhua, [Aug 17]). The statistics bureau blames extreme weather but still sees the full-year target supported (BigGo Finance, [Aug 17]). Exports remain the engine, up 23.9% on high-tech shipments (The Asian Banker, [Aug 14]).

Credit Impulse: July new bank loans posted a record contraction as credit demand faltered (Reuters, [Aug 14]), only the second contraction this year (The Standard (HK), [Aug 14]), yet Bloomberg reports credit growth exceeded forecasts despite the rare loan slump ([Aug 14]). Reconciled: headline financing, which includes bond issuance, beat; bank lending to households and firms shrank, leaving cumulative yuan loans up 10.38 trillion yuan in January–July (Xinhua, [Aug 14]). Price is loosening faster than volume, with banks testing repo-linked corporate loans (South China Morning Post, [Aug 14]). The 10-year yield fell to a 13-month low (Financial Times, [Aug 17]): the bond market reads the impulse as negative.

Property: July new home prices were stagnant as demand stayed weak (Reuters, [Aug 17]) and first-tier prices were flat, ending a four-month rebound (South China Morning Post, [Aug 17]). Bloomberg says prices fell at a faster pace ([Aug 17]), while the 70-city index fell 3.2% y/y, the smallest drop in five months (BigGo Finance, [Aug 17]): the annual comparison eased as the monthly decline deepened, and Caixin notes the recovery in smaller cities faltered ([Aug 18]). Property investment slumped nearly 20% (BigGo Finance, [Aug 17]) and land sale revenue posted its biggest drop in a decade (finance.biggo.com, [Aug 18]). Policy stays granular: top cities eased home-purchase rules (chinadailyhk, [Aug 12]) and Beijing widened use of a $1.6 trillion fund to spur spending on homes (Bloomberg, [Aug 18]).

Fiscal, Policy & Trade: Premier Li Qiang called for stabilising external demand as growth sputters (Reuters, [Aug 17]), and the State Council urged faster rollout of the "Six Networks" to boost domestic demand (BigGo Finance, [Aug 17]), a programme that could exceed 26 trillion yuan in the 15th Five-Year Plan with power grid and computing power first in line (BigGo Finance, [Aug 18]). Stimulus calls are growing (Bloomberg, [Aug 18]) and Caixin wants the fiscal taps opened ([Aug 18]), but China Daily still favours tailored easing over broad stimulus ([Aug 14]). On trade, the White House says more than 40 nations help China dodge tariffs (The Washington Post, [Aug 13]), Trump imposed 100% tariffs on some drones (The Japan Times, [Aug 14]), and a US-China investment board stalled before an expected Trump-Xi meeting (South China Morning Post, [Aug 13]).

Markets & Flows: The Hang Seng rebounded past 25,000 on chip and optical-communications gains (BigGo Finance, [Aug 17]) after a four-day rout (BusinessToday Malaysia, [Aug 16]), but mainland stocks fell on a semiconductor selloff (TradingView, [Aug 18]) as steel slumped (Finimize, [Aug 18]). MSCI added Zhipu AI and 32 other Chinese stocks to its index (CGTN, [Aug 13]). Reserve flows are the macro tell: China cut US Treasury holdings to an 18-year low (Benzinga, [Aug 18]) after buying the most gold since 2023 in July (GoldSilver, [Aug 12]).

New / Most Important: July's data sweep was a broad domestic miss: output, retail sales and investment below forecast, bank loans contracting and home prices stagnant. The first responses are targeted rather than macro: a wider housing fund, the Six Networks capex push and a premier focused on external demand. That makes this week's LPR the signal; a hold after this data set would confirm Beijing prefers loan-pricing reform and liquidity to rate cuts while the yuan sits near a three-and-a-half-year high.

Playbook ✓: Targeted easing, liquidity over rate cuts, weak property and a firm, managed yuan are on script.  |  ⚠️ Divergence: Gradual reflation assumed domestic demand would stabilise in the second half; July delivered retail sales at 0.6%, contracting loans and a property investment slump while exports carried growth. That mix transmits abroad unevenly. Iron ore rose only as China demand concerns met stimulus hopes (TradingView, [Aug 18]), leaving AUD exposed; it is already slipping despite a hawkish RBA. European exporters face a China that buys less and sells more, its surpluses putting the EU in a trade bind (Reuters, [Aug 17]), consumer brands feel the slowdown, with Nike at a 12-year low (BigGo Finance, [Aug 18]), and oil at $85.57 is a terms-of-trade tax on a slowing importer.
