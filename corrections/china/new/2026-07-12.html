🇨🇳 China  Mixed  ↓ easing bias

Macro Stance: The easing bias runs through words and the currency, not rates. China Daily stresses "appropriately accommodative" policy ([Jul 10]) and the PBOC vowed flexibility amid an uneven picture (SCMP, [Jul 9]), flagging "structural divergences" as the AI boom masks consumer weakness (BigGo Finance, [Jul 9]), a day after pledging to stay accommodative amid weak demand (Reuters, [Jul 8]). The fixing went below 6.8 per dollar for the first time since 2023 (Bloomberg, [Jul 10]), and the yuan holds near year-to-date highs as US-China dialogue resumes (BigGo Finance, [Jul 11]); Westpac sees sustained gains (Exchange Rates UK, [Jul 11]). June CPI rose 1% (Global Times, [Jul 9]) while producer inflation hit a near 4-year high (KLSE Screener, [Jul 9]), a "scissors gap" that points to weak domestic demand (BigGo Finance, [Jul 9]).

PMIs & Activity: The June PMIs — manufacturing 50.3, non-manufacturing 50.2 (Reuters, [Jun 29]) — rest on exports, and second-quarter GDP arrives in the coming week (MUFG Research, [Jul 10]), expected to show the economy cooled (Free Malaysia Today, [Jul 10]). Autos show the split: June car sales fell 23% (Just Auto, [Jul 10]) while passenger car exports rose 80% (AP News, [Jul 9]), and German carmakers' second-quarter China sales plunged 30%-41% (Fortune, [Jul 11]). May retail sales fell 0.6% (Il Sole 24 Ore, [Jun 15]) and fixed-asset investment was down 4.1% in the first five months (Macau Business, [Jun 16]). The IMF still upgraded China's 2026 forecast to 4.6% (Global Times, [Jul 8]), and Societe Generale says targeted stimulus supports growth (FXStreet, [Jul 10]).

Credit Impulse: May total social financing grew 7.7% (CGTN, [Jun 12]) on government bonds (Bloomberg, [Jun 12]) while new loans missed (Reuters, [Jun 12]); the June print is due. Credit is being disciplined, not expanded: China is cracking down on top ratings for corporate bonds (Financial Times, [Jul 11]), with up to 494.6 billion yuan of bonds at downgrade risk (BigGo Finance, [Jul 8]), and short-term LGFV bond sales were curbed (The Standard, [Jul 9]). Bloomberg sees the debt-cleanup milestone opening a fiscal rethink ([Jul 9]); in Hong Kong, HSBC plans to sell Hang Seng Bank's high-risk loans (The Standard, [Jul 10]).

Property: Vanke says its first-half loss may widen to $2.2 billion (Bloomberg, [Jul 10]), and Fitch expects new home sales to fall 11% to 13% in 2026 (Mingtiandi, [Jul 9]). Resale prices fell faster in June (Reuters, [Jun 30]) after new home prices fell faster in May (Reuters, [Jun 15]) and real-estate investment dropped 16.2% y/y in January–May (GMK Center, [Jun 18]). Demand for Soviet-style apartments shows the limits to the revival (The Edge Singapore, [Jul 11]), and land finance is distorting budgets: one city "sold" the same plot 18 times (SCMP, [Jul 8]).

Fiscal, Policy & Trade: The State Council unveiled a carbon-peak pathway (China Daily, [Jul 10]): supply-side targets, not a demand package. The external walls are rising. The EU is preparing economic protection measures in case of a trade war (The Brussels Times, [Jul 11]), after an anti-dumping probe into Chinese ducks (SCMP, [Jul 10]) and duties of up to 45% on tyres (Eunews, [Jul 9]); a Russia oil-sanctions bill threatening 500% tariffs on China and India won White House support (Tech Times, [Jul 11]); and Beijing expanded its own anti-sanctions toolkit (Al Jazeera, [Jul 10]). Farm trade with Washington still flows: soybean buying has moved beyond the initial benchmark (RFD-TV, [Jul 10]).

Markets & Flows: The Hang Seng closed up 0.6% (BusinessToday Malaysia, [Jul 11]) as Hong Kong headed for its strongest week in over a year on internet gains (Business Times, [Jul 10]), and Hong Kong is building a gold and yuan network that sidesteps dollar stablecoins (CryptoSlate, [Jul 11]). The yuan is now a spillover channel: AUD/USD climbed on yuan strength (Mitrade, [Jul 10]). Households still prefer bullion: China's biggest ETF is a US$13 billion gold fund (Business Times, [Jul 6]).

New / Most Important: The week's newest development is the external squeeze on the engine that carried the June PMI. Brussels is preparing protection measures, a White House-backed bill threatens 500% secondary tariffs, and German carmakers' China sales collapsed in the second quarter as competition heats up. With the yuan near year-to-date highs and domestic demand still weak, exports now face a firmer currency and higher walls just as second-quarter GDP arrives.

Playbook ✓: Weak domestic demand, targeted rather than broad easing and a managed firmer yuan are consistent with the "muddle-through" China consensus.  |  ⚠️ Divergence: Muddle-through assumes exports keep carrying growth while credit and property stabilise; this week both legs look shakier. The transmission to global assets runs through trade and currency rather than construction demand. European exporters take the hit directly, as German carmakers' China sales show. The AUD is trading the yuan (Mitrade, [Jul 10]) and record iron ore trade volumes (S&P Global, [Jul 10]), not a housing recovery, which leaves it exposed if Fitch's forecast of falling home sales proves right. EM FX gains from a firmer yuan anchor until tariffs bite. A cooler GDP print with weak June credit would tilt the stance bearish.
