🇨🇳 China  Mixed  → easing / stimulus

Macro Stance: China remains in counter-cyclical easing mode, now preparing a £40bn stimulus package for the financial sector amid concerns over sluggish growth, per The Guardian — notable because it targets bank balance sheets rather than infrastructure or consumption. The PBOC itself is not cutting: its reference rate came in firmer, 6.7787 vs. 6.7807 previous (www.tmgm.com, [Sep 4]), and Chinese banks are buying US Treasuries to curb yuan gains (IDNFinancials, [Sep 5]), even as the dollar and yuan face selling pressure as expectations for a September US rate hike recede (Moomoo, [Sep 5]). Easing runs through the balance sheet — net purchases of 50 billion yuan of sovereign bonds in August (TradingView, [Sep 2]) — and July CPI slowed to 0.5%, more than expected (BigGo Finance, [Aug 9]), with PPI growth at 3.5%, missing expectations (BigGo Finance, [Aug 8]).

PMIs & Activity: The August surveys stabilised. The official manufacturing PMI rebounded to 49.8 with new orders back in expansion (BigGo Finance, [Aug 30]); the private manufacturing PMI rose to 51.5, its longest expansion streak in five years (BigGo Finance, [Aug 31]); and private services activity quickened on stronger domestic demand (KLSE Screener, [Sep 3]). ING still sees the recovery stalling as the K-shaped divergence widens (ing think, [Sep 3]). Reconciled: the surveys capture exporters, AI hardware and private services, while household goods demand weakens — August NEV retail sales fell 4% to 1.07 million (CnEVPost, [Sep 3]), Bernstein sees China's luxury spending revival fading (Yahoo Finance UK, [Sep 5]), and China signals urgency over construction timelines amid an investment decline (South China Morning Post, [Sep 4]).

Credit Impulse: July bank loans posted a record contraction (Reuters, [Aug 14]) even as credit growth exceeded forecasts (Bloomberg, [Aug 14]); reconciled, government bonds carry the aggregate while private borrowing shrinks, and the bond leg strengthens as China speeds up issuance after a national meeting on investment (South China Morning Post, [Aug 31]). That is a demand problem, which a financial-sector package does not solve directly. Quality is under scrutiny: S&P Global Ratings warns of risks from China's generous credit ratings (Reuters, [Sep 3]), a China indicator that supports risk-taking in stocks and bitcoin is flashing red (CoinDesk, [Sep 2]), and Beijing is reforming property project financing with greater lead-bank oversight (The Asian Banker, [Sep 4]).

Property: China tries to look past the property slump (The Economist, [Sep 3]) with rules that move risk away from buyers: the new home finance regime means what you see is what you get (South China Morning Post, [Sep 3]), and 40-year mortgages have landed in Shanghai with age 35 as the cutoff for new loans (BigGo Finance, [Sep 3]). New home prices were stagnant in July (Reuters, [Aug 17]), property stocks fell as the rules upend the presale funding playbook (Reuters, [Aug 31]), and the reforms bring new pressure in the Evergrande aftermath (IndexBox, [Sep 5]).

Fiscal, Policy & Trade: The bilateral track is warming: Xi seeks to bring a large CEO delegation on his US visit (The Japan Times, [Sep 5]), a US business group is visiting China ahead of the Trump-Xi meeting (Forbes, [Sep 5]) and Greer is optimistic the two can stabilise ties (South China Morning Post, [Sep 4]). The friction is in supply chains and Europe: Chinese rare earth suppliers halted US shipments amid political concerns (Mining.com.au, [Sep 5]), Volkswagen will cut 50,000 more jobs to counter tariffs and Chinese competition (France 24, [Sep 4]), and Šefčovič says China must deliver by October or face harsher measures (Euronews.com, [Sep 2]); China Daily argues a new Plaza Accord won't fix global imbalances (China Daily, [Sep 5]). China and Egypt expanded their currency swap to 30 billion yuan (UA.NEWS, [Sep 5]).

Markets & Flows: Chinese profits rose 25.7% while the CSI 300 fell 9% and the Star 50 fell 29% (The Next Web, [Sep 5]) — the earnings boom that landed with a thud (Bloomberg.com, [Sep 4]). The tone improved into the weekend: Hong Kong stocks jumped 437.56 points as tech and financials fuelled a broad rally (BusinessToday Malaysia, [Sep 5]), and investors are turning to Chinese stocks as an alternative to crowded AI trades (Yahoo Finance, [Sep 5]), increasingly via CSI derivatives (Briefs Finance, [Sep 5]). The China-US yield disparity sits at a record (Nikkei Asia, [Sep 4]).

New / Most Important: The Guardian's £40bn financial-sector stimulus is structurally significant because it shifts Beijing from demand-side stimulus (property, infrastructure) to supply-side financial plumbing repair — the playbook Japan should have run in 1992, recapitalising banks before bad debts metastasise. The question is size relative to local government financing vehicle (LGFV) exposure and developer NPLs, and whether repaired banks find borrowers when July loans contracted at a record pace. J.P. Morgan's "Rush for Resources" piece aligns with China's resource hedging, visible in this week's rare earth shipment halt.

Playbook ✓: Rolling stimulus, a managed currency, financial sector support — the standard Beijing slow-burn deleveraging script.  |  ⚠️ Divergence: The private PMIs partially contradict the China-weak narrative: manufacturing at 51.5 and services quickening on domestic demand say either exporters and AI hardware are carrying the cycle or the official surveys understate private demand. The transmission is two-sided — stabilising surveys and bank support help iron ore and the AUD at the margin and could rotate capital toward value and EM, but an export-led China is costing European manufacturers jobs, and with WTI at $91.48 the terms-of-trade squeeze caps how much stimulus reaches Chinese import demand.
