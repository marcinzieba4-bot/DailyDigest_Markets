🇨🇳 China  Mixed  → policy divergent

Macro Stance: China is on its own path while the ECB hikes and the Fed and BOJ prepare to, and the PBOC leans against yuan strength through the fixing rather than rates. Its USD/CNY reference rate of 6.7698 compared with an estimate of 6.7083 (investingLive, [Sep 13]), with the yuan at a fresh three-and-a-half-year high despite weak guidance (The Edge Malaysia, [Sep 7]). August CPI rose 0.8% and PPI 3.8%, beating expectations (BigGo Finance, [Sep 8]), but the lift came from energy costs (Reuters, [Sep 9]) and consumer prices are still below target (Free Malaysia Today, [Sep 9]): imported cost-push, not domestic reflation, which keeps the easing bias intact. It also bought 20.2 tonnes of gold, its largest purchase since 2023 (Kitco, [Sep 8]).

PMIs & Activity: The official manufacturing PMI rebounded to 49.8 in August with new orders back in expansion (BigGo Finance, [Aug 30]), while the private gauge rose to 51.5, its longest expansion streak in five years (BigGo Finance, [Aug 31]), and the private services PMI rebounded to 51.4 (BigGo Finance, [Sep 2]). Reconciled: the official level is below 50 while the private surveys expand; trade shows why. Exports jumped 25% in August as the surplus widened (AP News, [Sep 7]) toward $806 billion (Bloomberg.com, [Sep 7]), driven by high-tech and AI demand (Reuters, [Sep 8]), but imports missed estimates (CNBC, [Sep 7]). Domestic demand is the weak leg: passenger car sales slumped 24% in August (Caixin Global, [Sep 9]), and the July print showed output up 4.5% and retail sales up only 0.6% (Reuters, [Aug 17]), with fixed-asset investment down 6.7% in the first seven months (Xinhua, [Aug 17]).

Credit Impulse: July bank loans posted a record contraction as credit demand faltered (Reuters, [Aug 14]) even as broad credit growth exceeded forecasts (Bloomberg.com, [Aug 14]); reconciled, government bonds carry the aggregate while households and firms do not borrow. Lenders are snapping up bonds as the loan slump deepens (Bloomberg.com, [Sep 8]). Beijing's answer is capital rather than demand: 300 billion yuan in special treasury bonds to boost the capital of financial firms (China Daily, [Sep 7]), a $54 billion injection after which bank and insurer stocks still fell (CNBC, [Sep 7]) and which The Economist says falls far short (The Economist, [Sep 8]). More capital raises lending capacity, not loan demand; the next credit print is the test.

Property: The presale overhaul is being priced through land and developer finance. Goldman sees the new rules cutting land sales by 30% (The Business Times, [Sep 7]) and Caixin reports the shift to completed-home sales cooling the land market (Caixin Global, [Sep 10]); developers in several regions are planning pre-emptive price rises before the August 28 policy takes effect (36Kr, [Sep 13]). Restructuring channels are narrowing: China halted a key debt-to-equity tool for ailing developers (The Standard, [Sep 9]) and regulators are scrutinising offshore debt-swap bonds (Caixin Global, [Sep 11]). Beijing home sales climbed and inventory shrank in the month after easing (Yicai Global, [Sep 10]). July's 70-city prices fell 3.2% y/y, the smallest drop in five months, while property investment slumped nearly 20% (BigGo Finance, [Aug 17]).

Fiscal, Policy & Trade: Support targets balance sheets, not households: the recapitalisation sits alongside a 15th Five-Year plan for building a financial powerhouse with nine PBOC action plans (BigGo Finance, [Sep 10]). The US track is thawing: the two sides are consulting on reciprocal tariff reductions covering $30 billion (Global Times, [Sep 10]) and China bought 1 million tons of US soybeans ahead of Xi's visit (Reuters, [Sep 9]), though Barron's warns the fragile truce could break before the Washington summit (Barron's, [Sep 9]). Europe is hardening: the EU wants initial Chinese action on the trade gap by early October (Reuters, [Sep 8]) and the EU Parliament is ready to take a tougher line (POLITICO.eu, [Sep 11]), even as German firms lift China investment while US outlays fall (Reuters, [Sep 13]).

Markets & Flows: Rate-hike fears dominate: the Hang Seng slid 3.3% over the week on the oil spike (BusinessToday Malaysia, [Sep 13]) after falling below 25,000 (Dimsum Daily, [Sep 10]). The China-US 10-year yield gap hit a record 317 basis points (bloomingbit, [Sep 9]), yet the yuan's steady rise is drawing foreign investors back (Caixin Global, [Sep 11]). AI capital markets are the bright spot: Enflame nearly tripled in its Shanghai debut (Reuters, [Sep 11]), Moonshot and DeepSeek eye IPOs (Growth Dragons, [Sep 11]) and DeepSeek hired a dealmaker as CFO ahead of a possible listing, while Shanghai AI Lab trends on US Twitter.

New / Most Important: The property reform is turning into a fiscal problem. Bloomberg reports the overhaul is straining local government income (Bloomberg.com, [Sep 13]), the direct consequence of a 30% hit to land sales, and Briefs Finance argues the August data may force stronger stimulus (Briefs Finance, [Sep 13]). With exports supporting output as property drags (www.tmgm.com, [Sep 12]), pressure builds for support that reaches households and local budgets, not just bank capital.

Playbook ✓: China as a net loser from Middle East oil disruption is textbook, and policy divergence holds: easing runs through bank capital and the fixing while the record yield gap caps rate cuts.  |  ⚠️ Divergence: The growth pulse transmits abroad through supply rather than demand. A 25% export surge with imports missing estimates adds Chinese competition for European exporters, while a domestic economy with car sales down 24% and land sales facing a 30% hit gives EM little and weighs on steel and iron ore demand. The AUD faces a second channel, China's currency push targeting Australia's biggest commodity exports (AFR, [Sep 13]). Oil at $101.29 is a terms-of-trade tax on a large crude importer, lifting PPI to 3.8% without lifting demand. A DeepSeek listing would give global capital China AI exposure that bypasses the weak domestic cycle.
