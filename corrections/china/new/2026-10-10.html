🇨🇳 China  Mixed  → easing bias, slow delivery

Macro Stance: The PBOC keeps an easing bias but delivers it in small steps, and the US 10Y at 5.24% keeps the rate gap wide. Having ramped up structural tools (BigGo Finance, [Oct 4]), it bought a net 100 billion yuan of sovereign bonds in September (TradingView, [Oct 9]), easing through the bond market rather than the policy rate. The currency side is firm: the PBOC said China has no need or intention to weaken the yuan for a trade edge (The Standard (HK), [Oct 8]), the yuan sits near a four-year high as the bank rebuts undervaluation claims (Briefs Finance, [Oct 9]) and the fix moved to 6.7330 (www.tmgm.com, [Oct 9]). Advisers want more: Li Xunlei argues monetary policy can be looser (BigGo Finance, [Oct 9]). August CPI rose 0.8% and PPI 3.8% (BigGo Finance, [Sep 8]), with China CPI due next week (Newsquawk, [Oct 9]).

PMIs & Activity: September brought the first official expansion in three months: manufacturing at 50.1, exactly the consensus, and non-manufacturing at 50.2 (investingLive, [Sep 29]), with the private RatingDog gauges stronger at 52.1 and 51.6 (investingLive, [Sep 30]), an AI-led rebound (Reuters, [Sep 29]) on top of a 25% August export jump (AP News, [Sep 7]). Domestic demand remains the weak leg: August retail sales rose 0.4% (investingLive, [Sep 14]) and Q3 growth is estimated below target at 4.4% (Nikkei Asia, [Oct 6]). The holiday was mixed: Bloomberg found weak spending (Bloomberg, [Oct 8]), while luxury retail traffic hit a two-year high (Yahoo Finance Singapore, [Oct 9]); September NEV wholesale sales are estimated at a 2026 high of 1.67 million (CnEVPost, [Oct 9]).

Credit Impulse: August bank loans rose less than expected (Reuters, [Sep 14]) and credit expansion missed forecasts (Bloomberg, [Sep 14]), with bond and equity financing exceeding loans for the first time (BigGo Finance, [Sep 14]): both measures missed, and government bonds carried what growth there was. Subsidies are the lever for household credit: the finance ministry says 100 billion yuan of interest subsidies leveraged 17 trillion yuan of consumer credit (BigGo Finance, [Oct 9]), while the small-bank purge accelerates (CHOSUNBIZ - Chosunbiz, [Oct 6]).

Property: The holiday delivered the first clear response to the mortgage subsidy: Shanghai new-home sales doubled and secondhand transactions in 11 cities jumped over 40% (BigGo Finance, [Oct 9]), Citi says Golden Week home sales beat in key cities (The Standard (HK), [Oct 8]) and home sales posted a modest recovery (Bloomberg, [Oct 8]); S&P sees a top-city recovery next year (Traders Union, [Oct 8]). The base is still weak: August home prices fell across 70 cities (BigGo Finance, [Sep 14]) and property investment fell 19.9% in eight months (Business Recorder, [Sep 15]). Developer repair continues, with PAG in talks for a majority stake in an Evergrande unit (Yahoo Finance, [Oct 9]).

Fiscal, Policy & Trade: Delivery is speeding up but stays incremental: Beijing lets local governments tap 550 billion yuan in unused debt quotas (Caixin Global, [Oct 9]), worth $82 billion of additional stimulus for provinces (Bloomberg, [Oct 9]), as China ramps up its fiscal push to meet the growth target (Reuters, [Oct 9]); critics still call it half measures (WSJ, [Oct 8]). On trade, the EU talks ended in a deal: China agreed to halve hybrid exports to the EU (Reuters, [Oct 9]) and the two sides averted a conflict with an 'understanding' on hybrid cars (Financial Times, [Oct 9]), though Breakingviews calls it a half-deal (Reuters, [Oct 9]). China will resume October fuel exports after the holiday pause (Reuters, [Oct 9]).

Markets & Flows: Reuters argues China's slow bull market is poised to take off (Reuters, [Oct 8]), while Yahoo Finance asks why the Chinese stock market is missing the AI rally (Yahoo Finance, [Oct 9]). The tape sides with the sceptics for now: China stocks ended the holiday-shortened week near a one-year low (TradingView, [Oct 9]) before rebounding on unconfirmed talk of regulator guidance (Bloomberg, [Oct 9]), as optical chip stocks extended their rout on fears of US curbs (South China Morning Post, [Oct 9]).

New / Most Important: Two moves on the same day changed the risk balance. The hybrid deal removes the nearest trade-war trigger with Europe at the cost of export volume, and the 550 billion yuan quota release puts fiscal money behind domestic demand to replace it. Together with the holiday housing data, it is the first week in which policy and demand moved in the same direction, but at a scale that secures the target rather than reflates.

Playbook ✓: Incremental delivery, quota releases and targeted subsidies instead of a 'Sept. 24-style' package, and a trade compromise that caps rather than ends friction, are on script.  |  ⚠️ Divergence: If the slow bull is real, it is a domestic valuation and housing story, not a global-growth spillover. Halving hybrid exports trims the export engine that drove the PMI back to 50.1, while the quota release funds local investment and the subsidy lifts home sales, so transmission shifts toward property: that helps iron ore and the AUD only if holiday sales turn into starts and investment, which August's 19.9% drop says has not happened yet. European autos get relief on volumes, and EM Asia faces less Chinese export pressure. A China re-rating without a commodity pull is a very different trade from synchronized reflation.
