🇨🇳 China  Mixed  → easing bias

Macro Stance: Rates are on hold, the easing runs through liquidity and the currency is the active front. The loan prime rates were left steady for a 15th consecutive month (Reuters, [Aug 19]), the one-year at 3.0% and the five-year at 3.5%, while the overnight tool has taken over from seven-day reverse repos (BigGo Finance, [Aug 19]) and a 500 billion yuan MLF operation kept medium-term liquidity flowing (BigGo Finance, [Aug 24]). The yuan surged to 6.72, a 42-month high (BigGo Finance, [Aug 20]), before the PBOC slowed gains with a weaker-than-expected fixing (Bloomberg, [Aug 19]) and then leaned hardest against yuan gains in six months (investingLive, [Aug 24]). Prices give it room: July CPI slowed to 0.5% and PPI to 3.5%, both missing expectations (BigGo Finance, [Aug 8]), reviving deflation risk (Bloomberg, [Aug 9]).

PMIs & Activity: The growth pulse is below 50 at home and strong abroad. July's official manufacturing PMI fell to 49.2 and non-manufacturing to 49.0 (FXStreet, [Jul 31]); the private factory index eased to 50.9 (Fibre2Fashion, [Aug 3]) and private services growth hit its slowest since September 2024 (Reuters, [Aug 4]). Hard data confirmed it: July industrial output grew 4.5% y/y and retail sales only 0.6% (Reuters, [Aug 17]), and fixed-asset investment fell 6.7% in the first seven months (Xinhua, [Aug 17]). Exports are the offset, jumping almost a quarter in July (Financial Times, [Aug 6]) on AI-driven shipments. Goldman warns Q3 growth could fall below 4.5% (BigGo Finance, [Aug 24]). First-half profits rose 18.7% as a tech boom masked the property slump (Caixin Global, [Jul 27]).

Credit Impulse: July bank loans posted a record contraction as credit demand faltered (Reuters, [Aug 14]), with new loans at -340B (CryptoRank, [Aug 15]); yet credit growth exceeded forecasts despite the rare loan slump (Bloomberg, [Aug 14]). Reconciled: the broad financing aggregate is holding up while the loan book itself shrank, so whatever impulse exists comes from outside bank lending. Policy is working on price, not volume: corporate loan rates fell below 3% (BigGo Finance, [Aug 14]), banks are embracing cheaper short-term loan rates (SCMP, [Aug 20]) and the subsidized loan program was expanded to revive demand (Caixin Global, [Aug 21]), with the SME loan cap raised to 75 million yuan. Liquidity is ample; demand for it is not.

Property: July new home prices were stagnant as demand stayed weak (Reuters, [Aug 17]); the 70-city annual decline narrowed to 3.2%, the smallest in five months, while property investment slumped nearly 20% (BigGo Finance, [Aug 17]). The monthly picture is softer than the annual one, with prices falling at a faster pace (Bloomberg, [Aug 17]) and first-tier prices flat, ending a four-month rebound (SCMP, [Aug 17]). Easing continues at the margin: Beijing widened use of a $1.6 trillion fund to spur spending on homes (Bloomberg, [Aug 18]) and Shanghai lowered second-home down payments (SCMP, [Aug 20]). The legacy is being closed out rather than resolved: Evergrande founder Hui Ka Yan was sentenced to life in prison (Reuters, [Aug 20]), a court accepted the Evergrande liquidation a day later (SCMP, [Aug 21]), and a flood of discounted auction homes is deepening the slump (Asia Times, [Aug 21]).

Fiscal, Policy & Trade: Fiscal policy is the lever being pulled. China pledged timely fiscal support to bolster growth (Reuters, [Aug 20]) and unveiled measures to boost weak consumption (SCMP, [Aug 21]). Delivery is slow: the $119 billion policy financing tool has begun project applications but faces a roll-out lag (Reuters, [Aug 24]), and a "policy addiction" warning signals a preference for targeted stimulus (SCMP, [Aug 24]). On trade, the US eyes overcapacity tariffs of 7.5% before the Xi-Trump talks (Bloomberg, [Aug 24]), Goldman estimates EU curbs threaten 27% of Chinese exports to the bloc (Bloomberg, [Aug 24]), and looming US sanctions on Iran put China's oil buying in the spotlight (Reuters, [Aug 24]).

Markets & Flows: Equities are trading supply and AI, not macro. The Hang Seng dropped nearly 500 points on Monday as Alibaba slumped 8pc (The Standard, [Aug 24]) on a first post-IPO placement to raise HK$80 billion (BigGo Finance, [Aug 24]). Unitree soared 629% on its debut (The News International, [Aug 19]) while the ChiNext tumbled 6.26% (BigGo Finance, [Aug 19]), and mainland capital pulled a net HK$10.4 billion from Hong Kong stocks (BigGo Finance, [Aug 20]). Chinese bonds are bucking the global selloff (CNBC TV18, [Aug 19]). US Treasury buybacks potentially redirecting capital toward Asia remain a flow positive.

New / Most Important: Beijing has chosen fiscal support and currency management over rate cuts. The PBOC's heaviest pushback against yuan strength in six months lands as Goldman warns Q3 growth could undershoot and the $119 billion financing tool is only now taking applications. Capping the yuan is the easing step the PBOC can take without touching the LPR; the next industrial-profit print will show whether AI-led earnings can keep masking a domestic economy whose loan book is shrinking.

Playbook ✓: LPR on hold, liquidity through overnight repos and the MLF, city-level property easing, targeted fiscal support and exports carrying growth are all on script.  |  ⚠️ Divergence: The market reads a 42-month high in the yuan as a vote of confidence; the PBOC treats it as a threat to an economy with a sub-50 PMI, contracting loans and property investment down nearly 20%. That transmits abroad through demand: the AUD softened as Beijing leaned on the yuan (investingLive, [Aug 24]), iron ore weakened despite stimulus (TradingView, [Aug 23]), and European exporters face weak Chinese consumption on top of the EU curbs. EM equity flows are hostage to Hong Kong's supply overhang, and WTI at 82.14 is a terms-of-trade tax on Chinese margins.
