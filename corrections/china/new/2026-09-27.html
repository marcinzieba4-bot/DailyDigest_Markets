🇨🇳 China  Mixed  → accommodative but constrained

Macro Stance: Accommodation runs through liquidity, not rates. The loan prime rates were held for a 16th month as the Fed hike narrows the room to ease (investingLive, [Sep 20]); instead the PBOC will offer banks up to 1 trillion yuan a day over the holidays (Bloomberg, [Sep 23]) and its third-quarter policy committee pledged to stay 'appropriately loose' (TradingView, [Sep 24]). The yuan hit a fresh multi-year peak as the PBOC eased its curb before the summit (Reuters, [Sep 21]), but a fix at 6.7468 against an estimate of 6.6971 (investingLive, [Sep 22]) marks the ceiling, and Societe Generale now sees yuan volatility rising as the truce runs to January (VT Markets, [Sep 26]). August CPI rose 0.8% and PPI 3.8% (BigGo Finance, [Sep 8]): energy-driven factory prices, barely positive consumer prices.

PMIs & Activity: The August surveys still set the level. The official manufacturing PMI rebounded to 49.8 with new orders back in expansion (BigGo Finance, [Aug 30]), below 50 for a second month, while the private gauge rose to 51.5 (BigGo Finance, [Aug 31]). Reconciled: the private survey expands, the official one does not. Hard data split the same way: output growth quickened to 5.2% (BigGo Finance, [Sep 14]), but retail sales rose only 0.4% (investingLive, [Sep 14]) and fixed-asset investment fell 7.2% in eight months (Xinhua, [Sep 15]). The freshest read is external, a 'surprise' jump in US orders ahead of the summit in a private survey (CNBC, [Sep 25]), while domestic margins erode: the share of loss-making firms is approaching 30% (BigGo Finance, [Sep 25]) and Gree and Haier see profits fall (Nikkei Asia, [Sep 26]).

Credit Impulse: August bank lending disappointed (Reuters, [Sep 14]) and credit expansion missed forecasts as loan demand sagged (Bloomberg, [Sep 14]), so loans and the aggregate do not conflict this month: both missed. The composition is the story: bond and equity financing exceeded loans in social financing for the first time (BigGo Finance, [Sep 14]), so issuance, not bank lending, carries the aggregate while households and firms decline to borrow. The PBOC is relaxed, holding rates as it signals slower credit (Chosunbiz, [Sep 20]), and regulators told e-commerce apps to stop selling loans (Sixth Tone, [Sep 21]). The one forced credit flow is defensive: banks were asked to keep Vanke loans off bad-debt books (Reuters, [Sep 22]).

Property: August home prices extended their decline across 70 cities, with tier-1 cities stabilising (BigGo Finance, [Sep 14]), and property investment fell 19.9% in eight months, the steepest decline on record (BigGo Finance, [Sep 14]). Relief this week was marginal: first-tier new home sales jumped over 10% (BigGo Finance, [Sep 23]) and Beijing now requires topped-out structures for presales (China Daily, [Sep 24]), while property stocks surged on mortgage-subsidy rumours that analysts called unlikely to be implemented (BigGo Finance, [Sep 23]).

Fiscal, Policy & Trade: The summit's tangible outcome came on Friday: tariff cuts on $30 billion in goods and an AI dialogue (Reuters, [Sep 26]), with China to buy 10 million tons of US coal in 2027 and 2028 (Bloomberg, [Sep 26]). It sits on a truce extended by only two months (Reuters, [Sep 23]), and Trump and Xi will meet twice more after the summit failed to resolve tensions (Financial Times, [Sep 26]); Greer says trade enters a 'managed' phase with the deficit set to fall to $140B (Yahoo Finance, [Sep 25]). Europe is moving the other way: the ECB finds China's industrial rise hits German manufacturers hard (Reuters, [Sep 22]) and the EU urges Britain to raise tariffs on Chinese cars (Reuters, [Sep 25]). At home, October stimulus expectations are cooling (BigGo Finance, [Sep 25]) ahead of the Central Committee plenum the Politburo has set for late October (U.S. News & World Report, [Sep 21]).

Markets & Flows: Mainland stocks posted their biggest one-day drop in a month on summit caution (The Star, [Sep 24]) before Shanghai closed for the Mid-Autumn Festival at 3,888.37 after a 1.22% slide (BBN Times, [Sep 25]), and Hong Kong dropped 1% on Friday as tech and insurers dragged (BusinessToday Malaysia, [Sep 26]). The rotation is internal: consumer stocks sit near decade lows as investors pile into AI (Investing.com, [Sep 26]). Reserves keep moving into gold, with China importing a record of over 1,000 tonnes this year (Financial Times, [Sep 22]).

New / Most Important: The summit finally produced a concession that can be measured: reciprocal tariff cuts on $30 billion of goods, coal purchases and an AI channel. It is a margin improvement for exporters already enjoying a surprise jump in US orders, not a demand shock, and it arrives as Bloomberg warns that China's economic 'regime shift' may sow risk for the world (Bloomberg, [Sep 26]). With stimulus expectations for October cooling, the policy offset is not coming soon. Domestically nothing has changed: consumer stocks near decade lows, a sub-50 factory PMI and credit that missed on both loans and the aggregate.

Playbook ✓: China continues to ease while the rest of the world tightens, textbook divergence: holiday liquidity and an 'appropriately loose' pledge against a Fed discussing hikes and an RBA about to hike.  |  ⚠️ Divergence: WTI's 10.6% run to 92.41 reflects a supply shock rather than China demand: China's factory PMI is below 50, its imports missed and property investment is down 19.9%, a negative signal for global growth bulls counting on a China reflation. The transmission runs through the AUD and iron ore, where Australia's factory PMI fell to 49.3 (Mitrade, [Sep 22]) as the RBA hikes into a five-year high in unemployment, and through European exporters, who face Chinese competition rather than Chinese demand. A tariff deal that lifts China's exports to the US does little for either.
