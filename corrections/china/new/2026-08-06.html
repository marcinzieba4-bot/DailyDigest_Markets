🇨🇳 China  Mixed  → Easing bias maintained

Macro Stance: Monetary policy is in easing mode, but the currency is moving the other way. The PBOC injected CNY 500 billion (BigGo Finance, [Aug 4]) and signalled continued monetary support in H2 (Fibre2Fashion, [Aug 4]), and China Daily writes that interest rate and RRR cuts may be on the table ([Aug 4]). The yuan hit its strongest in 3-1/2 years as trade tensions grew (The Standard (HK), [Aug 5]), with the central parity at 6.7 (BusinessToday Malaysia, [Aug 4]); foreign reserves held steady as external earnings stay in private hands (South China Morning Post, [Aug 5]). June CPI rose only 1% (Global Times, [Jul 9]) while PPI climbed 4.1% (finance.biggo.com, [Jul 8]), a margin squeeze rather than reflation.

PMIs & Activity: Every July gauge slowed. The official manufacturing PMI dropped to 49.2 (The State Council Information Office, [Jul 31]) and non-manufacturing to 49 (Macau Business, [Jul 31]); the private manufacturing PMI eased to 50.9 (Fibre2Fashion, [Aug 3]) and the private services gauge hit a two-year low as firms turned cautious (The Edge Malaysia, [Aug 5]). Reconciled: the private samples are still above 50 but decelerating, the official ones are contracting. China Daily expects factories to maintain a healthy pace ([Aug 5]) and the IMF forecasts 4.4% growth in 2026 (China Daily Global Edition, [Aug 5]), after Q2 GDP grew 4.3%, below the target range (Bloomberg, [Jul 15]). Consumption is soft: July NEV retail sales fell 2% to 970,000 (CnEVPost, [Aug 5]), even as CPCA saw NEV wholesale up 23% (CnEVPost, [Aug 4]), the gap being exports.

Credit Impulse: June new bank loans rose less than expected (The Star, [Jul 15]) and credit growth missed forecasts as the PBOC flagged a "new normal" (Caixin Global, [Jul 16]); household loans fell by 366.8 billion yuan in the first half (36Kr, [Jul 17]). A CNY 500 billion injection changes little while credit demand is this weak. Bad-debt manager China Cinda warned of a first-half profit drop as the property slump weighs (Caixin Global, [Aug 5]), a reminder of where the credit losses sit. The impulse reads flat, with households negative.

Property: The July data are deteriorating. The top 100 developers' sales slump deepened (Trivium China, [Aug 5]), housing prices fell 0.5% with sales volume weakening (Investing.com, [Aug 3]), and July was the worst building month since Covid (Wood Central, [Aug 1]). June's official data had property investment down 18 percent y/y in January–June (The Standard (HK), [Jul 15]). Breakingviews argues the chips lottery is no cure for the property bust (Reuters, [Aug 4]), and Xi's Shanghai tour offered no relief for the housing crisis (The Diplomat, [Aug 4]).

Fiscal, Policy & Trade: The Politburo's support remains incremental (Caixin Global, [Jul 31]) and insufficient to reverse the property and credit deflation cycle. Trade has escalated: China hit back at the US with sanctions and tighter drone export rules (Financial Times, [Aug 5]), blacklisting six US firms after fresh tariffs (WION, [Aug 5]) and targeting US firms after FCC and Xinjiang actions (Reuters, [Aug 5]), and opened its first foreign-trade national security investigation (China Daily, [Aug 5]). Washington will impose a 15% tariff in its polysilicon probe (Reuters, [Aug 5]) after planning a ban on Chinese data-centre components (NST Online, [Aug 4]). Beijing's global tax hunt going back decades (Financial Times, [Aug 5]) is hitting offshore wealth, with Prudential shares tumbling on a report China will tax offshore insurance returns (Reuters, [Aug 5]).

Markets & Flows: Onshore, stocks rose as a surge in chipmakers offset a tumble in optical module plays (Business Recorder, [Aug 5]), but stock account openings slowed in July amid the tech sell-off (South China Morning Post, [Aug 5]), while mainland investors extended their buying spree in Hong Kong (South China Morning Post, [Aug 5]). NerdWallet's "best-performing China stocks" piece shows retail interest in selective opportunities. A sovereign bond sale in Hong Kong raised over US$2 billion (South China Morning Post, [Aug 5]), and Argentina renewed its $19 billion China swap (Bloomberg, [Aug 5]).

New / Most Important: Beijing's retaliation changes the trade picture weeks before a planned summit. Sanctions, a blacklist and drone export curbs answer the US polysilicon tariff and the data-centre component ban, and the tax hunt adds a capital-account squeeze on offshore wealth. With developer sales sliding and every PMI slowing, the economy is entering a trade fight from a weak domestic position.

Playbook ✓: Selective stimulus, a managed but strengthening yuan and range-bound equities are on script.  |  ⚠️ Divergence: The consensus expected a managed truce into the summit; instead both sides are escalating while China's domestic demand weakens. That hits the China-specific channels: iron ore hit a year low (Tech Times, [Aug 4]) as China's steel body pushes a yuan-denominated price index (Global Times, [Aug 5]), BHP remains a top mining pick despite a China growth downgrade (TheBull.com.au, [Aug 4]), AUD stays exposed, and UK-listed insurers and banks feel the tax crackdown (The Times, [Aug 5]). Oil at $77.13 keeps the terms-of-trade tax on China's margins.
