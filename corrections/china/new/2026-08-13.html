🇨🇳 China  Mixed  → easing bias

Macro Stance: The PBOC's second-quarter monetary policy report keeps the easing bias explicit without delivering a move: it pledged a timely new policy rollout (Reuters, [Aug 12]) and to maintain loose monetary policy (Investing.com, [Aug 12]), and created a 1 trillion yuan relending facility for private enterprises (BigGo Finance, [Aug 12]). Liquidity is ample rather than generous, with 7-day reverse repo volume at zero for the first time since June (The Standard (HK), [Aug 11]). The yuan hit a 3.5-year high against the dollar (BigGo Finance, [Aug 11]), and the PBOC's new five-year plan rules out devaluation (FXStreet, [Aug 10]). Prices are cooling: July CPI rose 0.5% (CGTN, [Aug 9]), a six-month low (TradingView, [Aug 8]), and factory-gate inflation eased to a 3-month low (Reuters, [Aug 8]) with PPI up 3.5% (China Daily, [Aug 9]).

PMIs & Activity: July's official PMIs both sit below 50, manufacturing at 49.2 and non-manufacturing at 49.0 (FXStreet, [Jul 31]). The private gauges are still above water but losing altitude: the manufacturing PMI eased to 50.9 (Fibre2Fashion, [Aug 3]), a 4-month low (Reuters, [Aug 2]), and services growth hit its slowest since September 2024 (Reuters, [Aug 4]). Exports remain the offset, beating estimates in July on AI-driven shipments (CNBC, [Aug 6]). The domestic leg is weak: July new car sales slumped 21%, a 10th straight decline, even as exports surged 88% (finance.biggo.com, [Aug 11]). ICIS reads the July PMI as pointing to a weaker second half absent more stimulus (ICIS, [Aug 11]), after Q2 GDP growth weakened to 4.3% (Bloomberg, [Jul 15]).

Credit Impulse: The July credit print has not landed and the previews conflict. A Reuters poll sees July bank lending set to plummet amid subdued demand and a seasonal slowdown (Reuters, [Aug 7]), while Caixin expects July loans to rebound on a low base (Caixin Global, [Aug 7]). Reconciled: the base effect can flatter the year-on-year comparison even as the monthly flow collapses, and low loan growth is already fuelling easing bets (VT Markets, [Aug 8]). June set the tone, with new bank loans rising less than expected (The Star, [Jul 15]) and household loans down 366.8 billion yuan in the first half (36Kr, [Jul 17]). Asset quality is the other side of the ledger, as banks look to unload US$8.2b in bad credit-card loans (South China Morning Post, [Aug 11]). The relending facility addresses supply; the binding constraint is demand.

Property: City-level easing is running ahead of price stabilisation. Beijing further relaxed home-buying curbs (Reuters, [Aug 7]), following Shanghai (Bloomberg, [Aug 7]). A private survey shows housing prices fell 0.5% in July as sales volume weakened (Investing.com, [Aug 3]), and real home prices have sunk to their lowest level in 21 years (the deep dive, [Aug 3]). The official June data showed new home prices falling at a slower pace (Reuters, [Jul 14]) but property investment down 18 percent y/y in January–June (The Standard (HK), [Jul 15]). Expiring land leases are a new $148 billion threat Beijing is racing to solve (Bloomberg, [Aug 11]), and a key steel price at a near-decade low confirms the construction slump (Bloomberg, [Aug 6]).

Fiscal, Policy & Trade: The fiscal signal is about collection rather than spending: authorities are mounting a tax-collection crackdown in a sign of fiscal worry (Bloomberg, [Aug 12]), including a 20% offshore tax on the super-rich (South China Morning Post, [Aug 10]). Leaders are at their seaside retreat (The Economist, [Aug 11]), and former premier Zhu Rongji, architect of the market reforms, died at 97 (Reuters, [Aug 12]). On trade, Beijing is testing the truce weeks before Xi's visit (CNBC, [Aug 6]): the US imposed a 15% tariff on polysilicon (Nikkei Asia, [Aug 7]), the Senate passed a bill that could trigger 100% tariffs on China and India (Daily Finland, [Aug 9]), and China hit US and Mexican pecans with anti-dumping tariffs of up to 54.3% (Global Times, [Aug 10]) while accelerating US soybean buying ahead of the summit (AgroLatam, [Aug 10]).

Markets & Flows: Hong Kong is choppy: the Hang Seng fell 284 points as an oil surge stoked inflation fears (BigGo Finance, [Aug 11]), and China and Hong Kong shares slipped as Hormuz reopening hopes faded (Business Recorder, [Aug 12]). Regulators are clamping down on margin debt after the AI stock frenzy (Bloomberg, [Aug 10]). Gold is the domestic hedge of choice: the PBOC made its largest monthly purchase since 2023 (Kitco, [Aug 7]), and Chinese investors poured $1.2bn into gold ETFs (TradingView, [Aug 6]).

New / Most Important: The PBOC's Q2 report is the newest signal and it reads as a pre-commitment. A pledge of timely new policy and a 1 trillion yuan private relending facility arrive with both official PMIs below 50, CPI at a six-month low and a poll expecting July lending to plummet. With the yuan at a 3.5-year high and devaluation ruled out, the burden falls on rates and targeted credit, and the July credit data become the test of whether supply-side tools can fix a demand-side problem.

Playbook ✓: Easing bias, export resilience, weak domestic demand and city-level property relaxation are on script.  |  ⚠️ Divergence: The consensus treats the export beat as a cushion, but PMIs of 49.2 and 49.0 and a steel price at a near-decade low say domestic construction is contracting. Chinese port stocks are rising as steel demand softens (Hellenic Shipping News, [Aug 11]), leaving iron ore and AUD exposed just as the RBA leans hawkish; European exporters face a German trade deficit with China at record levels (Centre for European Reform (CER), [Aug 9]), and oil at $81.98 is a terms-of-trade tax on margins with PPI already easing.
