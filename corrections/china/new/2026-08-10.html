🇨🇳 China  Mixed  → easing bias

Macro Stance: The July price data have shifted the debate from cooling inflation to deflation risk. CPI rose 0.5% (CGTN, [Aug 9]), a six-month low that adds pressure for policy easing (Investing.com, [Aug 9]), and PPI rose 3.5% (China Daily, [Aug 9]), slowing on lower fuel prices (South China Morning Post, [Aug 9]); Bloomberg says the slower-than-expected gains revive deflation risk ([Aug 9]). CGTN's "upward trend" ([Aug 9]) and investingLive's energy-driven rebound ([Aug 9]) are not contradictions: prices are still positive year on year, but the energy impulse is fading and, as investingLive adds, the demand story hasn't changed. China Daily writes that interest rate and RRR cuts may be on the table ([Aug 4]), liquidity is ample after a CNY 500 billion injection (BigGo Finance, [Aug 4]), and the PBOC made its largest gold purchase since 2023 (Caixin Global, [Aug 8]). The yuan is firm near a 3-1/2-year high (The Star, [Aug 7]).

PMIs & Activity: The official manufacturing PMI fell to 49.2 and the non-manufacturing PMI to 49.0 (FXStreet, [Jul 31]); the private factory index eased to 50.9 (Fibre2Fashion, [Aug 3]), a 4-month low (Reuters, [Aug 2]). Trade carries the economy. Exports surged 23.9% and the surplus widened to $112.5B (Seeking Alpha, [Aug 7]); SCMP's narrower surplus ([Aug 7]) is the month-on-month view, and growth eased from June's 27% (Reuters, [Jul 13]) while beating forecasts (CNBC, [Aug 6]). Total trade expanded 19.2% in July (CGTN, [Aug 7]) as AI demand lifted export prices (Caixin Global, [Aug 7]). Consumption is weak: price war fears grip the EV market after woeful July sales (South China Morning Post, [Aug 3]), and Q2 GDP growth of 4.3% fell below the target range (Bloomberg, [Jul 15]).

Credit Impulse: July credit is still to come and the previews conflict. A Reuters poll sees bank lending set to plummet on subdued demand and a seasonal slowdown ([Aug 7]), while Caixin expects loans to rebound on a low base ([Aug 7]): a sharp drop from June's seasonal peak that still compares well with a weak July last year. VT Markets reads July credit demand as weak, with low loan growth fuelling easing bets ([Aug 8]). June set the tone, with new loans rising less than expected (The Star, [Jul 15]) and household loans down 366.8 billion yuan in the first half (36Kr, [Jul 17]). Record low yields on long-dated offshore yuan bonds (Bloomberg, [Aug 4]) are the market's verdict on demand.

Property: Policy is moving in the top tier: Beijing further relaxed home-buying curbs (Reuters, [Aug 7]), following Shanghai (Bloomberg, [Aug 7]). The data explain why. Housing prices fell 0.5% in July as sales volume weakened (Investing.com, [Aug 3]), the top-100 developer sales slump deepened (Trivium China, [Aug 5]) and seven-month sales of 1.8 trillion yuan are concentrating in fewer developers (Moomoo, [Aug 3]). The construction channel is the weakest link: a key steel price hit a near-decade low on the construction slump (Bloomberg, [Aug 6]), after property investment slumped 18 percent y/y in January–June (The Standard, [Jul 15]). China Cinda warns of a first-half profit drop as the slump weighs (Caixin Global, [Aug 5]).

Fiscal, Policy & Trade: SCMP reads the Politburo as cautious confidence ([Aug 3]), and Oxford Economics says an unspent fiscal arsenal targets a Q3 recovery (CNBC, [Aug 3]). The surprise tax on offshore trusts (CNBC, [Aug 4]) now reaches offshore insurance income (Reuters, [Aug 6]). Trade risk is rising on several fronts: the US Senate passed a Russia sanctions bill that puts India and China at risk of 100% tariffs (Fibre2Fashion, [Aug 9]), the US imposed a 15% tariff on polysilicon (Nikkei Asia, [Aug 7]), and Washington backs an Australian rare earth miner to cut out China (Financial Times, [Aug 9]) while Australia widened orders on Chinese investors in a rare earths firm (Bloomberg, [Aug 9]). Against that, Chinese state traders made large US soybean purchases (Reuters, [Aug 3]).

Markets & Flows: Hong Kong rode a rollercoaster, with the HSI ending the week 0.84% lower (BusinessToday Malaysia, [Aug 9]) after the insurer sell-off, while the Shanghai Composite gained 1.02% on a volume spike (BigGo Finance, [Aug 7]) and strong trade data lifted sentiment (The Economic Times, [Aug 7]). Bloomberg describes China unleashing its $28 trillion capital markets to challenge the US in AI ([Aug 9]). Flows stay supportive: mainland investors extended their Hong Kong buying spree (South China Morning Post, [Aug 5]), Morgan Stanley lifted its Hang Seng target to 28,400 (BigGo Finance, [Aug 5]) and Chinese investors poured $1.2bn into gold ETFs (TradingView, [Aug 6]).

New / Most Important: Over the weekend the read of July prices hardened from relief into deflation risk, and ING asks whether China's reflation trend is running out of steam ([Aug 9]). With a key steel price at a near-decade low, the PMIs below 50 and weak loan demand expected, the rate and RRR cuts China Daily floated are becoming the base case rather than an option; the July credit print is the next test.

Playbook ✓: Easing signals, top-tier property relaxation and AI-led exports are on script.  |  ⚠️ Divergence: The AUD near a two-month high rests on RBA hike risk, not China: iron ore at a year low (Tech Times, [Aug 4]) and steel at a near-decade low say the bulk channel is shut, so a dovish RBA surprise would leave AUD exposed. European exporters face a German trade deficit with China that keeps growing (Reuters, [Aug 9]) and Chinese EV sales at a new high in Europe (The Guardian, [Aug 9]). The Senate bill hits India and China together, a double shock for EM Asia, and oil at $81.43 is a terms-of-trade tax on factories whose output prices are slowing.
