🇨🇳 China  Mixed  → On hold / easing bias

Macro Stance: The PBOC has redefined its target. Governor Pan says slower loan growth is the new normal (Reuters, [Sep 15]) and that policy will rely less on loan growth targets and more on rate tools (Mitrade, [Sep 15]); Bloomberg calls him unfazed by the credit slump (Bloomberg.com, [Sep 15]). The bank is also preparing rules to limit banks' bond holdings (IDNFinancials, [Sep 15]). On the currency it strengthened the fix ahead of the Trump-Xi summit (The Edge Malaysia, [Sep 15]), setting the midpoint at its strongest in over three and a half years (BigGo Finance, [Sep 14]), though the yuan slipped as the dollar held near a two-week high (Finimize, [Sep 15]). August CPI rose 0.8% and PPI 3.8% (BigGo Finance, [Sep 8]) on energy costs (Reuters, [Sep 9]): imported cost-push, not reflation, so rates stay on hold with an easing bias while the Fed hikes.

PMIs & Activity: August output and demand diverged. Industrial output grew 5.2% (State Council Information Office, [Sep 15]), with robot output up 34.6% (Global Times, [Sep 15]), while retail sales rose only 0.4% against 0.6% expected (investingLive, [Sep 14]) and barely grew as the auto slump deepened (Caixin Global, [Sep 15]). Fixed-asset investment fell 7.2% in the first eight months (Xinhua, [Sep 15]) and Goldman cut its full-year GDP forecast to 4.5% (The Standard, [Sep 15]). The surveys had flagged the same split: the official manufacturing PMI was 49.8 with new orders back in expansion (BigGo Finance, [Aug 30]) and the private gauge 51.5 (BigGo Finance, [Aug 31]), with exports up 25% (AP News, [Sep 7]) and imports missing estimates (CNBC, [Sep 7]).

Credit Impulse: August bank loans rose less than expected after July's slump (Reuters, [Sep 14]) and total credit expansion missed forecasts as loan demand sagged (Bloomberg.com, [Sep 14]); bond and equity financing exceeded loans in social financing for the first time (BigGo Finance, [Sep 14]). Pan's framing is that slower credit growth can stabilise debt levels (Newsquawk, [Sep 15]), which makes the weak impulse a policy choice rather than a problem to fix. Support goes to balance sheets and cash flow instead: $43.9 billion for major state banks (China Daily Global Edition, [Sep 14]) and a crackdown on big firms delaying payments to SMEs (South China Morning Post, [Sep 15]).

Property: Home prices fell further in August (CNBC TV18, [Sep 15]), though major cities reported narrower declines (Xinhua, [Sep 15]) and pre-owned prices extended gains in top-tier cities (Caixin Global, [Sep 15]); reconciled, the national resale market is still weakening while tier-1 stabilises. Property investment fell 19.9% in the first eight months (Business Recorder, [Sep 15]). Breakingviews warns the property fix will hurt before it heals (Reuters, [Sep 15]): the presale overhaul is cooling the land market (Caixin Global, [Sep 10]) and straining local government income (The Straits Times, [Sep 14]).

Fiscal, Policy & Trade: Weak figures stoke calls for fiscal stimulus (Semafor, [Sep 15]), with Bloomberg saying the economy has entered the phase that determines 2026 stimulus (Bloomberg.com, [Sep 14]). The US track is busy: Bessent will meet He Lifeng this weekend (Reuters, [Sep 15]) in New York ahead of the summit (Financial Times, [Sep 15]), and the two sides are discussing tariff cuts on agriculture and energy (Bloomberg.com, [Sep 15]), even as National Herald reports Congress has cleared a 100 pc tariff bill targeting India and China (National Herald, [Sep 15]). Europe is hardening: Merz calls for trade tools against unfair Chinese competition (Bloomberg.com, [Sep 15]) and EU and Chinese trade chiefs hold a call on Thursday (POLITICO.eu, [Sep 14]).

Markets & Flows: China and Hong Kong stocks ended lower on the weak data (The Standard, [Sep 15]), the Hang Seng falling 1.00% to 24,667.24 on the oil surge and rate fears (BBN Times, [Sep 15]), while the FT blames a glut of AI listings for dragging Hong Kong lower (Financial Times, [Sep 15]). Capital is heading out: Chinese investors are rushing into US stocks as Beijing opens a wider path overseas (Reuters, [Sep 15]), with the 10-year yield gap at a record 317 basis points (bloomingbit, [Sep 9]).

New / Most Important: Pan's "new normal" is the day's signal. A day after the August loan miss and with Goldman cutting its growth forecast, the central bank is telling markets it will not chase credit volumes and will steer through rates instead. With the Fed poised to hike and the yield gap at a record, rate tools are constrained too, so the burden shifts to fiscal policy, which is exactly where the calls for stimulus are now pointed.

Playbook ✓: Divergence from the global tightening cycle continues: the PBOC holds rates with an easing bias while DM central banks hike, and it now says so explicitly.  |  ⚠️ Divergence: China is accepting a weaker credit impulse at the moment its growth is most export-dependent. Output at 5.2% with retail sales at 0.4% means more Chinese supply for European exporters, which is why Merz wants trade tools; record-weak property investment and soft imports give EM, iron ore and the AUD little support. Oil at $102.56 (+20.7% in 4 weeks) is an unambiguous negative for a large crude importer, adding cost without demand.
