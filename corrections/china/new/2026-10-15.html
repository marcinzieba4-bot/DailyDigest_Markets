🇨🇳 China  Mixed  → easing bias, constrained

Macro Stance: The PBOC is still adding liquidity while the Fed, ECB and RBA tighten: a 700 billion yuan outright reverse repo leaves October's net injection at 400 billion yuan, which BigGo reads as an easing stance (BigGo Finance, [Oct 14]). With US 10Y yields at 5.09%, the rate gap caps a benchmark cut, but it is not draining the currency: the fix firmed to 6.7249 from 6.7298 (FXStreet, [Oct 14]) and the yuan gained as markets weighed the inflation data (Business Recorder, [Oct 14]). Prices have turned the constraint inside out. September CPI rose 0.9% against 1.0% expected and 0.8% prior (investingLive, [Oct 14]), while PPI hit 5%, its fastest since June 2022 (CNBC, [Oct 14]), as producer prices were jolted by oil (Bloomberg, [Oct 14]). This is cost-push at the factory gate with soft household demand, not deflation, and it argues for targeted rather than broad easing.

PMIs & Activity: September's official manufacturing PMI returned to growth at 50.1, exactly at consensus and up from 49.8 (investingLive, [Sep 29]), with non-manufacturing at 50.2 (investingLive, [Sep 29]) and the RatingDog gauges at 52.1 and 51.6 (investingLive, [Sep 30]). Trade confirmed the factory signal: exports surged 36.3% with imports up 31% (Investing.com, [Oct 14]), beating forecasts (CNBC, [Oct 14]), and chip exports jumped 136% to US$45b on the AI frenzy (South China Morning Post, [Oct 14]). The household side stays weak: JPMorgan flags soft September home-appliance retail and weakening end-user demand (富途牛牛, [Oct 14]), passenger-vehicle exports rose 64% as retail sales dropped (TOKENPOST, [Oct 12]), and August retail sales grew 0.4% (investingLive, [Sep 14]). The IMF sees 4.5% growth in 2026 (Breakingthenews.net, [Oct 13]).

Credit Impulse: September's headline beat and the demand picture point in opposite directions, and both are right. New bank loans jumped to 1.08 trillion yuan, above forecast (TradingView, [Oct 14]), on stimulus and a seasonal boost (Reuters, [Oct 14]), a rebound after August's miss (Reuters, [Sep 14]). Underneath, the surprise masks a much deeper slump in credit demand (investingLive, [Oct 14]): household credit contracted by over 1 trillion yuan in the first three quarters (BigGo Finance, [Oct 14]), and The Economist calls falling consumer credit another warning sign (The Economist, [Oct 14]). The reconciliation is composition: policy-driven corporate and quarter-end lending lifted the total while households kept deleveraging, so the impulse improved in level but not in breadth.

Property: The mortgage subsidy moved holiday transactions, with new-home subscriptions in 50 key cities up over 15% year on year (Moomoo, [Oct 8]) and Shanghai new-home sales doubling (BigGo Finance, [Oct 9]), but one report calls the resale-led rebound a hollow narrative (IANS LIVE, [Oct 12]). Supply is retrenching, as first-tier land auctions cooled sharply in October under the ready-home sales rules (BigGo Finance, [Oct 13]), and creditors face losses from Aoyuan's asset fire sales (Bamboo Works, [Oct 13]). The household credit contraction is the clearest sign that mortgage demand has not turned. The August base was prices down across 70 cities and investment down 19.9% in eight months (BigGo Finance, [Sep 14]).

Fiscal, Policy & Trade: Fiscal space is the binding constraint: local governments may tap 550 billion yuan of unused quotas (Caixin Global, [Oct 9]), but debt interest has surged to 1.4 trillion yuan (BigGo Finance, [Oct 12]) and Fox Business puts debt above 305% of GDP (Fox Business, [Oct 14]). The trade data raise the external pressure: the surplus hit $938 billion for January to September (ABC News, [Oct 14]), the IMF urges China to shift away from exports (Reuters, [Oct 14]), Li Qiang vows more high-value imports (South China Morning Post, [Oct 14]), and EU leaders, pressured at home, weigh trade steps (Reuters, [Oct 14]) after capping hybrids at 350,000 a year (The Guardian, [Oct 12]).

Markets & Flows: The SSE Composite fell to 3,767.23, near a three-month low (BBN Times, [Oct 14]), as the AI selloff deepened and mixed inflation data weighed (The Economic Times, [Oct 14]); blue chips had already hit a 14-month low (Business Recorder, [Oct 13]) and regulators will curb futures quant trading (South China Morning Post, [Oct 13]). Corporate funding is cheap: Tencent raised $5.7b with low-interest yuan bonds dominating (Global Times, [Oct 14]). The EMM ex-China construction shows allocators still excluding China, and the Papergames backlash on X is a reminder of regulatory tail risk.

New / Most Important: One release day split the economy in two. Exports up 36.3%, PPI at a four-year high and loans above forecast describe a strong supply side; CPI missing at 0.9% and households cutting credit describe a weak demand side. The surplus heading for a record turns that imbalance into a trade problem with Europe and the IMF.

Playbook ✓: Property remains a drag, policy eases through liquidity and quotas rather than rates, and allocators stay underweight; all consistent with the muddle-through consensus.  |  ⚠️ Divergence: The rate differential is not forcing a yuan defence; the trade surplus is pulling the other way. Transmission to global assets runs through the export boom: imports up 31% support Asian suppliers and commodity exporters, but property investment down 19.9% limits what iron ore and the AUD can take from it. Chip exports up 136% tighten competition for Korean and Taiwanese tech. European luxury and autos face a household that is deleveraging and an EU now weighing trade steps. EM manufacturers absorb a surplus near $1 trillion. With positioning this light, the asymmetry on a genuine consumption package is extreme.
