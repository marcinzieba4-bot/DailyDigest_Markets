🇨🇳 China  Bearish  → easing bias but limited firepower

Macro Stance: The PBOC is easing through liquidity, not rates: it ran a 500 billion yuan MLF operation (BigGo Finance, [Aug 24]) and keeps conducting overnight reverse repos (Fibre2Fashion, [Aug 26]). The yuan is strong for the wrong reason, a weak dollar: it reached a three-year high (UA.NEWS, [Aug 27]) and stays calm in the face of US sanction threats over Iran (Livemint, [Aug 28]), while the PBOC is cautious on gains (SCMP, [Aug 27]) and ING sees the pace of appreciation slowing (tmgm.com, [Aug 26]); the WSJ argues the world needs to force a revaluation (WSJ, [Aug 28]). July CPI slowed to 0.5% and PPI to 3.5%, both missing expectations (BigGo Finance, [Aug 8]). Firepower is the constraint: the room to ease exists, the fiscal will does not.

PMIs & Activity: July's official manufacturing PMI fell to 49.2 and non-manufacturing to 49.0 (FXStreet, [Jul 31]), and a Reuters poll sees factory activity contracting again in August (Reuters, [Aug 27]). July industrial output grew 4.5% y/y and retail sales only 0.6% (Reuters, [Aug 17]); exports, up almost a quarter (Financial Times, [Aug 6]), are the only engine. Industrial profits rose 17.6% in January-July (Xinhua, [Aug 27]), growth at a seven-month low (Anadolu Ajansı, [Aug 27]). Consumer demand is weakest in cars: auto sales slumped 22% (GlobalChinaEV, [Aug 27]) and Toyota's sales fell for six straight months on weak China demand (The Japan Times, [Aug 28]). Goldman warns Q3 growth could fall below 4.5% (BigGo Finance, [Aug 24]).

Credit Impulse: July bank loans posted a record contraction as credit demand faltered (Reuters, [Aug 14]), yet credit growth exceeded forecasts despite the rare loan slump (Bloomberg, [Aug 14]); reconciled, the broad aggregate holds up while the loan book shrinks. Banks are not the constraint: the largest five reported their strongest first-half profit since the height of the property crash (Reuters, [Aug 28]) and Bank of China posted a 5.1% rise (Reuters, [Aug 28]), though China Everbright's net profit fell 24% as write-offs approached last year's full-year total (Moomoo, [Aug 28]). Regulators now overhaul real-estate credit rules (Caixin Global, [Aug 28]), and foreign firms secure record-low China bond rates (Briefs Finance, [Aug 28]): money is cheap, borrowers are scarce.

Property: Beijing moved. China will curb housing presales to shore up market confidence (Reuters, [Aug 28]), shifting away from the presale model (Caixin Global, [Aug 28]), extended the personal mortgage term cap to 40 years (BigGo Finance, [Aug 28]) and sketched future financing of the sector through the securities regulator (China Daily, [Aug 28]); SCMP calls it a stronger-than-expected package (SCMP, [Aug 28]) and KE Holdings jumped as much as 17% pre-market (TradingKey, [Aug 28]). Housing provident fund withdrawals were widened to renovations and property fees (BigGo Finance, [Aug 24]). The base it lands on is weak: July new home prices were stagnant (Reuters, [Aug 17]), a Reuters poll sees the property investment slump deepening (Reuters, [Aug 27]) and Vanke posted a $2.2 billion loss as debt pressure mounts (Bloomberg, [Aug 27]).

Fiscal, Policy & Trade: The vice-premier calls for proactive fiscal policy (China Daily, [Aug 27]) and S&P affirmed the A+ rating with growth above 4% on fiscal support (Reuters, [Aug 27]), but The Economist argues Beijing is tightening when it should be loosening (The Economist, [Aug 27]): state media signal no "bazooka" (Caixin Global, [Aug 25]), 7.7 bln yuan of overdue tax is being clawed from listed firms (The Standard, [Aug 26]) and a local surtax of 11%–13% has entered legislation (BigGo Finance, [Aug 28]). Trade offers relief: the US-China truce is likely to be extended by one year, with Xi to visit Washington with a business delegation (BigGo Finance, [Aug 28]), even as China opposes the 7.5% tariff plan and warns of countermeasures (Yeni Şafak English, [Aug 27]) and German industry presses Merz for a tougher China policy (Reuters, [Aug 28]).

Markets & Flows: Chinese stocks ended lower as US-China tensions weighed (Business Standard, [Aug 28]), with property shares offsetting biotech and chip losses (The Economic Times, [Aug 28]); the Hang Seng closed flat ahead of Warsh's speech (The Standard, [Aug 28]) and Chinese power stocks slumped after the US grid ban (SCMP, [Aug 28]). Shanghai plans subsidies to restart a dormant offshore debt market (Reuters, [Aug 28]).

New / Most Important: The property package is a stronger-than-expected policy move, and it is structural: curbing presales and stretching mortgages to 40 years lowers buyers' delivery and payment risk, but shifting away from presales also removes a funding channel weaker developers depend on, days after Vanke's loss. It stabilises confidence without adding the fiscal demand that profits, autos and a contracting PMI say is missing.

Playbook ✓: Structural property reform instead of a bazooka, a managed yuan and banks earning more while lending less are on script.  |  ⚠️ Divergence: In a week of major global central bank activity, synchronized DM tightening raises DM yield differentials against Chinese easing, and capital is rotating toward DM rather than into a China whose PMI, loan book and property investment are still contracting. That transmits through property and demand: the investment slump keeps iron ore trading stimulus headlines, rising on fresh signals (TradingView, [Aug 24]) after weakening despite them (TradingView, [Aug 23]), which leaves the AUD exposed; European carmakers and exporters face weak Chinese consumption and a harder German line; and WTI at 83.53 stays a terms-of-trade tax on Chinese margins.
