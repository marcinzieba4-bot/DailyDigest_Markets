🇨🇳 China  Mixed  → stability-first, no major easing

Macro Stance: Stability first: policy rates have not moved, and the PBOC's easing still runs through liquidity, with up to 1 trillion yuan a day on offer over the holidays (Bloomberg, [Sep 23]) and a pledge to step up counter-cyclical adjustments (Newsquawk, [Sep 24]). With US 10Y yields at 5.28%, the rate differential argues against a cut. The yuan is managed, not defended: the fix came at 6.7399 against a 6.7177 estimate (investingLive, [Sep 28]), which VT Markets reads as hinting at tighter onshore bands (VT Markets, [Sep 28]), and the yuan eased after the Trump-Xi meeting though exports underpin further strength (The Business Times, [Sep 28]). August CPI rose 0.8% and PPI 3.8% (BigGo Finance, [Sep 8]).

PMIs & Activity: The August official manufacturing PMI rebounded to 49.8 with new orders back in expansion (BigGo Finance, [Aug 30]), below 50 for a second month, while the private gauge rose to 51.5 (BigGo Finance, [Aug 31]). A Reuters poll sees factories rebounding in September as Beijing signals more aid (Reuters, [Sep 28]), and China's PMIs are front and centre in Asia's big data week (Finimize, [Sep 28]). The latest hard data show how narrow the expansion is: August industrial profits rose 4.2% (Briefs Finance, [Sep 27]), the weakest since the November slump (Bloomberg, [Sep 27]), while January-August profits rose 15.7% with electronics leading (CGTN, [Sep 28]) and high-tech manufacturing profits surged 54.7% (Global Times, [Sep 28]). August output growth quickened to 5.2% (BigGo Finance, [Sep 14]), but retail sales rose only 0.4% (investingLive, [Sep 14]) and fixed-asset investment fell 7.2% in eight months (Xinhua, [Sep 15]).

Credit Impulse: August bank lending disappointed (Reuters, [Sep 14]) and credit expansion missed forecasts as loan demand sagged (Bloomberg, [Sep 14]), so loans and the aggregate do not conflict: both missed, and bond and equity financing exceeded loans in social financing for the first time (BigGo Finance, [Sep 14]). Policy is now steering credit rather than expanding it: the PBOC and seven other agencies issued guidelines to broaden financing for the services sector (BigGo Finance, [Sep 28]), and banks were asked to keep Vanke loans off bad-debt books (Reuters, [Sep 22]). The bond market reads it two ways: Bloomberg warns a stimulus boost and higher bond supply threaten the debt rally (Bloomberg, [Sep 28]), while Citi recommends buying 30-year bonds on a weak growth view (Bloomberg, [Sep 28]). Reconciled: more supply, but not enough growth to lift yields for long.

Property: August home prices extended their decline across 70 cities, with tier-1 cities stabilising (BigGo Finance, [Sep 14]), and property investment fell 19.9% in eight months, the steepest decline on record (BigGo Finance, [Sep 14]). The State Council is now stepping up property and jobs support while tapping local debt quotas (BigGo Finance, [Sep 28]), and Shanghai has rolled out presale rules that require structural topping-out and prioritise completed homes (BigGo Finance, [Sep 28]). Marginal demand is firmer: first-tier new home sales jumped over 10% (BigGo Finance, [Sep 23]) and global investors see signs of recovery in China per JLL (South China Morning Post, [Sep 27]).

Fiscal, Policy & Trade: The cabinet pledged stronger policy support to meet the growth target (Reuters, [Sep 28]), which Bloomberg reads as stimulus to counter a worsening slowdown (Bloomberg, [Sep 28]); fourth-quarter local bond issuance is likely around 500 billion yuan (BigGo Finance, [Sep 28]). Externally the summit has been converted into lists: the US and China pledge tariff cuts on $60 billion of goods (Reuters, [Sep 28]), but China's farm list excludes soybeans (Reuters, [Sep 28]), and the EU is pushing China to accept import quotas (Euronews, [Sep 28]). Breakingviews warns China risks swinging the tax pendulum too far (Reuters, [Sep 28]).

Markets & Flows: Chinese stocks sank to a 13-month low as tech tumbled on oil and rate concerns (South China Morning Post, [Sep 28]), Shanghai sliding 1.67% to 3,823 (BBN Times, [Sep 28]), while the Hang Seng closed up 0.66% (24/7 Wall St., [Sep 28]); stocks then edged up on the policy support pledge (TradingView, [Sep 28]). The Hang Seng Tech index is down over 21% this year, yet ETF inflows exceed 4.2 billion yuan (BigGo Finance, [Sep 28]).

New / Most Important: The State Council has moved from liquidity to a growth pledge: stronger support to meet the target, local debt quotas tapped for property and jobs, and roughly 500 billion yuan of fourth-quarter local bonds. It lands the week the September PMIs are due and after the weakest profit growth since November, so the pledge is a response to the data, not a pre-emptive bazooka. Rates are unchanged and the yuan is managed: stability-first still describes it.

Playbook ✓: The stability-first approach, a sub-50 factory PMI and constrained PBOC easing are consistent with the consensus script that China is in a managed deceleration rather than a V-shaped recovery: quota-tapping and presale reform, not a rate cut.  |  ⚠️ Divergence: Beijing is adding bond supply while Citi buys 30-year bonds on weak growth: the bond market doubts the pledge will lift demand. The transmission runs through property and credit, both still contracting, so the AUD's rally on today's RBA hike gets no China demand leg and iron ore stays tied to a sector whose investment is down 19.9%. European luxury and auto exporters need the Chinese consumer, and with retail sales at 0.4% and consumer stocks shunned, they get Chinese competition instead. Oil's supply-driven run adds a terms-of-trade squeeze on importers.
