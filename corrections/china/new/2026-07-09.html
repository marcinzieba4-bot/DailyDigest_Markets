🇨🇳 China  Bearish  → easing bias constrained

Macro Stance: The PBOC pledged to maintain accommodative policy amid weak demand and external shocks (Reuters, [Jul 8]), and June prices show why: CPI rose 1% (chinadailyhk, [Jul 8]), slowing more than forecast (Bloomberg, [Jul 8]), while producer inflation jumped to a 4-year high (Reuters, [Jul 8]) on Iran-war input costs (Financial Times, [Jul 8]). That is a margin squeeze, not reflation. The bias is constrained by bonds and the currency: the PBOC cut bond purchases on concern over falling yields (Bloomberg, [Jul 2]) even as a 10-year sale drew record demand (Bloomberg, [Jul 8]), and the fix stays firm, with USD/CNY at 6.8047 versus 6.8088 (tmgm.com, [Jul 3]) and a Reuters estimate for the mid-point at 6.8018 (Moomoo, [Jul 8]); ING has tightened its CNY forecast band ([Jul 8]). Policy is building channels instead: the Southbound Bond Connect quota rose to 800 billion yuan (The Standard, [Jul 7]), Macau-issued debt was opened to the mainland (Macau Business, [Jul 8]) and gold buying was the largest since 2023 (Reuters, [Jul 7]).

PMIs & Activity: The June PMI upturn — manufacturing at 50.3, non-manufacturing at 50.2 (Reuters, [Jun 29]) — is export-led, and the hard data confirm the split. Car sales fell again while exports stayed strong (Reuters, [Jul 8]), NEV retail sales fell for a sixth month (CnEVPost, [Jul 8]) even as EV exports surged 150% (Yahoo Finance, [Jul 8]), and Mercedes-Benz sales declined on a worsening China slump (Bloomberg, [Jul 8]). May retail sales fell 0.6% (Il Sole 24 Ore, [Jun 15]) and fixed-asset investment was down 4.1% in the first five months (Macau Business, [Jun 16]), against exports up 19.4% (AP News, [Jun 9]). Forecasters look through it: the IMF upgraded China's 2026 forecast to 4.6% while downgrading global growth (Global Times, [Jul 8]) to 3% (Reuters, [Jul 8]), though China Daily concedes "some headwinds" ([Jul 8]).

Credit Impulse: The May print was two-track: total social financing up 7.7% (CGTN, [Jun 12]) and above forecasts (Bloomberg, [Jun 12]) on government bonds, while new loans missed as the property slump curbed demand (Reuters, [Jun 12]). Regulators are now tightening credit quality rather than adding quantity: a crackdown on ratings inflation (bloomingbit, [Jul 8]) puts up to 494.6 billion yuan of bonds at downgrade risk in the second half (BigGo Finance, [Jul 8]), bankers were told to avoid underwriting short-term LGFV bonds (Bloomberg, [Jul 8]), and Beijing took over Zhongbang Bank on credit risks (The Standard, [Jul 3]). That is deleveraging at the margin, the opposite of an impulse.

Property: Resale prices fell faster in June (Reuters, [Jun 30]) after new home prices fell at a faster pace in May (Reuters, [Jun 15]) and real-estate investment dropped 16.2% y/y in January–May (GMK Center, [Jun 18]). Bloomberg says demand for Soviet-style apartments shows the limits to the revival ([Jul 8]). Land-finance stress is now explicit: a Chinese city "sold" the same plot 18 times to inflate fiscal revenue (SCMP, [Jul 8]), and Caixin puts Nanning's land-fee inflation at $417 million ([Jul 7]). Top-100 developer sales of nearly RMB 1.6 trillion in the first half (Moomoo, [Jul 1]) are concentrated in top-tier cities (Caixin, [Jul 3]).

Fiscal, Policy & Trade: The Economist flagged that China "may struggle to fund Xi Jinping's tech ambitions" — a fiscal capacity constraint that is structurally new — and the Lowy Institute describes a welfare bill no one wants to pay ([Jul 8]). Beijing finalized its 2026 key-projects list (China Daily, [Jul 7]) but is withdrawing consumer support as NEV tax breaks end (chinadailyhk, [Jul 4]), and it lifted fuel export curbs for July (Reuters, [Jul 8]). On trade, China offered to defuse the EU clash after a Nordic trip (Bloomberg, [Jul 8]) ahead of von der Leyen's October deadline (Euronews, [Jul 3]), bought the most US soybeans since November 2025 (Farm Progress, [Jul 8]) and sought chip stability with the Dutch trade mission (SCMP, [Jul 8]).

Markets & Flows: Offshore and onshore have split. The Hang Seng closed at 24,199 with Alibaba up 12% (The Standard, [Jul 8]), Bloomberg says China's star is rising as South Korea's plunges ([Jul 8]), Zhipu AI raised $4 billion in a Hong Kong share sale (Reuters, [Jul 8]) and China may let top AI firms buy limited Nvidia H200 chips (Yahoo Finance, [Jul 8]). Onshore stocks hit a four-week low (TradingView, [Jul 8]), and households still prefer gold: China's biggest ETF is a US$13 billion gold fund (Business Times, [Jul 6]).

New / Most Important: June prices crystallise the bearish case: CPI slowed to 1% while PPI jumped to a 4-year high, a cost-push squeeze on margins and a sign that household demand is not absorbing supply. The PBOC answered with a pledge, not a cut, while regulators cull inflated ratings and short-term LGFV issuance. With Beijing's ability to fund its tech ambitions openly questioned, the easing bias exists but is constrained by fiscal capacity, the yuan and the credit cleanup.

Playbook ✓: Weak domestic demand, subdued construction and a grinding slowdown — consistent with the script.  |  ⚠️ Divergence: China's domestic stress is emerging while the yuan should theoretically benefit from dollar weakness, and capital is flowing only into a narrow offshore AI trade — the onshore "buy China on dollar weakness" trade is broken, suggesting structural outflows dominate cyclical FX considerations. The transmission runs through the growth pulse: cost-push PPI, falling car sales and weak property mean China exports goods disinflation while importing less for construction, so iron ore stays flat near $98 (Kalkine, [Jul 6]), a drag on AUD. European exporters face a weaker Chinese consumer, with Mercedes the example, plus the EU deadline. EM gains only if the AI flows broaden beyond Hong Kong tech.
