🇨🇳 China  Bearish  → easing bias, limited firepower

Macro Stance: The PBOC keeps easing through quantity, not price: a 1.2 trillion yuan outright reverse repo (巴士的報, [Oct 8]) and a net 100 billion yuan of sovereign bond purchases in September (TradingView, [Oct 9]) keep interbank conditions loose, and China's bond yields are falling as they surge everywhere else (Quartz, [Oct 8]). With US 10Y yields at 5.24%, the rate gap caps a benchmark cut, but the yuan is not the weak link: China rejects claims it is undervalued (The Express Tribune, [Oct 11]), it hit a four-year high against its basket (Bloomberg, [Oct 8]), the fix firmed to 6.7330 (www.tmgm.com, [Oct 9]) and Goldman sees 6.40 on a 12-month horizon (Exchange Rates UK, [Oct 11]). The binding limit is fiscal: debt-interest spending is planned at 19.2% of central outlays (TOKENPOST, [Oct 11]). August CPI rose 0.8% and PPI 3.8% (BigGo Finance, [Sep 8]); September inflation is due this week (BigGo Finance, [Oct 11]).

PMIs & Activity: September's official manufacturing PMI returned to growth at 50.1, exactly at consensus and up from 49.8, with non-manufacturing at 50.2 (investingLive, [Sep 29]); the private RatingDog gauges printed 52.1 and 51.6 (investingLive, [Sep 30]), with Reuters tying the factory expansion to the AI boom (Reuters, [Sep 29]). Domestic demand is the weak half: August retail sales rose 0.4% against 0.6% expected (investingLive, [Sep 14]) and industrial profit growth slowed to 4.2% (Bloomberg, [Sep 27]). September confirms the split, with the home car sales downturn continuing while exports retain momentum (Reuters, [Oct 11]) and weak holiday spending casting a shadow as markets reopened (Bloomberg, [Oct 8]). Nikkei estimates third-quarter growth at 4.4%, below target (Nikkei Asia, [Oct 6]).

Credit Impulse: August remains the latest print, and both measures missed: bank lending disappointed as credit demand stayed weak (Reuters, [Sep 14]) and total credit expansion missed forecasts (Bloomberg, [Sep 14]), with bond and equity financing exceeding loans in social financing for the first time (BigGo Finance, [Sep 14]), the government leg holding up while private demand did not. Beijing subsidises credit rather than igniting it, with 100 billion yuan of interest subsidies leveraging 17 trillion yuan of consumer credit (BigGo Finance, [Oct 9]), while the system shrinks at the margin as China shuts hundreds of banks to shore up its financial system (CNBC, [Oct 5]).

Property: The mortgage subsidy reached demand over the holiday. New-home subscriptions in 50 key cities rose over 15% year on year (Moomoo, [Oct 8]), Shanghai new-home sales doubled and second-hand transactions in 11 cities jumped over 40% (BigGo Finance, [Oct 9]), and Beijing new-home sales have picked up since October began (巴士的報, [Oct 11]). Top-100 developers sold 242 billion yuan in September (www.grihik.com, [Oct 8]). Balance sheets do not share the bounce: Vanke reported a 16.02 billion yuan loss (ad-hoc-news.de, [Oct 7]) and PAG is in exclusive talks for a stake in an Evergrande unit (Reuters, [Oct 8]). The base is August's, with prices down across 70 cities and investment down 19.9% in eight months (BigGo Finance, [Sep 14]).

Fiscal, Policy & Trade: The fiscal push is real but rationed. Local governments may tap 550 billion yuan of unused debt quotas (Caixin Global, [Oct 9]) and the NDRC pledges more policy tools (Bloomberg, [Oct 10]), yet debt interest costs are growing faster than any other budget category (Fortune, [Oct 11]), Michael Pettis notes the debt pile constrains new stimulus (Traders Union, [Oct 11]) and the SCMP argues Beijing is right to reserve its fiscal firepower (South China Morning Post, [Oct 11]). On trade, tensions with Europe came to a head (The New York Times, [Oct 7]) and ended in a deal capping Chinese hybrid imports at 350,000 a year (Bloomberg, [Oct 11]), with the EU now agreeing to discuss curbs on Chinese inverters (Bloomberg, [Oct 11]). Britain is set to levy tariffs on Chinese EVs (CNA, [Oct 5]). September export data is due this week (bloomingbit, [Oct 11]) after August's 25% jump (AP News, [Sep 7]).

Markets & Flows: China's securities watchdog is seeking investor ideas as the selloff deepens (Business Standard, [Oct 11]); blue chips hit an over one-year low on an AI supply-chain selloff (Zawya, [Oct 8]) and the Hang Seng a three-month low (The Standard (HK), [Oct 8]), with Alibaba rising as China limits sell orders (Benzinga, [Oct 9]). Breakingviews argues the slow bull is poised to take off (Reuters, [Oct 8]); the consumer side disagrees, with luxury's China hopes facing a reality check (The Business of Fashion, [Oct 11]).

New / Most Important: The weekend made the firepower limit explicit: debt interest at 19.2% of central outlays and rising fastest of any budget line, with Pettis and the SCMP both arguing for restraint. That is why the response to a weak consumer is quota releases and sell-order limits rather than a broad package, and why the bearish read holds despite the housing bounce.

Playbook ✓: Property developer stress, weak household confidence and local fiscal strain follow the balance-sheet-recession script; exports compensate for domestic demand, and announcements outpace delivered stimulus.  |  ⚠️ Divergence: Both export corridors are narrowing at once: US tariffs and now an EU cap on hybrids with inverters next and UK EV tariffs coming. A China that cannot export its way to growth and will not stimulate at scale transmits weakness through PMIs and property rather than through prices. The AUD and iron ore depend on a property recovery that has reached sales but not the 19.9% investment slump; European luxury and industrial exporters lose a consumer whose holiday spending disappointed; EM Asia competes with Chinese goods diverted from Europe. Beijing's toolkit is pinned not by the yuan but by fiscal arithmetic, so the policy floor is real while the ceiling on reflation is low.
