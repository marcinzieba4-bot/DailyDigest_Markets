🇨🇳 China  Mixed  → Cautious easing

Macro Stance: The Fed raised rates to 3.75%-4.00% in its first hike in three years, with more expected (U.S. Bank, [Sep 16]), while the PBOC is explicitly lowering its ambitions for credit. Governor Pan says slower loan growth is becoming the new normal (The Economic Times, [Sep 16]) and reflects economic upgrading (China Daily Global Edition, [Sep 16]), de-emphasising loan volume targets (BigGo Finance, [Sep 15]) and arguing slower credit growth can stabilise debt levels (Newsquawk, [Sep 15]). The fixing moved only to 6.7670 from 6.7698 (FXStreet, [Sep 15]) while the Reuters estimate for the next fix stood at 6.7241 (investingLive, [Sep 16]): the official rate stays on the weak side of models even as the yuan held its ground ahead of the Fed (The Star, [Sep 16]). August CPI of 0.8% and PPI of 3.8% (BigGo Finance, [Sep 8]) reflect energy costs (Reuters, [Sep 9]), not demand. A CKGSB survey found cautious investor sentiment in Q3 2026.

PMIs & Activity: Strong production, weak demand. Industrial output grew 5.2% in August (State Council Information Office, [Sep 15]) on high-tech and robot output (Global Times, [Sep 16]), while retail sales rose only 0.4% against 0.6% expected (investingLive, [Sep 14]) and fixed-asset investment fell 7.2% in the first eight months (Xinhua, [Sep 15]). Goldman cut its full-year GDP forecast to 4.5% (The Standard, [Sep 15]). The surveys match: the official manufacturing PMI was 49.8 with new orders back in expansion (BigGo Finance, [Aug 30]) and the private gauge 51.5 (BigGo Finance, [Aug 31]), with exports up 25% (AP News, [Sep 7]) and imports below estimates (CNBC, [Sep 7]). September has started soft, with NEV retail sales down 10% in the first 13 days (CnEVPost, [Sep 16]).

Credit Impulse: The August credit print was worse than the headline miss suggested: Caixin describes new loans and aggregate financing as plunging (Caixin Global, [Sep 16]), after bank loans rose less than expected (Reuters, [Sep 14]) and credit expansion missed forecasts as loan demand sagged (Bloomberg.com, [Sep 14]). Bond and equity financing exceeded loans in social financing for the first time (BigGo Finance, [Sep 14]). The weak impulse is now a stated policy choice; support goes to bank capital, with $43.9 billion for major state banks (China Daily Global Edition, [Sep 14]), and to SME cash flow, with a crackdown on big firms delaying payments (South China Morning Post, [Sep 15]).

Property: New home prices fell for a third straight month in August (Prop News Time, [Sep 16]), though major cities reported narrower declines (Xinhua, [Sep 15]) and pre-owned prices rose in top-tier cities (Caixin Global, [Sep 15]); reconciled, tier-1 is stabilising while the broad market is not. Property investment fell 19.9% in the first eight months (Business Recorder, [Sep 15]). Developer stress keeps surfacing: Vanke and Yu Liang were hit with consumption restrictions over an unpaid 5 million yuan debt (BigGo Finance, [Sep 16]) and firms hit by the crash are looking overseas for survival (Bloomberg.com, [Sep 16]).

Fiscal, Policy & Trade: Weak data stoke calls for fiscal stimulus (Semafor, [Sep 15]). The external climate hardened sharply: von der Leyen says the EU will use all tools to cut the China trade deficit (Reuters, [Sep 16]), which runs at €1bn a day (EU Today, [Sep 16]), and the EU asked China to voluntarily limit hybrid car exports (Financial Times, [Sep 16]). In Washington a Russia sanctions bill authorising tariffs of up to 100% on China and India passed (WWD, [Sep 16]), even as the two sides discuss tariff concessions to extend the truce (Bloomberg.com, [Sep 16]) and Bessent prepares to meet He Lifeng (Reuters, [Sep 15]); Beijing hosted an Iranian diplomat ahead of the summit (The New York Times, [Sep 16]).

Markets & Flows: China stocks rose on trade hopes and AI gains (Business Standard, [Sep 16]), though China AI stocks' pain grows as US rivals push for model curbs (Bloomberg.com, [Sep 16]). Outflow pressure is visible in a Nasdaq-focused ETF frenzy that sent premiums to 24% (Benzinga, [Sep 16]), with the China-US 10-year yield gap at a record on the policy split (Bloomberg.com, [Sep 10]). Chinese oil prices hit record highs after attacks on a Saudi pipeline (Financial Times, [Sep 16]).

New / Most Important: Brussels has moved from warning to intent. Von der Leyen's "all tools" pledge and the request to curb hybrid exports, together with the US sanctions bill, target the export channel that is carrying China's growth, on the same day the Fed hiked and in the same week the PBOC said it will accept slower credit. Growth now depends on an outlet that both major markets are narrowing.

Playbook ✓: Cautious sentiment, a weak credit impulse and policy incrementalism, as expected.  |  ⚠️ Divergence: Simultaneous tightening by the Fed and ECB, with the BOJ expected to hike, against a PBOC that is easing at the margin, is the widest monetary divergence in decades; it should structurally weaken the CNY and force a choice between currency and growth. The transmission runs through the growth pulse: the PBOC sounded an alarm on "unfavourable" iron ore pricing (AFR, [Sep 16]) and CISA urged steelmakers to cut production (GMK Center, [Sep 16]), a direct negative for iron ore and the AUD; European exporters face the competition the EU now wants to curb; and oil at $102.10 adds a terms-of-trade tax without lifting demand.
