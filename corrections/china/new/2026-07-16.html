🇨🇳 China  Mixed  → easing bias

Macro Stance: The PBOC signalled comfort with yuan levels and expects two-way moves (Bloomberg, [Jul 15]), and the yuan hit a four-week high (TradingView, [Jul 15]) even on a growth miss, which EBC notes barely moved USD/CNH ([Jul 15]). Easing remains in volume: liquidity to smooth tax payments and debt issuance (Bloomberg, [Jul 14]), with a floor that bills not be re-discounted below 0.5% (Reuters, [Jul 14]). Prices: June CPI rose 1% (Global Times, [Jul 9]) and producer inflation hit a four-year high (Reuters, [Jul 8]); Bloomberg labels the end of China's record streak of price declines "bad inflation" ([Jul 15]), cost-push rather than demand.

PMIs & Activity: Second-quarter GDP grew 4.3%, below the official target range (Bloomberg, [Jul 15]) and the slowest in more than three years (Euronews, [Jul 15]); first-half growth of 4.7% (Xinhua, [Jul 15]) remains "well within" the annual target on Beijing's framing (Global Times, [Jul 15]), but the quarter, not the half, is the signal. June itself was firmer: industrial output rose 5.3% y/y and retail sales returned to growth (BusinessLine, [Jul 15]), a surprise rebound (TradingView, [Jul 15]). The two retail measures differ: consumer-goods sales rose 1.3% in the first half (Ecns.cn, [Jul 15]), goods-and-services sales 2.7% (State Council Information Office, [Jul 15]). Investment is the hole, with the slide deepening as infrastructure joins property (Caixin, [Jul 15]), while exports jumped 27% y/y in June (Reuters, [Jul 13]) and the official manufacturing PMI was 50.3 (Reuters, [Jun 29]).

Credit Impulse: June new bank loans rose less than expected (The Star, [Jul 15]) and Bloomberg calls the credit expansion disappointing ([Jul 15]). That reverses May's picture, when total social financing grew 7.7% (CGTN, [Jun 12]) and topped forecasts (Bloomberg, [Jun 12]) on government bonds while loans missed (Reuters, [Jun 12]): now the headline and the loan book are both soft. Record consumer defaults undermine the push to boost spending (Reuters, [Jul 15]), and LGFVs that have added debt face curbs on new bond sales (Bloomberg, [Jul 15]). A central bank adviser is urging more debt (The Standard, [Jul 15]); the private sector is not taking it.

Property: Property investment slumped 18% y/y in January–June (The Standard, [Jul 15]), worse than the 16.2% fall in January–May (GMK Center, [Jun 18]). Prices are stabilising only at the top: first-tier home prices extended a four-month rebound (SCMP, [Jul 15]), while nationally new home prices declined at a slower pace (The Standard, [Jul 15]) and Reuters doubts a recovery ([Jul 14]). Developers face a fresh liquidity crunch (Nikkei Asia, [Jul 13]), and Vanke says its first-half loss may widen to $2.2 billion (Bloomberg, [Jul 10]).

Fiscal, Policy & Trade: Breakingviews says the GDP miss flags the cost of fiscal repairs (Reuters, [Jul 15]) and that the local-debt cleanup leaves a growth mess ([Jul 15]); Westpac sees a need for urgent stimulus ([Jul 15]), but Reuters expects support to stay calibrated ([Jul 14]) around a consumption plan targeting 60 trillion yuan of retail sales by 2030 (Caixin, [Jul 14]). Sinocism flags preparation for the July Politburo meeting ([Jul 13]). Trade pressure is building in Europe, where the surplus with the EU hit a record (Bloomberg, [Jul 14]) and a German engineering body urges broader EU duties (Reuters, [Jul 15]). Washington is softer: Trump's aides see China cheating on trade but shun reprisal (Bloomberg, [Jul 15]), and the Russia sanctions bill cuts the tariff threat from 500% to 100% (The Economic Times, [Jul 15]).

Markets & Flows: The Hang Seng rose for a fourth straight day to 24,681 on policy-driven buying of consumer stocks (BigGo Finance, [Jul 15]), mainland stocks held steady despite the GDP miss as investors shifted to consumer and financial shares (The Economic Times, [Jul 15]), and Alibaba and Baidu jumped on the Apple AI partnership (CNBC, [Jul 15]). SCMP argues the AI stock trade is intact despite a 10% pullback ([Jul 15]). Panda bond demand is surging (SCMP, [Jul 15]).

New / Most Important: June's data split cleanly. Output up 5.3% and a retail rebound show production and consumption holding, but loans missed, property investment fell 18% and infrastructure joined the decline, which is why the quarter printed 4.3% and below target. China is spending its income, not borrowing against the future, and the state is cleaning up local debt rather than levering it. Without a credit turn, the July Politburo meeting has to choose between a larger fiscal push and accepting growth at the bottom of the range.

Playbook ✓: Weak credit, a property-led investment drag, export strength and a stable, managed yuan are on script.  |  ⚠️ Divergence: Markets bought the consumer rebound; the growth pulse says the binding constraint is credit and investment. That matters for the transmission abroad. Mining shares led FTSE falls as China's growth slowed (Yahoo Finance UK, [Jul 15]), the clearest read-through to iron ore and AUD from an investment slump of this depth. European exporters face a record surplus and a machinery lobby now calling for duties, so the policy risk is rising for EU industrials. Oil loses a marginal buyer as Chinese crude imports sit at a near-decade low (Bloomberg, [Jul 13]). EM Asia keeps the AI-export tailwind, and a stable yuan limits contagion to regional currencies. A rebound in loan demand, not another liquidity operation, is what would change the read.
