🇨🇳 China  Mixed  → accommodative

Macro Stance: The PBOC stays accommodative in volume but is guarding the floor on price. It boosted liquidity to smooth tax payments and debt issuance (Bloomberg, [Jul 14]), injecting 669.5 billion yuan (Crypto Briefing, [Jul 14]), yet told some banks not to re-discount bills at rates below 0.5% (Reuters, [Jul 14]). That fits its pledge to keep policy accommodative amid weak demand (Reuters, [Jul 8]) without a headline cut. The yuan fixing went below 6.8 per dollar for the first time since 2023 (Bloomberg, [Jul 10]) before UOB saw consolidation after a sharper drop (Mitrade, [Jul 13]). Prices: June CPI rose 1% (Global Times, [Jul 9]) while producer inflation hit a four-year high (Reuters, [Jul 8]), and the GDP deflator is set to turn positive (Borneo Bulletin, [Jul 14]).

PMIs & Activity: Second-quarter growth was the slowest since 2022 as investment slumped (CNBC, [Jul 14]), missed the market forecast (Reuters, [Jul 14]) and missed the growth target for the first time since Covid (CNN, [Jul 14]); first-half GDP expanded 4.7% (chinadailyhk, [Jul 14]). ING calls the slowdown worse than expected on weak domestic demand ([Jul 14]). Supply is not the problem: industrial output growth was the strongest in three months (TradingView, [Jul 14]), the June official manufacturing PMI was 50.3 (Reuters, [Jun 29]), and exports jumped 27% y/y (Reuters, [Jul 13]) to a record $412 billion (Bloomberg, [Jul 13]), lifting first-half foreign trade 16.9% (Global Times, [Jul 14]). Demand is: crude oil imports plunged to the lowest in nearly a decade (Bloomberg, [Jul 13]).

Credit Impulse: May total social financing grew 7.7% (CGTN, [Jun 12]) and topped forecasts (Bloomberg, [Jun 12]), but new loans missed as the property slump curbed demand (Reuters, [Jun 12]) and households kept deleveraging (SCMP, [Jun 15]). Government bonds carry the impulse while regulators squeeze the riskier end: up to 494.6 billion yuan of bonds face downgrade risk under the ratings crackdown (BigGo Finance, [Jul 8]) and banks were asked to avoid underwriting short-term LGFV bonds (Bloomberg, [Jul 8]). The June loan figures are still pending; an investment slump of this size suggests no private-credit revival.

Property: June new home prices fell at a slower pace, though Reuters doubts a recovery ([Jul 14]), and Bloomberg reads the easing slump as a sign the market is stabilising ([Jul 14]). The flow data are weaker: real-estate investment fell 16.2% y/y in January–May (GMK Center, [Jun 18]), developers face a fresh liquidity crunch (Nikkei Asia, [Jul 13]), Vanke says its first-half loss may widen to $2.2 billion (Bloomberg, [Jul 10]), and the fiscal engine is sputtering as the land model cracks (WION, [Jul 14]).

Fiscal, Policy & Trade: Beijing's answer is a first five-year consumption plan targeting 60 trillion yuan in retail sales by 2030 (Caixin, [Jul 14]), and Reuters expects stimulus to remain calibrated ([Jul 14]); a Caixin-cited economist wants the local-debt plan expanded ([Jul 14]). Sinocism flags preparation for the July Politburo meeting on the economy ([Jul 13]). On trade, the surplus with the EU hit a record (Bloomberg, [Jul 14]), Brussels is signalling emergency import curbs (SCMP, [Jul 14]) and warns dialogue "will not suffice" (Euronews, [Jul 14]). Washington is easier: the Russia sanctions bill cuts the tariff threat on China and India from 500% to 100% (Deccan Herald, [Jul 14]), and a tariff probe is unlikely to derail an autumn Xi-Trump meeting (SCMP, [Jul 14]).

Markets & Flows: Hong Kong fell below 24,000 on Gulf risks (VT Markets, [Jul 14]) and then rebounded on the trade data (TradingView, [Jul 14]), while the Shanghai benchmark sits near a three-month low (BBN Times, [Jul 13]). The Chinese AI stock trade has fallen about 10% on Fed jitters, though SCMP argues the structural story is intact. Bonds are pricing policy: the 10-year yield fell on stimulus bets (TradingView, [Jul 14]). Equity supply is coming, with CXMT setting a listing date for Asia's biggest IPO of 2026 (The Economic Times, [Jul 14]).

New / Most Important: The second-quarter miss is a domestic-demand miss. Output growth at a three-month high and record exports mean the factories delivered; investment and consumption did not, and the shortfall below the target range is the first since Covid. Beijing has so far answered with liquidity for tax week, a floor on bill rates and a consumption plan with a 2030 horizon, not a demand package, which leaves the July Politburo meeting as the next real decision point.

Playbook ✓: A growth miss on weak investment, export strength, a slowing but still-falling property market and calibrated support are on script.  |  ⚠️ Divergence: Markets are trading the miss as a stimulus trigger, with bond yields falling on stimulus bets; the policy signal is that support stays calibrated. If Beijing again leans on supply rather than demand, the excess output keeps flowing abroad. That is the transmission to watch. European exporters face a record bilateral surplus and a Commission now talking about emergency curbs, which raises policy risk for EU autos and machinery. Construction-linked commodities lose: an investment slump with property investment falling by double digits weighs on iron ore and AUD regardless of stronger PMIs. Oil loses a buyer as Chinese crude imports sit at a near-decade low. EM Asia benefits only through the AI-hardware export chain.
