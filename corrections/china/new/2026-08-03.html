🇨🇳 China  Mixed  → easing bias

Macro Stance: The PBOC has answered the July data with words, not yet with cuts. It pledged timely adjustment of its policy tools (Reuters, [Aug 2]) and a moderately loose stance backed by more effective incremental measures in H2 (Global Times, [Aug 2]), with ample liquidity to be maintained (finance.biggo.com, [Aug 2]); one read of the Politburo is that RRR and rate cuts are likely in Q3 (BigGo Finance, [Jul 30]). The overnight reverse repo rate stayed at 1.25% (Reuters, [Jul 28]). The currency is the constraint: the yuan hit a three-year high despite the PBOC signalling discomfort (The Edge Malaysia, [Jul 31]), and Rabobank argues the rally may lose momentum (Exchange Rates UK, [Aug 2]), while the forex regulator pledged to open up the market and tighten capital oversight (South China Morning Post, [Aug 2]). June CPI rose only 1% (Global Times, [Jul 9]) while PPI climbed 4.1% (finance.biggo.com, [Jul 8]), a margin squeeze rather than reflation.

PMIs & Activity: The two July surveys diverge in level but agree in direction. The official manufacturing PMI dropped to 49.2 (The State Council Information Office, [Jul 31]) and non-manufacturing to 49 (Macau Business, [Jul 31]), as factories and construction slumped together (The Edge Malaysia, [Jul 31]); the private survey still shows factory growth, but at a four-month low (Reuters, [Aug 2]). Reconciled: the export-oriented private sample is slowing, the broader official sample with its construction and domestic weight is contracting, and Bloomberg reads it as a slump that reinforces signs of slowdown ([Aug 2]). Against June's 50.3 (Reuters, [Jun 29]) the break is clear. Q2 GDP grew 4.3%, below the target range (Bloomberg, [Jul 15]), first-half industrial profits rose 18.7% as a tech boom masks the property slump (Caixin Global, [Jul 27]), and US-bound shipments fell in July (CNBC, [Jul 30]).

Credit Impulse: June new bank loans rose less than expected (The Star, [Jul 15]) and credit growth missed forecasts as the PBOC flagged a "new normal" (Caixin Global, [Jul 16]); household loans fell by 366.8 billion yuan in the first half (36Kr, [Jul 17]). Cash at Chinese banks has swelled to a record as borrowing slows (Nikkei Asia, [Jul 28]): liquidity is ample, credit demand is not, which is why "ample liquidity" pledges change little. A dip in the debt ratio may not bode well for the economy (South China Morning Post, [Jul 31]), even as the local government debt clean-up makes progress (ISI Markets, [Aug 1]). The impulse reads flat, with households negative.

Property: June new home prices fell at a slower pace (Reuters, [Jul 14]) and first-tier prices extended a four-month rebound (South China Morning Post, [Jul 15]), but resale prices fell faster (Reuters, [Jun 30]) and property investment slumped 18 percent y/y in January–June (The Standard (HK), [Jul 15]). Households are walking away from homes and into portfolios (South China Morning Post, [Aug 1]) and July was the worst building month since Covid (Wood Central, [Aug 1]). The contrarian bid is commercial: investors are snapping up prime office space on a rebound bet (South China Morning Post, [Aug 2]).

Fiscal, Policy & Trade: The Politburo settled on incremental policy support (Caixin Global, [Jul 31]), a fiscal push without big stimulus (VT Markets, [Jul 31]); leaders pledged support without big new stimulus plans (Reuters, [Jul 30]), while Bloomberg reports officials will consider new stimulus after the slowdown ([Jul 30]). Reconciled: incremental measures now, more only if data worsen. On trade, China drew "red lines" around its economic model ahead of EU and US talks (Reuters, [Aug 2]), a stance Bloomberg warns will come at a cost ([Aug 2]); the US urged China to "fully meet" its rare-earths pledge (Nikkei Asia, [Jul 31]) as a Trump-Xi September summit takes shape (Firstpost, [Jul 31]).

Markets & Flows: Semiconductors are the dominant lens. CXMT surged 466% on its Shanghai debut (Reuters, [Jul 27]), though The Business Times calls the US$10 billion IPO a flop ([Jul 28]): the first-day pop measured domestic capital's appetite for chip self-sufficiency, not post-listing returns. Yahoo Finance frames it as leverage for Apple against Micron, and global semiconductor stocks pared losses amid fears of Chinese competition. Onshore, Chinese stocks were on track for their worst month in a decade (Financial Times, [Jul 30]), while Hong Kong rallied 3.7% on a five-day winning streak (BusinessToday Malaysia, [Aug 2]) and the CSRC unveiled measures to deepen mainland-HK market ties (chinadailyhk, [Aug 2]).

New / Most Important: The PBOC's H2 guidance is the policy answer to July: timely tool adjustment, moderately loose, ample liquidity. With the official PMI in contraction and the private gauge at a four-month low, that reads as a door opened to RRR and rate cuts. The constraint is that liquidity is not the bottleneck, as record bank cash shows, and a strong yuan already tightens conditions for exporters.

Playbook ✓: Domestic capital channelled into strategic tech, incremental easing and weak property are on script.  |  ⚠️ Divergence: Asia's other factories are firming, with South Korea's activity quickening on export demand (Reuters, [Aug 2]), while China's PMIs slide, so the drag is Chinese domestic demand rather than global trade. That concentrates the hit on China-specific channels: iron ore eased as faltering China demand offset stimulus hopes (Business Recorder, [Jul 28]), AUD and NZD stay exposed after NZD/USD slipped on weak China PMIs (VT Markets, [Jul 31]), and European exporters face a deepening China slump. The #PapergamesRuinsCNgaming hashtag trending on US Twitter is a reminder that Chinese tech sentiment abroad is deteriorating even as domestic capital piles in. Oil at $79.47 remains a terms-of-trade tax on China's margins.
