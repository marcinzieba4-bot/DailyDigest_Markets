🇨🇳 China  Mixed  → Easing bias, diverging from DM tightening

Macro Stance: China remains the outlier: the Fed, ECB and BOJ hiked this week while Beijing kept benchmark lending rates unchanged for a 16th month in September (Reuters, [Sep 19]). The bias is accommodative, but the room is shrinking: Yahoo Finance frames the hold as room for monetary easing narrowing (Yahoo Finance, [Sep 19]), Governor Pan calls slower loan growth the new normal (Reuters, [Sep 15]) and a central bank adviser warns AI could deepen the supply-demand imbalance (Reuters, [Sep 19]). The fixing carries the signal: the midpoint was raised for an eighth straight day (BigGo Finance, [Sep 17]) and the yuan is at its strongest since 2022 (Bloomberg.com, [Sep 17]), yet the fix ran far weaker than models, 6.7698 against an estimate of 6.7083 (investingLive, [Sep 13]), and has risen only to 6.7580 since (www.tmgm.com, [Sep 17]): appreciation guided, its pace capped. August CPI rose 0.8% and PPI 3.8% (BigGo Finance, [Sep 8]) on energy costs (Reuters, [Sep 9]), imported cost rather than demand.

PMIs & Activity: The official August manufacturing PMI rose to 49.8 with new orders back in expansion (BigGo Finance, [Aug 30]), the private gauge reached 51.5 (BigGo Finance, [Aug 31]) and private services rebounded to 51.4 (BigGo Finance, [Sep 2]). The hard data split the economy in two: industrial output rebounded to 5.2% (BigGo Finance, [Sep 14]), but retail sales grew 0.4% y/y against 0.6% expected (investingLive, [Sep 14]), urban unemployment hit 5.3% (TradingView, [Sep 14]) and fixed-asset investment fell 7.2% in eight months (State Council Information Office, [Sep 15]). Exports jumped 25% (AP News, [Sep 7]) while imports missed (CNBC, [Sep 7]). Reconciled: the PMI rebound is real but export- and AI-led on top of contracting domestic demand, hence Goldman's cut of its GDP forecast to 4.5% (The Standard, [Sep 15]).

Credit Impulse: Bank loans rose less than expected after July's slump (Reuters, [Sep 14]) and credit expansion missed forecasts as loan demand sagged (Bloomberg.com, [Sep 14]). The structure explains it: bond and equity financing exceeded loans in social financing for the first time (BigGo Finance, [Sep 14]), so securities issuance carries the aggregate while the loan book stalls. Pan's de-emphasis of loan volume targets (BigGo Finance, [Sep 15]) and new metrics to rein in banks' long-dated bond holdings (Reuters, [Sep 14]) show Beijing accepts a weaker impulse; the $43.9b injection into major state banks (China Daily Global Edition, [Sep 14]) is balance-sheet repair, not stimulus.

Property: August home prices extended their decline across 70 cities while tier-1 cities stabilised (BigGo Finance, [Sep 14]); Shanghai recorded a 3% annual new-home price gain (Trend News Agency, [Sep 19]): a tier-1 floor inside a national decline. Development investment plunged 19.9% in eight months, the steepest on record (BigGo Finance, [Sep 14]), and land sale revenue slumped nearly 30% (BigGo Finance, [Sep 18]), straining local government income (Bloomberg.com, [Sep 13]). Breakingviews says the fix will hurt before it heals (Reuters, [Sep 15]); new housing provident fund rules now let renovation and property fees be withdrawn (BigGo Finance, [Sep 19]), and Vanke and Yu Liang were hit with consumption restrictions over unpaid debt (BigGo Finance, [Sep 16]).

Fiscal, Policy & Trade: Fiscal revenue growth slowed as government spending contracted (Caixin Global, [Sep 19]), the pullback Bloomberg says stretched into August (Bloomberg.com, [Sep 18]); weak data have stoked calls for stimulus (Semafor, [Sep 15]), and Beijing will step up curbs on malicious price competition (Bloomberg.com, [Sep 19]). Trade is the active front: the US and China are discussing tariff cuts (Bloomberg, [Sep 15]), including on US LNG (Reuters, [Sep 18]), Washington is holding off on new China tariffs until after the Xi meeting (Bloomberg.com, [Sep 18]) and Bessent and He Lifeng meet on Sunday at JPMorgan headquarters (Reuters, [Sep 19]). The counter-current: Trump signed the bill targeting China's Russian energy imports days before Xi's visit (South China Morning Post, [Sep 19]), the EU will use all tools to cut its China deficit (Reuters, [Sep 16]) and Saudi Arabia quit a China-led cross-border currency platform (Financial Times, [Sep 19]).

Markets & Flows: Equities trade the summit, not the data. China stocks logged their best day in a month ahead of the Trump-Xi meeting (Business Recorder, [Sep 19]) and Hong Kong rebounded 146.49 points on an AI rally and easing oil (BusinessToday Malaysia, [Sep 19]), after dropping on the Fed hike (South China Morning Post, [Sep 17]). Bonds are bucking the global yield surge (Yahoo Finance, [Sep 17]), US Treasury holdings fell to the lowest since 2008 (Financial Times, [Sep 17]) and mainland investors are rushing into US stocks (Reuters, [Sep 15]).

New / Most Important: The September LPR hold is the cleanest read of Beijing's reaction function: with the Fed hiking and the yuan at a four-year high (Moneycontrol.com, [Sep 18]), the PBOC chose neither to cut into a weak domestic economy nor to let the rate gap drive the currency. Policy is waiting for the summit, which the White House has scheduled for next week (NTD News, [Sep 18]); the Bessent-He meeting on Sunday is the last staging post.

Playbook ✓: China holding while DM tightens is consensus, and rates on hold, credit allowed to slow and a firmer fixing into a negotiation is Beijing's standard pre-summit posture.  |  ⚠️ Divergence: A four-year-high yuan while the Fed hikes and the domestic pulse weakens is diplomacy, not fundamentals. The transmission runs through the data: an export-led PMI and 25% export growth keep Chinese competition intense for European exporters as Brussels hardens; a soft credit impulse and a record 19.9% fall in property investment cap steel demand, so iron ore rose only slightly as stimulus hopes outweighed demand concern (TradingView, [Sep 16]); and with imports missing, the AUD and EM exporters get little from the rebound. WTI at 96.08 adds a terms-of-trade tax on China's import bill.
