🇨🇳 China  Bearish  → easing bias

Macro Stance: The PBOC is talking global and acting cautiously at home. Its first solo five-year plan in over a decade eyes a global yuan (South China Morning Post, [Aug 11]), Deutsche became the first European clearing bank for the renminbi (Financial Times, [Aug 11]) and the central bank ruled out devaluation (FXStreet, [Aug 10]). The yuan hit a 3.5-year high against the dollar (BigGo Finance, [Aug 11]). Yet reverse repo volume fell to zero for the first time since June (The Standard, [Aug 11]): no fresh liquidity push. Prices argue the other way, with July CPI up 0.5% (Fibre2Fashion, [Aug 11]), PPI up 3.5% (China Daily, [Aug 9]) and inflation cooling faster than expected on weak demand (Caixin Global, [Aug 10]). The case for rate and RRR cuts is building; nothing has been delivered.

PMIs & Activity: ICIS reads the July PMI as pointing to a weaker second half absent more stimulus ([Aug 11]): the official manufacturing gauge fell to 49.2 and non-manufacturing to 49.0 (FXStreet, [Jul 31]), and the private factory index eased to 50.9 (Fibre2Fashion, [Aug 3]). Autos show the split economy. July new car sales slumped 21%, a 10th straight decline, while exports surged 88% to a record (finance.biggo.com, [Aug 11]); NEV sales rose 23.7% with exports up 1.5 times (CnEVPost, [Aug 11]). Reconciled: the growth is overseas, and the domestic market weakened further on subdued demand (WSJ, [Aug 11]). The FT describes a great jobs squeeze ([Aug 11]). Exports overall surged 23.9% (Seeking Alpha, [Aug 7]), but Q2 GDP growth of 4.3% was already below the target range (Bloomberg, [Jul 15]).

Credit Impulse: July credit is still to come, and the previews conflict: a Reuters poll sees bank lending set to plummet on subdued demand ([Aug 7]) while Caixin expects a rebound on a low base ([Aug 7]), which is a sharp drop from June's seasonal peak that still compares well with a weak July last year. VT Markets reads credit demand as weak ([Aug 8]). Household stress is surfacing: banks look to unload US$8.2b in bad credit-card loans (South China Morning Post, [Aug 11]), after household loans fell 366.8 billion yuan in the first half (36Kr, [Jul 17]) and June new loans rose less than expected (The Star, [Jul 15]). International Banker calls it a problem of unwanted credit ([Aug 10]).

Property: A new overhang has appeared: Bloomberg reports China racing to solve a $148 billion property threat as land leases end ([Aug 11]). Beijing eased home-buying curbs (Bloomberg, [Aug 10]), but housing prices fell 0.5% in July as sales volume weakened (Investing.com, [Aug 3]) and the top-100 developer sales slump deepened (Trivium China, [Aug 5]); state-backed China Jinmao, with Jan-July sales up nearly 8% (BigGo Finance, [Aug 11]), is the exception. Construction demand is the weakest link: a key steel price hit a near-decade low (Bloomberg, [Aug 6]) and port stockpiles rose further as steel demand softened (Hellenic Shipping News, [Aug 11]), after property investment slumped 18 percent y/y in January–June (The Standard, [Jul 15]).

Fiscal, Policy & Trade: SCMP argues the economy's problems have been diagnosed and asks whether policymakers will act ([Aug 11]); Foreign Policy says China has chosen to disappoint its consumers ([Aug 11]), as leaders gather for their seaside sojourn (The Economist, [Aug 11]). The 20% offshore tax on the super-rich (South China Morning Post, [Aug 10]) raises revenue but not demand. On trade, the US is moving past tariffs to focus on supply-chain revamps (Bloomberg, [Aug 11]) even as it imposes new tariffs on China's solar sector (Semafor, [Aug 11]); China moved to join Brazil's WTO fight over forced-labour tariffs (South China Morning Post, [Aug 11]) and imposed anti-dumping duties of up to 54.3% on US and Mexican pecans (Global Times, [Aug 10]), while its soybean pledge is tested as private crushers hold back (Bloomberg, [Aug 11]).

Markets & Flows: The Hang Seng fell 284 points as an oil price surge stoked inflation fears (BigGo Finance, [Aug 11]), and Newsweek argues investors are fleeing China's tech stocks ([Aug 11]) after Beijing clamped down on margin debt following the AI frenzy (Bloomberg, [Aug 10]). Earnings will test the rotation from AI to internet stocks (Bloomberg, [Aug 11]), and the index compiler plans to add 20 more stocks to Hong Kong's main tech tracker (South China Morning Post, [Aug 11]). The one hot corner is gold: the breakout rally sent Chinese bullion stocks near record highs (South China Morning Post, [Aug 11]). The Seoul Economic Daily's Korea-to-Taiwan rotation confirms that EM flows are bypassing the mainland.

New / Most Important: The domestic evidence stacked up in one day: a 21% slump in July car sales for a 10th month, banks dumping bad credit-card loans, a $148 billion land-lease overhang and a jobs squeeze. Against that, the PBOC let reverse repo volume fall to zero and spent its message on a global yuan. Until the leaders' summer meeting or the July credit print forces a response, the bearish case rests on policy inertia, not on the export data.

Playbook ✓: Weak domestic demand, a strong yuan, export-led growth and China off the institutional radar are on script.  |  ⚠️ Divergence: The AUD outperformed despite the RBA's hawkish tone, yet China's steel price sits at a near-decade low and port stockpiles build: the bulk channel is not what supports it, so AUD strength rests on a weak dollar and is exposed if Chinese construction slips further. China tightening its grip on Europe's car supply chain (Financial Times, [Aug 11]) while car exports surge 88% puts European carmakers on the wrong side, and EM Asian exporters compete with the same machine. Oil at $82.93 is a terms-of-trade tax on an economy already cutting prices.
