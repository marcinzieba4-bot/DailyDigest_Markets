🇨🇳 China  Mixed  → Easing bias maintained

Macro Stance: China is easing while the rest of the world tightens: as other economies tighten, it rolls out cash for Golden Week (bloomingbit, [Oct 1]), after the PBOC cut the PSL rate by 0.25 percentage point to 1.5% (Forex Factory, [Sep 29]) and boosted relending quotas by 700 billion yuan (BigGo Finance, [Sep 29]). The tools are structural, not a benchmark cut, and the currency is not the pressure point the 5.28% US 10Y would suggest: the fix was set at 6.7351 vs 6.7411 (www.tmgm.com, [Sep 30]), a 3.5-year high before the National Day holiday (The Standard (HK), [Sep 30]), with China's 10Y yield at an over one-year low (TradingView, [Sep 29]). Inflation is cost-driven: August CPI rose 0.8% and PPI 3.8% (BigGo Finance, [Sep 8]) on rising energy costs (Reuters, [Sep 9]).

PMIs & Activity: September's official manufacturing PMI returned to growth at 50.1 with non-manufacturing at 50.2 (investingLive, [Sep 29]), and the private RatingDog gauges printed 52.1 for manufacturing and 51.6 for services (investingLive, [Sep 30]). The 'beat' headlines (TradingView, [Sep 29]) and an official print exactly at the 50.1 consensus (investingLive, [Sep 29]) reconcile: the official survey met forecasts, the private surveys did the beating. State media credit stimulus (China Daily, [Oct 1]), but the driver is the AI boom (Nikkei Asia, [Sep 30]) lifting global factory activity (Reuters, [Oct 1]) after August exports jumped 25% (AP News, [Sep 7]). Domestic demand lags: August industrial profits grew at the weakest since November (Bloomberg, [Sep 27]), retail sales rose 0.4% (investingLive, [Sep 14]) and NEV retail sales fell 20% in the first 27 days of September (CnEVPost, [Sep 30]).

Credit Impulse: August bank lending disappointed (Reuters, [Sep 14]) and credit expansion missed forecasts (Bloomberg, [Sep 14]), with bond and equity financing exceeding loans for the first time (BigGo Finance, [Sep 14]): headline financing and bank loans both point to weak private loan demand. The package steers credit rather than igniting it: cheap loans help state firms pay overdue bills (Bloomberg, [Sep 29]) and banks are urged to finance AI and asset-light service firms (Caixin Global, [Sep 29]), while the bad consumer debt cleanup falters (The Business Times, [Sep 29]).

Property: The first central mortgage subsidy, 1 percentage point a year and up to nearly 50,000 yuan on a 1 million yuan loan (BigGo Finance, [Sep 29]) for first-time buyers (bloomingbit, [Sep 30]), lands on a market showing first signs of life: Beijing's September existing-home transactions topped 15,000 and new-home sales surged nearly 50% month-on-month (BigGo Finance, [Oct 1]), and the top 100 developers posted 2.26 trillion yuan in January-September sales (BigGo Finance, [Oct 1]). The stress has not gone: a Vanke loss underscores it (Traders Union, [Oct 1]), August home prices fell across 70 cities and property investment dropped 19.9% in eight months (BigGo Finance, [Sep 14]).

Fiscal, Policy & Trade: The cabinet pledged stronger support to meet the growth target (Reuters, [Sep 28]), with fourth-quarter local bond issuance likely around 500 billion yuan (BigGo Finance, [Sep 28]). The Economist sees a big shot in the arm (The Economist, [Oct 1]), but the mini-stimulus leaves investors wanting (Bloomberg, [Oct 1]) and BofA says more aggressive stimulus is unlikely for now (Yahoo Finance, [Oct 1]). Trade relief is slow: Greer says no date is set for the tariff cuts (Bloomberg, [Oct 1]) on $60 billion of goods (Reuters, [Sep 28]), and the US slows aircraft-part exports for leverage (Reuters, [Oct 1]). Europe is the harder front: a Commission official says China drives a worrying rise in EU imports (Reuters, [Oct 1]), the EU trade chief says 'tough' talks have yet to deliver (POLITICO.eu, [Oct 1]) and Beijing vows a response to any EU move (Reuters, [Sep 29]).

Markets & Flows: Mainland stocks sank to a 13-month low (South China Morning Post, [Sep 28]) and limped to a quarterly drop as the stimulus fell short (TradingView, [Sep 30]), the SSE Composite at 3,842.19 ahead of Golden Week (BBN Times, [Sep 30]). Hong Kong, closed for National Day on Oct 1 and reopening without Stock Connect (BBN Times, [Oct 1]), slumped most since March, led by financials (Yahoo Finance Singapore, [Oct 1]), the Hang Seng closing 2.6% lower (chinadailyhk, [Oct 1]). The yuan is the counterpoint, set for a seventh straight quarterly gain as exporters sell dollars (Business Recorder, [Sep 30]).

New / Most Important: Chinese refiners suspended October fuel exports to bolster stocks (Reuters, [Oct 1]), and oil jumped as Brent went above $100 (Yahoo Finance, [Oct 1]). This is China's newest transmission to global assets and it runs through supply, not demand: Beijing is hoarding refined product for a domestic economy whose PMI is only just above 50, tightening a strained market. Together with Hong Kong's slump and Bloomberg's verdict that the mini-stimulus leaves investors wanting, the message is a China that protects its own balance first.

Playbook ✓: Stimulus announced, delivery targeted and underwhelming, property still dragging: the playbook holds, with CGB yields low as the PBOC prioritizes liquidity and foreign equity money rotating toward US mega-cap AI winners.  |  ⚠️ Divergence: China's easing now runs directly against a global tightening impulse (the RBA at 15-year highs, ECB pressure, a restrictive Fed), and the usual transmission is inverted. In past cycles Chinese easing pulled EM Asia along and set a demand floor under commodities; this time the impulse is export- and AI-led, so it lifts Asian tech supply chains but not the property demand behind iron ore and the AUD, with property investment down 19.9% and credit missing. The oil channel now works against importers: a fuel-export halt adds to energy costs in India, Indonesia and Turkey just as dollar strength challenges FPI flows across EM Asia. European luxury and industrial exporters still face a Chinese consumer at 0.4% retail growth and an EU-China trade front that is escalating, not easing.
