🇨🇳 China  Mixed  → easing bias

Macro Stance: The PBOC added the most liquidity to the economy in five months to aid growth (Bloomberg, [Jul 23]) and is set to roll over 500 billion yuan of MLF, extending support for a third straight month (BigGo Finance, [Jul 23]), while loan prime rates stay on hold for a 14th consecutive month (The Business Times, [Jul 20]) and the market eyes a rate-cut window near the end of the third quarter (BigGo Finance, [Jul 21]). This is quantity easing, not cheaper credit. The yuan weakened slightly on the fixing (BusinessToday Malaysia, [Jul 17]) and UOB sees an upside bias below 6.7820 (TMGM, [Jul 20]), while a bond rally shows weak economic sentiment before the Politburo (Bloomberg, [Jul 22]). Prices are the wrong mix: June CPI rose only 1% (chinadailyhk, [Jul 8]) while PPI climbed 4.1% (BigGo Finance, [Jul 8]) to a near four-year high that squeezes manufacturers (Reuters, [Jul 8]).

PMIs & Activity: The surveys and the hard data point in different directions. The June official manufacturing PMI rose to 50.3 and the non-manufacturing PMI to 50.2 (Reuters, [Jun 29]); the private gauge capped the strongest factory quarter since late 2020 (Reuters, [Jun 30]). July PMIs are not yet out. Second-quarter GDP growth weakened to 4.3%, below the official target range (Bloomberg, [Jul 15]); June industrial output rose 5.3% y/y and retail sales returned to growth (BusinessLine, [Jul 15]), but retail sales of consumer goods were up just 1.3% in the first half (Ecns.cn, [Jul 15]), the car market heads for its worst year since 2021 (CNBC, [Jul 20]) and the CPCA expects July NEV retail sales to dip to about 980,000 (CnEVPost, [Jul 23]). Exports carry the cycle, up 27% y/y in June (Reuters, [Jul 13]).

Credit Impulse: The aggregate and the household picture diverge. The finance minister cites over 17 trillion yuan in new loans in the first half (BigGo Finance, [Jul 22]), yet June new loans missed forecasts (Bloomberg, [Jul 15]), credit growth undershot as the PBOC flagged a "new normal" (Caixin, [Jul 16]) and household loans decreased by 366.8 billion yuan in the first half (36Kr, [Jul 17]). Reconciled, corporate and state-linked borrowing carries the total while households deleverage, which is why extra liquidity lifts bonds more than spending.

Property: Prices are stabilising at the top while the financing base erodes. June new home prices fell at a slower pace, though Reuters doubts a recovery ([Jul 14]), and first-tier prices extended a 4-month rebound (SCMP, [Jul 15]). But property investment slumped 18% y/y in January–June (The Standard, [Jul 15]) and land-sales revenue plunged over 30% in the first half (BigGo Finance, [Jul 22]). Regulators are tightening developer funding, curbing a loan structure used by distressed developers (Bloomberg, [Jul 22]) and offshore loans for local real estate companies (The Standard, [Jul 23]); Vanke completed extensions on its 2026 public bonds with new funding (Caixin, [Jul 23]), and Central China Real Estate is selling two Henan landmarks for $443 million (BigGo Finance, [Jul 23]).

Fiscal, Policy & Trade: China clings to austerity despite a sharper slowdown (Bloomberg, [Jul 22]): first-half fiscal revenue rose 4.7% (Reuters, [Jul 22]) on stamp duty that surged 90% (BigGo Finance, [Jul 22]), and the finance minister's roadmap is a 100 billion yuan programme (BigGo Finance, [Jul 22]). Traders look to the Politburo for cues on the growth commitment (Bloomberg, [Jul 23]), and a new green-energy five-year plan targets a 30% wind and solar share by 2030 (BigGo Finance, [Jul 23]). On trade, the US imposed new tariffs on 60 economies as China-US talks on reciprocal cuts advance (BigGo Finance, [Jul 23]), with Beijing seeking opinions on tariff cuts covering $30 billion of trade (Reuters, [Jul 23]); the White House accuses Moonshot AI of stealing US technology (Anadolu Ajansı, [Jul 23]) even as both sides plan AI talks in September (Reuters, [Jul 22]).

Markets & Flows: China stocks fell on tariff concerns (TradingView, [Jul 23]) as AI and chip shares extended their correction (The Economic Times, [Jul 23]). The AI trade has already paid: mutual funds swung to a $287 billion profit on the AI binge (Caixin, [Jul 23]) and are pivoting to tech while cutting Hong Kong holdings (SCMP, [Jul 23]), but quant hedge fund losses topping 20% have rattled wealthy investors (Disruption Banking, [Jul 23]). The bond market defies the global slump (Seeking Alpha, [Jul 23]), and emerging-market assets rallied on China's tech support (Bloomberg, [Jul 21]).

New / Most Important: Beijing met the new US tariffs and the slowdown in the same session with two incremental tools. The PBOC added the most liquidity in five months, a quantity signal that stops short of a rate cut, while Beijing opened consultation on reciprocal tariff cuts covering $30 billion of trade just as Washington's new tariffs on 60 economies took hold. Liquidity without cheaper credit and tariff relief on a sliver of trade will not turn household demand; with austerity intact, the Politburo is where any genuine demand support would have to come from.

Playbook ✓: Liquidity-led easing, yuan management and discounted crude from sanctioned producers remain the expected toolkit.  |  ⚠️ Divergence: The tariff wave on 60 economies hits an economy whose 50.3 PMI already rests on exports, so displaced Chinese supply flows toward Europe and EM; the FT says China's assault on Europe's car market has barely begun ([Jul 22]), a direct risk to European autos. Asia's growth pulse is splitting along AI lines: Japan's factory output surged in July (Reuters, [Jul 23]) while India's private sector growth slumped to an over four-year low (Reuters, [Jul 23]). For commodities, an 18% property-investment slump and crude imports at the lowest in nearly a decade (Bloomberg, [Jul 13]) cap Chinese demand, a drag on iron ore and AUD that extra PBOC liquidity does not reverse.
