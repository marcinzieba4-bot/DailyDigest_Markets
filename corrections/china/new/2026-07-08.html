🇨🇳 China  Bullish  → easing bias

Macro Stance: The PBOC has flagged a supply-demand imbalance in the economy, and its policy mix is about channels rather than rates. It unveiled measures to cement Hong Kong's yuan hub status (Bloomberg, [Jul 7]), lifting the Southbound Bond Connect quota to 800 billion yuan (The Standard, [Jul 7]), and added gold for a 20th month, the most since 2023, even as bullion tumbled (Reuters, [Jul 7]); forex reserves fell more than expected in June (The Standard, [Jul 7]). Liquidity is ample: a trillion-yuan outright reverse repo ended a three-month tapering streak (BigGo Finance, [Jul 3]), though the PBOC cut bond purchases on concern over falling yields (Bloomberg, [Jul 2]). The fix leans firm: USD/CNY was set at 6.8047 versus 6.8088 (tmgm.com, [Jul 3]), which VT Markets reads as managed strength amid softer growth ([Jul 3]). Prices stay split: May CPI rose only 1.2% (China Daily, [Jun 10]) while factory-gate inflation hit a near four-year high (Reuters, [Jun 9]).

PMIs & Activity: June confirms an export- and tech-led upturn. The official manufacturing PMI rose to 50.3 and non-manufacturing to 50.2 (Reuters, [Jun 29]); the private factory gauge capped its strongest quarter since late 2020 (Reuters, [Jun 30]) though it grew less than expected (Bloomberg, [Jun 30]). May showed the split: exports jumped 19.4% (AP News, [Jun 9]) while retail sales fell 0.6% (Il Sole 24 Ore, [Jun 15]) and fixed-asset investment was down 4.1% in the first five months (Macau Business, [Jun 16]); NEV retail sales then fell 7% in June on preliminary CPCA data (CnEVPost, [Jul 3]). The World Bank reads two ways: TradingView reports a cut to its China outlook through 2027 ([Jul 6]), while Global Times stresses it held the 4.4% GDP forecast steady and sees upside ([Jul 7]) — compatible only if the headline number held while the later path was trimmed.

Credit Impulse: The last print is two-track. Total social financing grew 7.7% in May (CGTN, [Jun 12]) and topped forecasts (Bloomberg, [Jun 12]), but new loans missed as the property slump curbed demand (Reuters, [Jun 12]). The beat is government bonds; Goldman's shorthand is that households are lying flat and no longer borrowing (Binance, [Jul 5]). Stress is surfacing in small lenders: Beijing took over Zhongbang Bank citing credit risks (The Standard, [Jul 3]) and the SCMP warns the rescue "might not be the last" ([Jul 6]), while the cleanup now extends to inflated AAA bond ratings (Caixin, [Jul 7]). Capping LGFV bond yields (IFR, [Jul 2]) keeps local funding cheap but does nothing for loan demand.

Property: Resale home prices fell faster in June, a private survey shows (Reuters, [Jun 30]), after new home prices fell at a faster pace in May (Reuters, [Jun 15]) and real-estate investment dropped 16.2% y/y in January–May (GMK Center, [Jun 18]). Mettis Global sees no sign of a bottom ([Jul 7]) and The Real Deal notes two decades of price growth erased ([Jul 7]). Sales are concentrating: the top 100 developers sold nearly RMB 1.6 trillion in the first half with an 11.8% month-on-month rise in June (Moomoo, [Jul 1]), led by top-tier cities (Caixin, [Jul 3]). Restructuring runs under state direction: Vanke named a former Shenzhen financial regulator as president (Caixin, [Jul 3]).

Fiscal, Policy & Trade: Beijing finalized its 2026 list of key national projects (China Daily, [Jul 7]), but local finances are strained: Caixin reports Nanning inflated fiscal revenue by $417 million through a land-fee scheme ([Jul 7]), and NEV tax breaks are ending (chinadailyhk, [Jul 4]). Trade with Washington is thawing: China is buying more US soybeans (Bloomberg, [Jul 7]) after pledging $17 billion in annual farm purchases (BigGo Finance, [Jul 2]). Elsewhere it is hardening: von der Leyen says the EU will retaliate if China misses the October deadline (Euronews, [Jul 3]), and corporate Japan's rare-earth warnings are getting louder as China keeps the spigot closed (Reuters, [Jul 7]).

Markets & Flows: Bloomberg describes a big rotation sending Korean stocks tumbling and China surging ([Jul 7]); Chinese stocks in Hong Kong jumped the most in 14 months as tech rallied (Bloomberg, [Jul 7]), extending the break from global markets flagged by Reuters ([Jul 6]). Onshore is weaker: China stocks hit a new one-month low (TradingView, [Jul 7]) as property shares dragged (The Star, [Jul 7]). Households still prefer bullion: China's biggest ETF is now a US$13 billion gold fund (Business Times, [Jul 6]).

New / Most Important: The counter-cyclical bid into China while the rest of the world sells off on Iran risk is significant — it suggests China is being treated as a geopolitical hedge (non-aligned, a net oil importer benefiting from discounted Iranian crude). The new detail is where the money lands: Hong Kong-listed tech, rotated out of Korean chips, while onshore A-shares sink on property. The PBOC's Hong Kong package and its biggest gold purchase since 2023 fit the same pattern: Beijing is building yuan and bullion channels, not reflating. This is a flow-rotation story driven by relative valuation and geopolitical positioning.

Playbook ✓: Easing bias with weak demand — fits the script.  |  ⚠️ Divergence: China decoupling positively from a global risk-off event is genuinely new, but the growth pulse does not back it: an export-driven PMI at 50.3, missed loan demand and resale prices falling faster in June. The transmission to global assets runs through that gap. Construction demand stays weak, so iron ore is flat near $98 (Kalkine, [Jul 6]) and the AUD bounce lacks conviction (FOREX.com, [Jul 5]); European exporters absorb the surge: Goldman says Chinese exports hit Europe's growth more than the trade gap itself (Reuters, [Jul 2]). If flows into China accelerate while EM broadly weakens (Indonesia facing a potential frontier downgrade from S&P DJI), China becomes the contrarian EM long — in Hong Kong tech, not the domestic cycle. Watch A-share flows and the June credit print closely.
