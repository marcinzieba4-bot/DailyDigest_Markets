🇨🇳 China  Bullish  ↓ easing bias

Macro Stance: The PBOC pledged to keep policy accommodative amid weak demand and external shocks (Reuters, [Jul 8]) while flagging "structural divergences" as the AI boom masks consumer weakness (BigGo Finance, [Jul 9]). The currency is moving the other way: the PBOC set its strongest fix in three years (Business Recorder, [Jul 9]), then took the fixing below 6.8 per dollar for the first time since 2023 (Bloomberg, [Jul 10]). Prices are split: June CPI rose 1% (Global Times, [Jul 9]) and slowed more than forecast (Bloomberg, [Jul 8]), while producer inflation jumped to a four-year high (Reuters, [Jul 8]). The lean is bullish on policy and flows, not on household demand.

PMIs & Activity: The June official manufacturing PMI rose to 50.3 (Reuters, [Jun 29]) and non-manufacturing to 50.2 (Reuters, [Jun 29]); the private survey capped the strongest factory quarter since late 2020 (Reuters, [Jun 30]), while services activity slowed slightly (Reuters, [Jul 2]). May's hard data show the split underneath: industrial output growth quickened but retail sales and investment contracted (Reuters, [Jun 15]), with retail sales down 0.6%, the first decline since 2022 (Il Sole 24 Ore, [Jun 15]). Going into this week's releases, a Reuters poll expects June export growth to cool, with AI demand underpinning strength ([Jul 12]), and surveys see second-quarter growth slowing (Daily Sabah, [Jul 12]). The IMF nonetheless lifted its 2026 forecast to 4.6% (Global Times, [Jul 8]).

Credit Impulse: May total social financing grew 7.7% (CGTN, [Jun 12]) and topped forecasts (Bloomberg, [Jun 12]), but new loans missed as the property slump curbed demand (Reuters, [Jun 12]) and households kept deleveraging (SCMP, [Jun 15]). The impulse is government bonds, not private borrowing. Regulators are now leaning against the riskier end of the bond market: a crackdown on inflated AAA ratings (Caixin, [Jul 7]) leaves up to 494.6 billion yuan of bonds facing downgrade risk (BigGo Finance, [Jul 8]), and bankers were asked to avoid underwriting short-term LGFV bonds (Bloomberg, [Jul 8]). With June data due this week (WSJ, [Jul 10]), the read going in is a flat headline impulse and a negative private one.

Property: New home prices fell at a faster pace in May (Reuters, [Jun 15]) even as first-tier prices rose for a third month (SCMP, [Jun 16]), and real-estate investment fell 16.2% y/y in January–May (GMK Center, [Jun 18]). June brought no turn: a private survey shows resale prices falling faster (Reuters, [Jun 30]), while top-100 developer sales up 11.8% month-on-month in June (Moomoo, [Jul 1]) look seasonal. Developer stress is back: Vanke says its first-half loss may widen to $2.2 billion (Bloomberg, [Jul 10]), and Fitch sees new home sales falling 11% to 13% this year (Mingtiandi, [Jul 9]).

Fiscal, Policy & Trade: Beijing finalised its 2026 list of key national projects (China Daily, [Jul 7]) and Bloomberg sees a chance at a fiscal rethink after the debt-cleanup milestone ([Jul 9]), but Societe Generale describes the support as targeted (FXStreet, [Jul 10]). On trade, the thaw with Washington continues, with China dropping a 10% tariff on US farm goods including soybeans (AgWeb, [Jul 6]). Friction with Brussels is rising instead: EU duties of up to 45 per cent on Chinese tyres (Eunews, [Jul 9]), an anti-dumping probe into ducks (SCMP, [Jul 10]) and contingency measures for a trade war (The Brussels Times, [Jul 11]). A temporary helium export ban (SCMP, [Jul 10]) shows export controls remain Beijing's lever.

Markets & Flows: Hong Kong is set for its strongest week in over a year on China internet gains (The Business Times, [Jul 10]), after Chinese stocks in HK jumped the most in 14 months (Bloomberg, [Jul 7]); SCMP's report of overseas capital pouring into hard-tech giants fits the same pattern. The mainland lags: stocks hit a four-week low (TradingView, [Jul 8]) and fell as the week began (TradingView, [Jul 12]), and China's biggest ETF is now a US$13 billion gold fund as state stock-market support fades (The Business Times, [Jul 6]).

New / Most Important: The yuan is the signal going into a heavy data week. A fixing below 6.8 for the first time since 2023, from a central bank that promises accommodation, means Beijing is accepting currency strength just as the Reuters poll expects export growth to cool and surveys see the second quarter slowing. A firmer yuan imports disinflation into a CPI already at 1% and squeezes the southern factories that SCMP says are feeling the pinch despite a pickup in orders ([Jul 12]). The bullish case rests on flows into Hong Kong tech and hard-tech names, not on a domestic turn, which is why this week's trade, credit and GDP prints matter more than usual.

Playbook ✓: An accommodative PBOC, targeted rather than broad stimulus, a property drag and an export-led PMI are all on script.  |  ⚠️ Divergence: The consensus reads a strong yuan and a Hong Kong rally as reflation; the growth pulse says China is still exporting its excess supply. The June PMI is export-led, private credit is contracting and property investment is falling by double digits, so domestic absorption is not improving. Yuan strength is supporting AUD (Mitrade, [Jul 10]) and global iron ore trade hit a record on Chinese demand (S&P Global, [Jul 10]), but with new home sales seen falling 11% to 13%, construction-led steel demand is fading and that support looks cyclical. European exporters face the sharper edge, as tyre duties and duck probes are the visible part of a trade gap that a firmer yuan narrows only at the margin, while EM Asia rides the AI export cycle. A real turn needs loan growth, not bond issuance, to re-accelerate; until it does, Hong Kong's rally is flows, not fundamentals.
