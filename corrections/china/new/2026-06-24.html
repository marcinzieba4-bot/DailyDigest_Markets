🇨🇳 China  Mixed  → easing bias

Macro Stance: The PBOC is easing through plumbing, not price. Loan prime rates stayed unchanged for a 13th month (Reuters, [Jun 21]), but at the Lujiazui Forum the central bank hinted at a shift towards an overnight policy rate to match global peers (The Business Times, [Jun 17]); Capital Economics expects a narrower rate corridor but sees no signs of healthier credit ([Jun 19]). The yuan is the release valve: the fixing was weakened for a fourth session as the dollar advanced (Bloomberg, [Jun 23]), even as Germany's Merz seeks currency talks to fix an undervalued yuan (Bloomberg, [Jun 19]) and Lagarde urges talks on its undervaluation (SCMP, [Jun 23]). Prices split: May CPI rose 1.2% (China Daily, [Jun 10]), an unexpected stall despite the oil shock (Bloomberg, [Jun 9]), while factory-gate inflation hit a nearly four-year high (Reuters, [Jun 9]).

PMIs & Activity: May's official manufacturing PMI dropped to 50 (Xinhua, [May 31]) as factory activity stalled on weaker demand (Reuters, [May 30]); non-manufacturing rose to 50.1 (Macau Business, [May 31]). The private gauge slowed but still showed factory activity expanding for a sixth month (Reuters, [May 31]). The hard data diverge: industrial output growth quickened while retail sales and investment contracted (Reuters, [Jun 15]); retail sales fell 0.6%, the first decline since 2022 (Il Sole 24 ORE, [Jun 15]), and fixed-asset investment was down 4.1% in the first five months (Macau Business, [Jun 16]). Exports jumped 19.4% (AP News, [Jun 9]). June has not turned: the 618 festival saw flat e-commerce sales (Reuters, [Jun 22]).

Credit Impulse: May credit growth topped forecasts in a rebound from the lending slump (Bloomberg, [Jun 12]), yet new loans missed as the property slump curbed demand (Reuters, [Jun 12]) and households kept deleveraging (SCMP, [Jun 15]). The two reconcile through the mix: the beat came from outside bank loans, in line with the tilt to bonds from loans (Bloomberg, [Jun 17]), and the PBOC chief says slower credit growth may be the "new normal" (Caixin, [Jun 18]). The private impulse is impaired: a 2.2 trillion yuan pile of bad consumer debt threatens the economy (The Business Times, [Jun 18]).

Property: May new home prices fell at a faster pace on soft demand (Reuters, [Jun 15]), even as first-tier new home prices rose for a third month (SCMP, [Jun 16]) and top cities extended a secondhand-home price rebound (Caixin, [Jun 17]). Construction is the drag: property investment fell 16.2% y/y in January–May (GMK Center, [Jun 18]) and the steel market has entered a long plateau after the property crash (Bloomberg, [Jun 21]). Policies delivered an uptick in property sales (The Star, [Jun 19]), but developer stress persists: Oaktree and PAG are vying with Arkkan over a Chinese builder's debt plan (Bloomberg, [Jun 17]).

Fiscal, Policy & Trade: The fiscal stance is tightening into the slowdown: China pursued austerity with its first fiscal-gap cutback since 2023 (Bloomberg, [Jun 22]), the deficit narrowing for the first time in over two years despite slowing growth (Firstpost, [Jun 23]). Support is targeted instead: Beijing readies a fresh $9 billion consumer stimulus as retail sales shrink (Caixin, [Jun 18]) and CNY 200 billion of "Two New" funds to be fully deployed by end-June (BigGo Finance, [Jun 17]). On trade, Beijing added 10 US firms, including a rare-earth miner, to its export control list (Al Jazeera, [Jun 22]) and hit Australian beef with a 55% tariff once the quota was reached (Nikkei Asia, [Jun 19]). The EU is reportedly preparing tariffs on Chinese plug-in hybrids (Reuters, [Jun 19]); its trade chief is set to host China's commerce minister (Reuters, [Jun 22]).

Markets & Flows: Hong Kong prices the consumer slump. The Hang Seng hit its lowest level in a year on rate-rise expectations (SCMP, [Jun 18]) and then plunged 432 points through 23,300 as the HSCEI entered a technical bear market (BigGo Finance, [Jun 23]), a slide Bloomberg ties to spending woes ([Jun 23]). AI is the exception: Chinese AI stocks rallied on demand optimism and policy support (Bloomberg, [Jun 21]). Flows favour new supply: a Hynix ETF has toppled the Hong Kong Tracker Fund with $17 billion of assets (Bloomberg, [Jun 22]), echoing SK Hynix's $29B ADR plan, and China Resources New Energy drew 6.4 trillion yuan in retail bids for the biggest Shenzhen IPO (The Business Times, [Jun 23]).

New / Most Important: Beijing is tightening fiscal policy into a domestic-demand slump. The first deficit cutback since 2023 follows the first fall in retail sales in over three years, and the response stays targeted: a $9 billion consumer package, funds already in the pipeline and a hinted shift to an overnight policy rate, not a demand stimulus. Markets have answered: the HSCEI is in a technical bear market on spending woes, and the yuan fixing has been weakened for a fourth session, a release valve Berlin and the ECB now openly contest.

Playbook ✓: Weak consumption, falling home prices and booming exports met by unchanged LPRs and targeted support are on script.  |  ⚠️ Divergence: Policy leans on supply and the currency rather than demand: austerity, a weaker fixing and 19.4% export growth push excess output abroad. European exporters are squeezed: BMW's profit warning highlights China risk (Reuters, [Jun 17]), while plug-in hybrid tariffs and the yuan dispute raise trade-war risk for EU autos. Construction commodities lose: iron ore has sunk back below $100 on abundant supplies (Bloomberg, [Jun 17]) and property investment down 16.2% offers no demand offset, leaving the AUD exposed, with a 55% beef tariff on top. For oil, the China channel runs through Iran: Kpler expects Iranian oil to flow mainly to China despite the 60-day sanctions waiver ([Jun 23]), so the waiver frees few barrels for other buyers. EM fixed income wins, as Pimco sees China's export glut boosting emerging-market bonds (Bloomberg, [Jun 23]).
