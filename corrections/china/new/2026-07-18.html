🇨🇳 China  Mixed  → easing bias

Macro Stance: Policy rates are not the tool. A Reuters poll sees benchmark lending rates left unchanged for a 14th month in July ([Jul 17]), and the PBOC calls slower credit growth a "new normal" (Caixin, [Jul 16]) while easing through liquidity and a floor that bills not be re-discounted below 0.5% (Reuters, [Jul 14]). On the currency it sees a stable renminbi amid two-way fluctuations (China Daily, [Jul 16]), and the yuan weakened slightly as the PBOC lowered the daily fixing (BusinessToday Malaysia, [Jul 17]). Prices: June CPI rose 1% (Global Times, [Jul 9]) and producer inflation hit a four-year high (Reuters, [Jul 8]), cost-push that Bloomberg calls "bad inflation" ([Jul 15]).

PMIs & Activity: Second-quarter GDP grew 4.3%, below the official target range (Bloomberg, [Jul 15]), even as June industrial output rose 5.3% y/y and retail sales returned to growth (BusinessLine, [Jul 15]). The factory engine is AI- and export-led, with June exports up 27% y/y (Reuters, [Jul 13]) and the official manufacturing PMI at 50.3 (Reuters, [Jun 29]); Capital Economics calls AI the main driver of growth ([Jul 16]). Household demand is thin: Saucony saw a sharp slowdown in China retail growth in the second quarter while Anta and Fila managed low-single-digit gains (SGB Media, [Jul 17]), NEV retail sales fell 8% in the first two weeks of July (CnEVPost, [Jul 15]), and the WGC puts Chinese gold demand at a decade low in June (Mining.com, [Jul 17]).

Credit Impulse: This is the bottleneck. Household loans shrank by 366.8 billion yuan in the first half (36Kr, [Jul 17]), June credit growth missed forecasts (Caixin, [Jul 16]) and new bank loans rose less than expected (The Star, [Jul 15]), so the headline and the household book are both soft. Record consumer defaults undermine the push to boost spending (Reuters, [Jul 15]), and one of China's top investors argues finance, not AI, is the country's biggest bottleneck (CNBC, [Jul 16]). LGFVs that added debt face curbs on new bonds (Bloomberg, [Jul 15]), and foreign investors marginally offloaded onshore bonds in June (The Economic Times, [Jul 17]). The impulse is negative where it matters for consumption.

Property: Property investment slumped 18% y/y in January–June (The Standard, [Jul 15]). Price signals are confined to the top tier: China's top metros logged a fourth month of gains, lifting hopes of a housing recovery (Mingtiandi, [Jul 17]), while nationally new home prices declined at a slower pace (The Standard, [Jul 15]). Developers face a fresh liquidity crunch (Nikkei Asia, [Jul 13]), and shrinking household loans mean mortgage demand is not yet following the top-tier price turn.

Fiscal, Policy & Trade: Beijing eyes faster national infrastructure spending to steady growth amid a local investment slump (The Economic Times, [Jul 17]), in line with its bet on state-backed projects rather than broad stimulus (Reuters, [Jul 16]), and Caixin reports a rethink of the tax rebates that drove the export machine ([Jul 17]). The July Politburo meeting is being prepared (Sinocism, [Jul 13]). Europe is coordinating: Germany and France will draw up a roadmap on China trade imbalances (Bloomberg, [Jul 17]) by September (Le Monde, [Jul 17]) and vow to align on tougher measures (Financial Times, [Jul 17]). US signals are mixed: the Senate backed a 100% tariff bill on Russian oil buyers including China (The New Indian Express, [Jul 17]), while Washington lifted some Hong Kong sanctions (Reuters, [Jul 17]).

Markets & Flows: China stocks tumbled as CXMT's $8.6 billion IPO stirred liquidity concerns (TradingView, [Jul 17]) and faced steep weekly losses (The Economic Times, [Jul 17]); the Shanghai Composite breached 3,800 (BigGo Finance, [Jul 17]). The Hang Seng crashed over 2% in the tech sell-off (The Economic Times, [Jul 17]), with Z.ai sinking 28 percent (The Standard, [Jul 17]). Chinese AI also cut the other way abroad: the Nasdaq and S&P 500 dropped 1% after a Chinese AI breakthrough rattled tech stocks (CNN, [Jul 17]).

New / Most Important: The first-half household loan figure turns the "finance is the bottleneck" argument into data. Households are paying down 366.8 billion yuan of debt while the PBOC calls slower credit normal and keeps the loan prime rate on hold, so monetary easing has no transmission to consumption. Beijing's answer is faster infrastructure spending, which supports output and bulk demand but leaves the household balance sheet untouched. Rate cuts alone would not fix a credit-allocation problem; the Politburo meeting is where any shift toward household support would have to appear.

Playbook ✓: Household deleveraging, LPR on hold, investment-led support and a managed yuan are on script.  |  ⚠️ Divergence: The consensus sees an infrastructure push as stimulus; the credit data say it is substitution, state borrowing replacing household borrowing. The transmission follows that split. Faster national projects are a modest support for iron ore and AUD after BHP won higher quarterly prices despite China's buying curbs (Reuters, [Jul 16]), but an 18% property-investment slump caps the upside. Consumer-facing exporters lose: weak household demand keeps hitting German carmakers, whose China sales plunged (AP News, [Jul 11]), just as Paris and Berlin align on tougher trade measures. Oil gains little while Chinese crude imports sit near a decade low (Bloomberg, [Jul 13]). For EM, ODI sees silver linings from China's slowdown for some economies ([Jul 17]), mainly through cheaper inputs rather than demand.
