🇨🇳 China  Mixed  → easing bias

Macro Stance: China remains in a structural easing posture, but the PBOC's attention is on architecture and the currency rather than rates. It released a 15th Five-Year Plan for building a financial powerhouse with nine supporting action plans (BigGo Finance, [Sep 10]), and set a firmer fixing while keeping the yuan below market estimates to aid exports (VT Markets, [Sep 10]); UOB sees the yuan's uptrend eyeing a breakout (FXStreet, [Sep 10]) and Commerzbank says the PBoC defends its yuan narrative (FXStreet, [Sep 10]), with Beijing calling the yuan's global rise 'irreversible' (South China Morning Post, [Sep 10]). August CPI rose 0.8% and PPI 3.8% (Xinhua, [Sep 9]) on an AI and energy lift (China Daily, [Sep 10]); consumer prices are still below target (Free Malaysia Today, [Sep 9]).

PMIs & Activity: Every August survey improved. The official manufacturing PMI rebounded to 49.8 with new orders back in expansion (BigGo Finance, [Aug 30]), still a second month of contraction (CNBC, [Aug 30]); the private manufacturing PMI rose to 51.5 (BigGo Finance, [Aug 31]) and private services to 51.4 (BigGo Finance, [Sep 2]). The economy runs at two speeds, with record exports and a consumer who will not spend (International Finance, [Sep 10]): exports jumped 25% (AP News, [Sep 7]) and car exports in eight months surpassed the 2025 total (AP News, [Sep 10]), but imports missed estimates (CNBC, [Sep 7]) and passenger car sales slumped 24% (Caixin Global, [Sep 9]). July retail sales grew only 0.6% with industrial output up 4.5% (Reuters, [Aug 17]).

Credit Impulse: Banks are swapping loans for bonds as the loan slump deepens (Bloomberg.com, [Sep 8]), after July new loans posted a record contraction (Reuters, [Aug 14]) even as credit growth exceeded forecasts (Bloomberg.com, [Aug 14]); reconciled, the aggregate holds up while the loan book shrinks. Regulation is tightening at the edges: online lenders see profits plunge as new rules bite (Caixin Global, [Sep 10]), and the financial regulator's five-year agenda is to consolidate small and mid-sized institutions and hold the line against blowups (BigGo Finance, [Sep 10]). Reuters asks why the state insurer capital injections are coming now (Reuters, [Sep 10]); The Economist judges the bank capital boost to fall far short (The Economist, [Sep 8]).

Property: The shift to completed-home sales is cooling the land market (Caixin Global, [Sep 10]) as China changes its decades-old presale model (The Straits Times, [Sep 10]); Goldman sees land sales cut by 30% (The Business Times, [Sep 7]). Developer exits are narrowing: the securities regulator rejected mandatory convertible bond registrations from developers (BigGo Finance, [Sep 9]) and China halted a key debt-to-equity restructuring tool (The Standard (HK), [Sep 9]). July new home prices were stagnant (Reuters, [Aug 17]) and first-tier prices flat, ending a four-month rebound (South China Morning Post, [Aug 17]).

Fiscal, Policy & Trade: Europe is hardening while Washington talks. The EU unveiled 'buy European' procurement rules to counter China (The Guardian, [Sep 9]) and wants initial action on the trade gap by early October (Reuters, [Sep 8]); a Global Times editorial says that if the EU is determined to wage a trade war, let it try (Global Times, [Sep 10]), and Japan protests new Chinese measures against its chipmaking material (AP News, [Sep 8]). With the US, China bought 1 million tons of soybeans ahead of Xi's visit (Reuters, [Sep 9]), Washington says China is on track to fulfil farm purchases (South China Morning Post, [Sep 10]) and US business confidence in China rebounded from a record low (Nikkei Asia, [Sep 10]).

Markets & Flows: Stocks in China hit a 6-week low (TradingView, [Sep 10]) and the Hang Seng tumbled 289 points as the tech selloff deepened (The Standard (HK), [Sep 10]), even as bank stocks hit record highs with mid-year dividends topping 260 billion yuan (BigGo Finance, [Sep 10]); Chinese AI stocks have been on a tear while US investors have been left out. The China-US 10-year yield gap widened to a record on the policy split (Bloomberg.com, [Sep 10]), yet the yuan is resilient (CNBC TV18, [Sep 10]), Marsh sees the gap as unlikely to trigger capital flight (South China Morning Post, [Sep 10]) and foreign holdings of onshore yuan assets top $1.6 trillion (Caixin Global, [Sep 10]).

New / Most Important: China and the US are in consultations on reciprocal tariff reductions covering $30 billion, according to MOFCOM (Global Times, [Sep 10]), and Beijing hopes to agree at an early date (The Independent, [Sep 10]). It is the first concrete tariff relief on the table ahead of the Trump-Xi summit, and it lands as Europe moves the other way, so China's export surplus is being redirected rather than reduced. The Hormuz crisis and $100 oil remain a significant negative for China as a net energy importer, potentially overwhelming stimulus effects.

Playbook ✓: Soft domestic demand, with car sales down 24% and imports missing, confirms China's recovery remains sub-par and easing stays incremental.  |  ⚠️ Divergence: Chinese AI equities outperforming while the broader economy struggles mirrors the US narrow market: AI is becoming a global liquidity magnet regardless of domestic macro conditions. The transmission is split. Improving PMIs and export orders support iron ore demand expectations and the AUD at the margin, but weak imports cap the benefit for EM exporters, and MUFG says China trade tensions and CNY strength pose downside risks for the euro (FXStreet, [Sep 10]), a direct squeeze on European exporters. WTI at $100.12 is a terms-of-trade tax on China that eats into the gains the PMIs imply.
