🇨🇳 China  Mixed  → easing bias

Macro Stance: The PBOC has moved from holding to signalling. It pledged timely adjustment of its policy tools (Reuters, [Aug 2]) and China Daily writes that interest rate and RRR cuts may be on the table ([Aug 4]); meanwhile it injected CNY 500 billion (BigGo Finance, [Aug 4]) and plans a $74 billion reverse repo (Fibre2Fashion, [Aug 6]). The currency is the complication: the yuan hit its strongest in 3-1/2 years as trade tensions grew (The Standard, [Aug 5]), despite a weaker fixing (Business Recorder, [Aug 3]) and a PBOC signalling discomfort over the gains (The Edge Malaysia, [Jul 31]). Inflation leaves room: June CPI rose 1.0% while PPI climbed 4.1% (finance.biggo.com, [Jul 8]), a producer squeeze rather than demand-led pricing. July prices are not yet out.

PMIs & Activity: The official manufacturing PMI fell to 49.2 and the non-manufacturing PMI to 49.0 (FXStreet, [Jul 31]), with factories and construction slumping (Bloomberg, [Jul 31]) and orders shrinking (Asian Business Review, [Aug 6]). The private gauges are softer but not contracting: the factory index eased to 50.9 (Fibre2Fashion, [Aug 3]), a 4-month low (Reuters, [Aug 2]), and services growth hit its slowest since September 2024 (Reuters, [Aug 4]). Reconciled: the official survey, weighted to large domestic-facing firms, is below 50; the export-tilted private one is merely slowing. Exports are the offset. July exports beat estimates on surging AI-driven shipments (CNBC, [Aug 6]), jumping almost a quarter (Financial Times, [Aug 6]); Caixin sees exports offsetting weak investment ([Aug 6]). Consumption is the soft spot, with July NEV retail sales down 2% (CnEVPost, [Aug 5]) after Q2 GDP growth of 4.3% fell below the target range (Bloomberg, [Jul 15]).

Credit Impulse: July credit is still to come, so June frames the impulse: new bank loans rose less than expected (The Star, [Jul 15]), credit growth missed as the PBOC flagged a "new normal" (Caixin Global, [Jul 16]) and household loans fell 366.8 billion yuan in the first half (36Kr, [Jul 17]). The central bank promises enhanced financial support for the real economy in H2 (State Council Information Office, [Aug 3]), but demand is the binding constraint, and China selling long-dated offshore yuan bonds at record low yields (Bloomberg, [Aug 4]) is the market's verdict on it.

Property: The first July readings point the wrong way. Housing prices fell 0.5% as sales volume weakened (Investing.com, [Aug 3]) and the top-100 developer sales slump deepened (Trivium China, [Aug 5]). June had flattered, with new home prices falling at a slower pace (Reuters, [Jul 14]), but property investment slumped 18 percent y/y in January–June (The Standard, [Jul 15]). The financial tail is visible as China Cinda warns of a first-half profit drop on the slump (Caixin Global, [Aug 5]), and Xi's Shanghai tour offered no relief for the housing crisis (The Diplomat, [Aug 4]).

Fiscal, Policy & Trade: The Politburo pledged incremental policy support (Caixin Global, [Jul 31]), and Oxford Economics says China enters "game time" as an unspent fiscal arsenal targets a Q3 recovery (CNBC, [Aug 3]). The new fiscal lever is a surprise tax on offshore trusts (CNBC, [Aug 4]), which now reaches offshore insurance income (Reuters, [Aug 6]). On trade, Beijing is testing the truce weeks before Xi's visit (CNBC, [Aug 6]): Washington will impose a 15% tariff in its polysilicon probe (Reuters, [Aug 5]) and Chinese optical module makers slumped on a planned US import ban (Reuters, [Aug 4]), and China hit back with sanctions and tighter drone export rules (Financial Times, [Aug 5]). SCMP asks whether this "speed bump" could stop the Xi-Trump summit ([Aug 6]).

Markets & Flows: China stocks slid as an AI selloff hammered the chip supply chain (Business Recorder, [Aug 3]), and the Hang Seng slumped nearly 500 points as insurers sank (The Standard, [Aug 6]) while mainland shares ended mixed on rising gold stocks (marketscreener.com, [Aug 6]). Flows stay supportive: mainland investors extended their Hong Kong buying spree (South China Morning Post, [Aug 5]), Morgan Stanley lifted its Hang Seng target to 28,400 (BigGo Finance, [Aug 5]) and Chinese investors poured $1.2bn into gold ETFs (TradingView, [Aug 6]). The AI stock frenzy across Asia flagged by CNN is the regional backdrop.

New / Most Important: July exports beat estimates in the same week that the official PMIs sat in contraction and Washington and Beijing traded tariffs and sanctions. Growth is now external and AI-led, exactly the channel the polysilicon tariff and the components ban target, while a yuan at a 3-1/2-year high squeezes the same exporters. The burden shifts to the PBOC, where rate and RRR cuts are now openly discussed.

Playbook ✓: Incremental support, ample liquidity, weak property and export resilience are on script.  |  ⚠️ Divergence: Consensus reads the export beat as proof the economy is coping; the PMIs and the 0.5% July home-price fall say domestic demand is contracting beneath it. Iron ore hit a year low (Tech Times, [Aug 4]), so the bulk channel offers AUD little support just as the RBA debates a hike. The tax crackdown opens a direct channel into European financials, with British bank shares hit (The Times, [Aug 5]). EM Asian exporters compete with a Chinese export machine running hot, and oil at $78.33 is a terms-of-trade tax on factories already below 50.
