🇨🇳 China  Mixed  → easing bias

Macro Stance: Rates are on hold and the budget is doing the easing. China kept loan prime rates steady for a 15th month (Reuters, [Aug 19]), the 1-year at 3.0% and the 5-year at 3.5% (BigGo Finance, [Aug 19]), while expanding consumer and small-business loan subsidies (The Asian Banker, [Aug 21]); liquidity runs through the overnight window, with 7-day reverse repos at zero for seven straight sessions (BigGo Finance, [Aug 19]). The yuan surged to 6.72 per dollar, a 42-month high (BigGo Finance, [Aug 20]), even as the PBOC fixed at 6.7808, weaker than the previous fix (CryptoRank, [Aug 20]) and well above the 6.7262 Reuters estimate (investingLive, [Aug 20]), a deliberate brake after its move to slow yuan gains (Bloomberg, [Aug 19]). Prices leave room to ease: July CPI rose 0.5% and PPI growth slowed to 3.5%, both missing expectations (BigGo Finance, [Aug 8]).

PMIs & Activity: July's official manufacturing PMI fell to 49.2 and non-manufacturing to 49.0 (FXStreet, [Jul 31]); the private factory gauge eased to 50.9 (Fibre2Fashion, [Aug 3]) and private services growth was the slowest since September 2024 (Reuters, [Aug 4]). The hard data confirmed the slide: industrial output grew 4.5% y/y and retail sales just 0.6% (Reuters, [Aug 17]), and fixed-asset investment fell 6.7% in the first seven months (Xinhua, [Aug 17]). Exports remain the engine, jumping almost a quarter in July (Financial Times, [Aug 6]), while luxury sales plunge as the tax push hits rich shoppers (Livemint, [Aug 22]). Abroad, the August flash PMIs accelerated: the euro-zone manufacturing PMI reached a four-year high as export orders returned (EU Today, [Aug 22]), the US composite improved to 56 (Kitco, [Aug 21]) and Japan's manufacturing PMI jumped to 55.1 on chip and AI orders (BigGo Finance, [Aug 20]), a cycle China supplies but does not share.

Credit Impulse: July new bank loans posted a record contraction as credit demand faltered (Reuters, [Aug 14]), only the second contraction this year (The Standard (HK), [Aug 14]), yet Bloomberg reports credit growth exceeded forecasts despite the rare loan slump ([Aug 14]). Reconciled: headline financing, which includes bond issuance, beat; bank lending to households and firms shrank, leaving cumulative yuan loans up 10.38 trillion yuan in January–July (Xinhua, [Aug 14]). The expanded subsidised-loan programme (Caixin Global, [Aug 21]) targets that gap by cutting the effective cost of SME and consumer borrowing without touching the LPR; whether it revives demand is the August credit print's question.

Property: July new home prices were stagnant as demand stayed weak (Reuters, [Aug 17]); the 70-city index fell 3.2% y/y, the smallest drop in five months, while property investment slumped nearly 20% (BigGo Finance, [Aug 17]). A flood of discounted auction homes is deepening the slump (Asia Times, [Aug 21]). Policy is granular: Shanghai started a new round of easing (Bloomberg, [Aug 19]) and is lowering second-home down payments (South China Morning Post, [Aug 20]), and housing provident fund rules are being reshaped (China.org.cn, [Aug 21]). A court accepted Evergrande's liquidation a day after its founder's life sentence (South China Morning Post, [Aug 21]), with creditors still owed $300 billion (Fortune, [Aug 21]); as Barron's puts it, the developer is gone but the property crisis isn't ([Aug 22]).

Fiscal, Policy & Trade: China pledges greater fiscal support in the second half (China Daily, [Aug 22]) and is stepping it up as growth weakens and domestic demand falters (The Economic Times, [Aug 22]); Bloomberg reports new fiscal support policies are planned ([Aug 21]) and Xinhua says Beijing is ramping up policy support to unlock domestic demand ([Aug 21]), after the SME interest-subsidy loan cap was raised to 75 million yuan (BigGo Finance, [Aug 20]). Public budget revenue rose 5.8% in the first seven months (China Daily, [Aug 21]). Geopolitics is the new variable: China rejected a US call to support economic sanctions on Iran (Asia Financial, [Aug 21]), and Trump's plan to choke Iran's economy risks Chinese blowback (The Japan Times, [Aug 22]). On trade, Beijing rejected a US tariff report as Mexico plans new China curbs (South China Morning Post, [Aug 21]) and slammed EU foreign-subsidy rules (POLITICO.eu, [Aug 21]).

Markets & Flows: Hong Kong reclaimed 26,000 as the Hang Seng jumped 310.97 points (BusinessToday Malaysia, [Aug 22]), its fifth straight gain (Dimsum Daily, [Aug 21]), and the index expands to 95 members with Hua Hong and Weichai Power (The Standard (HK), [Aug 21]). A-shares closed higher as precious metals led (BigGo Finance, [Aug 21]) while investors await fiscal support (The Economic Times, [Aug 21]). Mainland capital pulled a net HK$10.4 billion from Hong Kong stocks (BigGo Finance, [Aug 20]), and China trimmed US Treasury holdings as the global reserve shift gathers pace (Firstpost, [Aug 18]).

New / Most Important: The fiscal turn is now the official line, and it arrives alongside a new external risk. Beijing pledges greater second-half support aimed at consumption and SMEs with no rate cut, while its refusal to back US sanctions on Iran puts China's oil supply and trade relationship in Washington's sights just as the domestic recovery needs both.

Playbook ✓: Rates on hold, targeted fiscal support, city-level property easing and a managed yuan are on script.  |  ⚠️ Divergence: The August flash PMIs show the US and euro-area cycle accelerating without China, whose July loans contracted. Transmission is uneven: AUD, which hit a two-month high on US Treasury buybacks (Moomoo, [Aug 19]) rather than Chinese demand, is now softening on a jobs miss. Europe splits: as Germany falters, Central Europe looks to China for growth (Bloomberg, [Aug 22]), while German exporters face a China that sells more than it buys. Iranian oil offers to Chinese buyers fell as the US blockade bites (Reuters, [Aug 21]), raising China's import bill with WTI at $87.06.
