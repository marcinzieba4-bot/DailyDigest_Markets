🇨🇳 China  Mixed  → easing bias

Macro Stance: The PBOC's overnight framework is in its first week: reverse repos debuted at 1.25% (Reuters, [Jun 28]), below the 1.35% a Bloomberg survey expected ([Jun 25]), and the cash injection was then doubled at an unchanged rate (Reuters, [Jun 29]). Quantity signals conflict: TradingView reports liquidity added [Jun 30] while BigGo Finance tallies a drain of over 1.16 trillion yuan [Jun 30] — daily top-ups against larger maturities, so overnight money is cheaper but the liquidity stock is not expanding. A PBOC adviser sees a potential rate cut but calls for targeted aid instead (Bloomberg, [Jun 24]). The yuan midpoint was set weaker for a third day (SCMP, [Jun 24]) and options traders have turned bullish on the dollar (Bloomberg, [Jun 30]). Prices stay split: May CPI rose only 1.2% (China Daily, [Jun 10]) while factory-gate inflation hit a near four-year high (Reuters, [Jun 9]).

PMIs & Activity: The growth pulse turned up in June, narrowly. The official manufacturing PMI rose to 50.3 (Reuters, [Jun 29]) from a flat 50 in May, on tech export demand (CNBC, [Jun 29]); non-manufacturing rose to 50.2 (Reuters, [Jun 29]). The private gauge capped its strongest quarter since late 2020 (Reuters, [Jun 30]), yet June grew less than expected (Bloomberg, [Jun 30]): a high average with momentum fading. May's hard data show the split: output growth quickened to 4.5% (China Daily, [Jun 16]) and exports jumped 19.4% (AP News, [Jun 9]), but retail sales fell 0.6% (Il Sole 24 Ore, [Jun 15]) and fixed-asset investment fell 4.1% in five months (Macau Business, [Jun 16]); industrial profit growth slowed for the first time in six months as weak domestic demand bit (Times of India, [Jun 27]).

Credit Impulse: Total social financing grew 7.7% in May (CGTN, [Jun 12]) and topped forecasts (Bloomberg, [Jun 12]), but new loans missed as the property slump curbed demand (Reuters, [Jun 12]): the beat is government bonds, the miss is private demand. The PBOC is urging banks to lend because demand remains weak (Reuters, [Jun 26]). With debt above 300% of GDP (UPI, [Jun 29]), the household and private-firm impulse is still negative.

Property: June's first datapoint is poor: resale home prices fell faster, a private survey shows (Reuters, [Jun 30]), after new home prices fell at a faster pace in May (Reuters, [Jun 15]). Real-estate investment fell 16.2% y/y in January–May (GMK Center, [Jun 18]). State capital is replacing private balance sheets: Shenzhen Metro's chairman now also chairs Vanke (BigGo Finance, [Jun 30]) and state developers are powering Shenzhen construction (Nikkei Asia, [Jun 29]), while Country Garden paid over 2.8 billion yuan in the half-year after its offshore restructuring (BigGo Finance, [Jun 30]). The private end is still breaking: Beijing land sales plunged 66% (Caixin, [Jun 25]) and the Evergrande Property Services stake sale collapsed (Caixin, [Jun 26]).

Fiscal, Policy & Trade: The 62.5 billion yuan third batch of trade-in funds (Global Times, [Jun 26]) is small against a retail contraction, and the State Council reviewed AI, trade and carbon plans (Dimsum Daily, [Jun 29]) with no demand package. Trade is where policy moves: the EU and China set an October deadline to reset ties (Bloomberg, [Jun 29]) over a €360bn deficit (The Guardian, [Jun 29]); Beijing and Washington agreed a trade council on tariffs (Global Times, [Jun 25]); and China imposed a 73.5% preliminary tariff on Canadian pea starch (Global News, [Jun 30]) and listed 20 Japanese entities for export controls (Reuters, [Jun 29]). The NYT casts China as a relative winner from the Strait of Hormuz crisis [Jun 29].

Markets & Flows: Hong Kong shares fell 11% in the first half for lack of an AI play (SCMP, [Jun 30]), and Chinese stocks trail the AI boom by the most since 2001 (Bloomberg, [Jun 29]), with the MSCI China index down over 15% this year (BigGo Finance, [Jun 30]). Onshore is bifurcated: ChiNext surged 35% in the half while nearly 70% of A-shares declined (BigGo Finance, [Jun 30]). The capital wall is rising: curbs on higher-yielding offshore debt (Bloomberg, [Jun 29]) and on TRS (SCMP, [Jun 24]), and major banks suspended individual gold trading (SCMP, [Jun 25]). Adam Tooze's Chartbook calls this China's "walled-in wealth", cut off from the AI boom.

New / Most Important: The June PMIs are the first upturn in the growth pulse in months, and the composition is the story: manufacturing at 50.3 on tech exports, services barely positive, and a private gauge missing June forecasts, while resale prices fell faster the same day. Beijing's response is stealth easing, a 1.25% overnight rate it would not even publish (Bloomberg, [Jun 29]), not a demand package, and walled-in savings go to bonds and gold rather than spending.

Playbook ✓: Property drag, weak consumption, no headline rate cut and a managed yuan drift are on script.  |  ⚠️ Divergence: The consensus wanted a mid-year stimulus pivot; instead an export-led PMI sits on a negative private credit impulse and falling home prices, so China exports its excess supply. Transmission runs three ways. European exporters face a record deficit and an October deadline, a direct risk to European industrials. Construction-linked bulk demand fades with real-estate investment down 16.2%, a drag on AUD and iron ore that export PMIs do not offset. And Bloomberg sees fuel export curbs lifted on ample supplies [Jun 30], more barrels for an oil market where WTI is already down 27.2% in four weeks. Until loan growth, not bond issuance, re-accelerates, Hong Kong rallies are flows, not fundamentals.
