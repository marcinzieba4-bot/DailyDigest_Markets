🇨🇳 China  Mixed  → easing bias, constrained by FX

Macro Stance: The easing bias is real but routed through structural tools. The PBOC cut the PSL rate by 0.25 percentage point to 1.5% (Forex Factory, [Sep 29]) and boosted relending quotas by 700 billion yuan (finance.biggo.com, [Sep 29]), a targeted push rather than a benchmark cut; with US 10Y yields at 5.28% and China's 10Y yield at an over one-year low (TradingView, [Sep 29]), a broad cut would widen a differential the yuan absorbs only thanks to exporter flows. The currency firmed as exporters converted dollars before the week-long holiday (Business Recorder, [Sep 29]) and was set for a seventh straight quarterly gain (Business Recorder, [Sep 30]), with the fix at 6.7351 (www.tmgm.com, [Sep 30]). That is the FX constraint now: a firmness resting on seasonal conversion, not capital inflows, which a broad cut could reverse. Prices leave room: August CPI rose 0.8% and PPI 3.8% (BigGo Finance, [Sep 8]).

PMIs & Activity: September brought the first official expansion in three months: manufacturing at 50.1, exactly the consensus, and non-manufacturing at 50.2 (investingLive, [Sep 29]), with the private RatingDog gauges stronger at 52.1 and 51.6 (investingLive, [Sep 30]). Reuters ties the factory rebound to the AI boom (Reuters, [Sep 29]). The domestic side lags: August retail sales rose 0.4% against 0.6% expected (investingLive, [Sep 14]) and industrial profits rose just 4.2%, the weakest since November (Bloomberg, [Sep 27]), while exports jumped 25% (AP News, [Sep 7]). Peak-season auto sales growth disappointed (WSJ, [Oct 2]); the National Day travel boom (Asia Financial, [Oct 2]) is so far the only holiday read, a services signal rather than a goods one.

Credit Impulse: August bank loans rose less than expected (Reuters, [Sep 14]) and credit expansion missed forecasts (Bloomberg, [Sep 14]), with bond and equity financing exceeding loans for the first time (BigGo Finance, [Sep 14]): both measures missed, and government bonds carried what growth there was. The new measures redistribute credit rather than ignite it, with cheap loans to help state firms pay overdue bills (Bloomberg, [Sep 29]). The newest item is balance-sheet repair: China closed 670 banks in 2025 (Yahoo Finance, [Oct 5]) as Beijing moves to shore up its financial system (CNBC, [Oct 5]), a clean-up that adds no lending capacity in the near term.

Property: The central government is subsidising mortgages for the first time, with a 1 percentage point annual rate subsidy (BigGo Finance, [Sep 29]) on eligible first-home loans (The Standard (HK), [Sep 29]). The first read is positive but narrow: Beijing's September existing-home transactions topped 15,000 and new-home sales surged nearly 50% month-on-month (BigGo Finance, [Oct 1]). The base is weak: August home prices fell across 70 cities (BigGo Finance, [Sep 14]) and property investment fell 19.9% in eight months (Business Recorder, [Sep 15]). Developers did not rally: property shares slid after the stimulus (Prop News Time, [Oct 2]) and Vanke's loss underscored market stress (Traders Union, [Oct 1]). The intent is to stabilise, not stimulate (ThinkChina, [Oct 2]).

Fiscal, Policy & Trade: Bloomberg sees the 'mini stimulus' securing the GDP target, not much more (Bloomberg, [Sep 29]), and Beijing is seen doing 'just enough' with targeted fiscal support (South China Morning Post, [Oct 2]). On trade, Brussels is the front line: the EU trade chief heads to China this week (EUobserver, [Oct 5]) for talks to avert a trade war (France 24, [Oct 5]), after China launched an anti-dumping probe into European chemical exports (Financial Times, [Oct 3]), while France and Germany push an EU rapid-response trade tool (Reuters, [Oct 5]). With Washington there is no timeline for the cuts on $60 billion of goods (Yahoo Finance, [Oct 1]), and Chinese refiners suspended October fuel exports (Reuters, [Oct 1]).

Markets & Flows: The package did not convince offshore money: the Hang Seng dropped 3%, Asia's worst, after the stimulus disappointed (NewsBytes, [Oct 2]), and the Hang Seng Tech index fell into a bear market on tax fears (Gotrade, [Oct 5]). Mainland stocks limped to a quarterly drop (Business Recorder, [Sep 30]). Bonds are the preferred exposure, with Citi betting on China's 30-year bonds (South China Morning Post, [Sep 29]).

New / Most Important: The newest development is the escalation before the EU-China talks. Days before Šefčovič arrives, Beijing opened a new anti-dumping probe (EU Today, [Oct 4]), while Paris and Berlin want EU 'storm gates' for China's trade surge (The Economist, [Oct 5]). With retail sales up 0.4% and property investment down 19.9%, exports are the one channel still growing, so a European tariff wall would hit the engine the PMI rebound runs on. The bank consolidation is the second signal: Beijing is cleaning up its financial system, not expanding credit.

Playbook ✓: Stimulus as a drip-feed, with structural rate cuts and a mortgage subsidy instead of a benchmark cut or a fiscal bazooka, is on script, as is the verdict that it secures the target without reflating.  |  ⚠️ Divergence: Transmission to global assets runs through exports, not demand. An AI- and export-led PMI above 50 supports Asian tech supply chains, but with credit missing, property investment falling and August imports below estimates (CNBC, [Sep 7]), Chinese demand gives iron ore and the AUD no property-led bid; the Australian dollar struggled despite the solid PMIs (FXStreet, [Sep 30]) as the RBA pushed consumer confidence to a 30-year low. European luxury and auto exporters face a weak Chinese consumer, with Nike's China sales down 26% (Retail Gazette, [Oct 2]), and now a trade fight. The fuel-export halt is a supply shock to oil, not a demand signal, and EM Asia competes with Chinese exports rather than selling into Chinese demand.
