🇨🇳 China  Mixed  → easing bias, limited signal

Macro Stance: The easing bias is now being expressed through the fixing. The PBOC set the USD/CNY reference rate at 6.7828 against an estimate of 6.7344 (investingLive, [Aug 30]), a clear lean against a yuan that had reached a three-year high (UA.NEWS, [Aug 27]); Global Times argues the yuan should not be the scapegoat for the West's waning competitiveness (Global Times, [Aug 30]) after the WSJ called for a forced revaluation (WSJ, [Aug 28]). Liquidity stays ample, with overnight reverse repos resumed ahead of month-end (Gulf Times, [Aug 29]) after a 500 billion yuan MLF operation (BigGo Finance, [Aug 24]). July CPI slowed to 0.5% and PPI to 3.5%, both missing expectations (BigGo Finance, [Aug 8]): price pressure is too weak to justify a strong currency.

PMIs & Activity: The August PMI is the first better print in months. The official manufacturing gauge rebounded to 49.8 with the new orders index back in expansion (BigGo Finance, [Aug 30]), contracting less than expected (CNBC, [Aug 30]) after July's 49.2 (FXStreet, [Jul 31]). The headlines split on emphasis: the WSJ sees the official gauges hinting at an impending turnaround (WSJ, [Aug 30]), AP stresses contraction despite an uptick in export demand (AP News, [Aug 30]), and Reuters says the factory slump eased but weak services signal an uneven recovery (Reuters, [Aug 30]). Reconciled: the level is still below 50 for a second month, the direction and orders have turned, and the improvement is external rather than domestic. July industrial profits rose 17.6% in January-July (Fibre2Fashion, [Aug 29]) at a seven-month low in growth (Anadolu Ajansı, [Aug 27]), and auto sales slumped 22% (GlobalChinaEV, [Aug 27]).

Credit Impulse: July bank loans posted a record contraction as credit demand faltered (Reuters, [Aug 14]), yet credit growth exceeded forecasts despite the rare loan slump (Bloomberg, [Aug 14]); reconciled, the broad aggregate holds up while the loan book shrinks. Lenders are thriving regardless: the largest five banks reported their strongest first-half profit since the height of the property crash (Reuters, [Aug 28]) and bank stocks surged on strong earnings (BigGo Finance, [Aug 30]), though Everbright's net profit fell 24% on heavy write-offs (Moomoo, [Aug 28]). Beijing is ramping up credit support in its overhaul of the property market (The Business Times, [Aug 29]), the most direct attempt yet to restart household borrowing.

Property: China moved to curb housing presales to shore up confidence (Reuters, [Aug 28]), raised the presale threshold under a new national standard (Caixin Global, [Aug 29]) and extended mortgage terms to 40 years (www.briefasia.com, [Aug 30]). Equity reaction diverged and both readings hold: property stocks surged limit-up on the mortgage change, then slid as the presale overhaul threatens developers' cash flow (BigGo Finance, [Aug 30]), a buyer-positive, developer-negative reform. Fierce land bidding has returned to parts of China, though whether the market has hit bottom is open (Nikkei Asia, [Aug 30]); Vanke's first-half loss widened as sales slumped (Caixin Global, [Aug 29]) and a Reuters poll sees the investment slump deepening (Reuters, [Aug 27]).

Fiscal, Policy & Trade: US-China economic diplomacy is active, not on ice, but it is adversarial at the G20 the US is hosting: Bessent says G20 countries should consider more trade barriers on China to cut imbalances (Yahoo Finance, [Aug 30]) and presses them to confront China's $1.2 trillion trade surplus (investingLive, [Aug 30]), while China rejects unilateral tariffs and urges dialogue (China Daily Global Edition, [Aug 30]). Bilaterally the truce is likely to be extended by one year, with Xi to visit Washington (BigGo Finance, [Aug 28]), even as China opposes the 7.5% tariff plan (Yeni Şafak English, [Aug 27]). At home fiscal policy is extracting: a local surtax of 11%–13% has entered legislation (BigGo Finance, [Aug 28]) and The Economist argues Beijing is tightening when it should loosen (The Economist, [Aug 27]).

Markets & Flows: The Hang Seng edged up 0.07% as Tencent and materials lifted the market (BusinessToday Malaysia, [Aug 29]) after mainland property shares offset biotech and chip losses (The Economic Times, [Aug 28]); bank stocks are the leadership on strong earnings, and Morgan Stanley pivots to A-shares while seeing Hong Kong regain momentum in late September (BigGo Finance, [Aug 27]). China's gold stash is surging (News.com.au, [Aug 29]).

New / Most Important: The August PMI rebounded to 49.8 with new orders back above 50, the first signal in the growth pulse that July's contraction is not deepening. It lands with the property package, so both the demand and the confidence legs have turned at the margin. The catch is the source: export orders, which is exactly the channel Bessent is asking the G20 to wall off.

Playbook ✓: China remains a policy-easing outlier in a global hiking environment, managing the yuan, reforming property and easing via liquidity rather than rates.  |  ⚠️ Divergence: The data turned just as the trade climate hardened: an export-led PMI rebound against a G20 push for barriers on a $1.2 trillion surplus. The transmission is two-sided. Better new orders and a property package support iron ore demand expectations and the AUD at the margin, but a recovery led by exports means more Chinese competition for European exporters already facing tougher EU and German lines, and weak services keep China's import demand soft for EM. WTI at 85.59 on the Iran strikes is a terms-of-trade tax that eats into the margin gains the PMI implies.
