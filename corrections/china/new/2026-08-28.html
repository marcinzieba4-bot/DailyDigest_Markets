🇨🇳 China  Mixed  → easing bias

Macro Stance: The easing bias runs through liquidity and a managed currency, not rates. The PBOC held the LPRs for a 15th month while expanding consumer and small-business loan subsidies (The Asian Banker, [Aug 21]), ran a 500 billion yuan MLF operation (BigGo Finance, [Aug 24]) and keeps conducting overnight reverse repos (Fibre2Fashion, [Aug 26]). The yuan is the pressure point: it reached a three-year high against the dollar (UA.NEWS, [Aug 27]) and edged higher in a tight range ahead of Jackson Hole (Business Recorder, [Aug 27]), with the PBOC cautious on gains despite dollar weakness (SCMP, [Aug 27]) after a weaker fix signalled tolerance for depreciation (VT Markets, [Aug 25]): the PBOC is applying the brakes, not reversing the yuan (Exchange Rates UK, [Aug 27]). July CPI slowed to 0.5%, more than expected (BigGo Finance, [Aug 9]), and PPI growth to 3.5%, missing expectations (BigGo Finance, [Aug 8]).

PMIs & Activity: The pulse stays below 50 into the August PMIs. July's official manufacturing PMI fell to 49.2 and non-manufacturing to 49.0 (FXStreet, [Jul 31]), the private factory index eased to 50.9 (Fibre2Fashion, [Aug 3]), and a Reuters poll sees factory activity contracting again in August (Reuters, [Aug 27]). July industrial output grew 4.5% y/y and retail sales only 0.6% (Reuters, [Aug 17]); exports jumped almost a quarter (Financial Times, [Aug 6]). Industrial profits rose 17.6% in January-July (Xinhua, [Aug 27]), growth hitting a seven-month low as the AI boom fails to mask weak demand (BigGo Finance, [Aug 27]), while semiconductor profits soared 18.5-fold (Global Times, [Aug 27]). Domestic demand is weakest in autos, where sales slumped 22% even as NEV penetration hit 64.3% (GlobalChinaEV, [Aug 27]).

Credit Impulse: July bank loans posted a record contraction as credit demand faltered (Reuters, [Aug 14]), yet credit growth exceeded forecasts despite the rare loan slump (Bloomberg, [Aug 14]). Reconciled: the broad financing aggregate is holding up while the loan book shrank, so the impulse comes from outside bank lending. Policy is targeting rather than expanding credit: the subsidized loan program was expanded to revive demand (Caixin Global, [Aug 21]) and banks are launching "token loans" to fund AI firms (Caixin Global, [Aug 26]), while online lender FinVolution reports China funding tightening (Investing.com, [Aug 27]).

Property: July new home prices were stagnant as demand stayed weak (Reuters, [Aug 17]); the 70-city annual decline narrowed to 3.2% while property investment slumped nearly 20% (BigGo Finance, [Aug 17]). A Reuters poll sees home prices falling slightly less this year but the investment slump deepening (Reuters, [Aug 27]), which reconciles China Daily's signs of rebound (China Daily Global Edition, [Aug 27]) with the developer data. Vanke posted a $2.2 billion loss as debt pressure mounts (Bloomberg, [Aug 27]), an H1 net loss of RMB 14.95B with revenue down 33% and a net debt ratio of 135.4% (TradingView, [Aug 27]), and its losses widen even as cities roll out new property measures (Nikkei Asia, [Aug 27]). Provident fund rules are being reshaped (China.org.cn, [Aug 21]) and liquidators can pursue PwC globally over Evergrande (Financial Times, [Aug 26]).

Fiscal, Policy & Trade: The vice-premier calls for proactive fiscal policy (China Daily, [Aug 27]) and signals fiscal reform to ease pressure on local governments (SCMP, [Aug 27]); S&P affirmed China's A+ rating and sees growth above 4% as fiscal support continues (TradingView, [Aug 27]). The Economist argues China should be loosening budgetary policy but is doing the opposite (The Economist, [Aug 27]), with 7.7 bln yuan of overdue tax demanded from over 100 listed firms (The Standard, [Aug 26]) and the $119 billion financing tool facing a roll-out lag (Reuters, [Aug 24]). On trade, China opposes the US plan for 7.5% tariffs and warns of countermeasures (Yeni Şafak English, [Aug 27]) as it weighs a response (Bloomberg, [Aug 27]), while its foreign minister calls on both sides to overcome obstacles before the summit (The Washington Post, [Aug 27]); a Dutch think-tank pushes the EU for tougher tariffs (EUobserver, [Aug 27]).

Markets & Flows: Mainland stocks ride the AI tape while Hong Kong lags: China stocks climbed on the Nvidia-led AI hardware rally (TradingView, [Aug 26]), but the Hang Seng fell to 25,565.74 as Nvidia euphoria faded (BBN Times, [Aug 27]) after a US power grid ban jolted Hong Kong stocks (BigGo Finance, [Aug 27]). Morgan Stanley pivots to A-shares and sees Hong Kong regaining momentum later in September (BigGo Finance, [Aug 27]).

New / Most Important: Vanke's results and the August PMI poll point the same way. Vanke lost RMB 14.95B in the half with net gearing at 135.4%, even as cities add property measures, and economists expect the factory PMI to stay in contraction. The credit and property legs of the growth pulse are still weakening, so the next easing step depends on fiscal policy that The Economist says is tightening.

Playbook ✓: Targeted easing, a managed yuan, developer stress and AI-led mainland equities are on script.  |  ⚠️ Divergence: A three-year high in the yuan alongside a sub-50 PMI and a deepening property investment slump is not a recovery signal; it is the dollar's weakness meeting a demand-starved economy. Transmission runs through property and steel: with Vanke stressed and the investment slump deepening, iron ore rose on stimulus signals (TradingView, [Aug 24]) only after weakening despite them (TradingView, [Aug 23]), leaving the AUD exposed; European exporters face weak Chinese demand and a tougher EU line, and WTI at $83.51 is a terms-of-trade tax on Chinese margins.
