🇨🇳 China  Mixed  → easing bias

Macro Stance: Monetary policy has handed the baton to the budget. China kept loan prime rates steady for a 15th month (Reuters, [Aug 19]), the 1-year at 3.0% and the 5-year at 3.5% (BigGo Finance, [Aug 19]), and liquidity runs through the overnight window, with 7-day reverse repos at zero for seven straight sessions (BigGo Finance, [Aug 19]). The yuan is the pressure point. It surged to 6.72 per dollar, a 42-month high (BigGo Finance, [Aug 20]), while the PBOC fixed the reference rate at 6.7808, weaker than the previous fix (CryptoRank, [Aug 20]) and well above the 6.7262 Reuters estimate (investingLive, [Aug 20]): a deliberate brake that follows its move to slow yuan gains (Bloomberg, [Aug 19]). Prices leave room to ease: July CPI rose 0.5% and PPI growth slowed to 3.5%, both missing expectations (BigGo Finance, [Aug 8]).

PMIs & Activity: July's official manufacturing PMI fell to 49.2 and non-manufacturing to 49.0 (FXStreet, [Jul 31]); the private factory gauge eased to 50.9 (Fibre2Fashion, [Aug 3]), a four-month low (Reuters, [Aug 2]), and private services growth was the slowest since September 2024 (Reuters, [Aug 4]). The hard data confirmed the slide: industrial output grew 4.5% y/y and retail sales just 0.6% (Reuters, [Aug 17]), output, retail sales and investment all missed forecasts (The Business Times, [Aug 17]), and fixed-asset investment fell 6.7% in the first seven months (Xinhua, [Aug 17]). Exports remain the engine, up 23.9% on high-tech shipments (The Asian Banker, [Aug 14]). The regional August flash readings split: Japan's manufacturing PMI jumped to 55.1 on chip and AI orders (BigGo Finance, [Aug 20]) while Australia's composite slipped from a six-month high (TradingView, [Aug 20]), a pattern that rewards China's tech supply chain, not its domestic demand.

Credit Impulse: July new bank loans posted a record contraction as credit demand faltered (Reuters, [Aug 14]), only the second contraction this year (The Standard (HK), [Aug 14]), yet Bloomberg reports credit growth exceeded forecasts despite the rare loan slump ([Aug 14]). Reconciled: headline financing, which includes bond issuance, beat; bank lending to households and firms shrank, leaving cumulative yuan loans up 10.38 trillion yuan in January–July (Xinhua, [Aug 14]). The new interest subsidies target exactly that gap by cutting the effective cost of SME and consumer borrowing without touching the LPR. The bond market reads the impulse as weak: Chinese bonds bucked the global selloff (CNBC TV18, [Aug 19]) with the 10-year near a one-year low (TradingView, [Aug 19]).

Property: July new home prices were stagnant as demand stayed weak (Reuters, [Aug 17]) and first-tier prices were flat, ending a four-month rebound (South China Morning Post, [Aug 17]). Bloomberg says prices fell at a faster pace ([Aug 17]), while the 70-city index fell 3.2% y/y, the smallest drop in five months (BigGo Finance, [Aug 17]): the annual comparison eased as the monthly decline deepened. Property investment slumped nearly 20% (BigGo Finance, [Aug 17]) and land sale revenue posted its biggest drop in a decade (finance.biggo.com, [Aug 18]). The response is city by city: Shanghai started a new round of property easing (Bloomberg, [Aug 19]) and is lowering second-home down payments (South China Morning Post, [Aug 20]). Evergrande's founder was jailed for life and the firm fined $2.4 billion (Asia Financial, [Aug 20]).

Fiscal, Policy & Trade: China pledged timely fiscal support to bolster growth (Reuters, [Aug 20]), the vice finance minister promising more policy support in a timely way (TradingView, [Aug 20]), and widened fiscal interest subsidies, raising the SME loan cap to 75 million yuan and covering credit-card instalments for the first time (BigGo Finance, [Aug 20]). Trade is fragmenting along bilateral lines: China urged the US to immediately withdraw its drone tariffs (Reuters, [Aug 20]), agreed a deal with Switzerland scrapping most levies (Bloomberg, [Aug 20]), and wields new legal powers against the EU as talks near (Bloomberg, [Aug 20]), while rare-earth exports critical to US aerospace rose (Reuters, [Aug 20]).

Markets & Flows: The Hang Seng climbed 0.80% to 25,698 (BBN Times, [Aug 20]) but A-shares fell across the board with the Shanghai Composite down 0.32% (BigGo Finance, [Aug 20]) after ChiNext tumbled 6.26% (BigGo Finance, [Aug 19]), leaving stocks headed for weekly losses (TradingView, [Aug 20]). Mainland capital pulled a net HK$10.4 billion from Hong Kong stocks (BigGo Finance, [Aug 20]), and materials names are emerging as new market leaders (Bloomberg, [Aug 20]). Beijing debuted offshore government bond futures to promote yuan use (Nikkei Asia, [Aug 20]) as China cut US Treasury holdings to an 18-year low (Benzinga, [Aug 18]).

New / Most Important: The fiscal pledge is the answer the LPR did not give. By subsidising SME and consumer interest through the budget, Beijing lowers the cost of credit where demand is weakest while sparing bank margins and avoiding a rate cut that would fight its own brake on a yuan at a 42-month high. It is targeted support, not a package, and July's loan contraction says demand rather than price is the binding constraint.

Playbook ✓: Rates on hold, fiscal subsidies, city-level property easing and a managed yuan are on script.  |  ⚠️ Divergence: Markets expected July's miss to force rate cuts; Beijing answered with subsidies and a firmer yuan, so China exports strength and imports weakness. Iron ore rose only as China demand concerns met stimulus hopes (TradingView, [Aug 18]) and Fortescue still hopes for a swift return to normal China trade conditions (Reuters, [Aug 19]); the Australian dollar weakened as PMIs failed to impress (Mitrade, [Aug 20]). Europe is turning confrontational, with Germany's finance minister saying China is "not playing by the rules" (Bloomberg, [Aug 20]), as European exporters face a buyer that takes less and sells more ahead of the euro-area flash PMIs. Oil at $86.91, down 2.7% in four weeks, is a smaller import tax but no demand signal.
