🇨🇳 China  Mixed  → easing bias

Macro Stance: China maintains an easing bias, but the PBOC is using words and the fixing rather than rates. It vowed flexibility amid an uneven economic picture (SCMP, [Jul 9]), flagged "structural divergences" as the AI boom masks consumer weakness (BigGo Finance, [Jul 9]) and pledged a more targeted approach (China Daily, [Jul 9]). The yuan is the active lever: the central rate was set at 6.7804 (investingLive, [Jul 9]), the strongest fix in three years (Business Recorder, [Jul 9]), and Goldman says the yuan is defying dollar strength (Exchange Rates UK, [Jul 9]). June prices argue for easing: CPI rose 1% (Global Times, [Jul 9]) as inflation cooled with oil (Caixin, [Jul 9]) while producer inflation hit a near 4-year high (KLSE Screener, [Jul 9]), a widening price "scissors gap" that points to weak domestic demand (BigGo Finance, [Jul 9]); the Straits Times sees reflation peaking as the Iran shock fades ([Jul 9]).

PMIs & Activity: The June PMIs — manufacturing at 50.3, non-manufacturing at 50.2 (Reuters, [Jun 29]) — describe an export- and tech-led economy, and June autos make the split stark: passenger car exports rose 80% while sales dropped at home (AP News, [Jul 9]), NEV exports topped 500,000 units for the first time (CnEVPost, [Jul 9]), and BMW and Volkswagen sales declined on a worsening slump in China (Yahoo Finance Singapore, [Jul 9]). May retail sales fell 0.6% (Il Sole 24 Ore, [Jun 15]) and fixed-asset investment was down 4.1% in the first five months (Macau Business, [Jun 16]). The IMF projected sluggish 3% global growth (Reuters, [Jul 8]) but upgraded China's 2026 forecast to 4.6% (Global Times, [Jul 8]): exports, not domestic demand, are doing the work.

Credit Impulse: May total social financing grew 7.7% (CGTN, [Jun 12]) and topped forecasts on government bonds (Bloomberg, [Jun 12]) while new loans missed (Reuters, [Jun 12]); the June print is next. Regulators are culling rather than adding: China curbed short-term bond sales by LGFVs (The Standard, [Jul 9]) and is cracking down on ratings inflation, with up to 494.6 billion yuan of bonds at downgrade risk (BigGo Finance, [Jul 8]). Bloomberg says the debt-cleanup milestone gives Beijing a chance at a fiscal rethink ([Jul 9]), and easier credit for gig workers is under consideration (chinadailyhk, [Jul 9]) — targeted, not broad. Seven of the world's 10 biggest banks are now Chinese (SCMP, [Jul 9]): scale without loan demand.

Property: Fitch expects new home sales to fall 11% to 13% in 2026 (Mingtiandi, [Jul 9]), after resale prices fell faster in June (Reuters, [Jun 30]), new home prices fell faster in May (Reuters, [Jun 15]) and real-estate investment dropped 16.2% y/y in January–May (GMK Center, [Jun 18]). Bloomberg sees demand for Soviet-style apartments showing the limits to the revival ([Jul 8]), and land finance is distorting local budgets: one city "sold" the same plot 18 times to inflate fiscal revenue (SCMP, [Jul 8]). In Hong Kong, HSBC is seeking buyers for risky property loans (Financial Times, [Jul 9]) as banks escalate a mortgage war with rebates of up to 1.5% (The Standard, [Jul 9]).

Fiscal, Policy & Trade: Beijing is planning supply and services rather than a demand package: a carbon-peaking plan for the 15th Five-Year Plan (Xinhua, [Jul 9]), NEVs at 30% of the car fleet by 2030 (CnEVPost, [Jul 9]) and a five-year road map to shield physical stores (SCMP, [Jul 9]). Volkswagen's decision to scrap half its product lineup under pressure from Chinese EVs underscores the threat China's industrial base poses to European incumbents, and Brussels is imposing duties of up to 45% on Chinese tyres (Eunews, [Jul 9]). With Washington the thaw continues: the SCMP asks whether China's biggest daily US soybean order confirms the Xi-Trump summit pact ([Jul 9]), and China may let top AI firms buy limited Nvidia H200 chips (Reuters, [Jul 9]).

Markets & Flows: BlackRock's strategist publicly endorsed China's growing appeal as a portfolio diversifier this week. Prices show a pause: the Hang Seng fell 169 points to 24,030 even as Zhipu AI rose 11% (The Standard, [Jul 9]) and Invezz says the rally has stalled ([Jul 9]), while onshore stocks extended gains (TradingView, [Jul 9]) and US-listed Chinese stocks surged on a policy boost (Moomoo, [Jul 9]). The PBOC bought 15 tons of gold in June (Nikkei Asia, [Jul 9]).

New / Most Important: The BlackRock diversification call is the key signal — institutional capital is beginning to reframe China not as a growth story but as a correlation play. The newest data fit that framing: the strongest fix in three years arrived alongside an 80% jump in car exports and falling domestic sales, the profile of an economy that grows by exporting while its currency is managed firmer. Low inflation, a firm yuan and a central bank promising flexibility make China a diversifier precisely because its rates and prices do not move with the US cycle.

Playbook ✓: Stimulus-lite approach continues; no bazooka — targeted credit, a firmer fix and plans instead of a package.  |  ⚠️ Divergence: BlackRock publicly making the diversification case for China is structurally significant: for years institutional flows left China on geopolitical de-risking, and the largest asset manager recommending re-allocation sets a floor under Chinese assets regardless of the macro data. The macro data are the catch. Growth comes from exports while property sales are forecast to keep falling, so the transmission to global assets is competition, not demand: European exporters feel it directly through Volkswagen's lineup cut, falling German car sales in China and new EU duties; iron ore and AUD get little from a construction sector still shrinking; EM bonds gain from goods disinflation and a firmer yuan. Watch Hong Kong-listed ETFs and A-share flows for confirmation, and the June credit print for any domestic turn.
