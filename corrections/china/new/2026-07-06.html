🇨🇳 China  Mixed  → Easing bias

Macro Stance: Easing is selective and funding-led. The PBOC kept overnight reverse repos at 1.25% while doubling the injection (Reuters, [Jun 29]), then signalled easing with a trillion-yuan outright reverse repo, ending a three-month tapering streak (BigGo Finance, [Jul 3]); it cut bond purchases on wariness over falling yields (Bloomberg, [Jul 2]), leaving net bond operations at only 10 billion yuan in June (Macau Business, [Jul 3]). Liquidity comes through repo, not outright bond buying, so the PBOC eases funding without validating the deflationary bond rally. The fix leans against weakness: USD/CNY was set at 6.8047 against 6.8088 (TMGM, [Jul 3]), firmer than estimates, and HSBC says structural forces still support the RMB (Exchange Rates UK, [Jul 4]). May CPI rose only 1.2% (China Daily, [Jun 10]) while factory-gate inflation hit a near four-year high (Reuters, [Jun 9]).

PMIs & Activity: Two speeds. The official manufacturing PMI rose to 50.3 and non-manufacturing to 50.2 (Reuters, [Jun 29]); the private factory gauge grew for a seventh month (China Daily, [Jul 2]) but less than expected (Bloomberg, [Jun 30]), and services slowed slightly (Reuters, [Jul 2]), by less than forecast (The Business Times, [Jul 3]). The tech side is booming: memory chip companies posted surging half-year earnings (Global Times, [Jul 5]) and robot exports hit nearly 20 bln yuan in January–May (Global Times, [Jul 5]). The consumer side is not: NEV retail sales fell 7% in June (CnEVPost, [Jul 3]) as the subsidy pullback bites (Nikkei Asia, [Jul 2]). In May, output growth quickened to 4.5% (China Daily, [Jun 16]) while retail sales fell 0.6% (Il Sole 24 Ore, [Jun 15]).

Credit Impulse: Total social financing grew 7.7% in May (CGTN, [Jun 12]) and topped forecasts (Bloomberg, [Jun 12]), but new loans missed as the property slump curbed demand (Reuters, [Jun 12]): government bonds, not private demand. Goldman's verdict is blunt: Chinese households are lying flat and no longer taking out loans (Binance, [Jul 5]). Stress sits at the edges: China took over private lender Zhongbang Bank, citing credit risks (The Standard, [Jul 3]), and Beijing is capping LGFV bond yields (IFR, [Jul 2]) while Guizhou's infrastructure boom runs into a wall of debt (Le Monde, [Jul 4]).

Property: Apollo says China's real-estate slump is entering its fifth year [Jul 5]. Asia Times describes a market in free fall as buyers wait for floor prices [Jul 3], consistent with resale prices falling faster in June (Reuters, [Jun 30]). Sales are concentrating, not recovering: top-tier cities overtook regional hubs (Caixin, [Jul 3]). Real-estate investment fell 16.2% y/y in January–May (GMK Center, [Jun 18]). Resolution is state-led: Vanke named a former Shenzhen financial regulator as president (Caixin, [Jul 3]) and Country Garden cleared its first offshore coupon (Caixin, [Jul 2]).

Fiscal, Policy & Trade: Support is receding at the margin: vehicle and vessel tax breaks for NEVs are ending (China Daily HK, [Jul 4]) and a tax crackdown is hitting listed firms (SCMP, [Jul 2]). Control is rising, with curbs on foreign investments (Times of India, [Jul 1]) and a landmark law over offshore tech transfers (SCMP, [Jul 1]). Iran's envoy says Beijing will get Hormuz concessions (Bloomberg, [Jul 4]). On trade, Beijing signals openness to cut the EU deficit (SCMP, [Jul 3]) while von der Leyen threatens retaliation if October is missed (Euronews, [Jul 3]); Breakingviews argues the EU can stand up to China if it hangs together (Reuters, [Jul 5]). With the US, farm tariff cuts are in train (Bloomberg, [Jul 2]) and US beef exports to China are set to pick up (Bloomberg, [Jul 5]).

Markets & Flows: Hong Kong stocks rose as the PMI improved (TradingView, [Jul 5]), with the Hang Seng up 1.28% on Friday to cap an uneven week (BusinessToday Malaysia, [Jul 5]); Macquarie says it is the best time to buy Chinese AI chip stocks (CNBC, [Jul 5]). CICC calls Hong Kong at an "intermittent low", with Hang Seng Tech down over 33%, cheap but lacking a policy catalyst (BigGo Finance, [Jul 5]). The state is stepping back: China's biggest ETF is now a gold fund as the national team retreats (Bloomberg, [Jul 5]), with retail gold trading being shut down (TipRanks, [Jul 1]).

New / Most Important: The national team's retreat is the week's signal. With China's largest ETF now a gold fund and Goldman finding households unwilling to borrow, the two pillars of a broad equity recovery, state buying and household credit, are both absent. What is left is the divergent market SCMP describes: chip and robot exporters with surging earnings on one track, property and consumers in balance-sheet repair on the other.

Playbook ✓: Funding-led easing, a managed yuan, falling home prices and receding subsidies are consensus.  |  ⚠️ Divergence: The two-track market is structural: SOE and tech stocks benefit from directed capital and self-sufficiency mandates while the private sector and property stay in balance-sheet recession. Broad China exposure (FXI, KWEB) is a poor expression; sub-sector picks are required, because capital is flowing into state-directed AI and semis while exiting consumer and property, a Japan-1990s-style sectoral divergence rather than a cyclical turn. The global transmission follows the same split. Iron ore rose on the Fortescue curbs and as the slump spurred buying (Bloomberg, [Jul 5]), a supply story, not a construction recovery, so AUD gets no durable terms-of-trade support with real-estate investment down 16.2%. European industry faces the export track head-on. And with WTI down 26.6% in four weeks and Beijing securing Hormuz concessions, China imports cheap energy and exports disinflation.
