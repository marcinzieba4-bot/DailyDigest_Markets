🇨🇳 China  Mixed  → accommodative but constrained

Macro Stance: The PBOC leans against yuan strength through the fixing, not rates. It set the USD/CNY mid-point at 6.7709 against an estimate of 6.7042 (Newsquawk, [Sep 9]) after the yuan hit a fresh three-and-a-half-year high despite weak guidance (The Edge Malaysia, [Sep 7]); later fixings were firmer but still kept the yuan below market estimates to aid exports (VT Markets, [Sep 10]). August CPI rose 0.8% and PPI 3.8%, beating expectations (BigGo Finance, [Sep 8]), but the lift came from energy costs (Reuters, [Sep 9]) and consumer prices are still below target (Free Malaysia Today, [Sep 9]): imported cost-push, not domestic reflation, which leaves the easing bias intact. The central bank also bought 20.2 tonnes of gold, its largest purchase since 2023 (Kitco, [Sep 8]).

PMIs & Activity: The official manufacturing PMI rebounded to 49.8 in August with new orders back in expansion (BigGo Finance, [Aug 30]), while the private gauge rose to 51.5, its longest expansion streak in five years (BigGo Finance, [Aug 31]), and the private services PMI rebounded to 51.4 (BigGo Finance, [Sep 2]). Reconciled: the official level is below 50 for a second month while the private surveys expand, and trade shows why. Exports jumped 25% in August as the surplus widened (AP News, [Sep 7]) toward $806 billion (Bloomberg.com, [Sep 7]), driven by high-tech and AI demand (Reuters, [Sep 8]), but imports missed estimates (CNBC, [Sep 7]). Domestic demand is the weak leg: passenger car sales slumped 24% in August (Caixin Global, [Sep 9]), and the July print showed output up 4.5% and retail sales up only 0.6% (Reuters, [Aug 17]), with fixed-asset investment down 6.7% in the first seven months (Xinhua, [Aug 17]).

Credit Impulse: July bank loans posted a record contraction as credit demand faltered (Reuters, [Aug 14]) even as broad credit growth exceeded forecasts (Bloomberg.com, [Aug 14]); reconciled, government bonds carry the aggregate while households and firms do not borrow. Lenders are acting accordingly, snapping up bonds as the loan slump deepens (Bloomberg.com, [Sep 8]). Beijing's answer is capital rather than demand: 300 billion yuan in special treasury bonds to boost the capital of financial firms (China Daily, [Sep 7]), a $54 billion injection after which bank and insurer stocks still fell (CNBC, [Sep 7]) and which The Economist says falls far short (The Economist, [Sep 8]). More capital raises lending capacity, not loan demand; the next credit print is the test.

Property: The presale overhaul is now being priced through land and developer finance. Goldman sees the new rules cutting land sales by 30% (The Business Times, [Sep 7]) and Caixin reports the shift to completed-home sales cooling the land market (Caixin Global, [Sep 10]). Restructuring channels are narrowing: China halted a key debt-to-equity tool for ailing developers (The Standard, [Sep 9]) and regulators are scrutinising offshore debt-swap bonds (Caixin Global, [Sep 11]). Demand flickers, with Beijing home sales climbing and inventory shrinking in the month after easing (Yicai Global, [Sep 10]). July's 70-city prices fell 3.2% y/y, the smallest drop in five months, while property investment slumped nearly 20% (BigGo Finance, [Aug 17]); Reuters called new home prices stagnant as demand stays weak (Reuters, [Aug 17]).

Fiscal, Policy & Trade: Support targets balance sheets, not households: the recapitalisation sits alongside a 15th Five-Year plan for building a financial powerhouse with nine PBOC action plans (BigGo Finance, [Sep 10]) and local officials were named and shamed over hidden debt (Caixin Global, [Sep 12]). The US track is thawing: China and the US are consulting on reciprocal tariff reductions covering $30 billion (Global Times, [Sep 10]), Beijing hopes to agree at an early date (AP News, [Sep 10]) and bought 1 million tons of US soybeans ahead of Xi's visit (Reuters, [Sep 9]), though Barron's warns the fragile truce could break before the Washington summit (Barron's, [Sep 9]). Europe is hardening: the EU wants initial Chinese action on the trade gap by early October (Reuters, [Sep 8]) and unveiled "buy European" procurement rules (The Guardian, [Sep 9]).

Markets & Flows: Rate-hike fears dominate. Mainland stocks hit a six-week low (TradingView, [Sep 10]), the Hang Seng fell below 25,000 as tech and financials slid (Dimsum Daily, [Sep 10]) and Hong Kong slipped again on rate-hike jitters (BusinessToday Malaysia, [Sep 12]). The China-US 10-year yield gap hit a record 317 basis points (bloomingbit, [Sep 9]), yet the yuan's steady rise is drawing foreign investors back (Caixin Global, [Sep 11]). AI is the exception: Enflame nearly tripled in its Shanghai debut (Reuters, [Sep 11]) and Chinese AI stocks have been on a tear while US investors are largely shut out (Morningstar, [Sep 11]).

New / Most Important: The newest read on the week's data is DBS's: exports support output as property drags (www.tmgm.com, [Sep 12]). August car data make the split concrete, with domestic auto sales down over 20% and exports near 40% of the total as the sole support (BigGo Finance, [Sep 12]). Growth is being exported rather than consumed, which is why Beijing recapitalises banks instead of stimulating households, and why Brussels is pushing back.

Playbook ✓: Accommodative but constrained holds: easing runs through bank capital and the fixing, and a record yield gap with the Fed expected to hike caps rate cuts.  |  ⚠️ Divergence: The growth pulse is two-speed, and it transmits abroad through supply rather than demand. A 25% export surge with imports missing estimates adds Chinese competition for European exporters, whose industry already warns of 300,000 factory job cuts (The Guardian, [Sep 6]). EM and the AUD get little from a domestic economy where car sales fell 24% and land sales face a 30% hit, the latter a direct drag on steel and iron ore demand. WTI at $100.05 is a terms-of-trade tax on a large crude importer, lifting PPI to 3.8% without lifting demand.
