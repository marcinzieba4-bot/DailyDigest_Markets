🇨🇳 China  Mixed  → easing bias

Macro Stance: Loan prime rates were held for a 14th consecutive month (The Business Times, [Jul 20]) and the market eyes a rate-cut window near the end of the third quarter (BigGo Finance, [Jul 21]). For now the PBOC's emphasis is the currency: it sees a stable yuan amid two-way fluctuations (State Council Information Office, [Jul 16]) and is deepening offshore yuan liquidity (Caixin, [Jul 16]); the yuan weakened slightly on the fixing (BusinessToday Malaysia, [Jul 17]) and UOB sees an upside bias below 6.7820 (TMGM, [Jul 20]). The bond market is doing the easing instead: a rally in Chinese bonds shows weak economic sentiment before the Politburo (Bloomberg, [Jul 22]). Prices are the wrong mix: June CPI rose only 1% (chinadailyhk, [Jul 8]) while PPI climbed 4.1% (BigGo Finance, [Jul 8]) to a near four-year high that squeezes manufacturers (Reuters, [Jul 8]).

PMIs & Activity: The surveys and the hard data point in different directions. The June official manufacturing PMI rose to 50.3 and the non-manufacturing PMI to 50.2 (Reuters, [Jun 29]); the private gauge capped the strongest factory quarter since late 2020 (Reuters, [Jun 30]). Yet second-quarter GDP growth weakened to 4.3%, below the official target range (Bloomberg, [Jul 15]). June industrial output rose 5.3% y/y and retail sales returned to growth (BusinessLine, [Jul 15]), but retail sales of consumer goods were up just 1.3% in the first half (Ecns.cn, [Jul 15]), the car market heads for its worst year since 2021 (CNBC, [Jul 20]) and youth joblessness risks rising as labour distress spreads (The Straits Times, [Jul 22]). Chatham House calls it a structural divergence ([Jul 22]): June exports were up 27% y/y (Reuters, [Jul 13]).

Credit Impulse: The aggregate and the household picture diverge. The finance minister cites over 17 trillion yuan in new loans in the first half (BigGo Finance, [Jul 22]), yet June new loans missed forecasts (Bloomberg, [Jul 15]), credit growth undershot as the PBOC flagged a "new normal" (Caixin, [Jul 16]) and household loans decreased by 366.8 billion yuan in the first half (36Kr, [Jul 17]). Reconciled, corporate and state-linked borrowing carries the total while households deleverage, so the impulse is real for state capex and negative for consumption.

Property: Prices are stabilising at the top while the financing base erodes. June new home prices fell at a slower pace, though Reuters doubts a recovery ([Jul 14]), and first-tier prices extended a 4-month rebound (SCMP, [Jul 15]). But property investment slumped 18% y/y in January–June (The Standard, [Jul 15]), land-sales revenue plunged over 30% in the first half (BigGo Finance, [Jul 22]), and Beijing is curbing a loan structure widely used by distressed developers (Bloomberg, [Jul 22]). Vanke survives on its state shareholder, securing a 519 million yuan loan from Shenzhen Metro at 2.29% (BigGo Finance, [Jul 21]), while KKR and AEW seek to sell China property assets at steep losses (Bloomberg, [Jul 20]).

Fiscal, Policy & Trade: China clings to austerity despite a sharper slowdown (Bloomberg, [Jul 22]). Fiscal revenue expanded 4.7% in the first half (Reuters, [Jul 22]), flattered by stamp duty that surged 90% (BigGo Finance, [Jul 22]); the finance minister's roadmap is a 100 billion yuan programme (BigGo Finance, [Jul 22]), consumer trade-ins generated 1.1 trillion yuan of sales (Xinhua, [Jul 22]), local debt is to be trimmed further (China Daily, [Jul 22]) and tax breaks on certain PV cells and battery products will end (PV Tech, [Jul 22]). A CNBC strategist expects better execution, not new fiscal plans ([Jul 22]), as investors eye the July Politburo meeting for clues (SCMP, [Jul 22]). On trade, China braces as new US global tariffs loom (SCMP, [Jul 22]), Rubio eyes trade and investment boards by Xi's visit (Bloomberg, [Jul 22]) and the US and China will hold AI talks in September (Reuters, [Jul 22]).

Markets & Flows: Chinese stocks jumped on buybacks and fund self-purchases (Caixin, [Jul 22]) after record inflows into a tech ETF broadened the rescue (Bloomberg, [Jul 21]), but the rotation is narrow: high-profile funds ditch consumer stocks to chase AI (Bloomberg, [Jul 22]) and Tencent slid the most in over a year in a gaming sell-off (The Straits Times, [Jul 22]). Emerging-market assets rallied on China's tech support (Bloomberg, [Jul 21]), and Beijing vows better panda bond ratings to lure foreign capital (SCMP, [Jul 22]).

New / Most Important: The first-half fiscal data show why the growth pulse is not turning: China clings to austerity even as the economy slows. Revenue is up 4.7% because the equity boom lifted stamp duty, while land-sales revenue, the local-government lifeline, fell by more than 30%. A 100 billion yuan roadmap is small against an 18% fall in property investment, and the bond rally says markets doubt more is coming. The July Politburo meeting is now the test of whether Beijing trades austerity for demand support.

Playbook ✓: China quietly accumulating discounted Iranian/Russian crude as a net beneficiary of lower-cost sanctioned oil, a rate hold and fiscal caution are on script.  |  ⚠️ Divergence: Austerity plus an export-led 50.3 PMI means China keeps supplying the world rather than buying from it. European exporters feel it first: the FT says China's assault on Europe's car market has barely begun ([Jul 22]). Commodity transmission is muted, as crude imports at the lowest in nearly a decade (Bloomberg, [Jul 13]) and an 18% property-investment slump cap demand for oil and construction inputs, a drag on AUD and iron ore that only steel exports offset. With the bond rally pricing weak domestic demand, EM gains from China's tech rescue rest on portfolio flows, not on Chinese import demand.
