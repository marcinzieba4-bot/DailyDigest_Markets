🇨🇳 China  Mixed  → easing bias continues

Macro Stance: The PBOC held rates and keeps easing through liquidity instead. China left loan prime rates steady for a 15th consecutive month in August (Reuters, [Aug 19]), the 1-year at 3.0% and the 5-year at 3.5% (BigGo Finance, [Aug 19]), as Reuters had expected despite economic weakness ([Aug 18]). Liquidity has moved to the overnight tool: after the first mid-month overnight reverse repo boosted bonds (Bloomberg, [Aug 13]), 7-day reverse repos have been at zero for seven straight sessions (BigGo Finance, [Aug 19]), and July corporate loan rates fell below 3% (finance.biggo.com, [Aug 14]). On the currency the bank is leaning against strength, slowing yuan gains with a weaker-than-expected fixing (Bloomberg, [Aug 19]) as the offshore yuan hit its strongest since 2023 (TradingView, [Aug 19]). Prices leave room to ease: July CPI rose 0.5% and PPI growth slowed to 3.5%, both missing expectations (BigGo Finance, [Aug 8]).

PMIs & Activity: July's official manufacturing PMI fell to 49.2 and non-manufacturing to 49.0 (FXStreet, [Jul 31]); the private factory gauge eased to 50.9 (Fibre2Fashion, [Aug 3]), a four-month low (Reuters, [Aug 2]), and private services growth was the slowest since September 2024 (Reuters, [Aug 4]). The hard data confirmed the slide: industrial output grew 4.5% y/y and retail sales just 0.6% (Reuters, [Aug 17]), output, retail sales and investment all missed forecasts (The Business Times, [Aug 17]), and fixed-asset investment fell 6.7% in the first seven months (Xinhua, [Aug 17]). August has started no better, with NEV retail sales down 15% in the first 16 days (CnEVPost, [Aug 19]). Exports remain the engine, up 23.9% on high-tech shipments (The Asian Banker, [Aug 14]).

Credit Impulse: July new bank loans posted a record contraction as credit demand faltered (Reuters, [Aug 14]), only the second contraction this year (The Standard (HK), [Aug 14]), yet Bloomberg reports credit growth exceeded forecasts despite the rare loan slump ([Aug 14]). Reconciled: headline financing, which includes bond issuance, beat; bank lending to households and firms shrank, leaving cumulative yuan loans up 10.38 trillion yuan in January–July (Xinhua, [Aug 14]). An unchanged LPR does nothing for that demand gap. The bond market has priced it: China's bonds bucked the global selloff on a policy divide with peers (CNBC TV18, [Aug 19]) and the 10-year yield lingers near a one-year low (TradingView, [Aug 19]).

Property: July new home prices were stagnant as demand stayed weak (Reuters, [Aug 17]) and first-tier prices were flat, ending a four-month rebound (South China Morning Post, [Aug 17]). Bloomberg says prices fell at a faster pace ([Aug 17]), while the 70-city index fell 3.2% y/y, the smallest drop in five months (BigGo Finance, [Aug 17]): the annual comparison eased as the monthly decline deepened, and Caixin notes the recovery in smaller cities faltered ([Aug 18]). Property investment slumped nearly 20% (BigGo Finance, [Aug 17]) and land sale revenue posted its biggest drop in a decade (finance.biggo.com, [Aug 18]). The response is city by city: Shanghai started a new round of property easing after the economy slowed (Bloomberg, [Aug 19]), following wider use of a $1.6 trillion housing fund (Bloomberg, [Aug 18]). Evergrande founder Hui Ka Yan was sentenced to life (Nation Thailand, [Aug 19]).

Fiscal, Policy & Trade: Premier Li Qiang called for stabilising external demand as growth sputters (Reuters, [Aug 17]), and the State Council urged faster rollout of the "Six Networks" to boost domestic demand (BigGo Finance, [Aug 17]), a programme that could exceed 26 trillion yuan in the 15th Five-Year Plan with power grid and computing power first in line (BigGo Finance, [Aug 18]). Stimulus calls are growing (Bloomberg, [Aug 18]) and Caixin wants the fiscal taps opened ([Aug 18]), but China Daily still favours tailored easing over broad stimulus ([Aug 14]). On trade, the White House says more than 40 nations help China dodge tariffs (The Washington Post, [Aug 13]), Mexico weighs tougher trade rules for China (South China Morning Post, [Aug 19]) and Beijing blocked firms from aiding the EU's JD.com probe (South China Morning Post, [Aug 19]).

Markets & Flows: A-shares plunged on heavy volume, with ChiNext down 6.26% as over 5,000 stocks fell (BigGo Finance, [Aug 19]) and the SSE Composite off 2.4% in a semiconductor rout (BBN Times, [Aug 19]), even as Unitree soared 460% on its IPO (Investor's Business Daily, [Aug 19]); the PBOC had already warned of a stock frenzy (The Standard (HK), [Aug 19]), and stocks rebounded as it stood pat (TradingView, [Aug 19]). Reserves keep rotating: China cut US Treasury holdings to an 18-year low (Benzinga, [Aug 18]) amid strong official gold buying in July (World Gold Council, [Aug 14]).

New / Most Important: The LPR hold is the answer to July's broad miss: no rate cut, liquidity through the overnight window, targeted property easing in Shanghai and a weaker fixing to slow the yuan. Beijing is choosing to protect bank margins and the currency's pace rather than cut the cost of credit, while loans contract and the equity market swings between a ChiNext rout and IPO euphoria.

Playbook ✓: Rates on hold, liquidity over cuts, city-level property easing and a managed yuan are on script.  |  ⚠️ Divergence: The consensus read a policy pivot into July's miss; the LPR says otherwise, so the transmission runs through weak domestic demand and strong exports. Iron ore rose only as China demand concerns met stimulus hopes (TradingView, [Aug 18]) and Fortescue hopes for a swift return to normal China trade conditions (Reuters, [Aug 19]), which keeps AUD heavy despite a hawkish RBA and a renewed China-Australia swap line expanded to $32.7 billion (Global Times, [Aug 19]). European exporters face a China that buys less and sells more, its surpluses putting the EU in a trade bind (Reuters, [Aug 17]), and falling oil, WTI down 6.6% in four weeks, eases the import bill without lifting demand.
