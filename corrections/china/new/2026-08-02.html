🇨🇳 China  Mixed  → easing bias

Macro Stance: Policy rates are on hold, the easing bias is explicit, and the currency has become the constraint. The PBOC set the overnight reverse repo rate steady at 1.25% (Reuters, [Jul 28]) and maintained liquidity support before month-end (TradingView, [Jul 28]); one read of the Politburo is that RRR and rate cuts are likely in Q3 (BigGo Finance, [Jul 30]). The yuan hit a three-year high despite the PBOC signalling discomfort over the gains (The Edge Malaysia, [Jul 31]), and the central bank is leaning against the rally with a stable daily fix (The Business Times, [Jul 31]). June CPI rose only 1% (Global Times, [Jul 9]) while PPI climbed 4.1% (finance.biggo.com, [Jul 8]), a margin squeeze rather than demand-pull reflation.

PMIs & Activity: The official manufacturing PMI dropped to 49.2 (The State Council Information Office, [Jul 31]) and the non-manufacturing PMI to 49 (Macau Business, [Jul 31]), as factories and construction slumped together (The Edge Malaysia, [Jul 31]) and services returned to contraction (TradingView, [Jul 31]). Against June's 50.3 (Reuters, [Jun 29]) this is a clean break, which CNBC ties to a demand slump and typhoons ([Jul 30]). Exports are no longer a reliable offset, with US-bound shipments falling in July after a brief recovery (CNBC, [Jul 30]). Q2 GDP grew 4.3%, below the target range (Bloomberg, [Jul 15]), and first-half industrial profits rose 18.7% as a tech boom masks the property slump (Caixin Global, [Jul 27]). The consumer is the weak leg: Nike's China sales have fallen 30% (CNBC, [Jul 29]) and BMW's profit fell as the China slump deepened (Investing.com, [Jul 30]).

Credit Impulse: June new bank loans rose less than expected (The Star, [Jul 15]) and credit growth missed forecasts as the PBOC flagged a "new normal" (Caixin Global, [Jul 16]); household loans fell by 366.8 billion yuan in the first half (36Kr, [Jul 17]). Cash at Chinese banks has swelled to a record as borrowing slows (Nikkei Asia, [Jul 28]): liquidity is ample, credit demand is not. The biggest banks have launched their first repo-linked loans (Yahoo Finance, [Jul 27]), but a dip in the debt ratio may not bode well for the economy (South China Morning Post, [Jul 31]), even as the local government debt clean-up makes progress (ISI Markets, [Aug 1]). The impulse reads flat, with households negative.

Property: June new home prices fell at a slower pace (Reuters, [Jul 14]) and first-tier prices extended a four-month rebound (South China Morning Post, [Jul 15]), but resale prices fell faster (Reuters, [Jun 30]) and property investment slumped 18 percent y/y in January–June (The Standard (HK), [Jul 15]). The demand base is eroding: Chinese families are walking away from homes and into portfolios (South China Morning Post, [Aug 1]), and July was China's worst building month since Covid (Wood Central, [Aug 1]). Vanke completed a board reshuffle as Xu Enli takes the helm for a turnaround battle (finance.biggo.com, [Jul 31]), and in Hong Kong builder Parkview missed an interest payment on a loan (The Business Times, [Jul 30]).

Fiscal, Policy & Trade: The Politburo read has settled on incremental policy support (Caixin Global, [Jul 31]), a fiscal push without big stimulus (VT Markets, [Jul 31]). Leaders pledged to support the economy without big new stimulus plans (Reuters, [Jul 30]), while Bloomberg reports top officials will consider new stimulus after the slowdown ([Jul 30]). Reconciled: incremental measures now, a larger package only if the data worsen, and July's PMIs are the first test. On trade, the US urged China to "fully meet" its rare-earths pledge (Nikkei Asia, [Jul 31]), China voiced "serious concern" over new US curbs (South China Morning Post, [Jul 31]) and a Trump-Xi September summit is taking shape (Firstpost, [Jul 31]); China says the US pledged to cap replacement tariffs at 20% (Bloomberg, [Jul 27]).

Markets & Flows: Chinese stocks are on track for their worst month in a decade (Financial Times, [Jul 30]) as chip stocks slump on a rotation away from tech (Bloomberg, [Jul 30]); AI IPOs have failed to lift the market (International Business Times, [Jul 31]), though the Politburo bolstered hope the state will support stocks (South China Morning Post, [Jul 31]). The PBOC is set to further boost panda bond issuance (China Daily, [Jul 31]), China completed its first Malaysia payment in digital yuan (South China Morning Post, [Aug 1]), and central banks led by China drove record Q2 gold demand (TipRanks, [Jul 30]).

New / Most Important: The construction leg is now the story. The non-manufacturing PMI at 49 captures what Wood Central calls the worst building month since Covid, and SCMP describes households shifting savings from homes to portfolios: property is losing its buyers, not only its builders. Leaders committed only to incremental support, so the lever is monetary, with RRR and rate cuts the base case, but a yuan at a three-year high tightens conditions for exporters as US-bound shipments roll over. BYD's overseas push on flash-charging technology shows where growth still is: abroad.

Playbook ✓: Incremental Politburo support, liquidity easing, weak property and a managed trade truce heading into a September summit are on script.  |  ⚠️ Divergence: The consensus treated the Politburo as the turning point; instead July's PMIs show contraction broadening to construction and services, with property investment down 18 percent. Iron ore eased as faltering China demand offset stimulus hopes (Business Recorder, [Jul 28]) and NZD/USD slipped to 0.5860 on weak China PMIs (VT Markets, [Jul 31]); the Australian dollar held firm as the PMI slipped and RBA hike bets faded (FXStreet, [Jul 31]). European carmakers absorb a deepening China slump, EM equity flows suffer as Chinese stocks head for their worst month in a decade, and oil at $84.67 stays a terms-of-trade tax on China's margins.
