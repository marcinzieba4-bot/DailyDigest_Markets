🧮 Earnings Pattern — What the Market Pays For

Rewarded: Forward visibility — a raise, a backlog or scarce supply. Micron beat and its shares spiked 15% on a record $41.5 billion quarter (Forbes, [Jun 24]); AeroVironment soared 28% on a beat as its backlog grew to $1.2 billion (CNBC, [Jun 29]); BlackBerry climbed 20% on an upbeat outlook (TradingView, [Jun 26]) as QNX powered growth (BNN Bloomberg, [Jun 25]); Halfords rose 13% as profit beat and margins hit a decade high (Yahoo Finance UK, [Jun 25]). Constellation is even rising despite a revenue decline (Barron's, [Jun 30]).

Punished: Beats paired with weaker guidance or rising costs. Accenture tumbled 16% on a guidance cut (Yahoo Finance, [Jun 18]); Carnival dipped 5% on weaker Q3 guidance despite the beat (TIKR.com, [Jun 24]); FedEx dipped 6% despite its beat (TIKR.com, [Jun 24]); Darden fell as Olive Garden weakness clouded its beat (Bloomberg.com, [Jun 25]); Cerebras fell 10% on a shrinking-margin forecast (CNBC, [Jun 23]). Warnings are sold hard: KPIT Tech crashed 16% after an FY27 warning (TradingView, [Jun 30]), and software got no credit for a raise — Guidewire slid after a beat and a higher outlook (StocksToTrade, [Jun 26]).

Pattern: The print matters less than the forward line: a raise, a growing backlog or scarce supply is paid for — even through a weak top line — while weaker guidance, cost inflation or margin pressure is sold on the day, however large the beat. Nike is the live test: it topped estimates but warned of further sales declines as its China slump deepens (Yahoo Finance, [Jun 30]).

Attention into EPS: Delta — Q2 previews look past the quarter to long-term signals (Seeking Alpha, [Jun 30]), and airline earnings are set for takeoff while the stocks may be left behind (Barron's, [Jun 26]): fuel relief is known, so guidance must beat it. Big tech faces AI spending scrutiny into Q2 (Yahoo Finance, [Jun 29]): capex raises without revenue to match risk the Cerebras treatment. Intel has set its Q2 report (Business Wire, [Jun 30]); Tesla (Yahoo Finance, [Jun 29]) and AT&T (Yahoo Finance, [Jun 30]) previews are out — guidance, not the beat, decides each move.

Playbook: Own scarce supply and backlog (memory, AeroVironment-style order books) on dips — analysts keep raising memory-cycle targets (Yahoo Finance, [Jun 27]) — and fade consumer and travel beats where costs or China weigh on the outlook. Risk: supply easing in memory, or the AI-spending debate turning on the hardware winners.
