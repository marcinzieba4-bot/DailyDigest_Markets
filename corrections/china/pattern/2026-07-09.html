🧮 Earnings Pattern — What the Market Pays For

Rewarded: a beat that comes with pricing power, backlog or a self-help plan. Micron soared 15% after blockbuster earnings (CNBC, [Jun 25]); AeroVironment soared 28% on its beat as backlog grew to $1.2 billion (CNBC, [Jun 29]); Halfords rose 13% as margins hit a decade high (Yahoo Finance UK, [Jun 25]); General Mills jumped 9.9% after its beat and up to $3 billion in savings (TradingView, [Jul 2]). Levi Strauss beat and raised guidance and its dividend (CNBC, [Jul 8]) — the same profile.

Punished: a beat the run-up had already priced, and any guidance cut. Samsung flagged a 19-fold profit jump but the shares slumped on jitters the AI boom may stall (The Straits Times, [Jul 7]); Tesla dropped 7% despite a blowout delivery beat (24/7 Wall St., [Jul 2]); Boku slumped 33% as it cut full-year guidance (Yahoo Finance UK, [Jul 8]); KPIT slid 16% on weak guidance (The Financial Express, [Jul 1]).

Pattern: the beat is table stakes; the market pays for the forward line — guidance, backlog, pricing — and punishes a guide cut far harder than it rewards a beat. After the AI rally, even record AI profit needs a bigger forward number.

Attention into EPS: US banks: BofA Securities expects JPMorgan and peers to beat (marketscreener.com, [Jul 7]) on a trading surge helped by the SpaceX IPO (Reuters, [Jul 7]) — a beat is priced; the outlook decides. Delta reports July 10 with the Street at $1.48 EPS (AlphaStreet, [Jul 6]) and trades near highs (Yahoo Finance, [Jul 8]); with the Iran oil surge, a fuel-cost guide is the risk. PepsiCo has underperformed Coca-Cola in 2026 into its print (Benzinga, [Jul 8]) — the General Mills template says savings, not volume, get paid. TSMC: GF Securities expects an upward guidance revision and higher capex (Moomoo, [Jul 8]) — the forward line it must deliver after Samsung. TCS: flat revenue is expected with margins seen falling on wage hikes (Outlook Business, [Jul 8]) — a low bar, but KPIT shows what a guide cut costs.

Playbook: own low-bar prints where a steady guide can re-rate (TCS, PepsiCo) and fade crowded beats that only match the run-up (TSMC without a guidance raise). Pair capital-markets-heavy banks against fuel-exposed Delta; the risk is oil falling back and flipping airlines to the rewarded side.
