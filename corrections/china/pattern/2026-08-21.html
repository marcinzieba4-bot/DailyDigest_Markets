🧮 Earnings Pattern — What the Market Pays For

Rewarded: A beat with a raised forward line and visible demand behind it. Estée Lauder jumped 11.8% on a sales beat (StockStory, [Aug 19]) and raised its margin guidance (Yahoo Finance, [Aug 19]); Target rose 5% on a Q2 beat and a sharply raised full-year outlook (24/7 Wall St., [Aug 19]); CSL rallied 18% on its earnings beat (Livewire Markets, [Aug 17]); CoreWeave popped 14% as revenue doubled on accelerating AI infrastructure demand (CNBC, [Aug 11]).

Punished: A clean EPS beat with a soft guide, a revenue miss or margin doubts. Walmart tumbled 9% after its outlook disappointed (CNBC, [Aug 20]); Tapestry sank 15% despite a Q4 beat because of its FY27 guide (TIKR.com, [Aug 14]); Advance Auto Parts plunged 21% as a revenue miss overshadowed its earnings beat (24/7 Wall St., [Aug 20]); Cisco dropped 7% on gross margin fears (24/7 Wall St., [Aug 13]), and Applied Materials fell 5% after a record Q3 (TIKR.com, [Aug 15]).

Pattern: The guide, not the beat, sets the price: a raised outlook with demand behind it is paid, while a soft guide, a revenue miss or margin doubt is sold even on a clean EPS beat — AI hardware included.

Attention into EPS: Nvidia — reports next week, with retail turned "extremely bearish" while Wall Street flags a $20B CPU opportunity (TradingView, [Aug 20]): a beat is priced, so only a raised data-centre guide pays. Marvell — jumped 6% as top Wall Street firms turned bullish ahead of its earnings (TradingView, [Aug 20]): expectations ran up into the print, the setup that sold Applied Materials. CrowdStrike — Benchmark sees a beat across the board (Stocktwits, [Aug 18]), and the stock then sank 7% despite a Truist target raise (24/7 Wall St., [Aug 19]): a reset bar puts it on the rewarded side if the guide holds. H&M — analysts raised forecasts ahead of the results (Il Sole 24 ORE, [Aug 18]): a higher bar in apparel, where Tapestry's guide was punished.

Playbook: Own beat-and-raise candidates whose bar has been reset, and fade names that rallied into the print. Pair long Ross Stores, which raised its profit forecast again (Reuters, [Aug 20]), against TJX, whose "self-inflicted" slowdown clouded its beat (Reuters, [Aug 19]). Risk: tariff refunds flatter this season's beats — Walmart is using $2.9 billion of them to lower prices (CBS News, [Aug 20]) — and will not repeat.
