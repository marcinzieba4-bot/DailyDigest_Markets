🧮 Earnings Pattern — What the Market Pays For

Rewarded: A top-line beat with demand behind it, or a raised profit view. Accenture had its best day ever (Barron's, [Oct 1]) on record big-ticket client bookings (Benzinga, [Oct 1]); Carnival surged 12% on its Q3 results (24/7 Wall St., [Sep 29]); McCormick rose 5% as an acquisition boosted sales (Yahoo Finance, [Oct 1]); Greggs soared on upgraded profit guidance (The Grocer, [Oct 2]); Tesla jumped 5% as Q3 deliveries beat expectations (Investing.com, [Oct 2]).

Punished: Revenue misses and profit warnings, whatever the EPS line says. Nike dropped 10% as results disappointed (Baystreet.ca, [Oct 2]); Concentrix fell 10% on sales below estimates (Yahoo Finance, [Sep 29]); IG Group plunged about 25% on a de facto profit warning (Share Talk, [Oct 2]); Integra LifeSciences tumbled after cutting its 2026 outlook (Yahoo Finance Australia, [Oct 2]); Jabil fell 4% despite a beat and higher outlook (Yahoo Finance, [Sep 30]).

Pattern: The EPS beat is universal — all 8 S&P 500 firms on the latest scoreboard beat EPS estimates (Seeking Alpha, [Oct 3]) — so it decides nothing. The top line and the guide do: revenue beats with bookings, traffic or AI demand behind them are bought, revenue misses and warnings are sold hard, and a raise already in the price, as at Jabil, earns nothing.

Attention into EPS: PepsiCo — reports next week (CMC Markets, [Oct 2]), with operational tailwinds that might not be enough to appease investors (Seeking Alpha, [Oct 1]): a pricing beat on soft volume is the Conagra profile, not McCormick's. Constellation Brands — Q2 earnings are expected to decline (Yahoo Finance, [Sep 29]) and it could be at its valuation floor (Seeking Alpha, [Oct 1]): only a firmer outlook pays. Big banks — Oppenheimer sees Goldman and Morgan Stanley facing quarterly earnings pressure (Yahoo Finance, [Oct 2]): after Jefferies, record revenue alone will not lift them. Airlines — TD Cowen cut Alaska Air, Delta and United targets ahead of Q3 (Yahoo Finance, [Oct 2]): fuel costs put them on the warning side.

Playbook: Own revenue-led beats with visible demand (cruise, IT services on AI bookings) and fade EPS-only beats with soft volumes into their prints (PepsiCo). Avoid fuel-exposed airlines into Q3. Risk: a Tesla-style low bar lets PepsiCo or the banks rally on "less bad".
