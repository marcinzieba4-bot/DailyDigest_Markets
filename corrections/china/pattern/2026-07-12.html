🧮 Earnings Pattern — What the Market Pays For

Rewarded: a beat with a clean forward line, or one that clears a low bar. AeroVironment soared 28% on its beat as backlog grew to $1.2 billion (CNBC, [Jun 29]); General Mills jumped 9.9% after its beat and up to $3 billion in savings (TradingView, [Jul 2]); Playtech jumped 18% as H1 beat expectations (NEXT.io, [Jul 9]); TCS rose 4% after Q1 (CNBC TV18, [Jul 10]) as AI momentum fuelled a revenue beat (Reuters, [Jul 10]).

Punished: a beat — even with guidance — that carries a cost or currency caveat. Delta's earnings and guidance beat expectations, yet the shares slid (Yahoo Finance, [Jul 10]) as sky-high fuel costs cut profit (WSJ, [Jul 10]); PepsiCo beat but fell anyway (Yahoo Finance, [Jul 9]) on a warning of higher commodity costs (Reuters, [Jul 9]); Fast Retailing slid on a yen warning after its results (NST Online, [Jul 9]); Samsung flagged a 19-fold profit jump but slumped on AI-stall jitters (Reuters, [Jul 9]).

Pattern: the margin line decides, not the beat: any input-cost, fuel or FX caveat outweighs the quarter and even a guidance beat, while low-bar names that simply deliver get paid. Even good numbers often aren't enough to impress (MarketWatch, [Jul 10]).

Attention into EPS: US banks: profit engines are expected to roar into earnings as Main Street keeps spending (Yahoo Finance, [Jul 11]), but Barron's asks whether investors will sell the news (Barron's, [Jul 10]) — a beat is priced, so the NII and fee outlook decides, as for Wells Fargo (TradingView, [Jul 9]). TSMC reports July 16, with three CoWoS signals set to test AI's spending ceiling (Tech Times, [Jul 11]): a beat alone is the Samsung profile; a guidance raise is the upside. ASML is seen likely to beat, "but that won't be enough" (Seeking Alpha, [Jul 10]) — orders and outlook carry the reaction. Netflix reports July 16 (Moomoo, [Jul 10]) with the stock down 42% from its high (The Motley Fool, [Jul 7]) — the TCS low-bar setup. Tesla: UBS expects a strong Q2 beat (TipRanks, [Jul 11]); its delivery beat was sold, so margins decide.

Playbook: own low-bar prints with no cost caveat (Netflix) and stay underweight crowded beats that need a raise (banks after the rally, TSMC). The risk: a trading blowout at the banks big enough to beat the whisper, or resumed U.S.-Iran hostilities (CNBC, [Jul 10]) pushing fuel and input costs into more guides.
