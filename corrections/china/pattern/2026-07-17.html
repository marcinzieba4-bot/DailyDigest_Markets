🧮 Earnings Pattern — What the Market Pays For

Rewarded: beat-and-raise where costs are falling or revenue is fee-driven. UnitedHealth blew past estimates and hiked its outlook as it reins in costs (CNBC, [Jul 16]), and the shares surged (Reuters, [Jul 16]); Abbott jumped on its beat and raised 2026 outlook (Yahoo Finance, [Jul 16]); BlackRock rallied 7% after its beat (TIKR.com, [Jul 16]); J.B. Hunt rose 9% on a Q2 beat (Yahoo Finance, [Jul 15]).

Punished: beat-and-raise with rising costs, a soft outlook or a crowded run-up. TSMC beat and raised guidance, but the shares slid (NAI500, [Jul 16]) as it added $100 billion of Arizona investment (CNBC, [Jul 16]); Netflix tumbled 8% after hours as its Q3 outlook missed (Moomoo, [Jul 16]); GE Aerospace's near-perfect report wasn't enough (Barron's, [Jul 16]); United fell as a weak outlook overshadowed its beat (Yahoo Finance, [Jul 16]); Elevance tumbled despite a raised guide (TIKR.com, [Jul 16]).

Pattern: the cost line, not the beat or even the raise, sets the reaction — falling medical costs or fee income get paid, while rising capex, fuel or medical spend gets sold. Blockbuster earnings aren't good enough anymore in the AI trade (Business Insider, [Jul 16]).

Attention into EPS: Alphabet and Tesla report next week (富途牛牛, [Jul 16]). For Alphabet, BofA Securities raised its target on cloud growth (Investing.com, [Jul 16]), but the AI spending question looms (TradingView, [Jul 15]) — on the TSMC template a capex jump is the risk unless cloud revenue outruns it. Tesla drew four target hikes (Investor's Business Daily, [Jul 14]) while Wells Fargo still sees a 67% drop (Yahoo Finance, [Jul 15]) — its delivery beat was already sold, so margins decide. Humana has rising earnings estimates (Yahoo Finance, [Jul 16]) — the UnitedHealth cost-control setup. Amazon: Wall Street is raising its target ahead of earnings (Watcher Guru, [Jul 16]) — capex is again the swing line.

Playbook: own cost-control stories into their prints (Humana on the UnitedHealth template) and underweight AI spenders where capex is the cost line (Alphabet, Amazon) until the guides show revenue outrunning spend. The risk is a cloud-growth beat big enough that the market pays for the capex, as BofA's call implies.
