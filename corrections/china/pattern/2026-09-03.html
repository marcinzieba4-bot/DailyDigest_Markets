🧮 Earnings Pattern — What the Market Pays For

Rewarded: Accelerating demand that the guide had not priced, above all in software. Snowflake spiked 22% on healthy results and AI coding momentum (CNBC, [Sep 2]); GitLab surged as much as 24% after beating across the board (TradingKey, [Sep 2]); Dell surged 9% after lifting its fiscal 2027 forecast on AI server strength (CNBC, [Sep 1]); Salesforce rocketed 22% for its second-best day ever (CNBC, [Aug 27]).

Punished: Beats where the bar was already extreme or the quality of growth was in doubt. Broadcom's shares sank in extended trading despite its beat (CNBC, [Sep 2]) as soft revenue guidance weighed (Moomoo, [Sep 2]); HPE delivered a blowout quarter and raised guidance, but its shares still fell (Yahoo Finance, [Sep 2]); Palo Alto tumbled as analysts weighed its organic growth and acquisitions (Investor's Business Daily, [Sep 2]); MongoDB tumbled despite an earnings beat (Investor's Business Daily, [Sep 2]).

Pattern: "Beat and raise" is no longer enough where expectations are extreme — AI hardware and richly valued software are sold unless the guide surprises and margins hold. What pays is demand the market had not modelled: Snowflake and GitLab rallied on acceleration, while Broadcom, HPE and MongoDB fell on prints that only met high hopes.

Attention into EPS: Zscaler — reports on September 3 with a preview arguing it could send the stock soaring (The Motley Fool, [Sep 2]): after Palo Alto's drop, security software is judged on organic growth, so a raise must come from demand, not deals. Oracle — poised to beat estimates again (Yahoo Finance, [Sep 1]) after Citi turned bullish following a 50% pullback (TradingKey, [Aug 28]): a low bar makes it a candidate for the rewarded side if AI demand shows. Adobe — Citi sees an 'achievable set-up' ahead of Q3 (Stocktwits, [Sep 2]): a modest bar fits the profile the market pays for. Lululemon — expected to beat earnings estimates (Yahoo Finance, [Aug 27]): a beat without a raised outlook would sit with the punished prints.

Playbook: Own low-bar, demand-led software into its prints (Adobe, Oracle) and fade AI hardware beats where expectations are extreme — the Broadcom and HPE reaction is the template. Risk: AI coding demand cools and the software rebound reverses, while a memory-supply fix revives the hardware trade.
