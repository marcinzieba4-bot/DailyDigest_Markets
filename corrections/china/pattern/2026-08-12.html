🧮 Earnings Pattern — What the Market Pays For

Rewarded: Revenue acceleration with a raised or held guide. CoreWeave popped 14% as revenue doubled on accelerating AI infrastructure demand (CNBC, [Aug 11]); Atlassian jumped 37.4% (Yahoo Finance, [Aug 6]); Embraer surged on record second-quarter revenue and raised guidance (Reuters, [Aug 10]); Airbnb jumped 17% after its beat (Yahoo Finance, [Aug 10]); CAVA jumped 10.2% on a sales beat and traffic gains (StockStory, [Aug 11]).

Punished: A beat with a softer outlook, or costs that eat the upside. Datadog fell 18% despite a beat and a guidance raise (TradingView, [Aug 6]); Sandisk and Western Digital tumbled despite strong quarterly results (CNBC, [Aug 6]); Monday.com fell as its guidance trumped the beat (Investor's Business Daily, [Aug 10]); Venture Global fell after beating as higher costs took a toll (Barron's, [Aug 11]); On Holding dropped 22% on a sales miss (Yahoo Finance, [Aug 11]).

Pattern: Earnings are beating Wall Street's boldest expectations (Barron's, [Aug 11]), so the beat itself earns nothing. The market pays for revenue that is accelerating — even heavy AI spenders such as CoreWeave are paid when revenue doubles — and sells beats that arrive with decelerating growth, a cautious guide or rising costs.

Attention into EPS: Nvidia — BofA sees it poised for a Q2 beat and a guidance raise on Vera Rubin demand (Yahoo Finance, [Aug 10]): with AMD sold on doubled data-centre sales, only an above-consensus guide pays. Cisco — raised its AI order target to $9 billion (The Motley Fool, [Aug 7]): booked AI orders sit on the rewarded side if revenue growth accelerates. Walmart — consolidating with Target and Costco ahead of earnings (Yahoo Finance, [Aug 10]); after PepsiCo's cut on a cautious US consumer (investingLive, [Aug 10]), margin guidance matters more than the sales beat. Deere — previewed with front-running as the risk (Seeking Alpha, [Aug 11]): a crowded beat would be faded unless orders rise.

Playbook: Own revenue accelerators into and after prints (AI infrastructure with booked demand, Embraer, CAVA-style traffic stories) and fade beats where growth decelerates or costs rise. Pair long accelerating AI infrastructure against hardware priced for perfection, where Sandisk and Western Digital fell on strong numbers. Risk: the 10Y climbing to 4.68% even as CPI cooled to 3.4% is a term-premium repricing that would hit long-duration winners hardest.
