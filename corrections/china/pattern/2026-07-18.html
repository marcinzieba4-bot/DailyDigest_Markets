🧮 Earnings Pattern — What the Market Pays For

Rewarded: beat-and-raise where costs are falling or revenue is fee-driven. UnitedHealth blew past estimates and hiked its outlook as it reins in costs (CNBC, [Jul 16]), and the shares jumped (Barron's, [Jul 16]); Abbott jumped on its beat and raised 2026 outlook (Yahoo Finance, [Jul 16]); BlackRock rallied 7% after its beat (TIKR.com, [Jul 16]); Cintas jumped 7% on a beat and a Bank of America upgrade (TIKR.com, [Jul 17]).

Punished: beat-and-raise with rising costs, a soft outlook or a crowded run-up. TSMC beat and raised guidance, but the shares slid (NAI500, [Jul 16]) as capex soared to $64 billion (BigGo Finance, [Jul 17]); Netflix sank 9% as a weak outlook overshadowed its beat (Yahoo Finance, [Jul 17]); Intuitive Surgical tumbled 11% despite topping expectations (TIKR.com, [Jul 17]); BNY slipped as a rising expense forecast offset its beat (TIKR.com, [Jul 17]); GE Aerospace's near-perfect report wasn't enough (Barron's, [Jul 16]).

Pattern: the cost line, not the beat or even the raise, sets the reaction — falling medical costs and fee income get paid; rising capex, expenses or a softer next quarter get sold, hardest where the stock had already run.

Playbook: own cost-control stories into their prints (Humana) and fade crowded beats where the next-quarter guide or capex carries the risk (Texas Instruments, Alphabet). The risk is a cloud or analog upturn strong enough that the market pays for the spend, which is what the cloud-growth focus is betting on.
