🧮 Earnings Pattern — What the Market Pays For

Rewarded: An AI revenue line, or a raised outlook against a low bar. ServiceNow jumped more than 7% on its beat (CNBC, [Jul 22]) as AI passed $1B in bookings (SiliconANGLE, [Jul 22]); GM rose after it boosted full-year guidance (Yahoo Finance, [Jul 21]); AMC rose 27% as record box office sales beat estimates (TIKR.com, [Jul 21]); UnitedHealth surged as earnings momentum returned (Reuters, [Jul 16]).

Punished: AI spending, and beats into an elevated bar. Alphabet beat but the shares fell on its capex surge (Investing.com, [Jul 22]) after it raised its capex forecast again (Reuters, [Jul 22]); GE Vernova beat and raised guidance yet fell anyway (Barron's, [Jul 22]); Texas Instruments beat but retreated on elevated expectations (Yahoo Finance, [Jul 22]); Danaher fell 11% after earnings (TIKR.com, [Jul 22]).

Pattern: The market pays for AI revenue and sells AI spending: ServiceNow's bookings were bought, while Alphabet's capex lift was sold even as cloud revenue nearly doubled (NZ Herald, [Jul 22]). Outside AI the guide decides, and a beat into a crowded bar sells.

Attention into EPS: Intel — a stunning target hike (Barchart.com, [Jul 21]) and job cuts in its data-centre and AI group ahead of earnings (Analytics Insight, [Jul 22]) precede a print that could expose the fault line beneath its AI comeback (TradingView, [Jul 22]): priced for a beat, so only the margin guide lifts it. Meta — analysts expect an earnings decline (Yahoo Finance, [Jul 22]) while BofA raised its target on AI revenue potential (Investing.com, [Jul 20]): a low bar helps, but a capex lift without AI revenue is the Alphabet sale. SK Hynix — reports on July 28 (Moomoo, [Jul 22]), with the question whether long-term agreements extend the cycle (Moomoo, [Jul 22]); memory is AI revenue, but a big beat may already be priced. American Airlines — due to report (Yahoo Finance, [Jul 22]) after Southwest lowered its 2026 profit floor as fuel costs blunted pricing gains (Reuters, [Jul 22]): the cost side of the pattern.

Playbook: Pair AI monetizers against AI spenders. Own names whose AI revenue still runs ahead of estimates (Dell raised guidance by $27 billion and the Street is still catching up (TIKR.com, [Jul 21])) and fade capex-heavy platforms into their prints. Risk: a Meta print with AI revenue visibly lifting ads, on a low bar, would squeeze the short leg the way ServiceNow ran.
