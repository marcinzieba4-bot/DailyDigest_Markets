🧮 Earnings Pattern — What the Market Pays For

Rewarded: A beat with a raised outlook tied to demand the market can see. Shopify jumped 25.1% on a strong Q2 (StockStory, [Aug 5]) as its revenue outlook beat estimates (The Business of Fashion, [Aug 5]); Palantir skyrocketed 29% on 'otherworldly' results (CNBC, [Aug 4]); Wayfair jumped 30% on its strongest US growth since 2020 (CNBC, [Aug 4]); Quanta jumped 17% after topping consensus and raising guidance (TIKR.com, [Jul 31]).

Punished: A beat with a softer forward line or a heavier cost bill. Sandisk slumped 11% as an underwhelming outlook overshadowed an AI demand surge (Yahoo Finance, [Aug 5]); Uber sank 5% after weak guidance despite revenue growth (CNBC, [Aug 5]); CVS hiked guidance yet fell after its preliminary 2027 guidance disappointed (Modern Healthcare, [Aug 5]); SpaceX hit a new all-time low as AI capex jumped in Q2 (Yahoo Finance, [Aug 5]); Google fell more than 6% on capex growth expectations despite a beat (Yahoo Finance, [Jul 23]).

Pattern: The guide, not the beat, sets the price — and the horizon is lengthening. A raised outlook backed by visible demand is paid; a beat paired with a soft next quarter, a disappointing next year or rising AI capex is sold, however large the beat.

Attention into EPS: Western Digital — set to report soon (Investopedia, [Aug 4]) after Sandisk's outlook-driven slump: memory priced for margin sits on the punished side unless its guide clears the bar. Astera Labs — rose another 8% ahead of earnings (Seeking Alpha, [Aug 4]): a run-up into the print is the AMD set-up, where only a raised AI guide holds the gain. CleanSpark — signed a $6.6B AI data-centre lease in its first HPC deal ahead of earnings (TradingView, [Aug 5]): contracted forward revenue is the rewarded profile. REA Group — down 30% in a year and cheapest in a decade (Market Index, [Aug 5]): a low bar means a steady guide can be paid.

Playbook: Own guide-raisers whose demand already shows in revenue — data-centre equipment and AI software — into their prints, and fade hardware priced above its own guidance. Pair long data-centre industrials against memory names after Sandisk. Risk: a memory-price rebound flips the short leg, and a hawkish Fed compresses multiples on both sides.
