🧮 Earnings Pattern — What the Market Pays For

Rewarded: A beat with a raised outlook on real demand. Aritzia surged the most in 18 months on earnings and a better outlook (Bloomberg.com, [Oct 9]); Accenture stock rallied after its earnings beat (CNBC, [Oct 1]); cruise stocks rallied as Carnival beat and saw record bookings for 2027 (Seeking Alpha, [Sep 29]); TCS rose 4% as AI revenue crossed $3.1 Bn (BW Businessworld, [Oct 9]).

Punished: Guidance cuts and sales trims, whatever the demand story. Delta missed and cut guidance on skyrocketing fuel costs (Yahoo Finance, [Oct 9]), and the stock fell (Benzinga, [Oct 9]) despite record revenue; Nike's stock dropped 10% as results disappointed (Baystreet.ca, [Oct 2]); AngioDynamics slipped 4% despite a beat (Yahoo Finance, [Oct 8]); Jabil fell despite a beat and strong guidance (Yahoo! Finance Canada, [Sep 30]).

Pattern: Earnings beats are now the norm (CNBC, [Oct 7]), so the guide decides: raises backed by volume, bookings or AI revenue are bought, and cost-driven cuts are sold even when demand is intact, as at Delta. The exception is the washed-out stock — PepsiCo, at its lowest since 2020 into the print (TradingView, [Oct 7]), rallied despite cutting its profit guidance (CNBC, [Oct 8]).

Attention into EPS: JPMorgan — reports this week with other big banks (CNBC, [Oct 11]) as Wall Street's record trading streak is set to cool (Financial Times, [Oct 11]): trading carried the last quarters, so the NII and credit guide decides. Bank of America — the options market signals a muted reaction (Benzinga, [Oct 11]): a low bar that a firm guide can clear. TSMC — reports October 15 (Moomoo, [Oct 11]) and is expected to post a sixth straight record profit (Reuters, [Oct 11]): the record is priced, only a raised outlook pays. ASML — a preview asks whether 55% sales growth can still surprise (Moomoo, [Oct 11]): the Jabil risk if orders merely meet it. United Airlines — Delta's cut puts its results in focus (Live and Let's Fly, [Oct 9]): fuel puts it on the warning side.

Playbook: Trade the guide, not the beat: own raisers with visible demand into their prints, fade fuel-exposed airlines into United, and prefer trading-light, deposit-rich banks only if credit holds. Risk: a PepsiCo-style low bar lets beaten-down banks rally on "less bad".
