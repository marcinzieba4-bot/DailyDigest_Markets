🧮 Earnings Pattern — What the Market Pays For

Rewarded: A beat with a raised guide, and companies that collect AI spending as revenue. Digital Realty surged 11% after crushing estimates and raising full-year guidance (TIKR.com, [Jul 26]); SLB gained 11% on a strong beat (TIKR.com, [Jul 26]) as its data-centre revenue leapt 80% (BigGo Finance, [Jul 27]); Medpace soared 15% after beating and raising guidance (TIKR.com, [Jul 23]); Baker Hughes beat and its shares climbed (Yahoo Finance, [Jul 27]).

Punished: The AI spenders and a weaker outlook, even on big beats. Alphabet fell more than 6% on capex growth expectations despite its beat (Yahoo Finance, [Jul 23]); Intel fell nearly 8% despite a big Q2 beat (Yahoo Finance, [Jul 24]); STMicro dropped 17% after its Q3 revenue outlook missed (Yahoo Finance, [Jul 23]); Shin-Etsu plunged as its full-year outlook missed forecasts (finance.biggo.com, [Jul 27]).

Pattern: Investors are giving tech a hard time despite strong earnings (Investopedia, [Jul 27]). Spending more is sold even on a beat; collecting that spending with a raised guide, or beating and raising outside the AI loop, is paid. The guide, not the beat, sets the move.

Attention into EPS: Four Big Tech reports and a Fed meeting make this the busiest week of the quarter (Yahoo Finance, [Jul 27]). Meta — can 33% ad growth outrun a $145 billion capex bill (tastylive, [Jul 27]): ad growth is the rewarded side, the capex bill the punished one. Microsoft — can Azure justify a $220 billion capex bill (tastylive, [Jul 27]): without faster Azure it is the Alphabet sale. Amazon — is Wall Street's AWS bar set too low (tastylive, [Jul 27]): a low bar helps only if AWS outruns its capex. Apple — Goldman raised its target, expecting outperformance on iPhone and Mac revenue (TipRanks, [Jul 27]), into its July 30 report (Moomoo, [Jul 24]): the least capex-heavy giant, closest to the rewarded side.

Playbook: Own the collectors, fade the spenders into their prints: a data-centre landlord or service company raising guidance (Digital Realty, SLB) against a hyperscaler held into its report. Risk: a spender that shows AI revenue keeping pace with capex would squeeze the short leg, and any capex cut would hit the collectors first.
