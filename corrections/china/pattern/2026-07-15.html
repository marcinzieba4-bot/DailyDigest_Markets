🧮 Earnings Pattern — What the Market Pays For

Rewarded: a beat powered by the boom's revenue engines — trading, dealmaking, AI hardware — or a raised guide. JPMorgan posted the highest quarterly profit ever by a U.S. bank (Reuters, [Jul 14]); Goldman beat with the stock up 2% (Fox Business, [Jul 14]); Wells Fargo shares rose after its beat (Yahoo Finance, [Jul 14]); Aehr Test Systems popped 40% after hours on a beat and upbeat FY27 guidance (TradingView, [Jul 14]). Playtech jumped 18% on its H1 beat (NEXT.io, [Jul 9]).

Punished: a cost line moving the wrong way, or budgets lost to AI hardware. IBM had its worst day on record, down 25%, after a Q2 earnings warning (CNBC, [Jul 14]) as the AI boom squeezes software budgets (Reuters, [Jul 14]); Citi's shares fell as expense worries overshadowed its profit beat (Reuters, [Jul 14]); Ericsson fell on disappointing results and a component-cost warning (Il Sole 24 ORE, [Jul 14]); HCA dropped as payer-mix shifts squeezed earnings (WSJ, [Jul 14]); Delta dipped despite its beat (TIKR.com, [Jul 13]).

Pattern: the market pays for revenue on the right side of the AI and markets boom and sells any beat whose costs rise — expenses, components, fuel. Hardware is "eating everyone's lunch" (MarketWatch, [Jul 14]); software and services are paying for it.

TSMC reports July 16 (The Motley Fool, [Jul 13]) after Q2 revenue beat guidance (Quartz, [Jul 13]): the beat is known; guidance and capex are the trade. Morgan Stanley: higher estimates are reshaping its earnings narrative (Yahoo Finance, [Jul 12]) — rewarded on trading unless Citi-style expenses appear. Netflix faces subscriber-churn and content-pipeline worries into Q2 (TradingView, [Jul 14]) — a low bar outside the AI-budget squeeze. UnitedHealth reports July 16 with the Street at $4.85 EPS (AlphaStreet, [Jul 13]); HCA's coverage losses put it on the cost side.

Playbook: pair AI hardware into its prints (ASML, TSMC) against software and IT services exposed to the IBM budget squeeze; the risk is a capex-plateau signal in TSMC's guide. Own trading-heavy banks still to report, but fade any beat that comes with higher expense guidance.
