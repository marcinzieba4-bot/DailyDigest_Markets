🧮 Earnings Pattern — What the Market Pays For

Rewarded: A beat with a forward line that clearly steps up, AI or not. Atlassian surged 30% after earnings (Barron's, [Aug 7]); Airbnb jumped 17% after results beat expectations (Yahoo Finance, [Aug 10]) and it raised guidance (Barron's, [Aug 7]); CoreWeave popped 14% as revenue doubled on accelerating AI infrastructure demand (CNBC, [Aug 11]); Palantir soared 12% on a blowout quarter, with U.S. commercial revenue up nearly 150% (CNBC, [Aug 3]).

Punished: A beat whose guide only meets a high bar, or rising costs. Datadog fell 18% despite a beat and a guidance raise (TradingView, [Aug 6]); Siemens plunged on a disappointing guidance raise (France 24, [Aug 6]); AMD beat and fell 8% (BeInCrypto, [Aug 4]) even as its data center sales doubled (CNBC, [Aug 4]); Cerebras plunged 14% after its second report since its IPO (CNBC, [Aug 12]); Meta fell 7.5% as investors fretted over more expenses (TradingView, [Jul 30]).

Pattern: Earnings are booming but stocks aren't showing it (Barron's, [Aug 7]): with beats near-universal, the market pays only for the size of the forward step-up against an elevated bar. A raise that clears the whisper is bought; a raise that merely confirms it is sold, most harshly in crowded AI hardware.

Attention into EPS: Applied Materials — AI-sector optimism points to opportunities for chip equipment (Moomoo, [Aug 12]) after five straight quarterly beats (TIKR.com, [Aug 9]): a sixth beat is priced, so only a stronger AI outlook pays, and a matched guide risks the AMD treatment. Nvidia — traders are setting up options plays around its report (Investor's Business Daily, [Aug 7]) as Bank of America argues it is too cheap versus chip peers (investingLive, [Aug 7]): valuation cushions the print, but the data-centre guide decides. Home Depot — the preview centres on CEO Ted Decker's leave of absence (Yahoo Finance, [Aug 12]): a leadership question leaves little room for a raise, the profile being sold. Deere — the risk is front-running (Seeking Alpha, [Aug 11]): after a run-up, a beat-and-hold could trade like Siemens.

Playbook: Own into the print only where estimates are still rising and the guide can step up, and fade crowded AI hardware on a merely matched guide — Cerebras plunged despite solid earnings and guidance (SiliconANGLE, [Aug 12]). A pair: long beat-and-raise names with demand visibility (Airbnb, CoreWeave) against beat-and-hold AI hardware. Risk: tech has stumbled while other sectors rose (Investopedia, [Aug 11]), and a deeper rotation would hit both legs.
