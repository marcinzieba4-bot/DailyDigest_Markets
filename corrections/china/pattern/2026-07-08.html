🧮 Earnings Pattern — What the Market Pays For

Rewarded: a beat that comes with pricing power, backlog or a self-help plan. Micron jumped 15% as soaring memory prices quadrupled revenue (CNBC, [Jun 24]); AeroVironment soared 28% on its beat as backlog grew to $1.2 billion (CNBC, [Jun 29]); Halfords rose 13% as margins hit a decade high (Yahoo Finance UK, [Jun 25]); General Mills jumped 9.9% after its beat and up to $3 billion in savings (TradingView, [Jul 2]).

Punished: a beat with a softer outlook, cost exposure or a peaking cycle. FedEx dipped 6% despite beating Q4 estimates (TIKR.com, [Jun 24]) as it warned on inflation and trade turmoil (CNBC TV18, [Jun 24]); Carnival dipped 5% on weaker Q3 guidance despite its beat (TIKR.com, [Jun 24]); Samsung's 1,800% profit jump met AI spending concerns (CNBC, [Jul 6]) and the shares dropped over 6% after the open (TradingKey, [Jul 6]); Tesla dropped 7% despite a blowout delivery beat (24/7 Wall St., [Jul 2]).

Pattern: the beat is table stakes; the market pays for the forward line — guidance, backlog, pricing — and sells any beat whose outlook carries fuel, tariff or war costs. After the AI rally, even record AI profit needs a bigger forward number.

Attention into EPS: US banks: bank stocks rallied ahead of earnings (Investor's Business Daily, [Jul 6]) and BofA Securities expects JPMorgan and peers to beat (marketscreener.com, [Jul 7]) on a trading surge helped by the SpaceX IPO (Reuters, [Jul 7]) — a beat is priced; the outlook decides. Delta reports July 10 with the Street at $1.48 EPS (AlphaStreet, [Jul 6]); it ran near highs with oil below $70 (Investor's Business Daily, [Jul 2]), so a fuel-cost guide puts it on the Carnival side. PepsiCo previews weigh buying before Q2 (Yahoo Finance, [Jul 3]) — the General Mills template says savings, not volume, get paid. TSMC carries a Street-high NT$3,800 Citi target (BigGo Finance, [Jul 6]), yet Barron's asks what Samsung's unrewarded beat means for it (Barron's, [Jul 7]): it needs raised guidance. Netflix is down 42% from its high with earnings due July 16 (The Motley Fool, [Jul 7]) — a low bar.

Playbook: own low-bar names where a guide-up can re-rate (Netflix) and fade crowded beats that only match the run-up (TSMC on an unchanged outlook). Pair capital-markets-heavy banks against fuel-exposed Delta into the prints; the risk is a renewed Iran ceasefire dropping oil and flipping airlines to the rewarded side.
