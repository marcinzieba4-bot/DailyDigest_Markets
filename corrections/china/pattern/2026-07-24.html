🧮 Earnings Pattern — What the Market Pays For

Rewarded: A beat with a raised guide, or an AI revenue line. Medpace soared 15% after beating and raising guidance (TIKR.com, [Jul 23]); RTX surged on a massive beat and raised guidance (ChartMill, [Jul 23]); Wabtec jumped 10% after earnings (TIKR.com, [Jul 23]); ServiceNow jumped more than 7% on its beat (CNBC, [Jul 22]) as it raised its subscription revenue forecast again on AI-driven demand (Reuters, [Jul 22]).

Punished: AI spending, cost shocks and a weaker next quarter, even on a beat. Alphabet fell more than 6% on capex growth expectations despite its beat (Yahoo Finance, [Jul 23]); Tesla plummeted 14% (Forbes, [Jul 23]) after a miss as AI spending soared (Yahoo Finance, [Jul 23]); American Airlines tumbled 8% as a fuel spike postponed its turnaround (CNBC, [Jul 23]); STMicro dropped 17% after its Q3 revenue outlook missed (Yahoo Finance, [Jul 23]).

Pattern: The forward line decides, not the beat. A raised guide or AI revenue gets paid; a bigger spending bill, a cost shock or a soft next-quarter outlook gets sold, and in AI the market now pays for revenue and sells capex.

Attention into EPS: Microsoft — the question is whether soaring capex or a record-low valuation will dominate (Moomoo, [Jul 23]): after Alphabet, a capex lift without faster AI revenue is a sale. Meta — analysts expect an earnings decline (Yahoo Finance, [Jul 22]) while BofA raised its target on AI revenue potential (Investing.com, [Jul 20]): the low bar helps if AI revenue shows. Amazon — fell 4% as a Senate China probe and AI-spending jitters weigh ahead of earnings (24/7 Wall St., [Jul 23]): some of the capex fear is already in the price. SK Hynix and Samsung — face a pivotal earnings test on AI swings (Bloomberg.com, [Jul 23]); memory is AI revenue, so the guide, not the beat, sets the move.

Playbook: The capex the market sells at the platforms is revenue at the suppliers. GE Vernova fell despite a beat-and-raise (Barron's, [Jul 22]), but Guggenheim raised its target to $1,450 (Investing.com, [Jul 23]) as its gas turbine backlog climbs to 116 GW (Utility Dive, [Jul 23]): own the supplier dip and fade the spenders into their prints. Risk: a capex cut at Microsoft or Meta would hit the supplier leg first.
