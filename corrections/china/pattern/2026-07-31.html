🧮 Earnings Pattern — What the Market Pays For

Rewarded: A raised forward line with demand behind it, AI spenders included. Microsoft surged 9% as Azure topped $100B and its Q4 beat lifted most price targets (24/7 Wall St., [Jul 30]); Starbucks jumped on strong Q3 results and a higher full-year outlook (Yahoo Finance, [Jul 30]); Bloom Energy surged 10% after raising its full-year revenue and profit forecast (TIKR.com, [Jul 29]); Arm rose 4% after its revenue and profit forecast beat (Yahoo Finance, [Jul 30]).

Punished: A weaker next quarter, even on a clear beat. Meta sank 9% after its Q3 revenue forecast missed estimates (Yahoo Finance, [Jul 29]); Apple dropped 7% after hours as China sales slowed and Tim Cook flagged memory supply constraints for Q4 revenue (Yahoo Finance, [Jul 30]); Reddit sank 11% even as results blew past estimates (CNBC, [Jul 30]); Alnylam tanked 29% as its ATTR outlook was cut (Fierce Pharma, [Jul 30]).

Pattern: The AI trade has split (CNBC, [Jul 30]) along one line: the forward revenue guide. Capex backed by booked demand is paid, a softer next quarter is sold, and the beat itself is only table stakes.

Attention into EPS: Chevron and ExxonMobil — higher oil prices have analysts predicting their best results in 15 quarters (Benzinga, [Jul 30]): a record is expected, so only a raised production or buyback line pays, as Halliburton's tumble on a tepid forecast showed (Reuters, [Jul 21]). AMD — reports on August 04 (Moomoo, [Jul 29]) after Susquehanna raised its target (Barchart.com, [Jul 30]): AI hardware has been sold on beats, so the data-centre guide decides. Palantir — a $200 target ahead of critical earnings (TradingView, [Jul 27]) sets a high bar for a light-asset AI software name (Moomoo, [Jul 29]): only a raised guide clears it.

Playbook: Own demand-backed guide-raisers and fade beats without a forward line. Prefer AI names whose guide is rising (Arm, Microsoft) over hardware squeezed by memory costs, where Qualcomm slid as higher costs clouded its profit forecast (Reuters, [Jul 30]). Risk: a memory-cost spike hits AI spenders and hardware alike, so the long leg is not immune.
