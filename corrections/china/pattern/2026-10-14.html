🧮 Earnings Pattern — What the Market Pays For

Rewarded: A beat where the line investors watch is the one that surprised. Goldman's shares rose on strong equities and IB revenues (Yahoo Finance, [Oct 13]); UnitedHealth rose 4% on a beat and raised full-year outlook (24/7 Wall St., [Oct 13]); Marston's jumped 18% as brokers lifted forecasts after a profit beat (Yahoo Finance UK, [Oct 13]); Accenture had its best day ever (Barron's, [Oct 1]) as AI demand boosted results (WSJ, [Oct 1]); Aritzia surged most in 18 months on a better outlook (Bloomberg.com, [Oct 9]).

Punished: Beats and even raises that came with a miss in the core franchise. J&J raised 2026 guidance (Investing.com, [Oct 13]) but its shares fell on weak medical device sales (Reuters, [Oct 13]); ASML raised its sales forecast for a third time, but a Q3 revenue miss sent the stock lower (TradingView, [Oct 13]); Citi's shares dropped even after fixed-income traders posted higher revenue (Bloomberg.com, [Oct 13]); Delta's stock fell on its fuel-driven cut (Benzinga, [Oct 9]); Nike sank to its lowest level since 2013 (Benzinga, [Oct 1]).

Pattern: Earnings beats are now the norm (CNBC, [Oct 7]) and great results won't be good enough this season (Yahoo Finance, [Oct 13]). The market pays for the driver line — equities trading, medical-cost control, AI bookings — and sells the headline beat or raise when that line misses, as at J&J and ASML.

Attention into EPS: TSMC — reports October 15 (Moomoo, [Oct 11]) with high growth likely but capex the risk (Seeking Alpha, [Oct 13]): the AI revenue line is priced, so a capex jump is the ASML-style trap. Bank of America — $1.2 billion in fines will weigh on earnings (Barron's, [Oct 13]): only NII or fee strength, Goldman-style, pays. United Airlines — analysts expect a decline in earnings (Yahoo Finance, [Oct 13]): fuel puts it with Delta. Abbott — investors weigh adding it ahead of Q3 (Yahoo Finance, [Oct 13]): after J&J, its medtech line is the whole trade.

Playbook: Own franchises where the driver line is accelerating (capital-markets banks over NII-reliant lenders, managed care after UnitedHealth's raise) and fade medtech-heavy names into their prints. Into TSMC, size for capex, not revenue. Risk: medtech already trades cheap, so a "less bad" Abbott could squeeze the fade.
