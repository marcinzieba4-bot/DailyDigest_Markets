🧮 Earnings Pattern — What the Market Pays For

Rewarded: A beat with a raised forward line and volume behind it. Ross Stores surged 9% after beating estimates and lifting guidance (TIKR.com, [Aug 21]); Deere rallied 7% as profit climbed and it raised its full-year forecast (TIKR.com, [Aug 21]); Estée Lauder jumped 11.8% on a sales beat (StockStory, [Aug 19]); CSL rallied 18% on its earnings beat (Livewire Markets, [Aug 17]); CoreWeave popped 14% as revenue doubled on accelerating AI infrastructure demand (CNBC, [Aug 11]).

Punished: A beat with slowing volume, a soft guide or margin doubts. Walmart beat and raised guidance, yet the stock crashed (24/7 Wall St., [Aug 21]) as its sales growth slowed (Reuters, [Aug 21]); Tapestry sank 15% despite a Q4 beat because of its FY27 guide (TIKR.com, [Aug 14]); Advance Auto Parts plunged 21% as a revenue miss overshadowed its earnings beat (24/7 Wall St., [Aug 20]); Cisco dropped 7% on gross margin fears (Yahoo Finance, [Aug 13]).

Pattern: The beat is table stakes; the market pays for accelerating volume plus a raised guide and sells any slowdown in the top line — even Walmart's raised guide could not offset slower sales growth.

Attention into EPS: Nvidia — its earnings could rescue a stalled-out market if it breaks the recent trend (MarketWatch, [Aug 21]): the beat is priced, so only a raised data-centre guide pays. Salesforce — climbed 5% before earnings as Agentforce topped $1 billion in annual sales (BigGo Finance, [Aug 21]): an AI revenue line is on the rewarded side, but the run-up raises the bar for the guide. Marvell — jumped 6% as top Wall Street firms turned bullish (TradingView, [Aug 20]) and Citi reset its target ahead of earnings (TheStreet, [Aug 21]): expectations ran up into the print, the setup that sold Applied Materials. Canada's big banks — four-quarter beat streaks (Investing.com Canada, [Aug 21]) but a tougher test after a stock selloff (Bloomberg.com, [Aug 21]); Bank of Montreal is expected to post C$3.74 a share (TradingView, [Aug 21]): a reset bar favours a beat-and-hold reaction.

Playbook: Own beat-and-raise names with visible volume, and fade AI names that rallied into the print. Pair long Ross Stores against TJX, whose "self-inflicted" slowdown clouded its beat (Reuters, [Aug 19]). Risk: tariff refunds flatter this season's beats — Walmart is using $2.9 billion of them to lower prices (CBS News, [Aug 20]) — and will not repeat.
