🧮 Earnings Pattern — What the Market Pays For

Rewarded: Accelerating demand with a guide that surprises. Snowflake jumped 23% as Q2 showed a third straight quarter of acceleration (TIKR.com, [Sep 3]); Samsara soared on a strong earnings beat and solid guide (Yahoo Finance, [Sep 3]); Dell's stock soared on blowout Q2 earnings and raised guidance (Barron's, [Sep 2]); Five Below rose 6% after a Q2 beat and a raised FY'26 outlook (Yahoo Finance, [Sep 3]).

Punished: A cut outlook, or a beat that only met extreme hopes. Lululemon shares plummeted on a guidance cut (CNBC, [Sep 3]); Broadcom fell as strong results were 'not enough to keep investors happy' (Yahoo Finance, [Sep 3]); HPE is falling despite its fiscal Q3 beat (Investor's Business Daily, [Sep 3]) on margin moderation (Seeking Alpha, [Sep 3]); Campbell's stock plunged as it slashed its dividend after sales and profit declines (Yahoo Finance, [Sep 3]).

Pattern: The beat is table stakes; the market pays for acceleration it had not modelled — software usage, AI servers, value-retail traffic — and sells any print where the guide, the margin or the dividend slips, even with strong results. Expectations, not the AI label, decide the reaction: Broadcom and HPE were priced for more than they delivered.

Attention into EPS: Oracle — faces its earnings test after a wild year riding the AI wave (Investor's Business Daily, [Sep 3]), and Barron's sees it as software's next big winner (Barron's, [Sep 3]): if usage accelerates like Snowflake's it sits on the rewarded side, but a hardware-style capex story would be judged like Broadcom. Adobe — RBC raised its target before an 'in-line' quarter (Yahoo Finance, [Sep 3]): an in-line print without acceleration is the punished profile. RH — expected to beat earnings estimates (Yahoo Finance, [Sep 3]) when it reports on September 10 (Yahoo Finance, [Sep 3]): a premium retailer, so the guide matters more than the beat, as Lululemon showed. Macy's — poised to beat estimates again (Yahoo Finance, [Sep 3]): only a raised outlook would move it.

Playbook: Own accelerating software and value retail into prints, and fade premium-priced names where only a beat is expected (Adobe, RH). Risk: rising yields compress the software winners too, and a Hormuz escalation hits every multiple at once.
