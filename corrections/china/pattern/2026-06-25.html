🧮 Earnings Pattern — What the Market Pays For

Rewarded: Beats with a stronger outlook built on scarce AI hardware and pricing power. Micron beat and its shares spiked 15% on a record $41.5 billion quarter (Forbes, [Jun 24]), with the chip shortage projected to last beyond 2027 (WSJ, [Jun 24]); Jabil surged after an AI-fueled beat (TradingView, [Jun 17]); Credo is up 21.2% after its beat and the DustPhotonics deal (Yahoo Finance, [Jun 13]). Outside tech a clean raise still paid: Johnson & Johnson climbed as it raised its outlook (International Business Times Australia, [Jun 24]).

Punished: Beats paired with weaker guidance or a higher cost of growth. Accenture tumbled 16% on a guidance cut (Yahoo Finance, [Jun 18]); Carnival dipped 5% on weaker Q3 guidance despite the beat (TIKR.com, [Jun 24]); FedEx dipped 6% despite its beat (TIKR.com, [Jun 24]); CarMax dropped 7% despite its beat (24/7 Wall St., [Jun 17]). Spending and margins hit AI names too: Oracle tumbled 11% on an increased capital raise and cash concerns (CNBC, [Jun 11]) and Cerebras fell 10% on a shrinking-margin forecast (CNBC, [Jun 23]). China exposure was sold outright — BMW dropped 8% on a profit warning (Global Banking & Finance Review, [Jun 17]).

Pattern: The beat is table stakes; the market pays for the forward line. A raised outlook backed by supply scarcity and pricing power is bought, while weaker guidance, cost inflation or heavier capex is sold on the day, however large the beat.

Attention into EPS: Nike — billed "a most critical quarter" (Seeking Alpha, [Jun 20]) as it may shake up its China business (WWD, [Jun 24]): a guidance story with China exposure, on the punished side unless the outlook turns. BlackBerry is expected to post 3 cents a share (TradingView, [Jun 23]), with Stifel seeing 36% more upside (TradingView, [Jun 24]); expectations ran up into the print, so a beat without a QNX raise risks a fade. McCormick — what to expect (Yahoo Finance, [Jun 24]) — and Darden (Yahoo Finance, [Jun 24]) are cost-and-volume tests where guidance, not the beat, decides the move.

Playbook: Own the names whose guidance is set by supply scarcity (memory and AI hardware) and fade beats in consumer, travel and IT services where the outlook is the swing factor, such as Nike. Risk: memory is crowded — options priced a 14% Micron move (Benzinga, [Jun 23]) — so any sign of supply easing hits the long side hard.
