🧮 Earnings Pattern — What the Market Pays For

Rewarded: A beat with a raised outlook on real demand. Aritzia surged the most in 18 months on earnings and a better outlook (Bloomberg.com, [Oct 9]); Accenture stock rallied after its earnings beat (CNBC, [Oct 1]); Carnival surged 12% on its Q3 results (24/7 Wall St., [Sep 29]); TCS rose 4% as AI revenue crossed $3.1 Bn (BW Businessworld, [Oct 9]).

Punished: Guidance cuts and sales trims, whatever the demand story. Delta missed Q3 estimates and cut guidance on skyrocketing fuel costs (Yahoo Finance, [Oct 9]), and the stock fell (Benzinga, [Oct 9]) despite record revenue; Nike's stock dropped 10% as results disappointed (Baystreet.ca, [Oct 2]); AngioDynamics slipped 4% despite a beat (Yahoo Finance, [Oct 8]); Jabil fell 4% despite a beat and a higher outlook (Yahoo Finance, [Sep 30]).

Pattern: Earnings beats are now the norm (CNBC, [Oct 7]), so the guide decides: raises backed by volume, bookings or AI revenue are bought, and cost-driven cuts are sold even when demand is intact, as at Delta. The exception is the washed-out stock — PepsiCo, at its lowest since 2020 into the print (TradingView, [Oct 7]), rallied despite cutting its profit guidance (CNBC, [Oct 8]).

Attention into EPS: JPMorgan and Bank of America — JPMorgan reports October 13 with $5.93 EPS expected (AlphaStreet, [Oct 8]) and BofA October 14 at $1.11 (AlphaStreet, [Oct 9]), with US banks in correction territory into results (Yahoo Finance, [Oct 9]): a low bar, so a firm NII and fee guide pays and a bare beat does not. TSMC — foreign brokerages set an NT$4,000 target into its call (BigGo Finance, [Oct 9]): demand is priced, so only a raised outlook extends the move. ASML — a preview says the high expectations may be justified (Seeking Alpha, [Oct 9]): the Jabil risk if orders merely meet them. United Airlines — Delta's profit cut puts United's next results in focus (Live and Let's Fly, [Oct 9]): fuel puts it on the warning side.

Playbook: Own guidance-raisers with visible demand into their prints and fade fuel-exposed airlines into United. Into bank week, prefer trading-heavy money centres over regionals. Risk: a PepsiCo-style low bar lets beaten-down banks rally on "less bad".
