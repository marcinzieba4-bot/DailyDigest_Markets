🧮 Earnings Pattern — What the Market Pays For

Rewarded: Forward visibility — a raise, a backlog or a credible cost plan. AeroVironment soared 28% as its backlog grew to $1.2 billion (CNBC, [Jun 29]); Progress Software is skyrocketing (Yahoo Finance, [Jul 1]) on AI demand and a raised outlook (TradingView, [Jul 1]); General Mills jumped 9.9% on a beat and up to $3 billion of savings (TradingView, [Jul 2]). Micron's beat still stands out — shares spiked 15% on a record $41.5 billion quarter (Forbes, [Jun 24]).

Punished: Beats paired with weaker guidance or rising costs — or a bar set too high. Carnival dipped 5% on weaker Q3 guidance despite the beat (TIKR.com, [Jun 24]); Cerebras fell 10% on a shrinking-margin forecast (CNBC, [Jun 23]); KPIT Tech tumbled 16% as weak Europe demand triggered a profit warning (Outlook Business, [Jul 1]); Tesla sank 7% despite a strong deliveries report (CNBC, [Jul 2]). Samsung posted an 1,800% profit jump, but AI spending concerns spooked investors (CNBC, [Jul 6]) and the shares dropped over 6% after the open (TradingKey, [Jul 6]).

Pattern: The forward line beats the print, measured against the bar: where the bar was low, a credible plan is bought even on a flat top line — General Mills beat with organic sales flat (TradingView, [Jul 1]) — while where expectations had run up, even a blowout is sold, as Tesla and now Samsung show.

Attention into EPS: TCS — Q1 results and key dates (Business Today, [Jul 6]) as Indian IT faces a muted Q1 on the AI shift and weak demand (Reuters, [Jul 6]): the punished side, but with a low bar. Delta — the Street expects $1.48 EPS on July 10 (AlphaStreet, [Jul 6]); Raymond James cut its rating ahead of Q2 (Yahoo Finance, [Jul 6]) while BofA sees a rare airline sweet spot (TheStreet, [Jul 4]): fuel savings should lift EPS, but that is priced, so it needs a raise. Banks rally ahead of earnings (Investor's Business Daily, [Jul 6]), lifting the bar for the big banks. TSMC — Citi set a street-high target ahead of its earnings call (BigGo Finance, [Jul 6]): after Samsung, a high bar.

Playbook: Fade the high-bar prints — Delta and the banks after their run-up — and buy AI hardware on post-print dips rather than into the print; memory stocks rebounded as analysts flagged a buying opportunity (Yahoo Finance UK, [Jul 6]). TCS is the low-bar long only if guidance holds. Risk: a TCS cut extends the KPIT pattern; strong bank guidance outruns the rally.
