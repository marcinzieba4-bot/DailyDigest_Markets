🧮 Earnings Pattern — What the Market Pays For

Rewarded: a beat with a clean forward line — backlog, savings or a guidance raise. AeroVironment soared 28% on its beat as backlog grew to $1.2 billion (CNBC, [Jun 29]); General Mills jumped 9.9% after its beat and up to $3 billion in savings (TradingView, [Jul 2]); Playtech jumped 18% as H1 beat expectations (NEXT.io, [Jul 9]) and it raised 2026 EBITDA guidance (iGamingFuture, [Jul 9]); WD-40 beat and the shares jumped after hours (Investing.com, [Jul 9]).

Punished: a beat that arrives with a cost, currency or peak-cycle caveat. PepsiCo's results beat but the stock fell anyway (Yahoo Finance, [Jul 9]) as it warned of higher commodity costs and faltering North American food sales (Reuters, [Jul 9]); Fast Retailing's Q3 profit jumped 45.7% with a raised forecast (The Straits Times, [Jul 9]), yet the shares slid on a yen warning (NST Online, [Jul 9]); Samsung flagged a 19-fold profit jump but slumped on jitters the AI boom may stall (Reuters, [Jul 9]); Tesla sank 7% despite strong deliveries (CNBC, [Jul 2]).

Pattern: the beat is table stakes; the market pays only for a forward line without a caveat. Any warning on commodity costs, FX or AI spend outweighs the quarter, even alongside a raised forecast.

Attention into EPS: Delta reports Friday (The Motley Fool, [Jul 9]) with the stock near highs (TheBull.com.au, [Jul 9]), and Jim Cramer thinks Delta and United can beat low forecasts (CNBC, [Jul 7]); with WTI down 21% in four weeks, a fuel-cost guide is now the upside lever. US banks: a rise in net interest income and fee income is seen lifting Wells Fargo's Q2 (TradingView, [Jul 9]) and BofA Securities expects JPMorgan and peers to beat (marketscreener.com, [Jul 7]) — rewarded only if the NII outlook holds. TSMC has lagged the chip sector into July 16 (NAI500, [Jul 9]); Citi sees it raising 2026 guidance on AI demand (Investing.com, [Jul 5]) — the forward line Samsung lacked. Netflix is down 42% from its high with earnings due July 16 (The Motley Fool, [Jul 7]) — a low bar.

Playbook: own low-bar prints with a credible guide-up (TSMC, Netflix) and fade beat-and-caveat staples on the PepsiCo template. Lean long airlines into results while fuel eases; the risk is an Iran re-escalation sending oil back up and turning the fuel line into the caveat.
