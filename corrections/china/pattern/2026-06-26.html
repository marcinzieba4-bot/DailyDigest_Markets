🧮 Earnings Pattern — What the Market Pays For

Rewarded: Beat-and-raise driven by scarce supply or a new growth engine. Micron beat and its shares spiked 15% on a record $41.5 billion quarter (Forbes, [Jun 24]), with the chip shortage projected to last beyond 2027 (WSJ, [Jun 24]); BlackBerry is up 23.7% on surging QNX and security earnings (Yahoo Finance, [Jun 25]) as it lifted its annual revenue forecast (BNN Bloomberg, [Jun 25]); Jabil surged after an AI-fueled beat (TradingView, [Jun 17]); Halfords rose 13% as profit beat and margins hit a decade high (Yahoo Finance UK, [Jun 25]).

Punished: Beats paired with weaker guidance or rising costs. Accenture tumbled 16% on a guidance cut (Yahoo Finance, [Jun 18]); Carnival dipped 5% on weaker Q3 guidance despite the beat (TIKR.com, [Jun 24]); FedEx dipped 6% despite its beat (TIKR.com, [Jun 24]); Darden fell as Olive Garden weakness clouded its beat (Bloomberg.com, [Jun 25]), forecasting annual profit below estimates on higher costs (BNN Bloomberg, [Jun 25]). Cerebras fell 10% on a shrinking-margin forecast (CNBC, [Jun 23]). China exposure was sold outright — BMW dropped 8% on a profit warning (Global Banking & Finance Review, [Jun 17]).

Pattern: The beat is table stakes; the market pays for the forward line. A raised outlook from scarce supply or a new engine (memory, QNX) is bought, while weaker guidance, cost inflation or margin pressure is sold on the day, however large the beat.

Attention into EPS: Nike — downgraded on concerns over another guidance cut (SGB Media, [Jun 23]) and billed "a most critical quarter" (Seeking Alpha, [Jun 20]); a cut is partly priced, but a possible shake-up of its China business (WWD, [Jun 24]) keeps it on the punished side unless the outlook turns. United Airlines — what to expect (Yahoo Finance, [Jun 25]) after falling fuel costs lifted it 6% (24/7 Wall St., [Jun 24]): cost relief fits the rewarded side, but it is now priced, so guidance must confirm it. Constellation Brands (TradingView, [Jun 25]) and Conagra (Yahoo Finance, [Jun 25]) are volume-and-cost tests where the outlook, not the beat, sets the move.

Playbook: Own the names whose guidance is set by supply scarcity (memory, AI hardware) and fade consumer and restaurant beats where costs eat the outlook, as at Darden — Nike and Constellation are the next tests. Risk: memory is crowded — Micron and SanDisk pulled back after fresh peaks (TradingView, [Jun 25]) — so any sign of supply easing hits the long side hard.
