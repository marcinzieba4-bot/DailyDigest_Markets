🧮 Earnings Pattern — What the Market Pays For

Rewarded: Beat-and-raise driven by scarce supply or a new growth engine. Micron beat and its shares spiked 15% on a record $41.5 billion quarter (Forbes, [Jun 24]), with the chip shortage projected to last beyond 2027 (WSJ, [Jun 24]); BlackBerry climbed 20% on an upbeat outlook (TradingView, [Jun 26]) as QNX powered growth (BNN Bloomberg, [Jun 25]); Jabil rallied as earnings and revenue topped expectations (Yahoo Finance, [Jun 17]); Halfords rose 13% as profit beat and margins hit a decade high (Yahoo Finance UK, [Jun 25]).

Punished: Beats paired with weaker guidance or rising costs. Accenture tumbled 16% on a guidance cut (Yahoo Finance, [Jun 18]); Carnival dipped 5% on weaker Q3 guidance despite the beat (TIKR.com, [Jun 24]); FedEx dipped 6% despite its beat (TIKR.com, [Jun 24]); Darden fell as Olive Garden weakness clouded its beat (Bloomberg.com, [Jun 25]), forecasting annual profit below estimates on higher costs (BNN Bloomberg, [Jun 25]); Cerebras fell 10% on a shrinking-margin forecast (CNBC, [Jun 23]). Software is sold even on a raise: Guidewire slid after a beat and a higher outlook (StocksToTrade, [Jun 26]).

Pattern: The beat is table stakes; the market pays for a raise built on something scarce (memory, QNX). Weaker guidance, cost inflation or margin pressure is sold on the day, and software gets no credit even for a raise. The winners are crowded too: Micron sank 6% to end a wild week (CNBC, [Jun 26]).

Attention into EPS: Nike reports next week (CMC Markets, [Jun 26]) against low expectations (TradingKey, [Jun 26]), after a downgrade on concerns over another guidance cut (SGB Media, [Jun 23]); with a cut priced, a steady outlook could lift it, but a second cut puts it squarely on the punished side. Airlines — earnings are set for takeoff but the stocks may be left behind (Barron's, [Jun 26]): fuel relief is known, so guidance must beat it; United is in focus (Yahoo Finance, [Jun 25]). Constellation Brands (TradingView, [Jun 25]) and Conagra (Yahoo Finance, [Jun 25]) are volume-and-cost tests where the outlook, not the beat, sets the move.

Playbook: Own scarce-supply guidance (memory, AI hardware) on dips rather than chasing it — analysts keep raising memory-cycle targets (Yahoo Finance, [Jun 27]) — and fade consumer and travel beats where costs eat the outlook. Nike is the asymmetric print: low expectations favour a relief move if guidance holds. Risk: a second Nike guidance cut, or supply easing in memory.
