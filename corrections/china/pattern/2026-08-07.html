🧮 Earnings Pattern — What the Market Pays For

Rewarded: A beat with a raised or strong forward line where the bar was not crowded. Atlassian jumped 37.4% on a strong Q2 (Yahoo Finance, [Aug 6]); Unity soared 17% after a blowout Q2 beat and a bullish outlook (Yahoo Finance Australia, [Aug 6]); Airbnb soared 9% on a beat and strong guidance for the third quarter (CNBC, [Aug 6]); Shopify jumped 25.1% on a strong Q2 (StockStory, [Aug 5]).

Punished: Any guide cut, and even a beat-and-raise in crowded corners. Honeywell Aerospace plunged 24% after a guidance cut (AskTraders, [Aug 6]); The Trade Desk sank over 26% on a miss and a weak outlook (Stocktwits, [Aug 6]); Datadog had its worst day in more than 6 years despite beating estimates (Barron's, [Aug 6]); Western Digital beat and raised its outlook but fell in premarket trading (Yahoo Finance, [Aug 6]); Restaurant Brands fell on Burger King's best quarter in years (Yahoo Finance, [Aug 6]).

Pattern: The outlook decides, and positioning sets the bar. A raise is paid where expectations were modest; where a stock is crowded — memory, peak-margin restaurants — even a beat-and-raise is sold, and a cut in a favoured end market is punished brutally.

Attention into EPS: Nvidia — AMD's results point to massive AI demand (Seeking Alpha, [Aug 5]), but AMD fell 8% on its own beat (BeInCrypto, [Aug 4]): the most crowded AI print needs a data-centre guide above the bar, not just a beat. Take-Two — all eyes are on GTA 6 updates (Seeking Alpha, [Aug 4]): a pure forward-line story, so launch timing sets the reaction. Barrick Mining — set to post Q2 earnings (Yahoo Finance, [Aug 6]) with gold at 4390: miners are under-owned against the metal, the profile where a solid guide gets paid. Simon Property — previews ask whether a beat is in store (Yahoo Finance, [Aug 6]): with mortgage rates at a one-year high, only raised guidance offsets the rate drag.

Playbook: Own outlook-raisers where the bar is low — application software showing AI monetisation, travel, gold miners — and fade crowded hardware into its print, where a beat-and-raise is already priced. Risk: softer hiring data, with ADP below expectations (Moomoo, [Aug 5]), could turn the rotation into a broad de-risking that hits winners too.
