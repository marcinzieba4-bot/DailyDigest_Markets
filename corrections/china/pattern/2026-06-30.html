🧮 Earnings Pattern — What the Market Pays For

Rewarded: Forward visibility — a raise, a backlog or scarce supply. Micron beat and its shares spiked 15% on a record $41.5 billion quarter (Forbes, [Jun 24]); AeroVironment soared 28% on a beat as its backlog grew to $1.2 billion (CNBC, [Jun 29]); BlackBerry climbed 20% on an upbeat outlook (TradingView, [Jun 26]) as QNX powered growth (BNN Bloomberg, [Jun 25]); Halfords rose 13% as profit beat and margins hit a decade high (Yahoo Finance UK, [Jun 25]). Winnebago even surged 14% on a miss (MarketBeat, [Jun 29]).

Punished: Beats paired with weaker guidance or rising costs. Accenture tumbled 16% on a guidance cut (Yahoo Finance, [Jun 18]); Carnival dipped 5% on weaker Q3 guidance despite the beat (TIKR.com, [Jun 24]); FedEx dipped 6% despite its beat (TIKR.com, [Jun 24]); Darden fell as Olive Garden weakness clouded its beat (Bloomberg.com, [Jun 25]), forecasting annual profit below estimates on higher costs (BNN Bloomberg, [Jun 25]); Cerebras fell 10% on a shrinking-margin forecast (CNBC, [Jun 23]). Software got no credit for a raise: Guidewire slid after a beat and a higher outlook (StocksToTrade, [Jun 26]).

Pattern: The print matters less than the forward line: a raise, a growing backlog or scarce supply is paid for — even through a miss — while weaker guidance, cost inflation or margin pressure is sold on the day, however large the beat.

Attention into EPS: Nike reports this week (TradingKey, [Jun 28]) with EPS and revenue estimate revisions still moving lower (Seeking Alpha, [Jun 29]): the cut is in the numbers, so guidance that holds fits the rewarded side; a further cut, the punished one. General Mills (StockStory, [Jun 29]) and Constellation Brands (Yahoo Finance, [Jun 29]) are volume-and-cost tests where the outlook, not the beat, sets the move. TSMC — UBS hiked its target ahead of Q2 earnings (Investing.com, [Jun 28]) — sits on the scarce-supply side. Big tech faces AI spending scrutiny into Q2 (Yahoo Finance, [Jun 29]): capex raises without revenue to match risk the Cerebras treatment.

Playbook: Own scarce supply and backlog (memory, TSMC, AeroVironment-style order books) on dips rather than chasing them — analysts keep raising memory-cycle targets (Yahoo Finance, [Jun 27]) — and fade staples and consumer beats where costs eat the outlook. Nike is the swing print: guidance that holds invites a relief move. Risk: supply easing in memory, or a second Nike cut.
