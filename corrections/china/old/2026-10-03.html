🇨🇳 China  Mixed  → easing bias, but incremental

Macro Stance: China remains in a fundamentally different monetary regime from the rest of the world — easing while the West tightens. The PBOC has maintained an accommodative posture through 2026, with periodic LPR trims and targeted RRR cuts aimed at supporting a property sector that continues to drag on the broader economy. The yuan remains under structural depreciation pressure given the massive rate differential with the US — a US 10Y at 5.28% versus substantially lower CGB yields creates relentless capital-outflow gravity. PBOC yuan fixings have likely been set persistently stronger than spot to manage the pace of depreciation rather than prevent it outright.

New / Most Important: The signal feed this week is relatively quiet on fresh China-specific headlines, but the global macro context has shifted against China on two fronts: (1) surging US and European yields increase the carry-trade incentive to move capital OUT of CNY assets, and (2) the tariff narrative remains actively discussed (trending on X). Copper at 6.49 (↓1.3% vs 4W ago) is a real-time demand proxy that suggests China's industrial and construction complex is not meaningfully re-accelerating. The Gold/Copper ratio at 641 — flagged as "FEAR-elevated" — is consistent with a world where safe-haven demand dominates industrial-demand optimism, a dynamic China's weakness feeds directly.

Playbook ✓: The consensus playbook has been "stimulus announced but insufficient, property still declining, deflation risk persists" — and nothing in this week's data disrupts that view. The capital-flow mechanic is also textbook: wide rate differentials and a weakening yuan drive portfolio outflows, which the PBOC partially offsets through intervention and capital controls. European and Australian exporters exposed to Chinese demand continue to face headwinds.  |  ⚠️ Divergence: The structural divergence that deserves attention is the breakdown in the traditional "China stimulus → commodity reflation → EM/AUD rally" transmission. In prior cycles, PBOC easing would eventually flow through credit creation into property, infrastructure, and commodity imports, lifting copper, iron ore, and the AUD. This cycle, the property sector is acting as a black hole for stimulus — credit isn't transmitting into physical demand because developers are deleveraging and households are refusing to re-lever. The result is that PBOC easing is producing yuan weakness and capital outflows rather than commodity demand. This explains why copper is falling despite Chinese easing and why the AUD is not benefiting despite the RBA hiking aggressively — Australia is importing tighter financial conditions from a rising-rate world while its main export customer fails to reflate. For global portfolios, this means commodity-linked EM and DM assets (Chile, Peru, Brazil, Australia) cannot rely on the China put that backstopped them in 2009, 2016, and 2020. The transmission is structurally impaired until property stabilizes, which requires household confidence — a social variable, not a monetary one.
