🇨🇳 China  Mixed  → easing bias, but constrained

Macro Stance: China remains in a structurally different monetary and fiscal posture from the rest of the world — still easing or holding while the West tightens. The PBOC has maintained an accommodative bias, but the yuan is under sustained depreciation pressure from the widening rate differential with the US (10Y at 5.34% and climbing). This creates a classic impossible trinity problem: Beijing wants lower domestic rates to support the property sector and local government refinancing, but every basis point of easing accelerates capital outflows and yuan weakness. Fiscal stimulus has been repeatedly promised but delivery has been incremental — special bonds and targeted LGFV restructuring rather than the bazooka the market keeps hoping for.

New / Most Important: The EU trade chief headed to Beijing this week amid a record trade surplus and escalating disputes over hybrid car tariff loopholes. This is significant: the EU-China trade axis is now the primary friction point, potentially more consequential near-term than US-China tensions. Europe is China's largest export market for EVs, and Brussels is tightening the screws precisely when China's export machine is one of the few bright spots in the growth picture. The "hybrid car loophole" detail suggests Chinese automakers have been structuring exports to avoid EV-specific tariffs — if Brussels closes this, it's a direct hit to China's auto export volumes. NerdWallet's "best-performing China stocks for October" piece suggests retail interest is perking up, which often marks either a genuine turn or a classic retail trap.

Playbook ✓: China's export surplus remains structurally large, the property sector remains in managed decline, and the policy response is gradual and targeted rather than transformative. Copper at 6.64 (↑0.7%) is stable but not surging — consistent with a China that is stabilizing without re-accelerating.  |  ⚠️ Divergence: The critical divergence is that China is now facing a two-front trade war — simultaneously navigating US tariffs/export controls AND EU tariff escalation. The EU front is structurally new and underpriced. Europe's trade investigation into Chinese EVs is different from US semiconductor restrictions because it targets China's highest-value-added manufacturing export — the sector Beijing has explicitly designated as the industrial policy successor to property. If both the US (tech/semis) and EU (autos/green energy) restrict Chinese exports, the surplus has to find new destinations in EM or compress, which feeds back into domestic overcapacity and deflation risk. The transmission to global assets: if China's export engine sputters, commodity demand growth disappoints (bearish copper, AUD, BRL), CGB yields compress further (widening the rate gap with the US), and the yuan weakens — which in turn pressures all Asian FX and forces other EM central banks to keep rates higher to defend currencies. European exporters with China revenue exposure (luxury, industrials, autos) face the double hit of retaliatory risk and weaker Chinese consumer spending.
