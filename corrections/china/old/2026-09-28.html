🇨🇳 China  Mixed  → easing bias but constrained

Macro Stance: China remains in a structural deceleration overlaid with cyclical easing attempts. The PBOC has been in an accommodative posture, but the effectiveness of rate cuts is blunted by the ongoing property sector deleveraging and weak consumer confidence. The yuan is under pressure from the widening US-China rate differential — with US 10Y yields surging to 5.25%, the carry incentive to move capital offshore is enormous, constraining how aggressively the PBOC can cut. Fiscal policy has relied on local government special bonds and targeted infrastructure spending, but the transmission to broader activity remains incomplete.

New / Most Important: The Trump-Xi summit prioritized "stability over big deal," which means no tariff rollback — the trade friction remains structural. China's continued control of rare earths loomed over the meeting, confirming Beijing is using its mineral leverage as a strategic bargaining chip rather than conceding ground. The copper price at $6.63 (↑1.1%) is modestly supportive, suggesting China's industrial base is not collapsing, but the muted gain vs. oil's 11.5% surge tells you this is a supply-shock world, not a China-demand-pull world. The gold/copper ratio at 627 (fear-elevated) is partly a reflection of global anxiety about the US-China structural rivalry.

Playbook ✓: The "managed deceleration" narrative holds — Beijing is drip-feeding stimulus without delivering the bazooka, keeping growth above their floor while tolerating slower credit creation. Foreign investors continue heading for the exit from Indian and broader EM equities (Bernstein's "bygone economic era" call on India large-caps), and China likely faces similar outflow pressure given the US yield advantage.  |  ⚠️ Divergence: The structural divergence is that China's rare-earth leverage is evolving from a trade weapon into a full geopolitical pricing mechanism. Rather than simply restricting exports, Beijing appears to be using supply control to extract concessions across multiple bilateral fronts simultaneously — semiconductors, AI, and military tech. This is a fundamentally new form of commodity weaponization that has no historical analog in the WTO era. The transmission to global assets runs through the entire EV/battery/defense supply chain: any tightening of rare-earth quotas directly hits European auto OEMs (who have no domestic supply), US defense primes, and Japanese electronics manufacturers. The market is not pricing the tail risk of a full rare-earth embargo because it seems "too extreme," but the summit's failure to produce a deal means the probability is rising, not falling. AUD is a second-derivative casualty — if China throttles industrial activity to manage the property downturn while simultaneously weaponizing mineral exports, Australia's bulk commodity volumes face a two-front squeeze.
