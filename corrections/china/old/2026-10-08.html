🇨🇳 China  Mixed  → easing bias / liquidity expansion

Macro Stance: China remains in a fundamentally different monetary regime from the rest of the world — easing while the Fed, ECB, and RBA hike. The PBOC has been maintaining accommodative conditions to support a fragile recovery, with the policy divergence against the US widening further after this week's hawkish FOMC minutes. The yuan is under persistent depreciation pressure from the rate differential, and the PBOC's management of the CNY fix vs spot remains a daily balancing act between supporting growth and preventing disorderly capital outflows.

New / Most Important: The standout data point this week is the RMB 4.66 trillion in foreign capital inflows reported Oct 08, with the headline framing these as "new buyers" repricing Chinese assets. This is a structurally significant signal that deserves unpacking. Despite persistent Western skepticism about China's growth trajectory, property stress, and geopolitical risk, a substantial volume of foreign capital is flowing in — likely a combination of EM-dedicated funds reallocating from overvalued Indian/LatAm positions, sovereign wealth funds diversifying away from US duration risk at 5.25% yields, and Asian institutional money front-running a potential PBOC easing acceleration. The repricing narrative suggests these flows are going into Chinese government bonds (where yields offer carry relative to yen-funded positions) and selectively into equities, particularly in sectors benefiting from policy support. This challenges the dominant "China uninvestable" narrative that has prevailed in Western allocator circles. If sustained, these inflows will support the yuan, ease the PBOC's FX management burden, and create a positive feedback loop for domestic asset prices.

Playbook ✓: EU-China trade tensions escalating with the trade chief's visit this week is on-script — the tariff/subsidy dispute over EVs, steel, and green tech is a slow-burn structural conflict. Copper at 6.57 (↑1.6%) holding steady suggests China's industrial base isn't collapsing despite headlines.  |  ⚠️ Divergence: The RMB 4.66 trillion inflow number directly contradicts the consensus positioning, which remains heavily underweight China across global portfolios. This is the classic capital-flow divergence that drives multi-quarter re-ratings. The transmission mechanism to global assets is direct: if foreign capital continues entering China, it reduces CNY depreciation pressure, which in turn eases the PBOC's constraint on further domestic easing. More easing means better activity data, which is bullish for copper (Australia's key export), EM commodity exporters, and European luxury/industrial exporters with China revenue. Conversely, it is mildly negative for the dollar — any stabilization in CNY removes one pillar of DXY strength. The gold/copper ratio at 632 (fear-elevated) suggests the market hasn't priced in a China re-acceleration scenario; if these inflows are real and sustained, that ratio should compress as copper outperforms gold. Watch Hong Kong-listed equities as the leading indicator — if Hang Seng tech names start breaking higher, it confirms the repricing is broadening beyond bonds.
