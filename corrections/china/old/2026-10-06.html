🇨🇳 China  Mixed  → easing bias, constrained by FX

Macro Stance: China remains in an accommodative monetary posture, but the PBOC's ability to ease further is constrained by the widening US-China rate differential — with US 10Y at 5.28% and Chinese CGB yields substantially lower, further cuts risk accelerating CNY depreciation. The signal feed shows the EU trade chief heading to China for talks, underscoring the ongoing trade-friction overlay. The copper price at 6.65 (↓1.3% vs 4W) and the elevated Gold/Copper ratio at 629 (FEAR-elevated) both reflect skepticism about Chinese demand impulse — if a real stimulus-driven industrial reacceleration were underway, copper would be leading, not lagging.

New / Most Important: The most important development this week is actually what is NOT in the feed: silence on new stimulus. After repeated promises of Politburo/State Council/NDRC intervention, the absence of concrete announcements is itself a signal. The EU-China trade engagement is notable — Brussels is navigating a delicate balance between tariff threats (particularly on EVs and solar) and maintaining export market access. The oil/copper ratio at 13.46 argues for a supply-shock narrative rather than a demand-driven commodity rally, which further undercuts the "China recovery" thesis. Property sector stress and LGFV refinancing pressures remain structural drags that stimulus-to-date has not resolved — the transmission mechanism from PBOC liquidity injections to actual credit creation in the real economy remains broken as long as household balance sheets are impaired by falling home prices and developers remain in distress.

Playbook ✓: Stimulus promises exceeding stimulus delivery is playing out as bears expected; copper weakness confirms insufficient demand pull. The yuan remains under pressure given the massive rate gap with the US, which limits PBOC room to cut.  |  ⚠️ Divergence: The structural divergence is that China's deflation-risk economy should theoretically be the largest beneficiary of global capital seeking growth — but capital is flowing OUT, not in. The combination of rising US yields (pulling capital toward the dollar), property-sector balance sheet damage, and geopolitical tariff risk (EU trade chief visit signals active friction) creates a triple lock on inflows. This matters globally because China has historically been the marginal bid for commodities, and its absence is showing up in copper and oil. For Australia, European exporters (especially autos and luxury), and EM commodity producers, the China channel remains the key macro transmission mechanism. If the PBOC allows further yuan weakness to compensate for the lack of fiscal firepower, it exports deflation to trading partners — particularly ASEAN and Korea — and risks triggering a competitive devaluation cycle in EM Asia.
