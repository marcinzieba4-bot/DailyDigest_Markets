🇨🇳 China  Mixed  → easing bias / stimulus posture

Macro Stance: The signal feed is notably thin on direct China data this week, which is itself informative—the market's attention has rotated away from China toward the US rate-hike drama and European bond selloff. The PBOC is likely maintaining its easing bias given persistent deflationary pressures and property-sector stress that have dominated prior cycles. The broader macro picture remains one where Beijing has repeatedly promised stimulus that underdelivers relative to the scale of the property and LGFV overhang.

New / Most Important: The most relevant signal for China this week comes indirectly: copper at 6.58 (↓1.7% vs 4W ago) and the elevated oil/copper ratio at 15.05 both suggest that global demand-pull from China's industrial economy remains weak. The gold/copper ratio at 633 (labeled "FEAR-elevated") is a classic capital-flow signal that real-economy demand (copper) is being outbid by fear-driven safe-haven demand (gold). Asian stock markets are churning on tech and China economy outlooks per the Yahoo Finance headline. Korea's stock investment losses are hurting domestic consumption, a second-order spillover from weak Asian equity markets that typically traces back to China sentiment and regional trade linkages.

Playbook ✓: The pattern of stimulus announcements followed by disappointing implementation continues. CGB yields remain structurally low as domestic capital has few alternatives with property uninvestable and equity markets listless. Foreign flows remain cautious given geopolitical risk and the persistent deflation/property overhang.

⚠️ Divergence: The real divergence is what's NOT happening. With US 10Y yields surging to 5.15% and potential Fed hikes on the table, the US-China rate differential is now extreme. This should be producing massive capital outflow pressure on the yuan, which in turn forces the PBOC into a policy trilemma: defend the currency (tighten liquidity, hurt growth), let it depreciate (risk capital flight and imported commodity inflation from $99 oil), or impose capital controls (damage foreign investor confidence). The copper weakness suggests the PBOC is choosing to accept a weaker growth impulse rather than aggressively ease and risk currency instability. The transmission to global assets is direct: weak Chinese demand = headwind for AUD (Aussie dollar already falling to July lows), European industrial exporters (VW flagging "enormous pressure"), and EM commodity exporters. Until Beijing delivers a fiscal package that credibly addresses the property debt stock—not just rate cuts—the deflationary gravity will persist.
