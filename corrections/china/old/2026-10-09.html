🇨🇳 China  Bullish  ↓ easing bias / accommodative

Macro Stance: The PBOC remains in an easing posture, diverging sharply from the Fed and ECB's hiking cycles. This monetary policy divergence creates persistent yuan depreciation pressure, which Beijing has been managing through daily fixings and FX intervention signals. Fiscal policy has turned incrementally more stimulative through State Council channels, with special bond issuance and targeted property-sector support measures accumulating over recent months. The structural picture remains one of an economy navigating deflationary pressures domestically while facing an increasingly hostile external trade environment.

New / Most Important: Reuters' Breakingviews ran a significant piece arguing China's "slow bull market is poised to take off" — this is a notable shift in Western sell-side framing, moving from perpetual bearishness to constructive. This matters because it signals potential for a reversal in the massive foreign outflows that have plagued Chinese equities over the past two years. On trade, Europe's tensions with China were described as "coming to a head" with the EU trade chief heading to Beijing, while the US tariff regime under Trump continues to redirect global supply chains. Copper at 6.69 (↑3.4% vs 4W) is quietly confirming that Chinese industrial demand is not collapsing — this is a key real-economy tell. The Oil/Copper ratio construct shows a supply-shock narrative dominating, but copper's bid suggests Chinese physical demand is providing a floor.

Playbook ✓: The consensus script says China is a value trap — cheap but uninvestable due to property overhang, LGFV risk, and geopolitical discount. That script has been correct for two years and the persistent foreign outflows reflect it. EU-China trade friction escalating is also on-script as Brussels follows Washington's containment posture.  |  ⚠️ Divergence: The Reuters "slow bull" piece represents a potential inflection in narrative that could catalyze a positioning reversal. Global allocators are at historic underweights to China — any shift from "uninvestable" to "tactical opportunity" would create a violent flow reversal given how much capital has been pulled. The transmission mechanic is critical: if foreign flows return to Chinese equities even modestly, it would strengthen the yuan (reducing PBOC's intervention burden), ease domestic financial conditions further, and create a positive feedback loop into commodities — particularly copper and iron ore — that would benefit AUD and EM commodity exporters. The Gold/Copper ratio at 630 (FEAR-elevated) would compress rapidly in this scenario. The EU-China trade chief visit is a wildcard: any de-escalation on EV tariffs or trade barriers would be a catalyst for European auto stocks (currently at distressed levels) and Chinese exporters simultaneously. The structural question is whether Beijing can deliver enough fiscal stimulus to stabilize property without triggering a debt spiral at the LGFV level — so far, the approach has been measured to the point of disappointment, but incremental rather than big-bang stimulus may be the more sustainable path.
