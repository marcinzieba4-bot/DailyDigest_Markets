🇨🇳 China  Mixed  → easing bias maintained

Macro Stance: China remains in a fundamentally different monetary cycle from the rest of the world — the PBOC is maintaining an easing bias while the Fed, ECB, RBA, and BOJ are all tightening or holding at restrictive levels. This divergence is the single most important global macro variable, as it drives CNY depreciation pressure, forces reserve management across EM Asia, and creates a liquidity vacuum that pulls capital toward dollar assets. The copper price at 6.73 (↑5.6%) is sending a constructive signal on industrial demand, but the gold/copper ratio at 621 (FEAR-elevated) suggests risk-aversion still dominates the broader macro read.

New / Most Important: The most material signal this week is the South China Morning Post highlighting Charlie Munger's disciple calling Chinese stocks an "undervalued opportunity." This is notable not for the call itself but for the timing — it arrives after a prolonged washout in foreign positioning. EU-China trade tensions are "coming to a head" per the NYT, with the potential for retaliatory tariff escalation on both sides. The absence of fresh stimulus announcements from the State Council or NDRC this week is itself a signal — Beijing appears to be in a holding pattern, likely waiting for post-US-election clarity before deploying the next fiscal bazooka. Meanwhile, the oil/copper ratio at 14.06 is falling (oil ↓10.6% vs copper ↑5.6%), which is classically a demand-led commodity signal rather than supply-shock — this is consistent with Chinese industrial activity stabilizing even as energy markets soften.

Playbook ✓: CNY weakness tracking the rate differential with the US is consensus and playing out. Foreign investors remain structurally underweight Chinese equities. Property sector stress continues to weigh on sentiment without a systemic credit event.  |  ⚠️ Divergence: The copper/oil divergence is the tell. Copper rising 5.6% while oil falls 10.6% in the same 4-week window is structurally significant — it suggests China's industrial and infrastructure complex is absorbing real physical demand (copper) while the energy-intensive, consumer-facing economy (oil as a transportation/consumption proxy) remains weak. This bifurcation maps onto what we see in policy: the State Council is directing credit into manufacturing, green energy, and infrastructure while consumption stimulus has been notably absent. For global portfolios, this means being long industrial metals and their Australian/Latin American producers while remaining cautious on energy. The transmission to global markets is direct: AUD is finding support from copper (RBA hawkishness + terms of trade), European luxury exporters remain exposed to weak Chinese consumer spending, and EM commodity exporters with copper/nickel exposure (Chile, Indonesia) are outperforming oil-centric peers. The "undervalued opportunity" thesis in Chinese equities is correct on a valuation basis but premature until Beijing delivers consumption-side stimulus or the property sector stabilizes — until then, the trade is commodities-as-China-proxy, not Chinese equities directly.
