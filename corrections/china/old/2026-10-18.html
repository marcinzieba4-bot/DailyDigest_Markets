🇨🇳 China  Mixed  → easing bias but constrained

Macro Stance: China remains in a structurally different cycle from the rest of the world. While the US, ECB, and RBA are all dealing with inflation re-acceleration and rate hike risk, the PBOC is operating in a disinflationary-to-deflationary environment with persistent property sector drag. The policy stance is easing-biased but constrained by yuan depreciation pressure — every time the Fed tightens, the PBOC's room to cut narrows because further rate differentials accelerate capital outflows and yuan weakness. This is the fundamental bind: China needs looser money domestically but can't afford the FX consequences externally.

New / Most Important: No dedicated China headlines appeared in this week's signal feed, which itself is a signal. The absence of fresh stimulus announcements, property stabilization news, or PMI surprises suggests Beijing is in a holding pattern — likely waiting to see the outcome of the US election and the Fed's October decision before deploying further fiscal firepower. Copper at 6.57 (down 0.6% over four weeks) and oil at 99.02 (down 1.3%) are both soft, consistent with weak Chinese demand impulse rather than a re-acceleration narrative. The Gold/Copper ratio at 636 (fear-elevated) tells us the commodities complex is pricing safety over growth — directionally bearish for China's industrial cycle.

Playbook ✓: The "stimulus promise, underwhelming delivery" pattern continues. Markets have learned that Politburo/State Council signaling does not reliably translate into credit creation or property stabilization at scale. Property developer stress remains the dominant transmission mechanism: weak home prices → household balance sheet contraction → retail consumption drag → CPI/PPI deflation → further PBOC easing desire → yuan pressure → constrained easing. This loop has been running for over two years now and shows no signs of breaking.  |  ⚠️ Divergence: The divergence that global macro funds should focus on is the growing disconnect between China's deflation trap and the global inflation re-acceleration everywhere else. Historically, China's deflation exported disinflationary pressure to developed markets through cheap goods. But if the US, EU, and Australia are all hiking into domestic inflation, the transmission channel has broken — tariffs, friend-shoring, and supply chain restructuring have partially severed the link. This means China cannot "export its way out" as easily as in prior cycles because destination markets are both protectionist and inflation-constrained. The implication for global capital flows is significant: CNY weakness is no longer an effective competitive devaluation tool when tariff walls absorb the FX advantage. Chinese government bond yields remain well below DM peers, making CGB carry deeply unattractive for foreign capital. The Hang Seng and CSI 300 [verify current levels] remain hostage to this structural bind. The key catalyst to watch is whether Beijing shifts from monetary stimulus (which isn't working) to genuine fiscal transfer — direct household income support rather than infrastructure spending — which would be a paradigm break for Chinese policymaking.
