🇨🇳 China  Mixed  → easing bias, truce stability

Macro Stance: China remains in a structural easing posture. The PBOC has been operating with an accommodative bias through LPR cuts and targeted RRR adjustments, though the feed does not provide a fresh print this week. The yuan remains under managed depreciation pressure, with the PBOC likely maintaining a tight fixing spread to prevent disorderly weakening. The fundamental backdrop is one where monetary easing has limited transmission — the classic "pushing on a string" problem when the property channel is impaired and private-sector confidence is low. Fiscal policy has been the primary lever, with special bond issuance and local government financing vehicles (LGFVs) doing the heavy lifting, though actual stimulus delivery continues to lag announcements.

New / Most Important: The most significant signal this week is the Trump-Xi truce extension, with both sides prioritizing stability over a "big deal." This is a de-escalation that removes tail risk from trade — the posture shifted from deal-seeking to coexistence. For markets, this means tariff rates are frozen at current levels rather than escalating, which provides a predictable planning horizon for Chinese exporters but does not unlock new demand. The copper price (6.62, ↑1.8%) is modestly supportive but not screaming Chinese reflation. The gold/copper ratio at 633 (FEAR-elevated) tells us global capital still prefers defensive positioning over a China-recovery bet. If China were genuinely reflating, we'd expect copper outperforming gold — the opposite is happening.

Playbook ✓: The truce extension is consensus — nobody expected an escalation heading into US midterms, and Xi has no incentive to provoke. Property sector weakness continuing to weigh on domestic demand and CGB yields remaining suppressed relative to global peers is all within the script.  |  ⚠️ Divergence: The divergence is in the transmission mechanism from Chinese policy to global assets. Historically, Chinese easing cycles lifted commodity demand (benefiting AUD, BRL, copper, iron ore) and European luxury/auto exports within 2-3 quarters. This cycle, the transmission is broken. Chinese credit creation is being absorbed by LGFV refinancing and property sector life-support rather than flowing into new fixed-asset investment or consumer spending. This means global assets that are priced for a Chinese recovery — Australian resource stocks, European luxury goods, industrial metals beyond copper — face a persistent air pocket. The AUD is particularly vulnerable because the RBA just hiked into a domestic slowdown (see below) while its biggest external demand engine isn't firing. For EM allocators, a China that eases but doesn't reflate is the worst of both worlds: it suppresses commodity export revenues while generating competitive currency pressure on Asian EM manufacturers. The truce stability actually reinforces this stagnant equilibrium — without trade shock escalation, there's no catalyst for Beijing to deploy the fiscal bazooka that markets keep waiting for.
