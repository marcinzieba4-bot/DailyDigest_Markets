🇨🇳 China  Mixed  → Easing bias maintained

Macro Stance: China remains in an easing posture with the PBOC maintaining accommodative liquidity conditions against a backdrop of persistent deflationary pressures and property sector stress. The yuan is under pressure from the widening US-China rate differential — with US 10Y at 5.28% and Chinese sovereign yields substantially lower, the carry incentive to move capital out of China is intense. The PBOC has likely been actively managing CNY fixings to prevent disorderly depreciation, but the fundamental gravity is toward weakness. Fiscal stimulus has been promised repeatedly via Politburo and State Council channels, but the transmission from announcement to real-economy impact continues to disappoint — a pattern now well into its second year.

New / Most Important: The signal feed this week is notably quiet on fresh China data releases, which itself is informative — the market's attention has rotated to the US labor market and European inflation. However, the cross-border capital flow dynamics are worsening structurally. With US yields surging to 5.28%, the cost of hedging CNY exposure for foreign investors has become prohibitive, accelerating the FPI outflow dynamic visible across EM Asia (the BOI/Alice Blue headline on dollar strength challenging FPI flows into India applies equally to China). Copper at 6.55 and falling (↓0.4% vs 4W) is a real-time referendum on China's demand trajectory — if the stimulus were gaining traction, Doctor Copper would be rising, not slipping. The Gold/Copper ratio at 636 (fear-elevated) further confirms that the market is pricing geopolitical hedging over China-led industrial demand.

Playbook ✓: The playbook of "stimulus announced, underwhelming delivery, property continues to drag" remains firmly intact. CGB yields likely remain low as the PBOC prioritizes liquidity, and the rate differential with the US keeps widening, pressuring the yuan. Foreign outflows from Chinese equities continue as global allocators rotate toward US mega-cap AI winners.  |  ⚠️ Divergence: The structural divergence is that China's easing cycle is now running directly against a global tightening impulse — the RBA just hiked to 15-year highs, the ECB faces pressure to hike again, and the Fed holds at restrictive levels. This is unusual: typically when China eases, it pulls EM Asia along with it and provides a demand floor for commodities. Instead, China's easing is being offset by dollar strength and capital outflows, meaning the stimulus leaks abroad rather than reflating the domestic economy. The transmission mechanism to global assets is through commodities — copper's failure to rally despite accommodative Chinese policy suggests that either (a) the property sector is absorbing liquidity without generating construction activity, or (b) LGFV debt restructuring is consuming fiscal resources that would otherwise reach the real economy. For AUD and EM commodity exporters, this is the key risk: China is easing but the demand impulse isn't arriving. European luxury and industrial exporters exposed to Chinese consumer demand remain vulnerable.
