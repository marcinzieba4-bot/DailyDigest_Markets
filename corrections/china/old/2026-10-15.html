🇨🇳 China  Mixed  → easing bias, constrained

Macro Stance: China is operating in a fundamentally different monetary universe from the rest of the world. While the Fed, ECB, RBA, and BOJ are all tightening or contemplating hikes, the PBOC remains in an easing posture — constrained by the need to defend the yuan against a strong dollar while simultaneously trying to reflate a domestic economy plagued by property-sector distress and deflationary pressures. The copper price at 6.56 (↓0.4% vs 4W ago) and the Oil/Copper ratio at 14.74 (rising = supply shock, not demand-led) tell you that China's industrial demand impulse remains anemic. This is a global liquidity bifurcation — dollar tightening colliding with yuan easing — that creates enormous cross-border flow stress.

New / Most Important: The signal feed is notably quiet on direct China data this week — no fresh PMI, credit, or property headlines — which itself is informative. The Papergames controversy trending heavily on X (#HoldPapergamesAccountable, #CNInvestigatePapergames, #中国上海应彻查叠纸) is a consumer sentiment/regulatory signal worth watching: it indicates rising social media-driven corporate accountability pressure in China's gaming/entertainment sector, reminiscent of the 2021 tech crackdown cycle. Global X's EMM (EM ex-China) ETF commentary being highlighted suggests institutional allocators continue to deliberately exclude China from EM allocations. This structural underweight means any positive surprise (stimulus escalation, property floor) would trigger a violent short-covering rally — but the flow setup remains hostile as long as the dollar is strengthening and US yields are surging.

Playbook ✓: Property sector remains a drag, deflationary undercurrents persist, and the PBOC is caught between easing impulses and currency defense — all consistent with the "muddle-through" consensus. The copper signal confirms no industrial acceleration.  |  ⚠️ Divergence: The structural divergence is the widening US-China rate differential. With the US 10Y at 5.09% and potentially moving higher on hike risk, while CGB yields are far lower due to PBOC easing, capital flow pressure on the yuan is intensifying. Normally this forces the PBOC to tighten or intervene heavily via the fixing, creating a domestic liquidity squeeze that undermines the stimulus objective. The fact that copper is flat-to-down while oil is elevated (supply shock dynamic) means China gets the worst of both worlds: expensive energy imports without the demand-driven commodity price signal that would indicate recovery. For global markets, the transmission mechanism runs through AUD (Australia's bulk commodity exposure), European luxury/auto exporters (demand disappointment), and EM sovereign credit (if China doesn't pull commodities higher, commodity-exporting EMs lose their growth catalyst). The EMM ex-China construction is a direct institutional expression of this pessimism — but it also means China positioning is so light that the asymmetry on any genuine fiscal bazooka is extreme.
