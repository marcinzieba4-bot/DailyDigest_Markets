🇨🇳 China  Bearish  → easing bias, limited firepower

Macro Stance: The PBOC remains in an accommodative posture but is constrained by the yuan's fragility against a surging US dollar and widening US-China rate differentials. With US 10Y yields at 5.24%, the spread against CGB yields is at or near historic wides, creating persistent capital outflow pressure and limiting the PBOC's room for further LPR cuts or RRR reductions without triggering disorderly yuan depreciation. Monetary easing has been incremental rather than transformative — liquidity injections through MLF and reverse repos are maintaining interbank stability but not transmitting into a meaningful credit impulse. Fiscal policy is doing the heavy lifting through special bond issuance and local government refinancing, but the scale remains insufficient relative to the property sector's ongoing balance sheet contraction.

New / Most Important: The dominant new signal this week is the escalation of EU-China trade tensions (NYT [Oct 7]). Europe's trade confrontation with China is "coming to a head" — this is structurally significant because the EU has been China's most accessible export market as US tariffs bite. If European tariffs on Chinese EVs, steel, or green-tech exports materialize or escalate further, it directly compresses China's trade surplus channel, which has been the primary offset to domestic demand weakness. Copper at 6.75 (↑6.6% vs 4W) is a tell — it suggests some restocking or supply-side tightness rather than Chinese demand pull, given the Oil/Copper ratio is at 13.90 and described as "supply shock narrative." The Gold/Copper ratio at 621 (FEAR-elevated) is consistent with global risk hedging rather than a China-driven industrial recovery.

Playbook ✓: The deflationary domestic cycle continues. Property developer stress, weak household confidence, and local government fiscal strain are all following the well-documented Japan-style balance sheet recession playbook. Export volumes have been compensating for weak domestic demand, and the trade surplus remains large. Policy announcements continue to outpace actual delivered stimulus.  |  ⚠️ Divergence: The critical divergence is the simultaneous closure of both major export corridors — the US through tariffs and now the EU through its own trade action. If Brussels follows through on trade restrictions, China faces a structural external demand shock layered on top of an unresolved domestic demand deficit. The transmission to global assets is direct and multi-channel: Australian exports and AUD are vulnerable as China's import demand for commodities softens; European luxury and industrial exporters (LVMH, BMW, BASF) lose their single largest growth market; and EM commodity exporters from Brazil to Indonesia face weaker terms of trade. Copper's recent strength likely reflects supply disruption rather than Chinese pull-through — the Oil/Copper ratio supports this interpretation. A China that cannot export its way out of deflation and will not fiscally stimulate at scale becomes a persistent global disinflationary force, which paradoxically could ease pressure on DM central banks but crushes EM capital flows. The PBOC's toolkit is not broken, but it is pinned: every basis point of easing widens the carry differential against the US and accelerates capital flight through both official and grey channels.
