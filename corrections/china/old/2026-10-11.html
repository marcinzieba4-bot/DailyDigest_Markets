🇨🇳 China  Mixed  → accommodative / easing bias

Macro Stance: The PBOC remains in an accommodative posture, maintaining easing bias via liquidity operations and LPR guidance while managing yuan depreciation pressure against a surging dollar. The yuan is under structural pressure from the widening US-China rate differential — with US yields at 5.24% and Chinese sovereign yields far lower, carry trade dynamics continue to pull capital westward. Fiscal policy has leaned into selective stimulus (special bonds, LGFV restructuring) but delivery continues to lag promises, a pattern that has persisted for over a year.

New / Most Important: Reuters this week published a notable "Breakingviews" piece arguing China's slow bull market is "poised to take off" — this is significant because it represents the first major Western financial media outlet turning structurally constructive on Chinese equities after two years of consensus bearishness. However, Yahoo Finance simultaneously highlighted that the Chinese stock market is missing the global AI rally, creating a paradox: the world's second-largest AI ecosystem is not being priced as an AI beneficiary. EU-China trade tensions came to a head this past week with the EU trade chief traveling to Beijing as Brussels prepared pre-enlargement trade reforms — any tariff escalation on Chinese EVs and green-tech exports would directly pressure the current account surplus that has been one of China's few macro bright spots.

Playbook ✓: Property sector remains in controlled deflation with no V-shaped recovery. LGFV restructuring is proceeding incrementally. Export data continues to benefit from supply-chain diversification flows (China→ASEAN→US triangle trade), partially insulating the trade surplus from direct tariff impact. Copper at 6.69 (↑3.4%) is consistent with Chinese manufacturing demand holding up better than sentiment surveys suggest.  |  ⚠️ Divergence: The structural divergence is China's exclusion from the global AI equity narrative despite being the world's largest semiconductor importer and having significant domestic AI capabilities (Baidu, Alibaba, ByteDance). This creates an asymmetric setup. If the "slow bull" thesis gains institutional traction, the re-rating catalyst won't be property recovery or consumption — it will be AI re-pricing. The transmission mechanic to global assets is significant: a Chinese equity re-rating would pull capital from US mega-cap tech (the relative trade), strengthen the yuan (reducing PBOC intervention needs and freeing up domestic liquidity), support AUD and copper (both are China beta plays — copper already at ↑3.4%), and pressure European exporters of luxury and autos who have been benefiting from weak Chinese demand keeping competition muted. The gold/copper ratio at 630 would likely compress sharply in a China re-rating scenario as copper outperforms defensive gold. The Indonesia sovereign wealth fund's call for stock market transparency this week also signals EM institutional capital is looking for new allocation targets — a transparent Chinese equity market reform would unlock flows currently sitting on the sidelines.
