🇨🇳 China  Bearish  → Easing but ineffective

Macro Stance: China is conspicuously absent from this week's signal feed, and that silence IS the signal. With the US 10Y at 5.23% and the PBOC needing to ease, the rate differential is at a punishing extreme that constrains Chinese monetary policy. Any aggressive PBOC easing would accelerate CNY depreciation against a strong dollar, forcing Beijing to choose between stimulating the domestic economy and defending currency stability. The copper price at 6.54 (↑0.6%) is barely moving — in a genuine Chinese recovery, copper would be surging. The Gold/Copper ratio at 641 ("fear-elevated") is screaming that the global market is pricing safety over industrial demand, which is a proxy for Chinese activity weakness.

New / Most Important: The feed's most relevant China-adjacent signal is Japan's carry trade dynamics and the broader EM capital flow pressure from dollar strength. Indonesia's central bank (BI) is preparing monetary strategies to safeguard the rupiah, and Vietnam flagged net foreign capital outflows and Fed rates as shadows over its market — both of these are downstream effects of the US-China rate differential that make the entire Asian capital flow complex fragile. The BOJ's hawkish debates (see Japan below) matter for China because a stronger yen would partially relieve CNY depreciation pressure, but BOJ hike bets faded this week, which is net-negative for the yuan. Without explicit PBOC signals in the feed, we must read the absence: no new stimulus announcements, no LPR cuts, no RRR moves — suggesting Beijing is in a holding pattern, probably because the currency constraint is binding.

Playbook ✓: Property sector remains structurally impaired; deflation risk persists; LGFV refinancing continues to absorb policy bandwidth without generating growth — this is the well-known muddle-through scenario.  |  ⚠️ Divergence: The structural divergence is that China has become the missing pillar in the global commodity demand story. Oil at 92.18 is elevated but driven by supply dynamics (US drillers adding rigs for multiple consecutive weeks suggests supply discipline, not demand surge). If China were genuinely recovering, we would see copper screaming higher, not flatlined at +0.6%. The Oil/Copper ratio at 14.10 is consistent with a supply shock narrative, not a demand-led commodity move — this ratio is the clearest indicator that China's industrial engine is not providing the marginal bid. For global portfolios, this means the commodity complex is fragile: any supply normalization in oil would expose the demand vacuum underneath. AUD, which should be a China-recovery play, is instead hostage to the RBA's domestic inflation fight (see Australia). European luxury and auto exporters who depend on Chinese consumer demand remain in the danger zone. The transmission mechanism to watch is whether Chinese authorities attempt a large fiscal package in Q4, which would be the only way to break the current capital-flow trap — but with the US hiking, any stimulus gets partially exported via a weaker CNY rather than recirculated domestically.
