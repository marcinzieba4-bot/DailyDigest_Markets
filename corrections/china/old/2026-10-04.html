🇨🇳 China  Mixed  → easing bias / accommodative

Macro Stance: The PBOC maintains an easing posture amid ongoing deflationary undercurrents and property sector fragility. Liquidity operations remain supportive, with the yuan under pressure as the US-China rate differential stays wide — the 10Y UST at 5.28% vs structurally lower CGB yields creates a gravitational pull on capital outflows. Fiscal policy rhetoric from the State Council continues to emphasize targeted stimulus, but the gap between promised and delivered remains the key market frustration.

New / Most Important: The most constructive signal this week came from Hong Kong: IPO proceeds more than doubled in the first nine months of 2026 to HK$388 billion, per Financial Secretary Paul Chan. This is a significant capital markets recovery story. NerdWallet's "7 Best-Performing China Stocks for October 2026" coverage signals that retail investor attention is returning to China exposure. The HK IPO surge is particularly noteworthy because it suggests that global and regional capital is re-engaging with Chinese corporate equity issuance after years of drought — a leading indicator of shifting flow dynamics. However, mainland macro data remains the missing piece: the feed carries no fresh PMI, CPI/PPI, or credit/TSF releases this week, meaning we're in an information vacuum on the real economy heading into Q4.

Playbook ✓: The HK IPO recovery fits the playbook of China re-opening its capital account channel through Hong Kong as the preferred conduit for offshore capital engagement. Copper at 6.55 (down 0.4% vs 4W) is consistent with muted Chinese industrial demand — no upside surprise from the factory floor.  |  ⚠️ Divergence: The HK IPO doubling is structurally significant and potentially under-appreciated by global allocators still anchored to the 2023-24 "China uninvestable" narrative. The mechanics matter: HK$388B in IPO proceeds implies substantial primary market demand, meaning real money — likely Middle Eastern sovereign wealth funds, Southeast Asian family offices, and mainland QDII — is underwriting new Chinese corporate equity at scale. This creates a positive feedback loop: successful IPOs attract more issuers, which attract more sellside coverage, which brings in more allocators. The transmission to global assets is through the commodity channel (renewed construction/infrastructure activity if fiscal stimulus finally arrives would lift copper, iron ore, and AUD) and through the European exporter channel (luxury goods, autos, industrials all leveraged to Chinese consumer recovery). The risk is that this IPO surge is a supply-side phenomenon — Chinese companies rushing to list while the window is open — rather than genuine demand-side recovery. Watch whether secondary market performance of new listings holds up; if post-IPO returns are negative, the window will close rapidly.
