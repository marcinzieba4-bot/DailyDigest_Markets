🇨🇳 China  Mixed  → accommodative but constrained

Macro Stance: China remains in a fundamentally different monetary regime from the rest of the world — while the Fed, ECB, RBA, and potentially the BOJ are all hiking or holding at restrictive levels, the PBOC has been in easing mode to counteract deflationary pressures and a property sector that refuses to stabilize. However, the strong dollar and rising US yields severely constrain the PBOC's room to cut further, as aggressive easing would accelerate CNY depreciation and capital outflows. This creates a policy straitjacket: the domestic economy needs lower rates, but the external balance sheet can't afford them.

New / Most Important: The signal feed this week is relatively quiet on China-specific data, but the global macro backdrop has shifted materially against Beijing. The US 10Y at 5.10% and a potential Fed hike create maximum pressure on the CNY fixing — every basis point the Fed adds widens the yield differential and incentivizes carry-trade outflows from China. Copper at 6.53 (↓1.3% vs 4W) is a real-time demand proxy for Chinese industrial activity, and its weakness while oil rises (Oil/Copper ratio at 15.53) tells a clear story: this is a supply-shock world, not a demand recovery. The elevated Gold/Copper ratio at 640 further confirms that global capital is pricing fear over growth — a terrible signal for China's export-dependent manufacturing base and its hopes of stimulus-driven reflation.

Playbook ✓: Property remains the dominant drag; stimulus announcements continue to underwhelm in scale relative to the balance-sheet hole; deflation risk persists as PPI likely remains negative; European and US tariff escalation continues to redirect supply chains. The Indonesian stock market plunge and MSCI threats (per NYT) are an indirect China signal — EM markets heavily exposed to Chinese demand are being re-rated downward.  |  ⚠️ Divergence: The structural divergence is that China is now caught in a global tightening vise it didn't create and can't escape through domestic policy alone. With every major DM central bank hiking, global liquidity is contracting — and China, as the world's largest marginal commodity buyer, absorbs this through weaker terms of trade and capital flight. The traditional playbook says "PBOC eases → credit impulse rises → commodities rally → AUD and EM benefit." That transmission mechanism is broken when the Fed is simultaneously tightening: dollar strength neutralizes any PBOC easing impulse before it reaches global asset prices. This is why copper is falling even as oil rises — China's demand signal is being overwhelmed by the supply-shock / dollar-strength regime. For global allocators, this means the China reflation trade (long AUD, long copper, long European luxury) is structurally impaired until either the Fed pivots or China delivers fiscal stimulus of a magnitude that offsets dollar headwinds — neither of which appears imminent. The CGB-UST yield differential at these levels is the widest in decades, creating a gravitational pull on capital away from Chinese assets that even State Council directives cannot easily reverse.
