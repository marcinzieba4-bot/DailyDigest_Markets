🇨🇳 China  Mixed  → easing bias, but constrained

Macro Stance: China remains in a structurally accommodative posture but the PBOC's room to ease aggressively is constrained by the widening rate differential with the US — with the Fed potentially hiking and US 10Y at 5.13%, any meaningful PBOC cut would accelerate CNY depreciation and capital outflows. The copper price (6.57, ↓0.6% vs 4W ago) and the Oil/Copper ratio at 14.99 (rising, indicating supply-shock narrative rather than demand-led commodity strength) together paint a picture of weak Chinese industrial demand. Copper's softness is a direct read-through on China's construction and manufacturing cycle — if the world's largest commodity consumer were reflating, copper would not be declining.

New / Most Important: The signal feed this week is notably quiet on direct China data releases, which itself is informative — it suggests no major policy surprise from the Politburo or State Council, and no credit/TSF data strong enough to generate headlines. The absence of positive data flow is bearish by omission. The Gold/Copper ratio at 636 (FEAR-elevated) has a direct China read: gold is being hoarded globally while the China-demand-sensitive industrial metal languishes. The Russell/Nasdaq ratio declining 3.5% vs 4W ago reflects mega-cap dominance, which in a capital flow context means global allocators are concentrating in US large-cap tech rather than rotating into China or EM exposure. This is the opposite of the flow pattern China needs to stabilize its equity markets and currency.

Playbook ✓: Copper softness, yuan pressure from US rate re-acceleration, and cautious PBOC all follow the script of an economy stuck between deflation risk and currency fragility. Property stress remains the structural overhang — no headlines suggest resolution. The fact that Indonesia (a key EM peer and China trade partner) is seeing its stock market plunge on index-exclusion threats underscores how fragile Asia-Pacific capital flows remain.  |  ⚠️ Divergence: The structural divergence is that China appears to be losing the "stimulus put" narrative. In prior cycles (2015-16, 2020, late 2022), mere signals of PBOC/fiscal easing were sufficient to trigger global reflation trades — long copper, long AUD, long EM. This time the transmission mechanism is broken for several reasons: (1) the property sector, historically the multiplier for Chinese stimulus, remains impaired with no sign of price stabilization; (2) US real rates at current levels create a gravity well for global capital that overwhelms any PBOC liquidity injection; (3) the Gold/Copper ratio at 636 tells you the market is pricing fear over growth — and China is the marginal growth story. The implication for global assets is profound: AUD weakness (the Aussie fell to its lowest since July per the feed), European exporter stress, and EM commodity producers all trace back to this broken Chinese transmission channel. Until copper inflects higher and the Gold/Copper ratio compresses, the "buy China stimulus" trade is a value trap.
