🇨🇳 China  Mixed  → stability-first, no major easing

Macro Stance: China's macro posture is defined by the Trump-Xi summit outcome this week, which per Chosunbiz "prioritized stability over a big deal." This framing is deeply telling: Beijing is managing external risk rather than seeking breakthroughs, which means the PBOC is constrained from aggressive easing because yuan stability is the implicit price of geopolitical détente. The copper price at 6.60 (↑0.2% vs 4W — essentially flat) and the oil/copper ratio at 13.71 (elevated, signaling supply-side rather than demand-side commodity dynamics) together suggest Chinese industrial demand is not accelerating. The gold/copper ratio at 633 (fear-elevated) is another read-through: global capital is not pricing a China-led reflationary impulse.

New / Most Important: The Trump-Xi stability compact is the most important China signal this week. By prioritizing stability over a big deal, both sides implicitly agreed to freeze the tariff/trade architecture in place rather than escalate or de-escalate meaningfully. For markets, this removes tail risk (no new tariff shock) but also removes upside catalyst (no trade liberalization). Taiwan's economic indicators staying strong for the 9th straight month (per NDC data) is a secondary signal — it confirms the tech supply chain running through TSMC is healthy, which benefits China's downstream electronics assembly sector even as direct US-China chip restrictions remain binding. The APAC outlook piece flagged "geopolitics, rates, AI demand" as the three forces shaping the region — all three are currently in a holding pattern for China rather than inflecting.

Playbook ✓: The stability-first diplomatic approach, flat copper, and constrained PBOC easing are all consistent with the consensus script that China is in a managed deceleration rather than a V-shaped recovery. Property sector headlines are absent from this week's feed, which itself is information — no new stimulus announcements, no new developer defaults making international news, suggesting the sector is in purgatory rather than crisis.  |  ⚠️ Divergence: The structural divergence is in the capital flow implications of a simultaneously tightening US and ECB while China holds steady. With US 10Y at 5.28% and rising, and the ECB debating further hikes, the interest rate differential against Chinese government bonds is widening again. This creates persistent yuan depreciation pressure that the PBOC must manage through FX fixing and reserve deployment, which in turn drains domestic liquidity. The stability compact with the US constrains Beijing from letting the yuan weaken materially as a release valve. This is a liquidity trap of a different kind: China cannot ease aggressively (yuan risk), cannot let the currency go (political compact), and cannot stimulate via property (structural overhang). The transmission to global assets runs through commodities (flat copper confirms no demand impulse), Australian exports (AUD is a derivative of Chinese demand), and European luxury/auto exporters who need Chinese consumer spending to recover. The absence of any Politburo or State Council stimulus headline in this week's feed, despite the summit opportunity to pair diplomacy with domestic announcements, suggests Beijing is in wait-and-see mode into Q4.
