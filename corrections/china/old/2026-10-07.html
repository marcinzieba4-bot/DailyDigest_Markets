🇨🇳 China  Mixed  → easing bias but constrained

Macro Stance: China remains in a structurally different monetary regime from the rest of the world — the PBOC is operating with an easing bias while every other major central bank is hiking or holding at peak. This policy divergence creates persistent CNY depreciation pressure, which constrains the PBOC's ability to cut rates aggressively. The wide US-China rate differential (US 10Y at 5.32% vs CGB yields substantially lower) continues to drive capital outflows and force the PBOC into active yuan management via daily fixing signals.

New / Most Important: The EU trade chief headed to China this week — this is a significant diplomatic signal as Brussels navigates the delicate balance between maintaining trade access and imposing tariff/subsidy disciplines, particularly around EVs and green tech. No new major stimulus headlines in this week's feed, which is itself the story: the gap between promised and delivered stimulus continues to frustrate markets. Copper at 6.65 (↓2.3%) and Oil/Copper ratio rising suggest demand-side softness from China's industrial economy rather than a global supply shock.

Playbook ✓: The deflationary impulse in China's domestic economy — weak consumer confidence, property sector stress, cautious local government spending — continues along the expected trajectory. Chinese excess savings are not converting to consumption. The property-LGFV complex remains the key transmission channel: without home price stabilization, household balance sheet repair stalls, and the wealth effect stays negative. Export competitiveness is being maintained through yuan weakness and productivity gains, but trade partners (EU and US) are increasingly pushing back via tariff and subsidy investigations.

⚠️ Divergence: The structural divergence is that a Seeking Alpha piece flagged a "global liquidity regime shift" may be taking place — and China is at the epicenter. Here is the mechanic: as US yields surge and the dollar strengthens, China's PBOC is forced to defend the yuan by selling USD reserves or tightening offshore liquidity, which drains global dollar liquidity precisely when the world needs it most. This is the reflexive trap — China's easing intentions are undermined by US tightening, which forces defensive PBOC action, which tightens global conditions further. The transmission to global assets runs through commodity demand (copper weakness confirming this), AUD pressure (see Australia below), and European luxury/industrial exporters whose China revenues are being compressed by both volume weakness and currency translation. The Gold/Copper ratio at 622 (fear-elevated) is partly a China demand story — when China's industrial economy is firing, copper rallies and this ratio compresses. Its elevation tells you the marginal commodity buyer (China) is not showing up. For global macro portfolios, China's inability to deliver escape velocity from its balance-sheet recession means the "reflation trade" in commodities, EM, and European exporters remains structurally impaired despite periodic policy announcement rallies.
