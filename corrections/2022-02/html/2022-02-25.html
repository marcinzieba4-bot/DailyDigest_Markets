🌍 Geographic Macro Overview

🇺🇸 United States  Mixed  March lift-off intact, war caps the pace

Macro Stance: The first day of war delivered a textbook panic-and-reversal on Wall Street, and the close matters more than the open. Al Jazeera reported the Dow plunging more than 800 points as Russia attacked Ukraine [1] , yet the S&P 500 finished at 4,288.70 index points, +1.50%, the Nasdaq 100 at 13,974.67 index points, +3.44%, and the Dow at 33,223.83 index points, +0.28% (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2022-02-24). The reversal is consistent with investors treating the Western response so far as a manageable growth shock rather than a recession trigger, and the bid for Treasuries may be easing the case for a more aggressive Fed. We stay Mixed: the S&P 500 is still -4.16% over five sessions, and the VIX still closed at 30.32 index points after slipping only 0.70 points.

Rates & Curve: Treasuries took the haven bid at the long end while the front end kept its Fed pricing. The 2Y closed at 1.54% and the 10Y at 1.96%, leaving 10Y-2Y at +42 bps and 10Y-3M at +164 bps (US Treasury, prior close 2022-02-24). Over 20 observations the 2Y is up 41 bps from 1.13% and the 10Y up 11 bps, a bear flattening that has cut the spread by 30 bps; over the last five observations the 2Y rose 2 bps while the 10Y fell 7 bps. The curve remains positively sloped. Reuters reported Treasury yields sliding as Russia invaded [2] , and a Reuters commentary argued the bond market is sending flawed signals on the US economy [3] , a caution against reading the long-end rally as a clean verdict on growth.

New / Most Important: The domestic data still support March lift-off. The Real Economy Blog charted initial jobless claims continuing to decline [4] , even as the Conference Board said consumer confidence declined for a second consecutive month in February [5] . The war reaches households through prices: CBS News described how the Ukraine crisis is already hitting Americans' wallets [6] , and ABC7 Chicago reported President Biden promising to soften the blow from rising gasoline prices as much as he can [7] . Policy is the second channel. Al Jazeera reported Biden unveiling new Russia sanctions over the invasion [8] , and Reuters quoted the White House saying all options are on the table [9] . Crude's verdict was restrained: Brent settled at 99.08 USD/bbl, +2.31% (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2022-02-24), after OilPrice.com reported prices soaring past $100 [10] .

Playbook ✓: Action: hold a barbell of quality mega-cap technology and US energy producers, funded from consumer discretionary and banks. Trigger: keep it while the S&P 500 holds its 4,288.70 close and the 10Y stays at or below its 1.96% close. KALB's report of a Fed governor opening the door to a half-point March hike [11] is the hawkish tail; if that view gains ground, cut growth exposure and shorten duration. What changes the call: Brent settling back above the $100 mark Reuters used to chart oil's journey from worthless in the pandemic [12] , which would turn the consumer energy tax from a risk into a forecast.

Dutch TTF gas settled at 134.32 EUR/MWh, +51.10% on the day and +92.44% over five sessions (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2022-02-24), while Al Jazeera reported Europe's gas prices soaring 62% as the crisis put fuel supply at risk [16] . New / Most Important: Banks with Russian franchises took the hardest hit: Societe Generale fell 12.15% and Raiffeisen Bank International 23.14% (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2022-02-24), even as Reuters reported SocGen saying its Rosbank unit operates in a normal manner [19] and UniCredit saying its Russian arm is self-funded and well covered [20] . Playbook ✓: Action: underweight euro-area cyclicals and banks with eastern exposure against energy-security beneficiaries; Reuters reported European defence stocks rising as Russia invaded [23] , and Rheinmetall gained 3.38% to 100.05 EUR (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2022-02-24). Trigger: hold while TTF stays far above 91.84 EUR/MWh, its level 20 sessions earlier. What changes the call: gas retracing most of the +51.10% jump, which would ease the stagflation squeeze and reopen the case for European cyclicals.

🇨🇳 China  Mixed  Easing bias versus the regulatory discount

Macro Stance: China meets the war shock with the opposite policy bias to the West, and that is the core of a Mixed stance. Reuters reported China's inflation slowing and leaving room for policy easing [24] , while Bloomberg described the People's Bank seizing the moment for stimulus as the Fed hikes [25] . Against that, the private-sector growth engines remain under regulatory pressure: Bloomberg reported Alibaba planning for a new normal of low growth as the crackdown bites [26] . Beijing's diplomacy adds a third layer, with PBS reporting that China avoided criticism of the Russian attack and called for talks [27] . Easing supports onshore assets; the crackdown and sanctions-adjacent risk cap offshore valuations.

PMIs & Activity: The latest activity evidence is soft. Reuters reported January factory activity contracting as COVID lockdowns bit, according to the Caixin PMI [28] , while China Daily Global Edition reported industrial profits rebounding in 2021 as production improved [29] . Profits are the backward-looking read; the PMI is the forward one, and it describes a demand-constrained economy rather than an overheating one. Autos are stable rather than strong: the Best Selling Cars Blog reported January wholesales up 0.9% in a stable market [30] . Policy-directed investment is the bright spot, with Global Times reporting China expected to add a record 75-90GW of solar capacity in 2022 [31] , the kind of state-steered capex that offsets weak private demand.

Credit Impulse: Credit is where the easing bias should show first. MacroMicro's tracking of LPR interest-rate swaps [32] is the market's gauge of further loan-prime-rate cuts, and slowing inflation gives the PBOC cover to keep nudging borrowing costs lower. The risk is that credit is being offered into weak demand: the January PMI shows activity had not yet responded to earlier easing. BNP Paribas Asset Management framed US-China monetary policy divergence as a defining theme [33] . The war widens that divergence, because a Fed that tightens while the PBOC eases narrows China's rate advantage, but cheaper domestic credit is exactly what property and local investment need. We would treat a clear acceleration in new loans as the trigger to add onshore cyclicals.

Property: Property remains a drag rather than a source of systemic contagion, but the drag is still deepening. CNBC reported Moody's downgrading Shimao as debt troubles drag on [34] , and The Standard reported Fantasia being sued over a bond default [35] . Forbes noted that Hong Kong tycoon Joseph Lau's fortune edged up despite an Evergrande loss [36] , a reminder that developer stress is still showing up in concentrated holders' fortunes. Hong Kong adds a separate problem: Al Jazeera reported the territory unveiling a $22bn budget as zero-COVID hammers businesses [37] . The policy response visible so far is incremental, and the downgrade cycle argues it is not yet enough to stabilise developer funding.

Fiscal, Policy & Trade: The policy calendar is the near-term swing factor. The South China Morning Post previewed what to expect from China's 2022 'two sessions' as Beijing plots its course [38] , where growth targets and fiscal quotas set the tone for the year. Trade is being rewired by the war: Reuters reported Russia's trade flow shifting towards China as sanctions start [39] , and Global Times quoted analysts saying Ukraine tensions will affect agricultural trade but with limited impact on China [40] . The Atlantic Council described Russia and China as partners in de-dollarisation [41] . The investor risk is secondary sanctions on Chinese firms that deepen Russian commerce; Moneycontrol examined what possible trade sanctions against Russia could mean for India and China [42] , and the answer depends on whether Western controls extend to third-country suppliers.

Markets & Flows: Onshore and offshore markets diverged on the first day of war. The Shanghai Composite fell 1.70% to 3,429.96 index points while the Hang Seng dropped 3.21% to 22,901.56 index points (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2022-02-24). Platform stocks took the brunt in Hong Kong: Alibaba fell 6.67% to 104.90 HKD, Tencent 3.87% to 406.32 HKD and Xiaomi 5.08% to 14.96 HKD. In the later US session, which delivered the reversal, the KWEB China internet ETF rose 0.95% and the Alibaba ADR slipped only 0.72% to 108.93 USD, while ASHR fell 1.04%. The offshore discount reflects regulation as much as war: Reuters reported fresh fears over a Beijing crackdown driving a China tech rout [43] , and The Japan Times reported China telling banks and state firms to report their exposure to Jack Ma's Ant [44] .

New / Most Important: Alibaba's latest outlook is the day's key China datapoint. Modern Retail reported the company betting on international growth as regulatory pressure grows [45] , a pivot that implies less room for growth at home. Over five sessions the Hong Kong shares are down 14.72% and the ADR 13.24% (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2022-02-24), so the reset met a stock that had already de-rated. Washington's pressure has not paused either: Reuters reported the US adding e-commerce sites operated by Tencent and Alibaba to its 'notorious markets' list [46] , keeping a US-policy overhang on the platforms even as Western attention turns to Russia.

Playbook ✓: Action: favour onshore exposure through ASHR over offshore platform names, and keep KWEB as a tactical trade only. Trigger: a two-sessions growth target and fiscal quota that confirm the easing bias, alongside a Caixin PMI that stops contracting. What changes the call: evidence that Chinese banks or exporters face Western secondary sanctions for absorbing diverted Russian trade, which would hit onshore and offshore assets together. ⚠ Divergence: policy is easing and inflation is slowing, yet Hong Kong-listed Alibaba fell 6.67% and Xiaomi 5.08% while the Shanghai Composite lost 1.70%; the binding constraint on offshore valuations is the regulatory discount, not the cost of money.

🇯🇵 Japan  Mixed  Imported inflation tests a patient BOJ

Macro Stance: Japan is a net energy importer facing a terms-of-trade hit, which makes the war a mild stagflation shock rather than a growth opportunity. Reuters reported consumer inflation in Japan's capital perking up, with the Ukraine crisis set to add pressure [47] . The Nikkei 225 fell 1.81% to 25,970.82 index points in Thursday's session after the holiday break (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2022-02-24), leaving it -5.42% over five sessions. SoftBank Group dropped 6.83% and Advantest 4.14%, while Tokyo Electron rose 1.12%. Reuters reported that a board reshuffle may open scope for a less dovish BOJ [48] , but energy-driven inflation is not the demand-pull variety the central bank wants to see. Mixed.

Activity & Currency: Activity was losing momentum before the war. CNBC reported February factory activity growth falling to a five-month low [49] , and The Guardian Nigeria News reported that GDP rebounded before the Omicron wave hit [50] . The dollar index rose 0.99% to 97.14 index points (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2022-02-24). With the BOJ still applying negative rates, as Asahi Shimbun reported in the case of MUFG Bank deposits [51] , while the US 2Y is up 41 bps over 20 observations, the widening rate gap is the channel through which imported energy costs can be amplified for Japanese households and margins. Fiscal room to cushion households is thin; Big Think noted that no country owes more than Japan relative to GDP [52] . EWJ fell only 0.58% in the US session, in a session that traded after the Tokyo close and during the US rebound.

Playbook ✓: Action: prefer semiconductor-equipment exposure such as Tokyo Electron over energy-intensive domestic cyclicals, and do not rely on the yen as a portfolio haven while the rate gap widens. Trigger: Tokyo inflation feeding into BOJ communication, given the board changes Reuters flagged. What changes the call: a rebound in the next factory survey, or a BOJ that explicitly looks through energy inflation, which would favour exporters such as Toyota, down 1.53% to 2,091.00 JPY (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2022-02-24).

🇦🇺 Australia  Mixed  RBA patience, commodity cushion

Macro Stance: Australia absorbed the first-day shock through its equity market and its miners. SMH reported the Ukraine crisis wiping more than $70b off the ASX in a 'sea of red' [53] ; the S&P/ASX 200 closed 2.99% lower at 6,990.60 index points, with BHP down 6.92% and Fortescue 4.18% (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2022-02-24). The cushion is commodity exposure: higher energy and food prices raise national income even as they squeeze households. Mixed.

Demand & Financing: Household demand faces a real-income squeeze. ABC reported wages recording their worst result in seven years [54] , and separately that a huge shortage of talent is pushing wages higher, but not as fast as the cost of living [55] . That is why the RBA can wait: WSJ reported the RBA's Harper backing a review and the central bank declining to follow the Fed on rate increases [56] . Grocers confirm the cost pressure, with Reuters reporting Coles flagging inflation as cost controls limited a first-half profit dip [57] and AFR reporting Woolworths cutting its dividend as lockdown costs crimped results [58] . Lenders are moving anyway; ANZ said it is lifting rates on some lending and savings accounts [59] , and Banking Day reported the RBA planning floating-rate repos with shorter tenors [60] . Commonwealth Bank fell 2.05% to 94.71 AUD (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2022-02-24).

Playbook ✓: Action: favour energy and food-linked exporters over banks and discretionary retailers. Trigger: the ASX 200 holding 6,838.30 index points, its level 20 sessions earlier. What changes the call: an RBA pivot toward early hikes, which the WSJ report argues against, or a slide in iron ore if China follows through on the iron ore single market The Australian reported it threatening [61] .

🇵🇱 Poland  Bearish  Frontline risk premium, NBP squeezed

Macro Stance: Poland is the EU's frontline economy in this war, and the market priced it as such. Bankier.pl described a crash on the Warsaw bourse and 'war panic' on the exchange [62] , while Miesięcznik Finansowy BANK reported every WIG20 company falling after the Russian attack [63] . The macro problem is that the shock lands on an economy already running hot: Forsal.pl reported GUS putting January inflation at 9.2% [64] , and 300Gospodarka reported Eurostat ranking Poland's 8.7% reading the fourth highest in the EU [65] . A weaker zloty imports more inflation just as growth confidence weakens, so the central bank faces a harder trade-off than its western peers. Bearish.

Markets: The sell-off was concentrated in banks and in companies with direct Ukrainian exposure. The Beta ETF WIG20TR tracker fell 10.38% to 31.50 PLN and the US-listed EPOL dropped 7.80% to 17.50 USD (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2022-02-24). Banks led the decline: PKO Bank Polski fell 16.82%, Bank Pekao 14.52% and mBank 14.56%. Clothing retailer LPP collapsed 25.19% to 9,650.00 PLN, Allegro fell 13.77% and coking-coal miner JSW 13.65%. Currency stress compounded the move: Rzeczpospolita reported the zloty under pressure and at its cheapest in months [66] , and Money.pl reported the currency losing ground after the Russian assault [67] . Rzeczpospolita summed up the consequences of the invasion as higher inflation and a weaker zloty [68] . The starting point was already fragile; Bankier.pl had reported the WIG20 at its lowest since May 2021 days before the invasion [69] .

Fiscal & Politics: Fiscal policy was stretched before the war, and security will stretch it further. Bankier.pl reported the government adopting the draft Homeland Defence Act and asked whether NBP profits will finance the armed forces [70] ; Money.pl warned that public debt will rise through the NBP and grow more costly over time [71] . Rate hikes raise that cost, as an expert explained to Business Insider Polska [72] . Money.pl reported Fitch's rating decision citing high inflation and problems with the European Commission [73] . Tax politics remain noisy: Biznes Wprost reported dismissals over the Polski Ład reform sweeping through the finance ministry [74] , and Wyborcza.pl's coverage put the war, Polski Ład and the Sejm's sitting side by side [75] . A rule-of-law deal, which EUobserver reported Poland edging toward [76] , would unlock EU funds and has become a larger swing factor for the fiscal path.

Corporate & Sectors: Corporate exposure to Ukraine is direct and visible. Wiadomości Handlowe reported LPP closing its Ukrainian stores and moving its office from Kyiv to Lviv [77] , Retailnet.pl reported CCC closing its stores in Ukraine [78] , and Bankier.pl reported Grupa Kęty halting production in Borodianka [79] . StockWatch.pl summarised listed companies limiting their Ukrainian operations amid market panic [80] . In banking, PKO BP's chief told Bankier.pl the group had prepared for all scenarios at its Ukrainian unit Kredobank [81] , but Business Insider Polska reported Poles withdrawing more cash from banks, as at the start of the pandemic [82] . Energy is the policy-sensitive sector: wnp.pl reported Russian crude accounting for less than half of Orlen's refinery throughput [83] , and Forsal.pl reported Orlen's chief pledging to terminate contracts with those manipulating fuel prices [84] . Orlen fell 7.68% to 62.96 PLN and PGE 8.90% to 6.57 PLN (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2022-02-24). Allegro adds a governance change, with Bankier.pl reporting that chief executive Francois Nuyts will step down [85] .

Geopolitics & Security: The security premium is now explicit. Wiadomości Onet reported the Polish army raising its combat readiness [86] and a Russian strike on military facilities 75 km from the Polish border [87] . A Rzeczpospolita poll found 71.1% of Poles believe Russia could attack Poland [88] . Allied reinforcement is the offset: Notes From Poland reported the UK sending an advanced air-defence system to boost NATO's eastern flank [89] , and Military Times reported American troops being welcomed in Poland amid tightening security [90] . Foreign Policy argued the crisis is turning Poland into a strategic player [91] , and NATO's North Atlantic Council issued a statement on Russia's attack on Ukraine [92] . Energy security matters as much as troops: Reuters had reported the Yamal-Europe pipeline flowing east as the crisis escalated [93] . InfoMigrants reported European countries preparing for refugees from Ukraine [94] , a humanitarian responsibility that could also carry fiscal and labour-market consequences for neighbouring states, Poland among them.

New / Most Important: The policy question is how the NBP answers a currency shock layered on top of January's inflation spike. Miesięcznik Finansowy BANK asked how the war will affect the Polish economy and decisions on rate hikes [95] . Rzeczpospolita reported that the MPC's February move lifted rates to their highest in almost nine years [96] , and The Banker reported Governor Glapiński taking aim at inflation [97] . The labour market gives less cover than before: Obserwator Gospodarczy reported unemployment rising for the first time in almost a year [98] , even though pb.pl had reported the average wage rising 9.6% in 2021 [99] , a wage base that makes second-round inflation effects more likely. Parkiet's assessment of the attack's consequences for markets [100] bears on a choice between tightening into the shock to defend the zloty and pausing to protect growth and the banks.

Playbook ✓: Action: stay underweight Polish equities, with banks the funding leg against energy-security and defence exposure elsewhere in Europe; XTB.com's list of four defence-sector stocks [101] points to defence as a theme for local investors. Trigger: further zloty weakness and fuel-price acceleration, which Agro Profil reported will speed up after the invasion [102] . What changes the call: an NBP response that stabilises the currency, a rule-of-law settlement that releases EU funds, and evidence that cash withdrawals are not turning into deposit flight; PKO Bank Polski at 35.95 PLN and the WIG20TR tracker at 31.50 PLN are the capitulation markers. ⚠ Divergence: higher rates should help Polish bank margins, yet PKO, Pekao and mBank fell between 14.52% and 16.82%; the market is pricing Ukraine exposure, deposit behaviour and Swiss-franc mortgage risk, where INFOR.PL reported falling optimism about settlements with banks [103] , above the rate tailwind.
