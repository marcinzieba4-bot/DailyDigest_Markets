🌍 Geographic Macro Overview

🇺🇸 United States  Mixed  Fed: March lift-off signalled, balance-sheet runoff next

Macro Stance: US growth looks strong in the rear-view mirror, and the Fed has stopped being patient. Fourth-quarter GDP grew at a 6.9% annualised pace, CNBC reported [1] , and the Washington Post noted the 5.7% full-year gain was the fastest since 1984 [2] . Reuters credited fiscal stimulus for that performance [3] , which is exactly why the impulse fades from here. Sentiment is rolling over: Business Insider reported that Americans' economic hopes plummeted to decade lows in January as Omicron spread [4] , and the White House is warning that the Omicron spike could skew the January jobs report [5] . We are Mixed: demand is intact, but the policy reaction function now dominates asset pricing.

Rates & Curve: The front end did the repricing. The 2Y closed at 1.18% and the 10Y at 1.79%, a 10Y–2Y spread of +61 bps (US Treasury, prior close 2022-01-31). Over twenty observations the 2Y rose 45 bps from 0.73% while the 10Y rose 27 bps from 1.52%, flattening the spread by 18 bps: a bear flattening (both up, 2Y up more) that still leaves a moderately steep curve. In the last five observations the 2Y added 19 bps against only 4 bps for the 10Y, so the market is pricing hikes, not a higher growth trajectory. Standard Chartered expects the curve to flatten and possibly invert this year, Reuters reported [6] , and a Reuters poll had penciled in three hikes this year [7] . With the 3M bill at 0.22%, the 10Y–3M gap is still +157 bps, so the curve is not yet signalling recession.

New / Most Important: The Fed's January 25–26 meeting [8] pointed to a March hike, CNBC reported [9] , with Reuters saying Powell vowed a sustained inflation fight [10] . Reuters' 'Mr. Nimble' framing captures the risk that the gradual script is gone [11] , and the plan to shrink the balance sheet adds a second tightening channel [12] . Equities absorbed it badly before bouncing: Reuters said the Nasdaq narrowly missed its worst January ever as Wall Street gained on the final session [13] . The S&P 500 closed at 4,515.55 index points, +1.89% on the session but -5.26% over twenty sessions; the Nasdaq 100 rose +3.29% to 14,930.05 yet sits -8.52% over the same window; the VIX fell -10.23% to 24.83 index points, still +7.61 points above its level twenty sessions earlier (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2022-01-31).

Playbook ✓: Action: keep a quality-over-duration tilt—own cash-generative megacaps, energy and banks, trim unprofitable growth into rebounds, and pair it with a 2s10s flattener. Trigger: add to the flattener while the 2Y keeps rising faster than the 10Y; keep trimming growth while the VIX stays well above its year-end level of 17.22 index points. What changes the call: a January payrolls report too weak to dismiss as Omicron noise, or a bear steepening in which the 10Y rises while the 2Y stalls, would argue for closing the flattener and re-adding duration-sensitive equities. Context: DXY at 96.54 index points (-0.75% on the session) shows the dollar is no longer a one-way hedge (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2022-01-31).

🇨🇳 China  Mixed  PBOC easing as the West tightens

Macro Stance: China's cycle is out of phase with the West: as the Fed prepares to tighten, Bloomberg reports that China's central bank is seizing the moment for stimulus [23] . The starting point is weakening. Reuters said January factory activity growth slowed and demand waned as COVID surged [24] , and an official had warned that factories face 'rather large' downward pressure in the first quarter, CNBC reported [25] . We are Mixed: policy is turning supportive and offshore equities have started to respond, but property and zero-COVID still cap the growth multiplier. With the Shanghai Composite's last completed session on 2022-01-28, the first onshore verdict on the easing is still pending.

PMIs & Activity: The official January PMIs describe deceleration rather than collapse. Reuters reported factory activity growth slowed [24] , Hürriyet Daily News carried the line that factory activity edged down [26] , and ABN AMRO argues the January PMIs indicate quarter-on-quarter growth will drop in the first quarter [27] . That follows 8.1% growth in 2021, the fastest in a decade, per Global Times [28] , with fixed-asset investment up 4.9 pct for the year, per Macau Business [29] . Industrial profits rebounded in 2021 as production improved, China Daily reported [30] , and total trade topped $6 trillion for the first time, according to Pandaily [31] . The mix matters: trade topped $6 trillion in 2021, and HSBC warns that disruptions in China can send 'ripple-effects' across the global supply chain, CNBC reported [32] .

Credit Impulse: The policy signal is clear even if credit data still have to confirm it. The PBOC cut key lending rates, including one for the first time in nearly two years, CNBC reported [33] , and also cut rates on medium-term loans and reverse repos, per Fibre2Fashion [34] ; the BBC tied the cuts to slowing growth [35] . The transmission problem sits in bank balance sheets: Nikkei Asia reports that Minsheng, a major Evergrande creditor, faces a wave of bad loans [36] , precisely the channel that blunts a rate cut. Our read is that the credit impulse turns up only if easing reaches developers and local governments; until then lower rates mainly compress yields and support duration rather than activity. A broadening of cuts beyond the policy corridor would be the confirmation signal.

Property: Property remains the binding constraint. The AFR reports Beijing is considering breaking up Evergrande to contain the crisis [37] , The Real Deal says Oaktree seized an Evergrande property worth $1B [38] , and Reuters chronicled the dashed dreams of Evergrande's showpiece resort island [39] . Bloomberg describes Chinese cities selling land to themselves as the crisis deepens [40] , which props up local fiscal receipts on paper while masking weak private demand. Forbes counts a $30 billion drop in top property tycoons' fortunes, with more losses expected [41] . The one stabilising datapoint is that new home prices fell less in December on promotions and easing curbs, Business Recorder reported [42] . That is a price-cut-led stabilisation, not a volume recovery.

Fiscal, Policy & Trade: Trade friction is sticky rather than escalating. CNN explains why Biden is keeping Trump's China tariffs in place [43] , the WTO said China can impose $645 million of retaliatory tariffs on US imports, per the Jersey Evening Post [44] , and the EU escalated its trade dispute with Beijing over Lithuania, the BBC reported [45] . In technology, the SCMP warns that US–Japan restrictions on tech exports to China risk a 'major impact' [46] , and Reuters reports Micron will shut DRAM chip design operations in Shanghai [47] . Domestically the policy mix is pro-growth but state-steered: CNBC notes another major Chinese tech firm expanding use of the country's digital currency [48] , a reminder that payments remain state-directed.

Markets & Flows: Offshore Chinese equities closed the lunar year on a rebound. The Hang Seng ended at 23,802.26 index points, +1.07% on the session and +2.27% over twenty sessions, and Tencent rose +2.43% to 447.54 HKD (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2022-01-31); the SCMP framed it as a January gain closing the Year of the Ox after a slump [49] . US-listed ADRs moved harder: KWEB jumped +9.74% to 37.41 USD and the Alibaba ADR +9.16% to 125.79 USD (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2022-01-31). Onshore, the Shanghai Composite's last session left it at 3,361.44 index points, -7.12% over twenty sessions (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2022-01-28). The gap is regulatory as much as macro: CNBC argues the crackdown on Chinese IPOs in the US could make investors think twice about betting on tech [50] .

New / Most Important: The newest signals are the January PMIs and the policy pivot. ABN AMRO's warning on first-quarter growth [27] lands as Bloomberg describes the PBOC using the Fed's tightening window to ease [23] . George Soros warned China is facing an economic crisis, CNN reported [51] , a view the onshore index has partly discounted but the ADR bounce has not. The Winter Olympics backdrop limits any near-term reopening: the Guardian notes sport, politics and Covid colliding in Beijing [52] , and Al Jazeera reports Hong Kong locking down homes in a COVID lesson taken from Beijing [53] . The signals conflict, but the direction of policy has changed.

Playbook ✓: Action: hold a modest overweight in offshore China internet (KWEB, Alibaba, Tencent) funded from onshore cyclicals, sized small. Trigger: add only if the reopening onshore session confirms the ADR move and the Shanghai Composite starts recovering toward its 3,619.19 index points reference; cut if the ADRs give back the session's gain. What changes the call: evidence that rate cuts are reaching developers, such as an Evergrande restructuring path that protects homebuyers, would justify rotating into onshore cyclicals; a fresh US delisting step would end the trade. ⚠ Divergence: offshore internet rallied as if easing were already working, while the official PMIs, Minsheng's bad loans and a -7.12% onshore index say transmission has not happened yet.

Banks gain on margins, but the household squeeze is building, which is why Rzeczpospolita's slowdown warning matters more than the headline growth rate.
