🌍 Geographic Macro Overview

🇺🇸 United States  Bearish  March lift-off, front end reprices

Macro Stance: Two shocks now push US equity multiples in the same direction. January consumer prices rose 7.5% from a year earlier, the hottest reading since 1982 and more than expected, according to CNBC. [1] Bloomberg described the gain as larger than forecast. [2] On Friday the White House added that Russia could invade Ukraine within the week, Stars and Stripes reported. [3] The S&P 500 fell 1.90% to 4,418.64 index points and the Nasdaq 100 lost 3.07% to 14,253.84 index points, while the VIX jumped 3.45 points to 27.36 index points (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2022-02-11). We are Bearish on duration-heavy US equity: the Fed is being pulled forward just as an exogenous risk premium is being added.

Rates & Curve: The two-year Treasury closed at 1.50% and the ten-year at 1.92%, leaving the 10Y−2Y spread at +42 bps on a positively sloped curve (US Treasury, prior close 2022-02-11). Over 20 observations the two-year has risen 59 bps from 0.91% while the ten-year is up only 22 bps from 1.70%, so the spread has compressed by 37 bps: a bear flattening, with both yields up and the two-year up more. The last five observations concentrate the move, with the two-year up 19 bps and the ten-year down 1 bp, after the ten-year briefly topped 2% for the first time since 2019 on the inflation print, according to CNBC. [4] Reuters has published an explainer on why the flattening matters. [5] The front of the curve is still steep, with 10Y−3M at +156 bps, so the Fed has room to hike before inversion becomes the dominant signal.

New / Most Important: The policy debate has moved from whether to how fast. Reuters reports that hot inflation fuels the case for a 'big-bang' Fed hike in March. [6] Against that, the Fed's Barkin told Reuters he would have to be convinced of the need for a half-point move. [7] Households are already absorbing the shock: Yahoo Finance describes US consumers as 'in a deep funk' [8] , and the Orange County Register reports the 30-year mortgage rate has surged to 3.69%, a two-year high. [9] The dollar index (DXY) rose 0.55% on Friday to 96.08 index points (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2022-02-11), tightening financial conditions further. The geopolitical layer is the new element: a war premium is now competing with the Fed for the same drawdown budget.

Playbook ✓: Action: stay underweight the Nasdaq 100 (QQQ closed at 347.06 USD, down 8.10% over 20 sessions) against the energy sector ETF (XLE, up 11.71% over the same span), and hold seven-to-ten-year Treasuries (IEF, up 1.04% on Friday) as the war hedge rather than adding equity beta (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2022-02-11). Trigger to press the trade: the VIX holding above 27.36 index points while the two-year stays at or above 1.50% (US Treasury, prior close 2022-02-11). What changes the call: a verifiable Russian pull-back that sends the VIX back toward its 20-session reference of 20.31 index points, combined with a softer next inflation print, would argue for covering growth underweights before the March meeting.

🇨🇳 China  Mixed  Easing into a property drag

Macro Stance: China is the one major economy easing while the Fed tightens, and that divergence is the core of our Mixed stance. Bloomberg describes the PBOC as seizing the moment for stimulus as the Fed hikes [21] , after it cut key lending rates, including one for the first time in nearly two years, according to CNBC. [22] The easing is starting to show up in credit, but it has not yet repaired the property chain or the consumer, and exporters face a more hostile trade backdrop. Investors are being asked to trust a policy pivot before the activity data confirm it.

PMIs & Activity: The latest factory read was soft: the Caixin PMI showed January activity contracting as Covid lockdowns bit, per Reuters. [23] That follows a strong 2021 base, with GDP growth of 8.1%, the fastest in a decade, as Global Times reported [24] , and fixed-asset investment up 4.9% in 2021, according to Macau Business. [25] China Daily reports industrial profits rebounded in 2021 as production improved. [26] Consumption is the weak leg: CNBC reports the holiday box office plunged 23% as theaters pushed prices to record highs. [27] On the supply side, Reuters says China plans to increase iron ore output and boost steel-scrap use [28] , a sign Beijing wants to contain input costs for the infrastructure push that China Briefing describes as the growth lever for 2022. [29]

Credit Impulse: The clearest early win for the easing camp is credit. Reuters reports Chinese government bond yields rose on faster January credit growth [30] , which is the bond market saying it believes the impulse is turning. Lower policy rates reinforce that, with Fibre2Fashion noting cuts to medium-term lending and reverse repo rates. [31] The mechanism matters for global assets: a rising Chinese credit impulse tends to support Asian cyclicals and industrial commodities with a lag, but this cycle the property sector is where much of the new credit needs to land, and developers' capacity to absorb it is impaired.

Property: Property remains the drag that keeps us from turning Bullish. Reuters reports regulators are giving developers easier access to escrowed presale funds [32] , a targeted liquidity release rather than a bailout. Evergrande says it aims to deliver 600,000 apartments in 2022 with no fire sales, per Reuters [33] , while the WSJ reports its CEO sold bonds before the profit warning [34] , a reminder that bondholder trust is broken. Nikkei Asia's reporting on concrete 'ghost towns' underlines the inventory overhang [35] , and in Hong Kong lived-in home valuations are dropping amid a fifth Covid wave, per the South China Morning Post. [36] Business Recorder reported in January that new home prices fell less in December on promotions and easing curbs [37] , so price stabilization is being bought with discounts. Escrow relief buys time; it does not create buyers.

Fiscal, Policy & Trade: Trade policy is turning more coercive in both directions. China suspended Lithuanian beef, dairy and beer imports as the Taiwan row grows, per Reuters [38] , and Taiwan, Canada and Japan have asked to join the EU's WTO case against China, according to Politico Europe. [39] The Phase One relationship is souring too: the Peterson Institute calculates China bought none of the extra $200 billion of US exports in the Trump-era trade deal. [40] Domestically, oversight keeps tightening, with Reuters reporting a fine for Ant-backed MYBank over credit-scoring rules. [41] On Russia, The Hill argues China is making sanctions irrelevant [42] , a stance that could raise secondary-sanctions risk for Chinese banks and exporters.

Markets & Flows: Onshore and offshore markets are telling different stories. The Shanghai Composite closed at 3,462.95 index points, down 0.66% on Friday and up 3.02% over five sessions, a window that reaches back before the Lunar New Year closure, but still 3.26% lower over 20 sessions; the Hang Seng finished at 24,906.66 index points, 4.92% higher over 20 sessions (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2022-02-11). In New York the China A-shares ETF (ASHR) fell 1.93% and the China internet ETF (KWEB) 3.44% (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2022-02-11). Regulatory risk remains sharp and idiosyncratic: WuXi Biologics sank by a record after landing on a US red-flag list, per the SCMP. [43] The SCMP's Macroscope column argues emerging markets are the ones to watch as tech-led US stocks lose their shine. [44]

New / Most Important: Technology self-sufficiency is the most important new strand. China's biggest chipmaker SMIC posted record revenue despite US sanctions, per CNBC [45] , and plans to boost capacity after doubling profits to US$1.7 billion, according to the SCMP. [46] At the same time ASML has flagged possible IP infringement by a Chinese 'little giant', per the SCMP [47] , which the Chinese company denies, according to Reuters. [48] Nikkei Asia reports ZTE is boosting its chip capabilities amid Huawei's crackdown woes. [49] Foreign capital is still committing to the premium consumer: BMW paid $4.2 billion to take control of its Chinese joint venture, Reuters reports. [50] Capital is flowing toward strategic self-sufficiency and premium consumption rather than the old property-led model.

Playbook ✓: Action: keep China at neutral, expressed through Hong Kong-listed platforms such as Tencent (453.04 HKD, up 6.84% over 20 sessions) rather than the A-share ETF (ASHR, down 4.07% over 20 sessions) (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2022-02-11). Trigger to upgrade: a second month of accelerating credit growth alongside a factory PMI back in expansion. What changes the call: new US listing or sanctions actions against Chinese firms, or an escrow scheme that fails to stop developer defaults. ⚠ Divergence: bond yields are rising on faster credit growth, which normally signals reflation, yet the Caixin factory gauge is contracting and holiday spending is weak; the credit impulse has not yet reached activity.

🇵🇱 Poland  Mixed  NBP hawkish on the front line

Macro Stance: Poland combines the most hawkish central bank in our coverage with the most direct exposure to the Russia–Ukraine risk, which is why we are Mixed rather than outright Bearish. The Monetary Policy Council (RPP) raised rates again, taking them to their highest level in almost nine years, according to Rzeczpospolita. [67] NBP Governor Adam Glapiński framed his press conference around being a hawk ('Ja jako jastrząb', or 'Me as a hawk'), Interia reported [68] , and Bankier credited him with strengthening the zloty [69] , which it later described as the strongest in eight months. [70] The inflation problem behind that stance is real: GUS put end-2021 inflation at 8.6%, per 300Gospodarka [71] , and Credit Agricole has raised its 2022 forecast to 7.5%, per Bankier. [72] The labour market keeps the pressure on, with average pay up 9.6% in 2021, per pb.pl [73] , and registered unemployment at 5.6% in January, per Radio Maryja citing the family and social policy ministry (MRiPS). [74]

Markets: Warsaw has lagged the hawkish story. The iShares MSCI Poland ETF (EPOL) fell 3.88% on Friday to 20.31 USD and is down 10.96% over 20 sessions, while the WIG20 total-return tracker lost only 0.55% to 37.94 PLN (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2022-02-11). The gap reflects mechanics: EPOL trades in dollars during New York hours, so it absorbs both currency translation and late-session US news, whereas the Warsaw close comes earlier in the day. Banks, the natural beneficiaries of higher rates, were heavy: Bank Pekao fell 2.24% to 133.15 PLN and PKO Bank Polski 1.04% to 47.65 PLN (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2022-02-11). Parkiet still argues bank shares on the GPW have the potential to extend their bull run [75] , while Bankier asks whether the market is still in a correction or already in a bear market. [76]

Fiscal & Politics: Fiscal numbers are better than feared, but the politics are messier. The finance ministry estimates the 2021 state budget deficit at 26.3 bn zł, per Bankier [77] , and Rzeczpospolita argues inflation is helping the government manage the public debt. [78] The Polski Ład tax reform has become a liability: deputy finance minister Jan Sarnowski, who was responsible for it, has lost his post, according to Business Insider Polska [79] , a dismissal PolsatNews ties to the problems with Polski Ład [80] , and Wyborcza.biz reports the next finance minister will be a woman. [81] Spending pressure is building, with pension indexation set to cost more than the budget assumed because of a higher index, per 300Gospodarka. [82] The food VAT cut is a double-edged tool that Credit Agricole says will contribute to higher inflation, per Bankier. [83] Relations with Brussels remain strained, with the EU set to withhold funds over the coal mine dispute, per DW. [84]

Corporate & Sectors: Corporate news is dominated by energy security and state champions. Orlen's CEO says jobs at Lotos will be maintained, per Bankier [85] , and INNPoland reports Orlen has raised its wholesale petrol price, with stations unwilling to absorb the increase. [86] KGHM has registered its copper cathodes on China's INE exchange, according to Inwestycje.pl [87] , and Parkiet reports Jacek Sasin and KGHM are travelling to the US in pursuit of small nuclear reactors. [88] Utilities are positioning for transition and rates: netka.gda.pl reports PGE's Baltica 3 offshore wind farm, of about 1045 MW, is planned to start in 2026 [89] , and WNP.PL reports Tauron bought CO2 allowances at 30 euro and locked in a fixed interest rate. [90] In consumer names, LPP is optimistic about the year but expects cost pressure to weigh on margins, per Bankier. [91] Prices show the split: the coking-coal miner JSW is up 20.74% over 20 sessions and KGHM 9.44% over five, while Dino Polska is down 20.29% and Allegro 18.62% over 20 sessions (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2022-02-11).

Geopolitics & Security: Poland is now the staging ground for NATO reinforcement. Reuters reported exclusively that the US will send 3,000 additional troops to Poland [92] , with ABC News reporting the 82nd Airborne deployment amid invasion concerns [93] , and Politico Europe reports the UK is sending 350 more troops to the Polish border. [94] TVN24 describes further military transports arriving as proof that threats are being taken seriously [95] , and Prime Minister Morawiecki has praised the UK's solidarity with Ukraine, Poland and NATO's eastern flank, per gov.pl. [96] Defence24 reports that Polish-made Piorun MANPADS have been exported to the US and provided to Ukraine, a commercial success for Mesko. [97] Energy is the economic channel: Money.pl reports businesses have received letters warning of gas-supply restrictions [98] , and a survey reported by Bankier finds Europeans fear Russia will attack Ukraine this year. [99]

New / Most Important: The most important new signal is the NBP's reserve strategy. Business Insider Polska reports Glapiński intends to buy another 100 tonnes of gold [100] , and Obserwator Gospodarczy calculates Poland could hold larger gold reserves than the UK or Saudi Arabia within a year. [101] Combined with Glapiński's pledge to keep urging tighter monetary policy, reported by PolsatNews [102] , the message is that the NBP is building a war chest and is prepared to defend real rates and the currency. The Stowarzyszenie Inwestorów Indywidualnych reads the latest hike as a sign the NBP has not yet said its last word. [103] For markets, that lowers the probability of a disorderly currency sell-off even if the security situation deteriorates.

Playbook ✓: Action: hold Polish banks, PKO Bank Polski and Bank Pekao, as the rate-sensitive expression, fund them by underweighting the broad EPOL ETF until the Ukraine risk clears, and use JSW and KGHM as the commodity hedge. Trigger to add: another RPP hike with the currency's strength intact and EPOL stabilizing above its Friday close of 20.31 USD (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2022-02-11). What changes the call: a Russian invasion that forces a flight from frontline assets, or fiscal slippage as Polski Ład repairs and pension costs pile up. ⚠ Divergence: the NBP is hawkish and Bankier reported the zloty at its strongest in eight months [70] , which should support local assets, yet EPOL is down 10.96% over 20 sessions and bank shares sold off on Friday; the currency and the equity market are pricing different risks.
