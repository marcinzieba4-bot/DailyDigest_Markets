🌍 Geographic Macro Overview

🇺🇸 United States  Mixed  March lift-off priced, pace contested

Macro Stance: The US is in a tightening cycle that has not formally started but is already working through the front end. January consumer prices rose 7.5% on an annual basis, the highest since 1982, CNBC reported [1] . Reuters read the January FOMC minutes as showing a Fed not wed to a particular pace of rate hikes [2] , while Al Jazeera stressed that officials are prepared to move faster if inflation persists [3] . That keeps a March move as the base case and leaves the size of the step as the market's live variable. Growth is not yet the constraint, with industrial production rebounding according to The Real Economy Blog [4] ; confidence is the weak link, and Yahoo Finance described consumers as being in a deep funk [5] .

Rates & Curve: The 2Y closed at 1.52% and the 10Y at 2.03%, a 10Y-2Y spread of +51 bps on a positively sloped curve (US Treasury, prior close 2022-02-16). Over 20 observations the 2Y is +48 bps from 1.04% and the 10Y +20 bps from 1.83%, so the spread has narrowed by 28 bps: a bear flattening, with both yields up and the 2Y up more. Over the last five observations the 2Y added +16 bps against +9 bps for the 10Y. Reuters said the curve is pricing Fed tightening and showing fears of a policy error [6] , and Bloomberg warned that Treasury market liquidity is eroding with the Fed's course a gamble [7] . Thin liquidity plus a fast-repricing front end keeps equity volatility high: the VIX closed at 24.29 index points, -1.41 points on the session (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2022-02-16).

New / Most Important: The minutes neither validated the half-point camp nor shut it out. Reuters had the Fed raising rates 25 bps in March, with calls for 50 bps growing louder [8] . Equities absorbed the readout calmly: the S&P 500 closed at 4,475.01 index points, +0.09% on the session but -2.45% over five sessions, the Nasdaq 100 slipped -0.12% to 14,603.64 index points and the DXY eased -0.30% to 95.70 index points (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2022-02-16). Politico adds a smaller wrinkle, reporting Republicans delaying the vote on Biden's Fed nominees and turning up the heat on Sarah Bloom Raskin [9] .

Playbook ✓: Action: stay underweight long-duration growth and keep portfolio duration short, expressing the rate view at the front end rather than through the 10Y. Trigger: a hawkish data surprise that lifts the 2Y further above 1.52% while the 10Y lags would extend the bear flattening and favour energy and banks over software. What changes the call: a VIX back below its 23.85 level of 20 sessions ago together with a 10Y-2Y spread that stops compressing from +51 bps would signal the March step is fully priced and justify rebuilding quality growth (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2022-02-16; US Treasury, prior close 2022-02-16). A Ukraine escalation would rally Treasuries but widen credit spreads, so it is a hedge, not an entry point.

🇨🇳 China  Bullish  Easing into a property drag

Macro Stance: China is the one major economy where policy is moving the other way. Al Jazeera reported the central bank chief vowing support amid weak links in the economy [20] , and Reuters reported slowing inflation leaving room for policy easing [21] . Bloomberg's January framing still holds: as the Fed hikes, China's central bank is seizing the moment for stimulus [22] . The stance is Bullish on the policy impulse, not on the cycle. The growth base is soft, property is still deleveraging and Hong Kong's Omicron wave is testing the zero-Covid model at an awkward moment.

PMIs & Activity: The latest activity data argue for support rather than celebration. Reuters reported January factory activity contracting as Covid lockdowns bit, according to the Caixin PMI [23] . That followed 8.1% GDP growth in 2021, the fastest in ten years, per Global Times [24] , and fixed-asset investment up 4.9% for the year, per Macau Business [25] . The annual numbers describe a year that has passed; the January PMI describes the economy now. Services face a fresh hit from Hong Kong, where the BBC described hospitals as overwhelmed amid a spike in cases [26] and Al Jazeera reported Xi telling the city to control the outbreak [27] . As long as the mainland keeps its own zero-Covid approach, a consumption rebound stays capped.

Credit Impulse: Credit is where the turn is visible. Reuters reported Chinese government bond yields rising on faster January credit growth [28] , a sign the bond market reads the lending surge as real. Bloomberg reported the PBOC pumping in more liquidity and spurring gains in Chinese stocks [29] , after CNBC reported January cuts to key lending rates, including one for the first time in nearly two years [30] . The mechanism matters: lower rates cut the cost of rolling local-government and developer debt, and a stronger credit impulse tends to lead industrial activity by several quarters. The limit is discipline, with the South China Morning Post reporting Beijing telling banks to support rural infrastructure without adding to hidden debt [31] .

Property: Property is the drag policy is cushioning rather than reflating. Reuters, citing a report, said China is giving property firms easier access to escrow funds [32] , and the Khmer Times reported affordable rental housing being excluded from property credit controls [33] : targeted relief, not a blanket reversal of deleveraging. Stress is still moving through the developer chain. Bloomberg, citing Caixin, reported China's Cedar unable to pay $3.1 billion overdue [34] , and the South China Morning Post reported Evergrande's chief executive dumping all his dollar bonds before the default [35] . Evergrande's vow to finish half of its units under construction this year, per Caixin Global [36] , helps households but offers bondholders little. Business Recorder reported December new-home prices falling less on promotions and easing curbs [37] , the first hint that support is reaching buyers.

Fiscal, Policy & Trade: Trade is turning from tailwind to friction. The BBC reported Washington accusing China of serious harm to workers through its trade practices [38] , and the Peterson Institute found China bought none of the extra $200 billion of US exports promised in the Trump-era deal [39] , a record that gives the administration reason to keep tariffs in place. Beijing is also using trade as a weapon, suspending Lithuanian beef, dairy and beer imports as the Taiwan row grew, Reuters reported [40] , while India banned 54 Chinese apps as tensions rose between New Delhi and Beijing, per the South China Morning Post [41] . On Russia, the South China Morning Post quoted Beijing saying Western sanctions would worsen the Ukraine crisis [42] , a reminder that a sanctions escalation could put Chinese banks and exporters in the crossfire.

Markets & Flows: Offshore equities are responding to the policy divergence faster than onshore. The Hang Seng closed at 24,718.90 index points, +1.49% on the session and +1.38% over 20 sessions, while the Shanghai Composite closed at 3,465.83 index points, +0.57% on the day but -3.66% over 20 sessions (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2022-02-16). Tencent is +3.64% over 20 sessions at 452.28 HKD, and Alibaba's Hong Kong line rose +3.46% on the session to 122.50 HKD. CNBC reported banks from Credit Suisse to Goldman Sachs saying it is time to buy Chinese stocks [43] , but flows are selective: Caixin Global reported Hillhouse buying electric-vehicle makers while ditching Alibaba and Bilibili [44] . The currency is the release valve, with Reuters reporting the yuan slipping as divergent China-U.S. monetary policy exerts downward pressure [45] .

New / Most Important: The new element is the explicit pledge of support from the central bank chief [20] arriving alongside softer inflation; together they make a further cut in policy rates or reserve requirements the path of least resistance. The second is Hong Kong, where the WSJ reported hospital wards overflowing as Omicron defies zero-Covid policies [46] , a direct hit to the city's consumption and to confidence in the strategy. The third is Washington's latest trade broadside: PBS carried the US charge that China's aggressive trade policies cause serious harm [47] , which raises the odds that tariffs stay.

Playbook ✓: Action: overweight offshore China internet against onshore A-shares, using KWEB, +2.91% over 20 sessions at 37.88 USD, against ASHR, -2.78% over 20 sessions at 36.71 USD (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2022-02-16). Trigger: a cut in policy rates or reserve ratios, or another month of faster credit growth, confirms the easing impulse. What changes the call: a developer default large enough to hit bank capital, or a Hong Kong outbreak forcing mainland restrictions. ⚠ Divergence: easing is being delivered into a contracting factory PMI and a property sector still unable to pay its bills, so the policy signal points up while the activity and credit-quality signals still point down.

🇵🇱 Poland  Mixed  Hiking into 9% inflation

Macro Stance: Poland is running its highest inflation of this century, as SII noted [63] , into the sharpest security risk on its border. The stance is Mixed because nominal growth and bank margins are strong while real incomes, the currency and the fiscal mix are all exposed to the same geopolitical shock.

Markets: The zloty is the shock absorber. Equities are cheaper but not calm: the iShares MSCI Poland ETF closed at 21.37 USD, +0.61% on the session and -2.64% over 20 sessions, and the WIG20 total-return tracker at 38.16 PLN is -4.46% over 20 sessions (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2022-02-16). That lag between loan and deposit pricing is the margin logic that would support Parkiet's view that listed banks can extend their bull run [76] .

Fiscal & Politics: The deficit looks contained, but inflation is doing much of the work. KGHM closed at 154.30 PLN, +5.94% over five sessions, and coking-coal miner JSW is +14.77% over 20 sessions at 47.01 PLN (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2022-02-16). In consumer names, CD Projekt released the next-generation Cyberpunk 2077 update with a free trial [91] , but Antyweb reported the director of The Witcher 3 leaving to found Rebel Wolves [92] . Geopolitics & Security: Poland is NATO's logistics hub for the crisis. Reuters reported the US sending 3,000 additional troops to Poland [94] , CNBC said US troops there are preparing to help refugees if Russia invades Ukraine [95] , and Notes From Poland reported a US welcome centre opening for citizens fleeing Ukraine [96] . Diplomacy is not producing relief: after talks with Lavrov, Foreign Minister Rau said European security is in a deep crisis, TVN24 reported [97] . The Atlantic Council warned an invasion could come with less than 12 hours' notice [98] , and NATO defence ministers issued a statement on the situation in and around Ukraine [99] . For markets, the shock would travel through the zloty's risk premium, through gas, given that IntelliNews describes Central Europe as still tied to Russia's pipeline network [100] , and through the fiscal cost of any refugee inflow.

New / Most Important: Three developments matter most. First, 9.2% inflation [64] keeps the RPP hiking, which supports bank earnings but squeezes consumer stocks: Allegro fell -3.82% on the session and is -14.61% over 20 sessions at 34.33 PLN, and LPP is -9.49% over 20 sessions (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2022-02-16). Second, the BBC reported Poland and Hungary losing the EU funding fight over laws [101] , which hardens the standoff over EU money. Third, the security premium rises and falls with Moscow's signals, which is why the zloty's recovery [71] remains fragile. Put together, the domestic data argue for higher rates and a stronger currency, while the border argues for a risk premium, and the banks are the one place where the rate side of that tension pays.

Playbook ✓: Action: overweight Polish banks against consumer discretionary, holding PKO Bank Polski at 47.21 PLN and Bank Pekao at 135.25 PLN, up +2.04% on the session, against Allegro and LPP (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2022-02-16). Trigger: another RPP hike or an upside core-inflation surprise extends the margin story. What changes the call: an invasion that sends the zloty sharply weaker and forces emergency action from the NBP would hit banks through risk premia, while an EU funds compromise would favour domestic cyclicals instead. ⚠ Divergence: rate hikes and a tight labour market argue for bank earnings upside, yet PKO Bank Polski is -3.65% over five sessions while KGHM and JSW rally, so the market is pricing security risk and commodity scarcity ahead of the domestic rate story.
