Macro Stance: Moscow's order to send what it calls 'peacekeepers' into Ukraine's separatist regions, as Australia's ABC explained [1] , has turned a geopolitical overhang into an energy and margin shock just as the Federal Reserve prepares to tighten. New / Most Important: President Biden imposed new Russia sanctions and decried a 'beginning invasion', Al Jazeera reported [8] : that is his characterisation of the Donbas deployment, not a report of a full-scale assault. The domestic channel is margins. Home Depot beat estimates and sees sales growth ahead for 2022, CNBC reported [9] , yet Reuters said a margin warning cast a shadow over the quarter [10] , and the stock fell -8.85% (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2022-02-22). Consumer confidence slid in The Real Economy Blog's chart of the day [11] , and mortgage rates hit their highest since May 2019, Realtor.com reported [12] .

Playbook ✓: Action: hold US equity beta underweight, with proceeds in intermediate Treasuries and energy producers, and hedge through index puts rather than outright shorts while the VIX sits at 28.81 index points (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2022-02-22). Trigger to add: an S&P 500 close below 4,304.76 index points with the 2Y yield pushing above 1.56% (US Treasury, prior close 2022-02-22), the signature of a Fed tightening into a supply shock. What changes the call: a verifiable pull-back from the Donbas that sends Brent back toward 86.27 USD/bbl, its level 20 sessions ago, or a Fed that signals a single quarter-point start; either revives quality growth.

🇨🇳 China  Bearish  Easing central bank, tightening regulators

Macro Stance: China is running two policies at once, and the latest session priced the tighter one. The central bank is easing, with CNBC reporting cuts to key lending rates, including one for the first time in nearly two years [24] , yet Reuters attributed a China tech rout to fresh fears over Beijing's regulatory crackdown [25] . The Hang Seng fell -2.69% to 23,520.00 index points and the Shanghai Composite -0.96% to 3,457.15 index points (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2022-02-22). Bearish on offshore China equity: lower rates are real, but regulation is compressing the profit pools of the index heavyweights, and Russia's isolation raises a secondary-sanctions question for Chinese counterparties.

PMIs & Activity: The last factory read pointed down: Reuters reported January activity contracting as COVID lockdowns bit, on the Caixin PMI [26] . Demand is soft rather than collapsing. Best Selling Cars Blog put January vehicle wholesales up +0.9% in a stable market [27] , and China Daily reported industrial profits rebounding in 2021 as production improved [28] . Pricing power survives at the premium end, with Reuters reporting Starbucks and Tim Hortons raising prices in China [29] , while Schindler warned on profit and China after its fourth-quarter earnings fell, Reuters reported [30] , a possible sign that construction-linked demand remains weak. Hong Kong is the near-term drag: the BBC reported hospitals overwhelmed amid a spike in cases [31] .

Credit Impulse: The credit impulse is being pushed from the policy side. Fibre2Fashion reported cuts to rates on medium-term loans and reverse repos [32] , Bloomberg described the central bank seizing the moment for stimulus as the Fed hikes [33] , and its chief vowed support amid 'weak links', Al Jazeera reported [34] . Reuters reported inflation slowing and leaving room for easing [35] , and BNP Paribas Asset Management frames the result as US-China monetary policy divergence [36] . The mechanism matters: cheaper funding lifts credit only where borrowers want to borrow, so with private developers impaired the impulse runs first through state firms and infrastructure, which favours onshore banks and builders over consumer platforms.

Property: Property is being loosened at the margin, not rescued. China excluded affordable rental housing from property credit control, the Khmer Times reported [37] , a carve-out that channels lending to state-sanctioned housing without reopening credit to private developers. Stress keeps surfacing at developer level, with The Standard reporting Fantasia being sued over a bond default [38] . In Hong Kong, the South China Morning Post argued the Northern Metropolis plan will not affect home prices [39] , so the territory offers no offset while its Omicron wave runs. For equities, construction-linked banks and materials remain a funding-cost trade, not a recovery trade, until sales stabilise.

Fiscal, Policy & Trade: External friction is rising. Reuters reported Washington adding e-commerce sites operated by Tencent and Alibaba to its 'notorious markets' list [40] , PBS carried the US charge that China's trade policies 'cause serious harm' [41] , and the EU initiated a WTO dispute over Chinese intellectual-property enforcement [42] while Reuters reported new EU anti-dumping tariffs on Chinese steel fasteners [43] . Beijing sanctioned Lockheed and Raytheon again over Taiwan arms sales, Reuters reported [44] . The Russia crisis cuts the other way: as sanctions start, Russia's trade flow is shifting towards China, Reuters reported [45] , and the Atlantic Council called the two partners in de-dollarisation [46] . That widens the secondary-sanctions risk for Chinese banks and exporters that absorb the slack.

Markets & Flows: Regulation is the swing factor. The WSJ reported that ordered cuts to food-delivery fees sparked a tech selloff [47] , and Bloomberg said the policy wiped $26 billion off Meituan's value [48] . Alibaba's Hong Kong line fell -3.05% to 111.40 HKD and is -15.29% over 20 sessions, Tencent is -5.36% over five sessions at 422.46 HKD, and the KWEB China internet ETF fell -4.24% to 34.09 USD from its 2022-02-18 close (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2022-02-22). Onshore exposure is holding up better: the ASHR CSI 300 tracker is -4.00% over 20 sessions at 36.24 USD, against -5.74% for the Hang Seng. Business Insider had noted Tencent joining the world's ten most valuable companies as Meta slid [49] , a ranking the latest rout puts at risk.

New / Most Important: The new information is the reach of supervision. The Japan Times reported Beijing telling banks and state firms to report their exposure to Jack Ma's Ant [50] , bringing financial-sector oversight, not just platform conduct, back into play. The South China Morning Post reported a US$10 million fine on a live-streamer for tax evasion [51] , and the WSJ said Chinese tutoring companies are taking a big financial hit amid the crackdown [52] . Enforcement is broadening across consumer-facing sectors just as investors were positioning for an easing-led rebound, which is why the rate cuts have not stabilised the platforms.

Playbook ✓: Action: stay underweight Hong Kong-listed China internet (KWEB, Alibaba) and express any easing view through onshore exposure such as ASHR at 36.24 USD (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2022-02-22). Trigger to revisit: another policy-rate cut paired with an explicit signal that the fee and fintech reviews are complete. What changes the call: the Hang Seng reclaiming 24,952.35 index points, its level 20 sessions ago. ⚠ Divergence: monetary policy is easing and inflation is slowing, which normally supports multiples, yet the regulatory and sanctions discount is widening, so investors are being offered lower rates in exchange for lower earnings visibility.

Geopolitics & Security: Security risk has become operational. Rzeczpospolita reported the Foreign Ministry not ruling out evacuating Polish diplomats from Ukraine [93] , and Gazeta Prawna reported the prime minister raising the cyber-threat alert level [94] . Jarosław Kaczyński said Poland must be secure and Ukraine must be saved, Gazeta reported [95] , and Jerzy Buzek said the country must be prepared for Russian aggression, Rzeczpospolita reported [96] . Allied hardware is arriving: Reuters reported the US announcing Abrams tanks for Poland as the Russia threat mounts [97] , and The National Interest reported additional US F-15s sent to bolster NATO's eastern flank [98] . Notes From Poland reported a trilateral Ukraine, UK and Poland agreement 'in face of Russian aggression' [99] , and wnp.pl reported work on the Belarus border barrier running on three shifts [100] . The economic link is energy, with WysokieNapiecie asking what will flow through Baltic Pipe and Yamal a year from now [101] .

New / Most Important: Money.pl's warning that Russia's escalation threatens Ukraine with economic collapse and that the problems will not spare Poland [102] captures the transmission: trade disruption, refugee pressure and dearer energy imports landing on an economy already running high inflation. Gas pricing is already distorting behaviour, with Money.pl reporting firms switching en masse to consumer gas tariffs [103] and wnp.pl reporting that legal changes alarmed gas customers before PSG moved to reassure them [104] . The defence-financing proposal [78] is the most market-relevant fiscal development, because funding the army from central-bank profit, if adopted, would shift the burden onto the NBP balance sheet in the middle of a tightening cycle.

Playbook ✓: Action: stay underweight Polish banks against energy and commodity producers (KGHM, JSW, Orlen), and keep złoty exposure hedged.
