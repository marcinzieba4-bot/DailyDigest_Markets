🇺🇸 United States  Mixed  Fed lift-off intact into a war shock

Macro Stance: Friday's violent relief rally has already been partly overtaken. That rally priced the first sanctions package; The New York Times then reported the US and Europe will bar some Russian banks from SWIFT [3] . Mixed: US earnings are less exposed than Europe's, but multiples rest on a Fed that experts quoted by Business Insider say will not rescue a meltdown [4] .

Rates & Curve: The curve says the Fed matters more than the war. The 2Y closed at 1.55% and the 10Y at 1.97%, leaving 10Y-2Y at +42 bps, with the 3M at 0.33% and 10Y-3M at +164 bps (US Treasury, prior close 2022-02-25). Over 20 observations the 2Y rose 37 bps from 1.18% and the 10Y 16 bps from 1.81%, so the spread compressed by 21 bps: the curve is positively sloped and the 20-observation move is a bear flattening, both up with the 2Y up more. Over five observations the 2Y added 6 bps and the 10Y was unchanged, meaning the safe-haven bid Reuters described on invasion day [5] only anchored the long end. Xinhua reported a Fed official signalling a March hike remains on track despite Ukraine [6] .

New / Most Important: Two shifts matter. First, the SWIFT step, which CBC said Canada is joining [7] , moves sanctions from named lenders to the payment rails, raising settlement risk for anyone still clearing Russian flows. Second, demand was already softening: The Conference Board said consumer confidence declined for a second consecutive month in February [8] , and Builder reported pending home sales falling for a third straight month in January [9] , against the largest annual consumer-price gain in 40 years [10] . Corporate cash is going to buybacks, with CNBC reporting a record $27 billion Berkshire repurchase in 2021 [11] . The DXY slipped 0.54% to 96.62 index points on Friday (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2022-02-25) after Reuters had reported the dollar near a two-year high on invasion day [12] .

Playbook ✓: Action: hold US quality large caps and energy over long-duration growth, and keep index hedges on while the VIX sits at 27.59 index points. Trigger to add risk: the S&P 500 holding Friday's 4,384.65 close as markets absorb the SWIFT measures, with the VIX failing to regain 30.49, its late-January level (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2022-02-25). What changes the call: a front end that keeps rising while the 10Y stalls would deepen the bear flattening from +42 bps and signal tightening into a growth shock; Reuters has already reported louder calls for 50 bps rather than 25 bps in March [13] . The State of the Union on March 1 [14] tests whether Washington pairs sanctions with energy-price relief.

Mixed: onshore policy support is real, but the offshore market is pricing regulatory and geopolitical risk at the same time.

PMIs & Activity: The last private factory gauge was weak: Reuters reported January Caixin activity contracting as COVID lockdowns bit [26] . Against that, China Daily reported industrial profits rebounding in 2021 as production improved [27] , and Best Selling Cars Blog put January vehicle wholesales up 0.9% in a stable market [28] . Power investment is the bright spot: Global Times reported an expected record 75-90GW of solar additions [30] . The next activity readings are the February surveys; until then the read is soft manufacturing and policy-led investment.

Credit Impulse: The policy direction is easier even before the credit numbers confirm it. Reuters reported that slowing inflation leaves room for policy easing [31] , and Bloomberg described the People's Bank seizing the moment for stimulus as the Fed hikes [32] . That divergence is the core of the China call: the US 2Y has risen 37 bps over 20 observations (US Treasury, prior close 2022-02-25) while Beijing leans the other way. The constraint is transmission through a stressed property sector and a tighter grip on platform finance; The Japan Times reported regulators telling banks and state firms to report exposure to Jack Ma's Ant [33] . Easing channelled through state lenders and infrastructure should lift onshore equities before it lifts household consumption.

Property: Property is still triage rather than recovery. Reuters reported Evergrande selling four projects to state-owned firms for $337 mln [34] , and SCMP described Chinese firms picking away at its assets [35] . CNBC reported Moody's downgrading Shimao as debt troubles drag on [36] , and The Standard reported Fantasia being sued over its bond default [37] . The pattern is a controlled transfer of viable projects into state hands, which limits systemic contagion but leaves private developers' offshore bondholders at the back of the queue. For equities, it means the property drag on steel, building materials and household wealth persists even as policy eases at the margin.

Fiscal, Policy & Trade: The next policy signal is the annual 'two sessions', which SCMP previewed as Beijing plotting its course [38] ; the growth target and fiscal quota will set the tone for the quarter. Hong Kong is already spending, with Al Jazeera reporting a $22bn budget as zero-COVID hammers businesses [39] . Trade cuts both ways. Reuters reported Russia's trade flows shifting towards China as sanctions start [40] , and Global Times quoted analysts saying the agricultural-trade impact on China is limited [41] . Doubts over the phase-one deal, which SCMP asked was a 'historic failure' [42] , and the Peterson Institute's finding that China bought none of the extra $200 billion of US exports [43] make secondary measures a tail risk if Beijing is seen to cushion Moscow.

Markets & Flows: Offshore internet is where the discount is applied. Tencent closed at 402.71 HKD, down 9.79% over five sessions, and Alibaba's Hong Kong line at 105.30 HKD, down 11.88%, while KWEB fell 9.04% and FXI 6.80% over the same span; ASHR, the onshore CSI 300 proxy, rose 0.22% (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2022-02-25). Reuters tied the rout to fresh fears of a regulatory crackdown [44] , and Bloomberg reported Alibaba planning for a new normal of low growth [45] . Primary-market appetite has faded too: Reuters reported Shein shelving its US IPO plan again on uncertain markets [46] , and WSJ reported a US block-trading investigation hitting Morgan Stanley's plans to expand in China [47] . The yuan is the counterpoint, with China Daily arguing it will remain an investor safe haven [48] .

New / Most Important: The war has sharpened the Taiwan question for portfolios. Reuters reported Beijing insisting Taiwan is 'not Ukraine' as the island raised its alert level [49] , and separately Taiwan warning of Chinese aircraft in its air defence zone [50] . TSMC said it will comply with export-control rules on Russia [51] , aligning Taiwan's leading chipmaker with Western sanctions. Foreign industrial capital is still committing to the mainland: Reuters, citing sources, reported Tesla planning a new Shanghai plant to more than double its China capacity [52] .

Playbook ✓: Action: prefer onshore exposure such as ASHR, at 36.62 USD, over offshore internet such as KWEB, at 34.10 USD (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2022-02-25). Trigger: a two-sessions growth target and fiscal package that confirms the easing bias [38] . What changes the call: Western moves toward secondary sanctions on Chinese banks that facilitate Russian trade, or a default chain spreading from private developers to state-linked ones. ⚠ Divergence: policy is easing and the Shanghai Composite is stable, yet Tencent fell 9.79% in five sessions and private developers keep defaulting, so the easing has not reached the assets global investors actually own.

Geopolitics & Security: The security premium is being met with physical reinforcement. Notes From Poland reported the UK sending an advanced air-defence system [98] , GOV.UK announced UK forces arriving on NATO's eastern flank [99] , and Onet reported the Polish army raising combat readiness [100] . Public opinion has shifted, with a Rzeczpospolita poll finding 71.1% of Poles think Russia could attack Poland [101] , and Moscow has retaliated in the air, banning flights from Bulgaria, Poland and the Czech Republic, per Al Jazeera [102] . Al Jazeera reported tens of thousands fleeing to Poland [103] , and Radio-Canada reported officials pledging help to all Ukrainians fleeing the war [104] , a humanitarian commitment with fiscal and housing costs. Energy routing is the other front: Reuters reported Yamal-Europe flows running east [105] , raising the strategic value of Baltic Pipe, which WysokieNapiecie examined [106] .

New / Most Important: The weekend validated Warsaw's hard line: CBC reported a widening group of countries moving to block Russian access to SWIFT [7] , and POLITICO reported the EU agreeing €500M in arms and aid for Ukraine's military in a 'watershed' move [107] . That makes Poland the logistics hub for Western support and a plausible target for Russian counter-measures. For the economy, Bankier's analysis that immigrants will first seek shelter before entering the labour market [108] implies a near-term cost to public finances ahead of any labour-supply dividend, while banks waiving fees for Ukrainians, per Business Insider Polska [109] , is a small fee-income cost the sector is choosing to carry.

Playbook ✓: Action: stay underweight Polish duration and zloty-sensitive assets, and fade Friday's bank-led bounce rather than chase it; within equities prefer commodity exporters such as KGHM and JSW over discretionary names with Ukrainian store networks such as LPP. Trigger: a renewed slide in the WIG20TR tracker below Friday's 33.93 PLN close alongside further zloty weakness would confirm the risk premium is rebuilding. What changes the call: an RPP move large enough to stabilise the currency, or a negotiated ceasefire that restores value to Polish firms' Ukrainian businesses. ⚠ Divergence: domestic buyers stepped in hard on Friday, with PKO up 13.27% and Pekao up 17.14% (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2022-02-25), and the NBP is publicly calming fears of cash-machine queues [110] , so local positioning may already be cleaner than the currency's weakness implies.
