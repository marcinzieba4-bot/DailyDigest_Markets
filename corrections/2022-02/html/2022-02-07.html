🌍 Geographic Macro Overview

🇺🇸 United States  Mixed  Lift-off priced; curve bear-flattening

Macro Stance: The US enters February with growth strong enough to validate lift-off and a market arguing about speed. Al Jazeera reports the economy added 467,000 jobs in January, calming Omicron fears [1] , and Reuters describes a labour market that shrugged off the Omicron surge with the economy strong ahead of rate hikes [2] . That follows an ISM manufacturing PMI of 57.6 index points [3] , a December in which job openings exceeded unemployed workers by 4.6 million, per CNBC [4] , and fourth-quarter GDP growth of 6.9%, per The Real Economy Blog [5] . The stance is Mixed, not Bullish, because that strength removes the Fed's reason to wait: the policy put has been struck lower, and equity multiples rather than earnings are carrying the adjustment.

Rates & Curve: The 2Y Treasury closed at 1.31% and the 10Y at 1.93%, leaving 10Y-2Y at +62 bps (US Treasury, prior close 2022-02-04). Over 20 observations the 2Y rose 43 bps from 0.88% while the 10Y rose 20 bps from 1.73%, so the spread narrowed 23 bps: a bear flattening, in a curve that is still moderately steep. The move accelerated last week, with the 2Y up 16 bps and the 10Y up 15 bps over five observations. Reuters explains why the flattening matters [6] , Standard Chartered expects the curve to flatten and possibly invert this year [7] , and Credit Suisse warns an inversion may be a false-positive recession signal [8] . Tipswatch notes the 30-year real yield has risen above zero [9] , removing the negative real-rate subsidy beneath long-duration equity.

New / Most Important: The swing factor is pace, not direction. Reuters argues the Fed is unlikely to start hiking with a bang [10] , whereas the Fed's Bullard sees three successive hikes to start tightening [11] . Equities absorbed the jobs data: the S&P 500 closed at 4,500.53 index points, +1.55% over five sessions but -4.16% over 20, and the Nasdaq 100 rose +1.66% on the week to 14,694.35 index points (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2022-02-04). VIX eased to 23.22 index points, -16.05% over five sessions, and the dollar index (DXY) fell -1.84% to 95.48 index points. Fiscal support is fading, with PBS reporting Manchin says Build Back Better is dead [12] , and Fed leadership is in limbo: Reuters reports Powell will serve as chair pro tempore as he awaits Senate action [13] .

Playbook ✓: Action: stay underweight front-end duration and unprofitable long-duration growth; prefer cash-generative mega-caps, energy and money-centre banks that monetise a higher 2Y. Trigger: the 2Y holding at or above 1.31% while 10Y-2Y compresses further from +62 bps (US Treasury, prior close 2022-02-04) confirms the bear-flattening regime. What changes the call: a soft next CPI print or a bond rally that drags the 2Y back toward the 0.88% of 20 observations ago would show the hiking path is over-priced; rotating back into quality growth such as the Nasdaq 100, still -6.79% over 20 sessions (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2022-02-04), would then offer the better risk-reward. A VIX spike that reverses last week's -16.05% decline would argue for cutting gross exposure first.

🇨🇳 China  Mixed  Easing bias meets weak activity

Macro Stance: China comes back from the Lunar New Year with policy leaning against a slowing economy. Mainland shares have not traded since the Shanghai Composite closed at 3,361.44 index points, -4.57% over five sessions and -7.12% over 20 (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2022-01-28), so onshore prices still embed pre-holiday pessimism. Hong Kong reopened more constructively: the Hang Seng closed at 24,573.29 index points, +3.24% from its last pre-holiday session and +5.51% over 20 sessions (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2022-02-04). The stance is Mixed. Bloomberg says the central bank is seizing the moment for stimulus as the Fed hikes [23] , yet CNN reports George Soros warning that China is facing an economic crisis [24] .

PMIs & Activity: Activity going into the holiday was soft. Reuters reports January factory activity contracted on the Caixin measure as COVID lockdowns bit [25] , Hürriyet Daily News says official factory activity edged down [26] , and ABN AMRO concludes the January PMIs indicate quarter-on-quarter growth will drop in Q1 [27] . The base is demanding: Global Times reports GDP grew 8.1% in 2021, the fastest in a decade [28] , with fixed-asset investment up 4.9%, per Macau Business [29] . Geopolitical Futures ties zero-COVID to manufacturing risk [30] , and HSBC tells CNBC that disruptions in China can ripple across global supply chains [31] . The Caixin services survey still to come is the next test of whether Omicron curbs are spreading from factories to consumers.

Credit Impulse: Monetary policy has clearly turned. CNBC reports the central bank cut key lending rates, including one for the first time in nearly two years [32] , after the BBC reported rate cuts as growth slowed [33] ; Fibre2Fashion details the cuts to medium-term loans and reverse repos [34] . The direction is unambiguous but transmission is not: cuts lower the price of credit, while developers' balance-sheet stress limits demand for it. BNP Paribas Asset Management argues China's debt vulnerability keeps defying dire predictions [35] , a reminder that the state-bank system can absorb losses while growth slows. The credit impulse should turn up from policy, but with a lag that keeps the first quarter weak and leaves equities trading the promise rather than the data.

Property: Property remains the drag. Caixin Global reports the property market made a bleak start to 2022 [36] , and Business Recorder says December new home prices fell less only because of promotions and easing curbs [37] . Reuters describes dashed dreams at China Evergrande's showpiece resort island [38] , and The Real Deal reports Oaktree Capital has seized a second Evergrande property [39] , evidence that creditors are enforcing rather than waiting for a rescue. Until sales stabilise, developers cut land purchases, local governments lose land revenue and the steel chain stays weak; Reuters notes 2021 iron ore imports retreated from a record on steel curbs [40] .

Fiscal, Policy & Trade: Beijing's mix combines easing with control. The WSJ describes Xi Jinping's push to scale China's three big mountains under common prosperity [41] , and the South China Morning Post reports JD.com's founder donating US$2 billion to charity amid the tech crackdown [42] . Trade friction persists: Reuters reports a US trade official says China failed to meet Phase 1 commitments [43] , while PBS reports the House passed a bill to boost US chip production to compete with China [44] . The external pivot is toward Moscow: Reuters reports Russia and China agreed a 30-year gas deal via a new pipeline, to settle in euros [45] , and Putin hailed $117.5 bln of China deals [46] . The BBC says China joined Russia in opposing NATO expansion [47] , which raises secondary-sanction risk for Chinese banks and exporters.

Markets & Flows: Offshore China has been the relative winner. FXI closed at 37.98 USD, +4.92% over five sessions, KWEB at 36.44 USD, +6.89%, and Alibaba's US ADR at 122.22 USD, +6.07%, while ASHR, the onshore CSI 300 tracker, gained only +1.56% (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2022-02-04). In Hong Kong, Tencent closed at 454.94 HKD, +9.67% over 20 sessions, and Alibaba rose +4.97% to 120.40 HKD on reopening. EV ADRs led: Li Auto rose +19.73% over five sessions, XPeng +15.86% and NIO +14.64%. CnEVPost reports China accounted for 53% of global passenger NEV sales in 2021 [48] , and the South China Morning Post says sales surged at Xpeng, Li Auto and NIO [49] . The gap between offshore gains and the stale onshore close is the immediate reopening risk.

New / Most Important: Geopolitics has moved ahead of data. CBS News reports the Beijing Winter Olympics opened under a cloud of controversy [50] , and DW says Russia and China criticised NATO after Putin and Xi's talks [51] ; Al Jazeera notes Taiwan condemned the contemptible timing of the partnership [52] . For investors the practical question is whether Beijing becomes Russia's sanctions buffer. Reuters reports Rosneft agreed a 100 mln T oil supply deal with CNPC [53] , deepening exactly the energy ties that Western sanctions would target. That widens the tail risk for state banks and commodity traders even as domestic easing supports equities.

Playbook ✓: Action: stay long offshore China internet and EV exposure (KWEB, Alibaba, Li Auto) sized for volatility, and avoid property-linked credit. Trigger: a mainland reopening that narrows the gap between the Shanghai Composite's stale close of 3,361.44 index points (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2022-01-28) and Hong Kong's +3.24% reopening gain, plus further central-bank easing, would confirm the policy-led rebound. What changes the call: a weak services survey, a fresh developer default, or US secondary sanctions on Chinese entities over Russia. ⚠ Divergence: policy is easing and offshore prices are rising, yet the Caixin factory gauge is contracting [25] , the property market made a bleak start to the year [36] and the onshore market fell -7.12% over 20 sessions into the holiday; the rally is pricing policy, not activity.

New / Most Important: The February MPC decision is the domestic catalyst and energy bills are the political one. That is the shield's blind spot: VAT relief at the pump does little for small firms' gas costs. Coface's head warns of a rise in insolvent companies, per Rzeczpospolita [105] , which would land first on bank asset quality in consumer and SME books. Higher rates therefore help bank margins now but raise credit costs later.

Playbook ✓: Action: overweight Polish banks (Pekao, PKO BP) against utilities, refiners and discretionary retail (PGE, Orlen, LPP), while keeping total Poland exposure underweight and hedged. Trigger: an MPC hike of the size Pekao expects, with the WIG20TR tracker holding above 37.53 PLN (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2022-02-04), would confirm that the rate benefit outweighs the security discount. What changes the call: a Russian escalation that hits the zloty and Polish risk premia, or a weaker currency that forces faster hikes and tips households and SMEs into arrears. ⚠ Divergence: banks are pricing a stronger rate path while the index and the zloty are pricing a security and stagflation shock; both cannot persist for long, and a hawkish NBP strengthens the bank leg only if the currency stabilises.
