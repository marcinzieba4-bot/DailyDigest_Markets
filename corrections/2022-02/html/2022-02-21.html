🌍 Geographic Macro Overview

🇺🇸 United States  Bearish  Fed lift-off meets war risk

Macro Stance: The US goes into a holiday-shortened week with two shocks stacked on top of each other: a Fed about to start hiking into the hottest inflation in four decades, and a Russia–Ukraine standoff that Washington now describes in near-certain terms. Reuters reported US consumer prices posting their largest annual gain in 40 years as inflation became widespread [1] , and Al Jazeera reported the Fed is prepared to move faster if that persists [2] . The S&P 500 closed at 4,348.87 index points, -0.72% on the session and -1.58% over five sessions, while the VIX held at 27.75 index points (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2022-02-18). We are Bearish: activity is firm, but policy and geopolitics both skew against equity multiples.

Rates & Curve: The Treasury par curve remains positively sloped, but the 20-observation move is a clean bear flattening: the 2Y rose 46 bps to 1.47% from 1.01%, the 10Y only 17 bps to 1.92% from 1.75%, and the 10Y-2Y spread compressed 29 bps to +45 bps (US Treasury, prior close 2022-02-18). The last five observations carry the geopolitical signal — the 2Y slipped 3 bps and the 10Y was unchanged as haven demand capped the sell-off. Reuters described the flattening as the curve pricing Fed tightening alongside fears of a policy error [3] , and Eurodollar futures are already betting a hawkish Fed could ease slightly in 2024 [4] . With 10Y-3M still +157 bps, the front end is doing the tightening while the long end questions how long the cycle can run. The DXY at 96.04 index points, +0.25% on the session (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2022-02-18), shows the dollar absorbing risk aversion rather than reversing.

New / Most Important: The weekend raised the invasion probability decisively. The BBC reported Biden saying he is convinced Putin has decided to invade [5] , the Guardian reported US warnings that Russia has amassed up to 190,000 troops on Ukraine's borders [6] , and Reuters said Putin would oversee nuclear drills [7] . Fed messaging is split: Bullard again called for a front-loaded campaign, per the WSJ [8] , while the New York Fed president saw no need for a big March move, according to China Daily [9] . Reuters reported the Fed is set to raise rates 25 bps in March as calls for 50 bps grow louder [10] . With US cash markets shut for Presidents' Day, the weekend's news is first repriced at home on Tuesday.

Playbook ✓: Action: stay underweight long-duration growth against pricing-power staples and energy, and carry a gold hedge. Trigger to add risk: concrete progress toward a Biden–Putin summit while the S&P 500 recovers its 20-session reference of 4,397.94 index points (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2022-02-18). Trigger to press the hedge: a Tuesday open below the 4,348.87 close with the VIX above 27.75. What changes the call: credible de-escalation plus a Fed that leans toward 25 bps would let the curve steepen from +45 bps and relieve multiples; an invasion would make the 10Y's haven bid, rather than the 2Y's hike pricing, the dominant rates driver.

Playbook ✓: Action: underweight euro-area equities, especially Russia-sensitive banks, against the US. Trigger to cover: diplomatic progress plus TTF holding below 73.76 EUR/MWh. What changes the call: a pipeline interruption would push gas higher and turn the ECB's gradualism from a support into a stagflation problem, while a durable de-escalation would favour the cheaper European cyclicals first.

🇨🇳 China  Mixed  Onshore easing, offshore squeeze

Macro Stance: China is running the opposite cycle to the US — easing into a slowdown while Washington tightens into inflation — and its equity market is splitting accordingly. The Shanghai Composite closed at 3,490.76 index points, +0.66% on the session and +0.80% over five sessions, while the Hang Seng fell -1.88% to 24,327.71 index points (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2022-02-18). The difference is regulation: onshore investors trade the policy put, offshore investors trade platform risk. BNP Paribas Asset Management's analysis of US–China monetary policy divergence [21] sets the macro frame, and Reuters' report that the yuan slipped under that divergence [22] shows the currency beginning to share the adjustment. Mixed: constructive on A-shares, cautious on internet platforms.

PMIs & Activity: Momentum entered the year soft. Reuters reported January factory activity contracting on the Caixin PMI as COVID lockdowns bit [23] , after Global Times reported 2021 growth of 8.1%, the fastest in a decade [24] . Fixed-asset investment rose 4.9% in 2021, per Macau Business [25] , and China Daily reported industrial profits rebounding on production [26] — a supply-side recovery handing over to softer domestic demand. Reuters said slowing inflation leaves room for policy easing [27] . Omicron is the swing factor: Reuters reported Hong Kong 'overwhelmed' as infections hit a record [28] , and Al Jazeera reported Xi telling Hong Kong to control the outbreak [29] . The next official PMI will show whether the post-holiday restart is lifting activity.

Credit Impulse: The credit pulse is being pushed from the top. Al Jazeera reported the PBOC governor vowing support amid 'weak links' [30] , and Bloomberg reported the PBOC pumping in more liquidity, spurring gains in Chinese stocks [31] . That follows January's cuts to key lending rates — one for the first time in nearly two years, per CNBC [32] — and to medium-term and reverse-repo rates, per Fibre2Fashion [33] . Bloomberg described China's central bank seizing the moment for stimulus as the Fed hikes [34] . The constraint is local-government leverage: the SCMP reported banks being told to support rural infrastructure without adding to hidden debt [35] . The impulse should turn up, but it is being rationed toward infrastructure rather than used to reflate property.

Property: Property remains the policy test. The Khmer Times reported China excluding affordable rental housing from property credit controls [36] — targeted relief, not a broad bailout. Corporate stress is still surfacing: Bloomberg, citing Caixin, reported China's Cedar unable to pay $3.1 billion overdue [37] , a reminder that credit stress is not confined to property developers. Onshore investors are reading these as idiosyncratic for now; the Shanghai Composite's +0.80% over five sessions (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2022-02-18) shows onshore equities holding up despite them. A stabilisation in home sales would confirm that view; a missed payment from a larger name would break it.

Fiscal, Policy & Trade: Policy is simultaneously supportive and punitive. The WSJ reported China ordering cuts to food-delivery fees, sparking a tech selloff [38] , and Bloomberg said the policy wiped $26 billion off Meituan's value [39] — common prosperity is now a margin story, not only an antitrust one. Trade friction is rising on two fronts: Reuters reported the US adding e-commerce sites operated by Tencent and Alibaba to its 'notorious markets' list [40] , the BBC reported Washington accusing China of 'serious harm' to workers through trade [41] , and Reuters reported the EU imposing anti-dumping tariffs on Chinese steel fasteners [42] . On Russia, the SCMP reported Beijing warning that Western sanctions will worsen the Ukraine crisis [43] , which could leave Chinese banks and exporters exposed if sanctions widen.

Markets & Flows: The tape shows the split cleanly. KWEB fell -5.04% on the session and Alibaba's US ADR -4.37%, while the CSI 300 tracker ASHR rose +2.22% over five sessions (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2022-02-18). Tencent in Hong Kong fell -1.88% to 446.40 HKD yet is still +6.94% over 20 sessions, and Business Insider reported Tencent joining the world's ten most valuable companies as Meta slid [44] . Allocators are rotating rather than exiting: Caixin reported Hillhouse buying NEV makers while ditching Alibaba and Bilibili [45] , and CNBC reported banks from Credit Suisse to Goldman Sachs saying it is time to buy Chinese stocks [46] . Lenovo announced its selection as a Hang Seng Index constituent [47] , which should add passive demand for hardware over platforms.

New / Most Important: The food-delivery fee order is the most important signal because it shows Beijing still willing to regulate margins directly even while the PBOC eases. Offshore platforms fell into the weekend, and Pinduoduo -6.05%, Baidu -5.22% and JD.com -3.61% on the session (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2022-02-18) show investors applying the same template across the sector. NPR reported the Beijing Winter Games coming to a close [48] , which turns attention back to the policy calendar. The next loan prime rate fixing is the test of whether January's cuts extend.

Playbook ✓: Action: own onshore beta through ASHR against an underweight in offshore platforms through KWEB, sized as a pair. Trigger: a loan prime rate or reserve-ratio cut, or wider property credit carve-outs, to add to the long leg; another fee or data directive to add to the short. What changes the call: a Russia-sanctions spillover into Chinese banks or a broad Hong Kong lockdown would hit both legs, so the pair stays small. ⚠ Divergence: over 20 sessions the Hang Seng is +0.89% while the Shanghai Composite is -0.87% (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2022-02-18) — offshore had been outperforming until the latest five sessions, and Tencent's 20-session gain says part of the platform discount may already be priced.
