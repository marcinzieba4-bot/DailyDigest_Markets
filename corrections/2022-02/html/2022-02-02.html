🇺🇸 United States  Mixed  March lift-off priced; curve bear-flattening

Macro Stance: The US starts February with growth decelerating from a strong base and a Federal Reserve committed to tightening into it. Reuters reported the Fed is likely to hike in March as Powell vowed a sustained inflation fight [1] , and a Reuters poll of economists expects three hikes this year to tame unruly inflation [2] . The data argue for patience on growth but not on inflation: the January ISM manufacturing index eased to 57.6 from 58.7 [3] [4] , still firmly expansionary, while CNBC counted 4.6 million more job openings than unemployed workers in December [5] . Fiscal support is fading as well, with Senator Joe Manchin declaring Build Back Better 'dead' [6] . Our stance is Mixed: earnings momentum is genuine, but the policy path caps how much investors will pay for it.

Rates & Curve: The Treasury curve is doing the classic pre-hike move. The 2-year yield closed at 1.18% and the 10-year at 1.81%, a 10Y-2Y spread of +63 bps (US Treasury, prior close 2022-02-01). Over 20 observations the 2-year rose 40 bps from 0.78% and the 10-year 18 bps from 1.63%, compressing the spread by 22 bps: a bear flattening, with the curve still moderately steep. In the latest five observations the 2-year added 16 bps against 3 bps for the 10-year, so the front end is still carrying the repricing. Standard Chartered expects the curve to flatten and possibly invert this year [7] . Equities have stopped fighting it: the S&P 500 closed at 4,546.54 points, +0.69% on the day and +4.36% over five sessions, VIX fell 11.56% to 21.96 points and DXY eased 0.16% to 96.39 points (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2022-02-01).

New / Most Important: The decisive news came after the bell. Reuters reported that Google propelled record Alphabet revenue, driving the shares up 8% [8] , with a 20-for-1 stock split on top [9] ; AMD rose 10% after issuing a strong 2022 sales outlook [10] , while Electronic Arts' revenue forecast missed estimates after the Battlefield flop [11] . The message is that investors will still pay for visible forward growth in a rising-rate tape, while a soft forecast leaves nowhere to hide. The macro backdrop is less forgiving: CNBC warns that after a huge year for growth the economy is about to 'slam into a wall' [12] , and the Nasdaq only narrowly missed its worst January ever [13] .

Playbook ✓: Action: stay long quality mega-cap growth that has just reset expectations higher (Alphabet's record quarter, AMD's 2022 outlook), funded from long-duration Treasuries and unprofitable growth, and keep 2s10s flatteners on. Trigger: the 2-year holding at or above 1.18% while the S&P 500 holds its 4,546.54-point close (US Treasury, prior close 2022-02-01; Yahoo Finance, prior close 2022-02-01) would confirm that equities can absorb a March lift-off. What changes the call: a VIX reversal that erases the 11.56% one-day drop, or a growth scare that drags the 2-year back toward 0.78%, would favour duration over equities and end the flattener.

🇨🇳 China  Mixed  PBOC easing through a holiday-thin market

Macro Stance: China is closed for the Lunar New Year, so investors are working from a pre-holiday data set and a policy bias that is turning steadily more supportive. The economy finished 2021 strong on paper, with Global Times reporting GDP growth of 8.1%, the fastest in a decade [24] , but momentum entering 2022 is weak. The People's Bank of China has already moved: the BBC reported rate cuts as growth slows [25] , and CNBC noted a cut to key lending rates, one of them the first in nearly two years [26] . Bloomberg's framing is the right one: as the Fed hikes, China's central bank is seizing the moment for stimulus [27] . Our stance is Mixed: policy is easing, but property and COVID controls still set the growth path.

PMIs & Activity: January surveys confirmed a soft start. Reuters reported that official factory activity growth slowed as demand waned with COVID surging [28] , while the Caixin gauge showed outright contraction as lockdowns bit [29] . ABN AMRO concludes the January PMIs indicate quarter-on-quarter growth will drop in the first quarter [30] , echoing an official who warned in January that factories face 'rather large' downward pressure in the first quarter [31] . Some 2021 data were firmer: industrial profits rebounded as production improved [32] , and fixed-asset investment rose 4.9% [33] . The mismatch matters: backward-looking strength gives Beijing room to be patient, while the forward indicators argue for more easing before activity can stabilise.

Credit Impulse: Until the next lending data, the credit impulse has to be read through policy. The January moves, cuts to medium-term lending and reverse-repo rates reported by Fibre2Fashion [34] on top of the lending-rate cut, lower the marginal cost of credit, but transmission depends on banks' willingness to lend into property. Nikkei Asia's report that major Evergrande creditor Minsheng faces a wave of bad loans [35] shows the problem: lenders carrying developer losses ration risk even as funding costs fall. The likely sequence is a turn in the impulse from a low base, led by policy-directed lending rather than households, which helps materials and infrastructure before it helps consumption.

Property: Property remains the binding constraint. AFR reported that Beijing is considering breaking up Evergrande to contain the crisis [36] , and Oaktree Capital has seized a second Evergrande property in China [37] , a sign that creditors are enforcing on collateral. Reuters' insight on the developer's showpiece resort island [38] captures the stranded-asset problem, while Bloomberg found cities selling land to themselves as the property crisis leaves cities selling land to themselves [39] . The one stabilising sign is pricing: new home prices fell less in December on promotions and easing curbs [40] . Until sales volumes respond to lower rates, local-government finances remain the channel through which property drags on growth.

Fiscal, Policy & Trade: Infrastructure ambition is back on the table, with the South China Morning Post listing six controversial mega-projects under discussion [41] , but external friction is building again. A U.S. trade official said China failed to meet its 'Phase 1' commitments [42] , and CNN explains why Biden is keeping Trump's China tariffs in place [43] . The WTO has ruled that China can impose 645 million dollars of retaliatory tariffs on U.S. imports [44] , and the EU has escalated its trade dispute with Beijing over Lithuania [45] . CNBC adds that a crackdown on Chinese IPOs in the U.S. could make investors think twice about betting on Chinese tech [46] . Policy therefore pulls two ways: domestic easing supports the cycle, while external pressure caps the multiple investors will pay for it.

Markets & Flows: Mainland and Hong Kong markets are shut, so the last prints are stale: the Shanghai Composite closed at 3,361.44 points, down 4.57% over five sessions and 7.12% over 20 (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2022-01-28), while the Hang Seng finished at 23,802.26 points, up 2.27% over 20 sessions (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2022-01-31). The South China Morning Post noted the Hang Seng logged a January gain but closed the Year of the Ox with a slump [47] . U.S.-listed proxies kept trading: KWEB rose 0.67% to 37.66 USD and FXI 0.66% to 38.19 USD, while the A-share tracker ASHR is down 6.48% over 20 sessions (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2022-02-01). Offshore internet up, onshore A-shares down: investors are rewarding the policy pivot in platform names while onshore pricing still reflects growth risk.

New / Most Important: The new element is the overlap of the Winter Olympics, COVID controls and the post-holiday restart. Axios argues COVID threatens China's Olympic prestige [48] , and Hong Kong is locking down homes in a lesson taken from Beijing [49] . Supply chains are the transmission channel: HSBC warns that disruptions in China can create ripple effects across the global supply chain [50] . Iron ore is the early tell, with Singapore futures falling more than 6% [51] before mainland traders return.

Playbook ✓: Action: stay overweight offshore China internet through KWEB and the Alibaba ADR (BABA), which closed at 127.22 USD and is up 6.78% over five sessions (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2022-02-01), and stay underweight onshore A-shares via ASHR until property sales stabilise. Trigger: a further PBOC rate or reserve-requirement cut after the holiday, with the Hang Seng holding above its 23,274.75-point level of early January. What changes the call: a post-holiday COVID wave that forces factory closures, or a fresh U.S. delisting push, would hit the offshore leg hardest. ⚠ Divergence: policy is easing and offshore internet is rallying, yet the PMIs, the Caixin contraction and iron ore's slide say real activity is still losing momentum.

🇵🇱 Poland  Mixed  Rate hikes meet the anti-inflation shield

Macro Stance: Poland combines the region's strongest growth with one of its sharpest inflation problems. But CPI ended 2021 at 8.6% [72] , the highest in 21 years [73] , and Credit Agricole has lifted its inflation forecast to 7.5% for 2022 and 5.5% for the following year [74] . Obserwator Gospodarczy still expects industry to be the economy's driving force in 2022 [75] . Rzeczpospolita warns that a slowdown is being masked by statistics, with inflation and rates already curbing appetites [76] . Our stance is Mixed: growth supports earnings, but inflation, a weak zloty and war risk on the eastern border keep the risk premium high.

Markets: Polish equities trade at a discount that already reflects much of the bad news. Strefa Inwestorów notes that Warsaw investors fear a crash and a crisis, which is why the market is valued really low [77] , and Parkiet reported a rebound after the declines [78] . Outside finance the tape is mixed: Allegro rose 3.28% to 38.83 PLN, while the retail broker XTB fell 2.78% to 16.11 PLN. The currency is the weak link: FXMAG calls the zloty once again the worst-performing currency [80] , which imports inflation and complicates the central bank's job.

Fiscal & Politics: Fiscal policy has become an inflation tool. The government's Anti-Inflation Shield 2.0 started on February 1, Gov.pl announced [81] , with lower VAT on food, energy and fuel [82] . The trade-off is that the shield lowers headline CPI mechanically while supporting demand, and Credit Agricole has warned the food VAT cut will add to inflation [86] . Polski Ład adds noise: Rzeczpospolita tallies who gains or loses in take-home pay [87] . Governance risk sits on top, as Rzeczpospolita reports Glapiński's candidacy for a second NBP term is poorly rated [88] and the Sejm is delaying the election of Monetary Policy Council members [89] .

Corporate & Sectors: Corporate news splits along energy and rates lines. Comparic noted Orlen shares at a one-month low despite record crude prices [90] , and Subiektywnie o finansach issues a big warning against the Orlen–Lotos merger [91] ; Orlen closed at 71.72 PLN, -4.58% over 20 sessions. Gas pricing is a live dispute, with wnp.pl reporting that Gazprom wants PGNiG to pay a surcharge [92] . Coal is the winner: JSW rose 6.30% to 42.04 PLN and is up 18.76% over 20 sessions after Bankier flagged its accelerating rebound [93] . KGHM gained 2.29% to 142.75 PLN, though its workforce is getting a bonus plus a 10% pay rise [94] . CD Projekt, at 180.94 PLN, benefits from gaming M&A, as Investing.com Polska tied its rise to Sony's Bungie deal [95] . Consumer names lag: Dino fell 8.63% over five sessions to 312.50 PLN, and Pepco is back at its IPO price [96] (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2022-02-01). Utilities are the laggards: PGE, which has just published locations for new wind farms [97] , is down 7.51% over 20 sessions at 7.56 PLN, a reminder that the energy transition needs capital just as funding costs rise.

Geopolitics & Security: Poland is on the front line of the Russia–Ukraine standoff, and the security response is accelerating. Reuters reports Britain, Poland and Ukraine are in cooperation talks over the Russian threat [98] , which TVN24, citing the BBC, describes as a new alliance linking London, Kyiv and Warsaw [99] . TWZ reports Poland will send shoulder-fired anti-aircraft missiles to Ukraine [100] . Moscow's rhetoric is hardening: at a press conference with Viktor Orbán, Putin called NATO's enlargement to include Poland a fraud, Interia reported [101] , and TVN24 has reported Russian airborne troops in Brest near the Polish border [102] . Warsaw has contingency plans to evacuate diplomats from Ukraine [103] . Energy is the economic channel: in a 300Gospodarka interview, Andrzej Sikora argues Poland will cope without Russian gas, but it will not be cheaper [104] .

New / Most Important: The most important new fact is that the shield is now live: Biznes Wprost notes that lower VAT on fuel and food is not the only change taking effect [105] . Measured CPI will fall for tax reasons, which markets could misread as genuine disinflation. Business Insider Polska argues that a real reduction in inflation is all in Glapiński's hands [106] , and Coface's chief expects a rise in insolvent companies [107] . For investors that means bank earnings upgrades now and credit costs later.

Playbook ✓: Action: hold Polish banks (PKO BP, Pekao, mBank) as the cleanest expression of rising WIBOR, keep the position smaller than the rate case alone would justify, and avoid domestic consumer names until real wages recover. Trigger: mBank holding above 455.00 PLN, its close of January 3 (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2022-01-03), while WIBOR keeps rising. What changes the call: a military escalation on the Ukrainian border, or an NBP leadership outcome that weakens the zloty further, would turn banks from rate winners into risk-premium losers, and a sharp tax-driven fall in CPI could cap rate expectations. ⚠ Divergence: strong growth and rising bank stocks point to resilience, yet the zloty's weakness, record inflation and Russian troops near the border argue the risk premium should be rising, not falling.
