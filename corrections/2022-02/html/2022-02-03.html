🇺🇸 United States  Mixed  March lift-off priced; earnings split

Macro Stance: Activity is still running hot enough to justify tightening, but the impulse is fading just as policy turns. CNBC reported fourth-quarter GDP growing at a 6.9% pace despite Omicron [1] , the January ISM manufacturing PMI printed 57.6 index points after 58.7 in December [2] [3] , and CNBC counted 4.6 million more job openings than unemployed workers in December [4] . Against that, CNBC argued the economy is about to 'slam into a wall' after a huge growth year [5] and Business Insider reported Americans' economic hopes falling to decade lows in January [6] . The equity market has stopped arguing with the Fed and is discriminating between companies that fund their own growth and those that need cheap capital to reach profits.

Rates & Curve: The two-year Treasury yield closed at 1.16% and the ten-year at 1.78%, a 10Y−2Y spread of +62 bps (US Treasury, prior close 2022-02-02). Over twenty observations the two-year has risen 39 bps from 0.77% against just 12 bps for the ten-year from 1.66%, compressing the spread by 27 bps: the curve shape is moderately steep, but the twenty-observation move is bear flattening (both up, 2Y up more). Over five observations the front end added 3 bps while the ten-year fell 7 bps, the signature of a market pricing lift-off rather than a growth scare. Reuters explained why the flattening matters [7] , Standard Chartered expects the curve to flatten and possibly invert this year [8] , and Credit Suisse counters that an eventual inversion may be a 'false positive' recession signal [9] .

New / Most Important: The Fed's January statement pointed to a March hike [10] , Reuters described Powell tearing up the gradualist script [11] , and the Fed's Bullard now sees three successive hikes to start tightening [12] . Equities answered with a relief rally: the S&P 500 closed at 4,589.38 index points, +0.94% on the session and +5.50% over five sessions but still -4.26% over twenty (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2022-02-02), after Reuters reported the Nasdaq narrowly missed its worst January ever [13] . The VIX, at 22.09 index points, has fallen 30.88% in five sessions yet sits 5.18 points above its early-January level, and DXY slipped 0.47% to 95.94 index points (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2022-02-02). Across two evenings, the earnings tape split violently between Alphabet's beat and Meta's first decline in daily users, so index calm masks wide single-stock dispersion. Morgan Stanley's Sheets still sees more pain ahead for US stocks [14] , a caution the relief rally has not dispelled.

Playbook ✓: Action: keep equity beta near neutral but tilt it toward cash-generative leaders (energy, semiconductors with guidance support, Alphabet) and away from social-advertising and consumer-fintech growth; keep front-end Treasury duration short. Trigger: add risk only if the S&P 500 holds above 4,589.38 index points while the two-year stops rising from 1.16%. What changes the call: a renewed jump in the two-year that compresses the spread well below +62 bps with the VIX back above 22.09 index points would signal flattening turning into a growth scare; then rotate toward health care and staples.

🇨🇳 China  Mixed  PBOC easing vs. contraction

Macro Stance: China is the one major economy easing into a global tightening cycle, and with mainland exchanges shut for the Lunar New Year after the final Year of the Ox session, the divergence has not yet been tested by onshore trading. Bloomberg's framing is the right one: as the Fed hikes, China's central bank seizes the moment for stimulus [26] . The People's Bank cut key lending rates in January, including one for the first time in nearly two years [27] , because growth is slowing [28] . The stance is Mixed: policy is moving in the right direction, but activity, property and external demand are deteriorating at the same time, and none of the easing has yet visibly reached credit or housing.

PMIs & Activity: January surveys confirm the loss of momentum. Reuters reported the Caixin factory gauge contracting as COVID lockdowns bit [29] , while the official PMI showed growth slowing as demand waned during the virus surge [30] ; ABN AMRO reads the January PMIs as pointing to weaker quarter-on-quarter growth in the first quarter [31] . An official had already warned that factories face 'rather large' downward pressure in the first quarter [32] . The base is strong — Global Times reported 2021 GDP growth of 8.1%, the fastest in a decade [33] , and industrial profits rebounded as production improved [34] — but zero-COVID controls, which the New York Times suggests may outlast the virus [35] , make a services-led rebound hard to underwrite.

Credit Impulse: The credit impulse is the missing link. The PBOC has cut rates on medium-term loans and reverse repos [36] , and inflation showing signs of easing as energy and food price rises cool [37] gives it room to do more. But rate cuts lift activity only when banks are willing to lend and developers and local governments are willing to borrow, and both channels are impaired by the property clean-up. George Soros warns that China faces an economic crisis [38] ; that is a minority view, but the burden of proof now sits with the next aggregate-financing data. As long as developers absorb credit to refinance old obligations rather than to build, headline easing will flatter the policy optics more than it lifts the impulse.

Property: Property remains the main drag. Caixin described the market as off to a bleak start in 2022 [39] , and the Evergrande workout is being run by the state and creditors rather than by buyers: the AFR reported Beijing considering breaking up Evergrande to contain the crisis [40] , The Real Deal said Oaktree Capital has seized a second Evergrande property [41] , and Reuters chronicled the dashed dreams of its showpiece resort island [42] . December new-home prices fell less on promotions and easing curbs [43] , which is the template for the response — local loosening rather than a national bailout. MoneyWeek is openly arguing for shorting the largest real-estate broker [44] , a reminder that transaction-linked equities, not only developers, carry the volume risk.

Fiscal, Policy & Trade: Fiscal policy is the lever to watch, and the SCMP's survey of six controversial mega-projects under discussion [45] points to infrastructure as a possible swing factor. Trade is the external constraint. A US trade official said China failed to meet its Phase 1 commitments [46] , which complicates any tariff relief, and the EU escalated its dispute with Beijing over Lithuania [47] . HSBC warns that disruptions in China can send ripple effects across the global supply chain [48] , which matters because the trade boom — Pandaily reported 2021 imports and exports topping $6 trillion for the first time [49] — was the growth engine now fading. The Winter Olympics add a reputational overlay, with Axios reporting that COVID threatens China's Olympic prestige [50] .

Markets & Flows: The Shanghai Composite went into the holiday at 3,361.44 index points, -0.97% on its final session and -7.12% over twenty sessions (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2022-01-28), while the Hang Seng closed at 23,802.26 index points, +1.07% (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2022-01-31); the SCMP noted it logged a January gain while closing the Year of the Ox with a slump [51] . US-listed proxies kept trading and gave back ground: Alibaba's ADR fell 3.41% to 122.88 USD, Pinduoduo slid 3.04% and KWEB lost 2.89%, though over twenty sessions KWEB is still +5.66% and JD.com +14.03% (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2022-02-02). Capital raising is cautious rather than closed: Yahoo Finance reported fintech PingPong weighing a $1 billion Hong Kong IPO [52] .

New / Most Important: The most important signal is the gap between offshore and onshore pricing. ASHR, the CSI 300 proxy, is -5.51% over twenty sessions while FXI is +5.49% (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2022-02-02), so offshore large caps have beaten domestic A-shares tied to property and credit. Corporate behaviour may be adapting to the common-prosperity politics that the Lowy Institute calls a Catch-22 [53] : Forbes reported JD.com CEO Richard Liu donating more than $2 billion of shares to charity [54] . EV demand is a bright spot, with the SCMP reporting sales surging at Xpeng, Li Auto and NIO [55] , although CNBC noted Xpeng's units dipped from December [56] .

Playbook ✓: Action: stay with offshore platform and EV exposure (KWEB, BABA, LI) over onshore cyclicals and property-linked names, sized small until mainland trading resumes. Trigger: add if the Shanghai Composite holds 3,361.44 index points on reopening and the next credit data show the January cuts reaching new lending. What changes the call: another PMI contraction or a disorderly Evergrande break-up would turn the stance Bearish, while a property-easing package that revives sales would justify rotating into onshore cyclicals. ⚠ Divergence: the PBOC is easing while the Caixin PMI contracts and property sales stall, and offshore FXI (+5.49%) and onshore ASHR (-5.51%) twenty-session returns point in opposite directions.
