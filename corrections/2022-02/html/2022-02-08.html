🇺🇸 United States  Mixed  Front-end repricing

Macro Stance: The US economy is running hot enough to force the Federal Reserve's hand, and markets are repricing the front end faster than equities can rotate. Reuters described a labour market that shrugged off the Omicron surge and an economy that looks strong ahead of rate hikes [1] . Al Jazeera reported 467,000 jobs added in January, a print that calmed Omicron fears [2] . That follows CNBC's report that the Fed pointed to a hike coming in March, and Reuters' account of Bullard backing three successive hikes to start tightening [3] [4] . Growth is not the problem; the price of money is. The S&P 500 closed at 4,483.87 index points, -0.37% on the session and -4.13% over 20 sessions (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2022-02-07). Stance: Mixed.

Rates & Curve: The Treasury curve has made the classic pre-hike move. The 2Y yield stands at 1.30% and the 10Y at 1.92%, leaving 10Y-2Y at +62 bps (US Treasury, prior close 2022-02-07). Over 20 observations the 2Y rose +43 bps from 0.87% while the 10Y added only +16 bps, a -27 bps spread change: bear flattening on a curve that is still moderately steep. Over five observations both legs climbed together, the 2Y by +12 bps and the 10Y by +13 bps, which suggests the strong payroll print lifted the long end as well as policy expectations. Reuters explained why the flattening matters, and Credit Suisse argued that an eventual inversion may be a false-positive recession signal [5] [6] . With the 30-year real yield rising above zero, per tipswatch.com, the discount-rate shock now hits long-duration equity directly [7] .

New / Most Important: The equity tape is driven by dispersion inside mega-cap tech rather than by the index level. Meta Platforms (FB) closed at 224.91 USD, -5.14% on the session and -28.20% over five sessions, after what CNBC called the largest one-day value drop in stock market history (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2022-02-07) [8] . Amazon moved the other way, +5.59% over five sessions, after Bloomberg reported a record market-value gain [9] . The Nasdaq 100 closed at 14,571.25 index points, -0.84%, while the Russell 2000 rose +0.51%. VIX sits at 22.86 index points, -1.55% on the session and +21.86% over 20 sessions, and the dollar index (DXY) is at 95.40 index points, -1.18% over five sessions (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2022-02-07).

Playbook ✓: Action: stay underweight long-duration growth against energy and banks while the front end reprices, expressed as short QQQ against XLE and XLF. Trigger: add if the 2Y pushes further above 1.30% while the 10Y lags, extending the bear flattening (US Treasury, prior close 2022-02-07). What changes the call: a softer next CPI print, or a 10Y that rises faster than the 2Y, would say the market is pricing growth rather than tightening, which would favour re-entering quality tech. CNN's warning that markets face a wild ride until the Fed really starts raising rates is the right volatility framing: sell VIX spikes only once lift-off is fully priced [10] .

🇨🇳 China  Mixed  Easing against a property drag

Macro Stance: China is easing while the rest of the world tightens, and that divergence is the core of the call. Bloomberg framed the People's Bank of China as seizing the moment for stimulus as the Fed hikes, after CNBC reported cuts to key lending rates, including one for the first time in nearly two years [23] [24] . The problem is transmission: easing has not yet reached the sectors that set the growth path, above all housing and private consumption. Mainland equities greeted the first session after the Lunar New Year closure with relief. The Shanghai Composite closed at 3,429.58 index points, +2.03% against its last pre-holiday close, yet it remains -5.78% over 20 sessions (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2022-02-07). Stance: Mixed.

PMIs & Activity: Activity entering the holiday was soft at the margin. Reuters reported the Caixin manufacturing PMI contracted in January as COVID lockdowns bit [25] . The annual numbers flatter: Global Times reported 2021 GDP growth of 8.1%, the fastest in a decade, and Macau Business put fixed-asset investment growth at 4.9% [26] [27] . Composition matters more than the headline. China Daily reported industrial profits rebounding in 2021 as production improved, but Geopolitical Futures examined the strain between zero-COVID and manufacturing, and the New York Times described abandoned fruit as a side-effect of strict virus policy [28] [29] [30] . The supply side is intact; domestic demand needs credit.

Credit Impulse: The rate cuts are the start of a credit impulse, not proof of one. Fibre2Fashion reported cuts to rates on medium-term loans and reverse repos, and the BBC tied the January moves to slowing growth [31] [32] . Swaps linked to the loan prime rate, tracked by MacroMicro, are the market's gauge of how much further easing is expected [33] . BNP Paribas Asset Management argues China's debt vulnerability keeps defying dire predictions, the optimistic case for renewed leverage [34] . Regulation still bites at the edges of the system: Reuters reported Ant-backed MYBank fined for violating credit-scoring management rules [35] . The impulse should turn over the coming months; what is not yet visible is credit flowing back to developers.

Property: Property remains the binding constraint. Caixin described a bleak start to 2022 for the market, and The Real Deal reported Oaktree seizing a second Evergrande property, which shows creditors enforcing rather than waiting [36] [37] . Business Recorder reported new home prices fell less in December on promotions and easing curbs, an early sign that restrictions are loosening at the margin [38] . MoneyWeek is openly arguing for a short in China's largest real estate broker, a reminder that transaction volumes, not list prices, are what investors fear [39] . For iron-ore exporters this is the key channel, and Reuters reported China plans to increase domestic iron ore output and boost the use of steel scrap [40] .

Fiscal, Policy & Trade: Trade policy is drifting toward friction with Washington and alignment with Moscow. A US trade official said China failed to meet its Phase 1 commitments, per Reuters [41] . Reuters also reported a 30-year Russia–China gas deal via a new pipeline, to be settled in euros, and Putin hailing $117.5 billion of China deals [42] [43] . The BBC reported China joining Russia in opposing NATO expansion, which raises the sanctions exposure of Chinese banks and energy buyers [44] . At home, the WSJ describes Xi seeking to scale China's 'three big mountains' in pursuit of common prosperity, and the South China Morning Post reported JD.com's founder donating US$2 billion amid the tech crackdown [45] [46] . Fiscal support is more likely to flow through infrastructure than through households.

Markets & Flows: The split on reopening was telling: mainland A-shares rallied while Hong Kong platforms fell. Alibaba closed at 115.00 HKD, -4.49%, and its US ADR at 114.82 USD, -6.05%, with the Verdict reporting SoftBank looking to sell Alibaba shares while its Arm deal bogs down (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2022-02-07) [47] . Tencent, at 453.99 HKD, is still +14.37% over 20 sessions, and the Hang Seng, at 24,579.55 index points, is +7.30% over the same span, so offshore China had already outperformed into the holiday. KWEB fell -2.36% and FXI -0.61%, while ASHR, which traded through the closure, edged up +0.03% (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2022-02-07). The pattern argues for onshore cyclicals over offshore platforms facing both regulation and a potential forced seller.

New / Most Important: The most important development is the widening policy gap with the Fed. With the US 2Y at 1.30% after a +43 bps rise over 20 observations, Chinese easing compresses the rate differential and limits how far the PBoC can cut without pressuring capital flows (US Treasury, prior close 2022-02-07). Second, the Russia alignment: if Western sanctions follow any move on Ukraine, Chinese energy buyers sit in the crossfire, and the Rosneft–CNPC supply deal reported by Reuters deepens that exposure [48] . Third, electric vehicles: CnEVPost reported China accounted for 53% of global passenger NEV sales in 2021, yet NIO is -18.12% and XPeng -18.18% over 20 sessions [49] (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2022-02-07).

Playbook ✓: Action: overweight onshore A-share cyclicals through ASHR and underweight Hong Kong-listed platforms through KWEB until the forced-seller overhang clears. Trigger: a further cut to the loan prime rate or a visible pickup in property transactions would confirm the credit impulse and justify adding. What changes the call: BABA breaking below its 114.82 USD close on confirmed SoftBank selling would hit offshore sentiment broadly, and a fresh wave of developer defaults would undermine the onshore leg (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2022-02-07). ⚠ Divergence: policy is easing and A-shares rallied +2.03% on reopening, but the January Caixin factory reading contracted and Caixin's own property coverage describes a bleak start, so the rally runs ahead of activity [25] [36] .
