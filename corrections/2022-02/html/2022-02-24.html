🇺🇸 United States  Bearish  Lift-off meets a geopolitical premium

Macro Stance: Risk assets enter Thursday carrying two shocks at once: a Federal Reserve that has all but promised a March lift-off [1] and a Russia–Ukraine escalation that has moved from troop build-up to recognition of the separatist 'republics' [2] and the first Western sanctions [3] . New / Most Important: Biden imposed new sanctions on Russia and described the troop deployment into the Donbas as the beginning of an invasion, Al Jazeera reported [9] ; DW then reported Moscow saying the separatists had asked Putin for military help [10] . That is the escalation path markets fear. At home, data are softening at the margin rather than breaking: the Conference Board said consumer confidence declined for a second consecutive month in February [11] , and mortgage rates hit their highest since May 2019 [12] . Brent at 96.84 USD/bbl and WTI at 92.10 USD/bbl (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2022-02-23) pass straight into the headline inflation the Fed is fighting, which is why this energy shock tightens rather than loosens the policy constraint.

Playbook ✓: Action: stay underweight long-duration growth—the Nasdaq 100 closed at 13,509.43 index points, -7.60% over five sessions (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2022-02-23)—and hold the 10Y as the portfolio hedge rather than cash. Trigger to add risk: the S&P 500 reclaiming its 20-session reference of 4,356.45 index points with the VIX back below its 20-session reference of 31.16 index points. What changes the call: a credible diplomatic track—CNN reported the White House saying Biden agreed to meet Putin 'in principle' as long as Russia does not invade [13] —or a Fed that signals a measured start and a slower balance-sheet runoff.

🇨🇳 China  Mixed  Easing bias, regulatory overhang

Macro Stance: China is the one major economy leaning toward easier money while the Fed prepares to tighten, and that divergence is the core of the call. Reuters reported slowing inflation leaving room for policy easing [24] , and Bloomberg described the central bank seizing the moment for stimulus as the Fed hikes [25] . Onshore equities are behaving accordingly: the Shanghai Composite rose 0.93% to 3,489.15 index points and is down only 1.94% over 20 sessions (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2022-02-23). Offshore is different: the Hang Seng closed at 23,660.28 index points, -4.28% over five sessions, after fresh fears of a regulatory crackdown hit internet platforms [26] . Stance: Mixed—supportive macro policy, hostile micro policy.

PMIs & Activity: Activity data point to a soft start. Reuters reported that January factory activity contracted as COVID lockdowns bit, according to the Caixin PMI [27] , even though industrial profits rebounded in 2021 as production improved [28] . January car wholesales were stable at +0.9%, with Volkswagen back in positive territory, according to Best Selling Cars Blog [29] . Investment-led pockets remain strong: Global Times reported China is expected to add a record 75-90GW of solar capacity this year [30] . The weak link is regional, with the South China Morning Post describing a rust-belt province facing economic decline that unveiled a population plan [31] . The next official and Caixin PMI prints are the test of whether easing is reaching manufacturers.

Credit Impulse: Credit is where the easing has to show up. Fibre2Fashion reported the central bank cutting rates on medium-term loans and reverse repos [32] , and MacroMicro tracks the loan prime rate swap curve that transmits those cuts [33] . BNP Paribas Asset Management asked what US–China monetary policy divergence means for investors [34] , and the answer so far is a steadier onshore market. The counterweight is supervisory: The Japan Times reported regulators telling banks and state firms to report their exposure to Jack Ma's Ant [35] , a reminder that lending to platform finance is still being audited rather than encouraged. A durable impulse needs developers and local governments borrowing again; until the next credit data show that, easing is a cushion rather than a catalyst.

Property: Property remains a drag on sentiment rather than an acute shock. The Standard reported Fantasia being sued over its bond default [36] , and the WSJ reported that Evergrande's chief executive sold bonds before a profit warning [37] , the kind of governance story that keeps foreign high-yield buyers away. Forbes noted that Hong Kong tycoon Joseph Lau's fortune edged up despite an Evergrande loss [38] . Steel-input demand is being managed from the top: Reuters reported Beijing urging some iron ore traders to reduce inventory [39] , and The Australian said China threatened an iron ore single market [40] . Both point to policymakers capping raw-material prices rather than reflating construction, which limits upside for the developer complex.

Fiscal, Policy & Trade: Trade friction is broadening rather than easing. Reuters reported the US adding e-commerce sites operated by Tencent and Alibaba to its 'notorious markets' list [41] , the WTO recorded an EU dispute complaint over Chinese intellectual-property enforcement [42] , and the EU imposed anti-dumping tariffs on Chinese steel fasteners [43] . The South China Morning Post explained why China's industrial subsidies are so controversial [44] . Russia is the new variable: Reuters reported Russia's trade flow shifting towards China as sanctions start [45] , and Moneycontrol examined what trade sanctions against Russia would mean for India and China [46] . Secondary-sanction risk is a tail that investors have only begun to price.

Markets & Flows: The offshore internet complex is where policy risk is concentrated. Reuters said fresh fears over a Beijing regulatory crackdown drove a China tech rout [26] , after the WSJ reported an order to cut food-delivery fees sparking a selloff [47] and Bloomberg said the policy wiped $26 billion off Meituan's value [48] . Tencent closed at 422.65 HKD, -6.55% over five sessions, and Alibaba's Hong Kong line at 112.40 HKD, -8.24%; the US-listed KWEB fell 11.06% and Pinduoduo 15.37% over the same span (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2022-02-23). Onshore exposure held up far better, with ASHR, the CSI 300 tracker, down only 0.27% over five sessions. Investors are separating macro China, which is easing, from platform China, which is still being regulated.

New / Most Important: The most important development is that enforcement keeps landing even as macro policy eases. The South China Morning Post reported a US$10 million fine on a live-streamer for tax evasion [49] , and the WSJ said tutoring companies took a big financial hit amid the crackdown [50] ; Asia Times argued Meituan is getting back on track [51] . The question for Alibaba's next quarterly report is whether management can show stabilisation: its US ADR fell 2.84% to 109.72 USD on 2022-02-23 and 13.09% over five sessions (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2022-02-23).

Playbook ✓: Action: favour onshore A-share exposure (ASHR at 36.46 USD) over offshore internet (KWEB at 33.63 USD), funding the switch by trimming Hong Kong platforms. Trigger: the Shanghai Composite reclaiming its 20-session reference of 3,558.18 index points would confirm that easing is outweighing external risk. What changes the call: regulators explicitly ending the fee and platform campaigns, or credit data showing easing reaching borrowers, would make the offshore discount investable; secondary sanctions tied to Russia trade would hit both legs. ⚠ Divergence: easing-friendly macro policy and a resilient onshore index sit against an offshore internet sector still being punished by regulators—the two Chinas are pricing different regimes, and one of them is wrong.

🇵🇱 Poland  Bearish  Frontline risk meets NBP tightening

Macro Stance: Poland is the EU economy where the Russia–Ukraine escalation, high inflation and rate hikes collide most directly. Stance: Bearish for Polish risk assets, because policy is tightening into an external shock on the country's own border.

Markets: Warsaw has priced fear faster than most of Europe. Bankier reported the WIG20 at its lowest since May 2021 amid fear in the equity market [73] ; the WIG20 total-return tracker fell 3.07% to 35.15 PLN on the day and 7.89% over five sessions, while the US-listed EPOL fell 10.64% over five sessions to 18.98 USD (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2022-02-23). Consumer names cracked too: LPP fell 10.23% and Allegro 6.52% on the day, the latter after StockWatch flagged new lows [74] . The currency has been more orderly. Business Insider Polska reported the euro trading above a closely watched level against the zloty on 22 February [75] , yet StockWatch described the zloty as keeping its cool [76] , and a Parkiet interview pitched bonds as an opportunity in the fight against inflation [77] .

Fiscal & Politics: Fiscal policy is pivoting toward defence and leaning on the central bank. Money.pl reported Fitch's rating decision citing high inflation and problems with the European Commission [83] . Polski Ład remains a liability: Biznes Wprost reported dismissals at the finance ministry over it [84] , Money.pl described it causing alarm in firms [85] , and Next Gazeta reported that a critic of the reform has become an adviser to the NBP president [86] . EUobserver reported Poland edging toward a rule-of-law deal with Brussels [87] .

Corporate & Sectors: Company news is constructive even as prices fall, which is what a de-rating looks like. Allegro is opening its platform in English to EU countries, Business Insider Polska reported [88] , and Wiadomości Spożywcze said its parcels can now be collected at Auchan stores [89] . Strefa Inwestorów reported CD Projekt shares opening higher after Elon Musk praised Cyberpunk 2077 again [90] , yet the stock closed 0.99% lower at 169.40 PLN (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2022-02-23). Orlen's Czech unit Unipetrol posted good results, Rynek Kolejowy reported [91] , but Orlen fell 2.15% to 68.20 PLN. PGE wants to move its headquarters from Warsaw to Lublin, StockWatch reported [92] , and the shares lost 3.63% to 7.21 PLN. Coking-coal producer JSW is up 16.64% over 20 sessions at 44.09 PLN even after the CBA flagged irregularities at JSW Koks on a 289 mln zł investment [93] . KGHM fell 4.16% to 151.00 PLN; separately, Rynek Prawniczy reported state counsel acting for Poland in a copper investment arbitration [94] .

Geopolitics & Security: Poland is becoming a frontline logistics and military hub. Reuters reported the US announcing Abrams tanks for Poland as the Russian threat mounts [95] , and The National Interest reported additional US F-15s sent to bolster NATO's eastern flank [96] . Notes From Poland reported a UK–Poland–Ukraine trilateral agreement 'in face of Russian aggression' [97] . At home, the prime minister raised the cyberspace threat alert level, Gazeta Prawna reported [98] , the foreign ministry did not rule out evacuating Polish diplomats from Ukraine, according to Rzeczpospolita [99] , and Jarosław Kaczyński said Poland must be safe and Ukraine must be saved [100] . Energy security is the economic transmission: Reuters reported Yamal-Europe gas flowing east [101] , and WysokieNapiecie examined what will flow through Baltic Pipe and Yamal a year from now [102] . InfoMigrants reported European countries preparing for refugees from Ukraine [103] , a fiscal and labour-market variable for Poland if the conflict widens.

New / Most Important: The risk premium is shifting from Russian rhetoric to Ukrainian economic stress. Money.pl warned that Ukraine faces economic collapse as Russia escalates and that the problems will not bypass Poland [104] , while Foreign Policy argued the crisis is turning Poland into a strategic player [105] . INNPoland pointed out that without Ukrainian workers Poland's high GDP growth would be hard to achieve [106] . A disrupted Ukraine is therefore a supply shock to the Polish labour market as well as a demand shock to exporters, and it lands with GUS inflation already at 9.2% [67] .

Playbook ✓: Action: stay underweight Polish banks and discretionary retailers—Pekao, mBank, LPP and Allegro—and hold Polish exposure through liquid, hedged vehicles; prefer short-duration local bonds over equities while the NBP is still hiking. Trigger: the WIG20 tracker reclaiming its 20-session reference of 38.49 PLN would signal the geopolitical risk premium is fading. What changes the call: de-escalation with Ukraine's economy stabilising would restore the rate-sensitive bank earnings story investors were paying for a month ago, and a defence-led fiscal expansion would then support domestic industrials. ⚠ Divergence: the zloty is described as keeping its cool while the WIG20 sits at its lowest since May 2021—either the currency is underpricing the risk or equities are overshooting.
