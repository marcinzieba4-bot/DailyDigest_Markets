🇺🇸 United States  Bearish  March lift-off; hike-size debate

Macro Stance: The US reopens after Presidents' Day with a stale tape and a new shock. The S&P 500 closed 18 February at 4,348.87 index points, -0.72% on the day and -1.58% over five sessions, the Nasdaq 100 fell -1.14% to 14,009.54 index points and the VIX ended at 27.75 index points (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2022-02-18). Those prints predate Monday, when Reuters reported Vladimir Putin saying Russia must recognise Ukraine's breakaway regions [1] and the Atlantic Council analysed Moscow's order of troops into Ukraine [2] . Joe Biden had already said he was convinced Putin had decided to invade, the BBC reported [3] . The stance is Bearish: cash equities have not yet traded the recognition, and a Fed still debating the size of its first hike offers no policy cushion.

Rates & Curve: Treasury pricing is the binding constraint. The 2Y closed at 1.47% and the 10Y at 1.92%, leaving 10Y-2Y at +45 bps (US Treasury, prior close 2022-02-18). Over 20 observations the 2Y rose +46 bps from 1.01% against +17 bps for the 10Y, a -29 bps compression: bear flattening on a curve that remains positively sloped. Over the last five observations the 2Y is -3 bps and the 10Y +0 bps, the first sign that haven demand is offsetting hike repricing. Reuters described a curve pricing Fed tightening and showing fears of policy error [4] , and its poll still has 25 bps in March while calls for 50 bps grow louder [5] . A Fed governor opened the door to a half-point March hike, KALB reported [6] , while the New York Fed president saw no need for a big one, per China Daily [7] . The DXY at 96.04 index points (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2022-02-18) shows a firm but orderly dollar.

New / Most Important: The decisive change is that the Ukraine risk moved from troop counts to legal facts. Russian lawmakers' request for recognition had given Putin another card to play, CBS News said [8] . Washington's diplomacy was explicitly conditional: CNN reported the White House said Biden agreed to meet Putin in principle as long as Russia does not invade [9] . At home, Reuters said existing home sales accelerated as investors elbowed out first-time buyers [10] , and mortgage rates of 3.92% are the highest in 33 months, the Orange County Register noted [11] . With US inflation at a 40-year high, as Global Data put it [12] , a growth scare does not automatically buy a dovish pivot.

Playbook ✓: Action: run US equity exposure below benchmark into the first post-holiday session, funding overweights in energy (XLE) and staples (XLP) from long-duration growth (QQQ, ARKK). Trigger: an S&P 500 close below 4,348.87 index points with the VIX holding above 27.75 index points confirms the recognition shock is being priced rather than faded. What changes the call: sanctions that spare energy flows plus clear Fed pushback against a half-point opener would let the 2Y retrace part of its +46 bps rise and reopen the case for quality technology.

🇨🇳 China  Mixed  PBOC easing; platform-fee crackdown

Macro Stance: China is the one major economy easing into the shock, but policy is pulling in two directions. The central bank cut key lending rates in January, including one for the first time in nearly two years, CNBC reported [21] , and Bloomberg framed it as the PBOC seizing the moment for stimulus as the Fed hikes [22] . Yet regulators also ordered cuts to food-delivery fees, sparking a tech selloff, according to the WSJ [23] . The Shanghai Composite was flat (-0.00%) at 3,490.61 index points on 21 February but +1.80% over five sessions, while the Hang Seng fell -0.65% to 24,170.07 index points (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2022-02-21). Mixed: onshore support versus offshore regulatory risk.

PMIs & Activity: Activity entered February soft but stabilising. Reuters reported January factory activity contracting as COVID lockdowns bit, per the Caixin PMI [24] , while China Daily said industrial profits rebounded in 2021 as production improved [25] . Demand-side price pressure is cooling: Reuters said inflation slowed, leaving room for policy easing [26] . Reuters columnist Andy Home noted that metals imports boomed in 2021 and so did exports [27] , a reminder that external demand carried the cycle. Pricing power survives at the premium end, with Starbucks and Tim Hortons raising prices in China, Reuters said [28] , but Schindler warned on profit and China after a fourth-quarter earnings fall [29] . Hong Kong adds a drag: the BBC reported its hospitals overwhelmed amid a spike in cases [30] . The next official PMI print is the test of whether easing is reaching factories.

Credit Impulse: The credit signal is turning more supportive, but transmission is the open question. Al Jazeera reported the PBOC chief vowing support amid 'weak links' [31] , and Fibre2Fashion detailed cuts to medium-term loan and reverse-repo rates [32] , which lower bank funding costs. Cheaper money only becomes an impulse if developers, local governments and households want to borrow, and that demand has been the missing link. BNP Paribas Asset Management highlighted the US-China monetary policy divergence [33] : the more the Fed tightens while Beijing eases, the more the yuan, which Reuters said slipped under that divergence [34] , becomes the pressure valve and limits how far rates can fall. The read is an impulse improving at the margin, not yet a broad credit upswing.

Property: Property is the weak link the PBOC chief alluded to, and Beijing's chosen channel is targeted. Khmer Times reported that China excluded affordable rental housing from property credit controls [35] , a carve-out that supports social-housing construction without validating speculative demand. The January rate cuts [21] help mortgage borrowers at the margin, but a sales recovery requires confidence that pre-sold homes will be delivered, which rate cuts alone do not provide. Steel and iron ore demand therefore stays policy-managed: Reuters reported China urging some iron ore traders to reduce inventory [36] , a sign that officials want to prevent restocking from running ahead of construction. The signal to watch is a broadening of credit exemptions beyond rental housing; until then property is a drag, not a driver.

Fiscal, Policy & Trade: Trade friction is rising on several fronts. The US added e-commerce sites run by Tencent and Alibaba to its 'notorious markets' list, Reuters reported [37] , and the BBC said Washington accused China of 'serious harm' to workers through trade [38] ; the Peterson Institute found China bought none of the extra $200 billion of US exports promised in the Trump-era deal [39] . The EU imposed anti-dumping tariffs on Chinese steel fasteners [40] . Geopolitics cuts both ways: Beijing sanctioned Lockheed and Raytheon again over Taiwan arms sales [41] , and China said Western sanctions on Russia would worsen the Ukraine crisis, per the South China Morning Post [42] . Domestically, the fee order is regulation as redistribution, from platforms to merchants, and Bloomberg said it wiped $26 billion off Meituan's value [43] .

Markets & Flows: Offshore platforms bore the brunt. Tencent fell -5.23% to 423.03 HKD and Alibaba -3.85% to 114.90 HKD on 21 February (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2022-02-21), after the Alibaba ADR lost -4.37% and KWEB -5.04% on 18 February (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2022-02-18). The onshore tape was steadier, with ASHR +0.77% to 36.82 USD on 18 February and +2.22% over five sessions. Sell-side calls had turned constructive: CNBC reported banks from Credit Suisse to Goldman Sachs saying it is time to buy Chinese stocks [44] , and Business Insider noted Tencent joining the world's ten most valuable companies as Meta slid [45] . Index changes keep hardware in focus, with Lenovo selected as a Hang Seng Index constituent [46] .

New / Most Important: The new information is regulatory rather than macro: the food-delivery fee order [23] revived the template of policy-driven margin transfers just as foreign banks turned positive. Russia's move on the Donbas adds a second layer. The Atlantic Council described Russia and China as partners in dedollarization [47] , so if Western measures extend to dollar clearing, secondary-sanction risk for Chinese banks and commodity traders becomes a tail to price. Hong Kong's outbreak is now a political priority, with Xi urging the city to control surging cases, New Hampshire Public Radio reported [48] , which raises the odds of tighter restrictions on mainland-Hong Kong traffic.

Playbook ✓: Action: prefer onshore A-shares (ASHR, Shanghai Composite) over offshore platforms (KWEB, Tencent) while the fee order is digested. Trigger: add offshore only if Tencent stabilises above 423.03 HKD while the Shanghai Composite holds its +1.80% five-session gain. What changes the call: a broad property-credit loosening would make the trade more pro-cyclical, while a second platform pricing order or Russia-linked secondary sanctions would hit both legs. ⚠ Divergence: banks call Chinese stocks a buy [44] and the onshore index is rising, yet Tencent fell -5.23% and KWEB -5.04% in consecutive sessions; easing and regulatory tightening are being priced at the same time.

New / Most Important: The decisive new fact is Moscow's move on the Donbas: Reuters reported Putin saying Russia must recognise the breakaway regions [1] , and the Kremlin published the president's address [94] . For Poland that turns a diplomatic crisis into a potential refugee, sanctions and energy-flow shock.
