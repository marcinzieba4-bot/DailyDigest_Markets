🌍 Geographic Macro Overview

🇺🇸 United States  Mixed  March lift-off priced, CPI is the swing

Macro Stance: Mixed. The United States goes into the January consumer-price report with a relief rally already banked and the front end still marching toward a March lift-off. The S&P 500 rose 1.45% to 4,587.18 index points and the Nasdaq 100 gained 2.10% to 15,056.96 index points, yet both remain down 2.67% and 4.97% over 20 sessions (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2022-02-09). Growth gives the Fed no reason to wait: payrolls rose 467,000 in January despite Omicron, Al Jazeera reported [1] , and Reuters described a labour market that shrugged off the surge with the economy strong ahead of rate hikes [2] . December inflation ran at 7%, the highest since 1982, according to CNBC [3] . With demand that firm, a hot print confirms the tightening path rather than shocking it; the open question is the size and speed of the first moves.

Rates & Curve: The curve remains positively sloped but is flattening quickly. The 2-year Treasury closed at 1.36% and the 10-year at 1.94%, leaving 10Y-2Y at +58 bps and 10Y-3M at +168 bps (US Treasury, prior close 2022-02-09). Over 20 observations the 2-year rose 46 bps from 0.90% while the 10-year rose 19 bps from 1.75%, a bear flattening that took 27 bps off the spread; in the last five observations alone the 2-year added 20 bps and the 10-year 16 bps. NBC Connecticut reported the 10-year hitting 1.96% as inflation data came into focus [4] , and tipswatch.com noted the 30-year real yield rising above zero [5] , the discount-rate mechanism behind the de-rating of long-duration equity. Risk appetite improved without a dollar squeeze: the VIX fell 1.48 points to 19.96 index points and DXY slipped 0.16% to 95.49 index points (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2022-02-09).

New / Most Important: The January CPI print is the next and decisive input. Reuters framed December's report as inflation close to peaking [6] , so a re-acceleration would overturn the comfort that a peak is near. The Fed already pointed to a March hike, CNBC reported in January [7] , and Reuters argued the Fed is unlikely to start with a bang [8] ; a strong core reading would challenge exactly that gradualist assumption and push more tightening into the 2-year. The Financial Times captured the mood with a piece on markets losing trust in central banks [9] . Strong demand is showing up in trade as well: robust imports drove the 2021 trade deficit to a record, Reuters reported [10] .

Playbook ✓: Action: run a barbell into the print, overweight cash-generative, rate-tolerant sectors (energy, money-centre banks) against an underweight in long-duration growth, and express rates views through curve flatteners rather than outright duration. Trigger to press: a hot core reading that lifts the 2-year above its 1.36% close while the S&P 500 fails to hold 4,587.18 index points. What changes the call: a soft print that re-steepens 10Y-2Y from +58 bps and pulls the VIX back below its 20-session reference of 18.41 index points would argue for rebuilding quality growth, starting with the semiconductor leaders.

Playbook ✓: Action: hold a tactical overweight in euro-area cyclicals, chiefly autos and banks, funded from rate-sensitive defensives. Trigger: TTF holding below its 20-session reference of 78.74 EUR/MWh while the STOXX 600 recovers toward 486.20 index points. What changes the call: an escalation around the Belarus drills that sends TTF back above 78.74 EUR/MWh, at which point the exposure rotates into energy and defence.

🇨🇳 China  Bullish  Easing bias, property drag

Macro Stance: Bullish, tactically and offshore. China is easing while the Fed tightens, a divergence Bloomberg framed as the central bank seizing the moment for stimulus [22] . The starting point is decent rather than distressed: GDP grew 8.1% in 2021, the fastest in a decade, the Global Times reported [23] , but the BBC reported rate cuts as growth slowed [24] , and policy is now explicitly counter-cyclical. Offshore equities are pricing the pivot first. The Hang Seng rose 2.06% to 24,829.99 index points and is up 5.69% over 20 sessions, while the onshore Shanghai Composite added 0.79% to 3,479.95 index points but is still down 3.21% over the same span (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2022-02-09).

PMIs & Activity: Activity argues for more support, not less. Reuters reported that January factory activity contracted on the Caixin PMI as COVID lockdowns bit [25] , a reminder that zero-COVID disruption hits private manufacturers first. Fixed-asset investment rose 4.9% in 2021, Macau Business reported [26] , and industrial profits rebounded as production improved, according to China Daily [27] , likely as much a commodity-price story as a volume one. Consumer inflation is easing as energy and food cool, the Khmer Times reported [28] , which leaves room to ease. Discretionary demand looked soft over the holiday: the box office plunged 23% as theatres pushed prices to records, CNBC reported [29] .

Credit Impulse: The credit impulse is turning through price rather than quantity so far. The People's Bank cut key lending rates, including one for the first time in nearly 2 years, CNBC reported [30] , and trimmed medium-term lending and reverse-repo rates (Fibre2Fashion [31] ). MacroMicro's tracking of LPR swaps [32] is the cleanest market gauge of how much further easing is priced. The constraint is leverage: BNP Paribas Asset Management argues China's debt vulnerability has so far defied dire predictions [33] , which supports a measured cycle rather than a flood. Regulators are still disciplining fintech lending, too: Ant-backed MYBank was fined over credit-scoring rules, Reuters reported [34] . The confirmation to watch is loan growth broadening to private borrowers.

Property: Property remains the brake. Evergrande aims to deliver 600,000 apartments in 2022 and says there will be no fire sales, Reuters reported [35] , a plan the South China Morning Post described as an attempt to sell its way out of debt [36] . Delivery targets protect households and suppliers, but they do not restore developer cash flow or local-government land revenue. Nikkei Asia's report on concrete ghost towns making the bubble visible [37] underlines the overhang. Pricing was less bad at year-end: new home prices fell less in December on promotions and easing curbs, Business Recorder reported [38] . In Hong Kong, lived-in home valuations are dropping in a fifth COVID wave, the South China Morning Post said [39] .

Fiscal, Policy & Trade: Trade policy cuts both ways. China missed the purchase targets it promised under the Trump-era deal, CNN reported [40] , and the Peterson Institute found China bought none of the extra $200 billion of US exports [41] , ammunition for Washington hawks rather than for tariff relief. Total trade still topped $6 trillion for the first time in 2021, Pandaily reported [42] . The pivot toward Moscow is now commercial: Russia and China agreed a 30-year gas deal via a new pipeline settled in euros, Reuters reported [43] , and Rosneft agreed a 100 mln tonne oil supply deal with CNPC [44] . Beijing also plans to raise domestic iron ore output and steel-scrap use, Reuters reported [45] , capping imported-ore pricing power.

Markets & Flows: Price action favours offshore internet over onshore A-shares. Tencent rose 2.72% to 458.55 HKD and is up 12.29% over 20 sessions, while Alibaba's Hong Kong line jumped 6.83% to 118.80 HKD (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2022-02-09). FOREX.com attributed the Alibaba rally to a SoftBank announcement [46] , days after Verdict reported SoftBank looking to sell Alibaba shares as the Arm deal bogged down [47] , so stake-supply risk remains. XPeng's inclusion in the Shenzhen-Hong Kong Stock Connect [48] opens a southbound channel into EV names. Regulatory risk persists: the Hang Seng slipped as WuXi Biologics sank by a record after a US red-flag list, the South China Morning Post reported [49] . KWEB gained 3.86% to 38.46 USD, while ASHR is still down 2.71% over 20 sessions.

New / Most Important: Two stories matter most. Evergrande's delivery-first plan shifts the sector from default headlines to execution risk, and markets will reward evidence that pre-sold homes are handed over [35] . Beijing's alignment with Moscow hardened, with China joining Russia in opposing NATO expansion, the BBC reported [50] , which raises secondary-sanctions risk for Chinese banks and commodity buyers if the West moves on Russia. Common prosperity still shapes tech: JD.com's founder donated US$2 billion to charity amid the crackdown, the South China Morning Post reported [51] . EVs remain the growth exception, with China taking 53% of global passenger NEV sales in 2021, CnEVPost reported [52] .

Playbook ✓: Action: overweight Hong Kong-listed internet and EV names (Tencent, Alibaba, XPeng, NIO, Li Auto) against neutral onshore A-shares; Barron's quoted one analyst calling XPeng, NIO and Li Auto a rare opportunity [53] . Trigger to add: the Hang Seng holding above its 20-session reference of 23,493.38 index points while the Shanghai Composite reclaims 3,595.18 index points. What changes the call: a new US red-flag or sanctions action against Chinese firms, or Evergrande slipping on deliveries. ⚠ Divergence: offshore internet is rallying, Tencent up 12.29% over 20 sessions, while the Shanghai Composite is down 3.21% and the Caixin PMI shows contraction [25] ; equity relief is running ahead of the credit and housing data that would confirm it.

🇯🇵 Japan  Bearish  BoJ anchor, chip-capped exporters

Macro Stance: Bearish. Japan is exposed to the global de-rating of growth and chip-equipment names without a domestic policy offset. The Nikkei 225 rose 1.08% to 27,579.87 index points but is down 4.12% over 20 sessions, with Tokyo Electron down 13.78%, Advantest 13.20% and Sony Group 13.36% over the same span (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2022-02-09). Earnings confirm that supply, not demand, binds: Toyota's profits fell as the chip shortage hit production, the BBC reported [54] , and Nissan raised its profit outlook but sees chips limiting growth, according to Reuters [55] . Sony slashed its PS5 sales forecast on the same constraint (Fox Business [56] ). SoftBank Group jumped 5.85% to 5,612.00 JPY in the session after it dropped the Arm sale in favour of an IPO, Reuters reported [57] .

Activity & Currency: Domestic demand lacks the wage engine behind Western inflation: the Japan Times asked why Japan's wages aren't rising [58] , even as Reuters reported a Cup Noodle price hike testing student budgets [59] . Policy is pinned. The Asahi reported the BoJ applying a minus interest rate to MUFG Bank deposits [60] , and Reuters reported in January that the BoJ was debating how to message an eventual hike [61] , debate rather than action. With the US 2-year up 46 bps over 20 observations (US Treasury, prior close 2022-02-09) and the BoJ anchored, the widening rate gap is the classic channel for yen weakness, which flatters exporters' translated profits but raises import bills. The US and Japan agreed to cut Trump-era steel tariffs, Reuters reported [62] .

Playbook ✓: Action: underweight Japanese chip-equipment and consumer-electronics growth (Tokyo Electron, Advantest, Sony) against a neutral weight in autos, where Toyota's 2,317.50 JPY close leaves it only 1.78% below its 20-session reference (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2022-02-09). Trigger to add: a hot US inflation print that lifts the 2-year further. What changes the call: the Nikkei 225 reclaiming its 20-session reference of 28,765.66 index points alongside evidence that chip supply is easing for automakers.

🇵🇱 Poland  Mixed  Hawkish NBP, frontline risk

Macro Stance: Mixed. Poland pairs an increasingly hawkish central bank with a frontline position in the Russia standoff. The Monetary Policy Council raised rates again, to their highest in nearly 9 years, Rzeczpospolita reported [72] , against inflation that rose to 8.6% at the end of 2021, according to 300Gospodarka [73] . The labour market is tight: registered unemployment was 5.6% in January, the family ministry said via RadioMaryja.pl [74] , and average pay rose 9.6% in 2021, pb.pl reported [75] . Yet Rzeczpospolita warned of a slowdown masked by statistics as inflation and rates curb appetites [76] . Equities price the risk rather than the growth: the WIG20 total-return tracker is down 7.12% over 20 sessions (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2022-02-09).

Markets: Glapiński's press conference was the market event. Interia described an unusual conference in which the NBP governor cast himself as a hawk [77] , and PolsatNews called it colourful, with talk of hawks, doves and asparagus [78] . Bankier.pl reported that Glapiński strengthened the złoty against the euro [79] ; a firmer currency is the transmission channel the NBP wants, because it imports disinflation. Equities were mixed on the session: PKO Bank Polski was flat at 49.00 PLN (+0.06%), mBank fell 2.22% to 484.00 PLN, Allegro rose 4.46% to 35.16 PLN and CD Projekt 2.88% to 183.60 PLN (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2022-02-09). In dollar terms EPOL rose 1.31% to 21.62 USD but is down 3.83% over 20 sessions. Parkiet's verdict that bulls are having their five minutes again [80] reads as a bounce, not a trend change.

Fiscal & Politics: Fiscal politics is noisy rather than restrictive. Miesięcznik Finansowy BANK reported experts calling the Polski Ład tax reform unfixable and urging an entirely new law [81] , Polityka said finance minister Tadeusz Kościński had been made an example of over it [82] , and Wyborcza.biz reported that the next finance minister will be a woman [83] . Business Insider Polska ran a poll on how many people understand Polski Ład at all [84] . Inflation is flattering the state's debt arithmetic, Rzeczpospolita noted [85] , and pension indexation will be higher than forecast [86] , supportive for consumption but costly for the budget. The EU front is worse: Brussels will withhold funds from Poland over a coal mine dispute, DW reported [87] , with The Guardian specifying an unpaid fine [88] , even as Politico Europe described Warsaw taking a half step back to cool legal conflicts [89] .

Corporate & Sectors: Corporate news centres on energy consolidation and cost pressure. Orlen's chief executive said jobs at Lotos will be maintained, Bankier.pl reported [90] , and Orlen called the merger financially beneficial (RadioMaryja.pl [91] ), while MOL's deputy chief executive told Rzeczpospolita the Hungarian group will become one of the most important players [92] . Orlen rose 2.32% to 73.26 PLN (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2022-02-09). INNPoland asked whether the era of 5.20 zł petrol is over after Orlen raised its wholesale price [93] . KGHM registered its copper cathodes on China's INE exchange, Inwestycje.pl reported [94] ; the stock rose 1.78% to 145.65 PLN. LPP expects a good year but says cost pressure will hit margins, Bankier.pl reported [95] , and its shares are down 14.76% over 20 sessions to 16,000.00 PLN. Tauron bought CO2 allowances at 30 euro and locked in a fixed interest rate, WNP.PL reported [96] ; Tauron rose 5.52% to 2.54 PLN, and coking-coal miner JSW is up 19.75% over 20 sessions to 48.80 PLN. PGE says it is pressing ahead with offshore wind procurement, GospodarkaMorska reported [97] , and PortalMorski quoted Domański saying up to 18 GW of Baltic capacity is possible by 2040 [98] ; PGE rose 3.42% to 8.10 PLN.

Geopolitics & Security: Poland is NATO's logistics hub for the crisis. The first US reinforcement troops arrived, with more expected, Reuters reported [99] , after a preparatory group arrived ahead of a deployment of 1,700 troops, Notes From Poland reported [100] . Britain will send 350 more troops to the Polish border, Politico Europe reported [101] , and Reuters reported US troops in Poland preparing for Americans potentially fleeing Ukraine [102] , contingency planning that investors are likely to read as a higher conflict probability. Voice of America described the Baltic states and Poland as increasingly alarmed by a revanchist Russia [103] , and Bankier.pl cited a survey in which Europeans fear Russia will attack Ukraine this year [104] . The UK Defence Secretary and his Polish counterpart reaffirmed their commitment to European security (GOV.UK [105] ), and Interia ran satellite images under the headline that Warsaw is within missile range [106] . The premium is visible in prices: the WIG20 tracker's 7.12% 20-session loss compares with 2.65% for the STOXX 600 (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2022-02-09).

New / Most Important: The NBP's message is that tightening continues and reserves will be hardened. Business Insider Polska reported that Glapiński intends to buy another 100 tonnes of gold [107] , and PolsatNews quoted him saying he will urge further monetary tightening [108] , raising the prospect of higher mortgage instalments. Pekao had forecast a 50 bp February move, StockWatch.pl reported [109] , and Morgan Stanley expects dynamic rate rises in Poland, according to Strefa Inwestorów [110] . The tension is that Biznes Wprost already sees first signs of inflation easing [111] , while Credit Agricole warned the food VAT cut will ultimately add to inflation [112] ; a hawkish NBP into a mechanically lower print risks over-tightening a slowing economy. Money.pl noted Czech rates are twice Poland's [113] , a gap that frames how much room Polish rates could still have.

Playbook ✓: Action: overweight Polish banks with a defined geopolitical hedge, and underweight domestic consumer names exposed to cost and rate pressure. Bank Pekao is up 1.43% over 20 sessions to 137.90 PLN while Dino Polska is down 15.09% to 299.40 PLN (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2022-02-09). Trigger: confirmation of further hikes, a firmer złoty and the WIG20 tracker reclaiming its 20-session reference of 41.44 PLN. What changes the call: an escalation around Belarus or Ukraine, which would hit Polish assets first regardless of rate support. ⚠ Divergence: the NBP is tightening into what Rzeczpospolita calls a statistically masked slowdown [76] while the złoty strengthens; rate support for banks coexists with rising credit and consumer risk and heavy geopolitical exposure.
