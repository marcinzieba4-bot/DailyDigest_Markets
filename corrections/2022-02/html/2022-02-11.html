🌍 Geographic Macro Overview

🇺🇸 United States  Bearish  Hot CPI forces front-end repricing

Macro Stance: January's consumer price report strengthened the case that the March lift-off the Fed signalled on January 26 (CNBC) [1] will be the first of several moves. Prices rose 7.5% from a year earlier, more than expected and the fastest pace since 1982, according to CNBC [2] , and Bloomberg described a larger-than-forecast gain [3] . Reuters' wording matters most: inflation is becoming widespread [4] , a broad-based pressure that policy has to lean against rather than look through. The labour market removes any excuse to wait after January payrolls rose by 467,000 (Al Jazeera) [5] . Our stance is Bearish for duration-sensitive US risk: the economy is strong, but the price of money is rising faster than earnings estimates can adjust.

Rates & Curve: The 2Y Treasury closed at 1.61% and the 10Y at 2.03%, a 10Y–2Y spread of +42 bps, with 10Y–3M at +163 bps (US Treasury, prior close 2022-02-10). Over twenty observations the 2Y rose +69 bps from 0.92% while the 10Y rose +29 bps from 1.74%, so the spread moved -40 bps: a bear flattening (both up, 2Y up more) on a curve that is still positively sloped. The front end did the work, adding +42 bps to the 2Y in five observations against +21 bps on the 10Y. CNBC reported the 10Y topping 2% for the first time since 2019 [6] . Reuters' explainer on the flattening curve is the right frame [7] , because when the short end leads, financial conditions tighten through mortgage and corporate borrowing costs before the curve says anything about recession.

New / Most Important: Equities absorbed the shock unevenly. The S&P 500 fell -1.81% to 4,504.08 index points and the Nasdaq 100 -2.33% to 14,705.64, while the VIX jumped +19.79%, or +3.95 points, to 23.91 (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2022-02-10). The US Dollar Index barely moved, +0.06% at 95.55 index points (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2022-02-10), so markets read the print as a Fed-path shock rather than a dollar-funding one. Reuters reported that hot inflation fuels the case for a 'big-bang' Fed hike in March [8] , but the Fed's Barkin said he would have to be convinced of the need for a half-point move [9] . Mortgage rates surged to a two-year high, with the 30-year at 3.69% (Orange County Register) [10] , which pulls the rate shock straight into housing demand.

Playbook ✓: Action: run US equities underweight duration: trim long-dated growth, real estate and utilities, and fund cash-generative financials, energy and short-dated Treasuries. Trigger: the 2Y holding above 1.61% with the 10Y above 2% confirms the faster hiking path, and another leg of bear flattening extends the underweight. What changes the call: softer inflation data (Fortune argues inflation likely peaked months ago [11] ) or more officials echoing Barkin's resistance to a half-point start would let the front end rally and reopen the growth trade. A VIX retreat toward its 17.62 reading of twenty sessions ago (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2022-02-10) would be the confirming signal.

🇪🇺 European Union  Mixed  ECB pushback versus energy-led inflation

Macro Stance: Europe sits between two ECB messages. After the February 3 meeting Lagarde was no longer ruling out a rate hike this year (Al Jazeera) [12] , yet days later she said there was no need for big tightening because inflation would hold at target (Yahoo Finance UK) [13] . Reuters' conclusion that the ECB 'put' for euro-zone bond markets is some way off [14] is the operative one: the bank will not lean early against wider spreads. Our stance is Mixed, supportive for banks and value, hostile for duration and periphery bonds.

Activity & Inflation: Price pressure is still energy-led. Eurostat figures published by the European Commission show energy prices rising across the euro area in 2021 [15] , Dutch inflation hit its highest in 40 years (IamExpat) [16] , and Bankier noted that European inflation surprised economists again [17] . Dutch TTF gas closed at 74.35 EUR/MWh, -7.32% over five sessions (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2022-02-10), a relief for industry that does not undo the household shock already locked in: EDF Energy will lift UK bills by an average £693 a year after the price-cap rise (Money Saving Expert) [18] .

New / Most Important: Local indexes were steady: the STOXX 600 slipped -0.21% to 472.35 index points and the DAX added +0.05% to 15,490.44 (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2022-02-10). European cash markets close before the US session ends, so the USD-listed VGK, -1.28% (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2022-02-10), captures more of the CPI shock than the local benchmarks do. Geopolitics is the overlay: Chancellor Scholz told Russia not to underestimate 'our resolve' (DW) [19] , while Ukraine insists Nord Stream 2 should never open (Sky News) [20] .

Playbook ✓: Action: prefer European banks and energy over long-duration growth and core bonds. Trigger: continued outperformance of the Euro Stoxx 50, -2.75% over twenty sessions, against the Nasdaq 100 at -7.54% (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2022-02-10), alongside an ECB that tolerates higher yields. What changes the call: a signal of ECB spread protection, or a Russian escalation that pushes TTF back above its 75.15 EUR/MWh level of twenty sessions ago, squeezing margins faster than higher rates lift bank earnings.

🇨🇳 China  Mixed  Easing bias, property triage

Macro Stance: China is the one major economy actively easing while the Fed prepares to tighten, and that divergence is the investable fact. The PBOC cut key lending rates, including one for the first time in nearly 2 years (CNBC) [21] , and Bloomberg framed the move as the central bank seizing the moment for stimulus while the Fed hikes [22] . The growth base is respectable but losing speed: GDP grew 8.1% in 2021, the fastest in a decade (Global Times) [23] , yet fixed-asset investment rose only 4.9% (Macau Business) [24] . We hold a Mixed stance because easing is real while its transmission runs through a property sector still in triage.

PMIs & Activity: The Caixin manufacturing PMI showed January factory activity contracting as COVID lockdowns bit (Reuters) [25] . Mass-market consumer signals point the same way, although premium spending is holding up: Coty reported strong results boosted by China (Asia Financial) [26] and Swire Properties sees luxury mall revenue growing across China (The Business of Fashion) [27] . CNBC reported the holiday box office plunging by 23% as theatres pushed ticket prices to records [28] , an early sign that household demand is price-sensitive even in a holiday week, and Bloomberg's explainer on why some Chinese are 'lying flat' [29] adds a social dimension to that restraint. Industrial profits rebounded in 2021 (China Daily) [30] , so margins entered the slowdown from strength, which buys policymakers time rather than forcing urgency.

Credit Impulse: The policy signal is easing, but credit transmission is selective. Beyond the benchmark-rate cuts, the PBOC cut rates on medium-term loans and reverse repos (Fibre2Fashion) [31] , while regulators fined Ant-backed MYBank for violating credit-scoring rules (Reuters) [32] and the central bank tightened rules on cash withdrawals and deposits to target money laundering (Global Times) [33] . The combination is deliberate: cheaper money for approved channels, tighter supervision for platform and shadow credit. BNP Paribas Asset Management argues China's debt vulnerability keeps defying dire predictions [34] , which is the bull case for the impulse; the bear case is that developers and households, not banks, are the bottleneck, and lower rates do little when borrowers will not borrow.

Property: Property remains the blockage. Reuters, citing a report, said China is giving property firms easier access to escrow funds [35] , which, if confirmed, would be the most practical step so far because it would release developers' own presale cash instead of adding leverage. Evergrande aims to deliver 600,000 apartments in 2022 and says there will be no fire sales (Reuters) [36] , and it vowed to finish half of the units under construction this year (Caixin Global) [37] . Governance questions undercut the plan: WSJ and the South China Morning Post reported that Evergrande's CEO sold bonds before the profit warning and default [38] [39] . Nikkei Asia's 'ghost towns' report [40] is a reminder that unsold inventory, not just funding, caps new starts, even though December new-home prices fell less on promotions and easing curbs (Business Recorder) [41] .

Fiscal, Policy & Trade: Trade policy is opening a second front. China suspended Lithuanian beef, dairy and beer imports as the Taiwan row grew (Reuters) [42] , and Taiwan, Canada and Japan asked to join the EU's WTO case against China (Politico Europe) [43] . With Washington the scorecard is poor: CNN reported China missed the trade targets it promised to Trump [44] , and the Peterson Institute found China bought none of the extra $200 billion of US exports in the deal [45] . Volumes are still large, with 2021 imports and exports topping $6 trillion for the first time (Pandaily) [46] , and Beijing plans to raise iron ore output and use more steel scrap (Reuters) [47] . Politically, China joined Russia in opposing NATO expansion (BBC) [48] , raising the risk that Western sanctions on Russia spill into secondary-trade pressure on Chinese exporters.

Markets & Flows: Hong Kong is pricing the easing faster than the mainland. The Hang Seng closed at 24,924.35 index points, +4.96% over twenty sessions, while the Shanghai Composite at 3,485.91 is -2.79% over the same window (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2022-02-10). Tencent is +10.12% over twenty sessions at 460.07 HKD, and Bloomberg reported Didi shares climbing as Tencent boosted its stake [49] . US-listed proxies traded the CPI shock instead: FXI fell -1.21% and KWEB -1.64%, and Alibaba rose +2.86% in Hong Kong while its ADR fell -1.99% (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2022-02-10). Genuine flow channels are widening, with XPeng's shares included in the Shenzhen-Hong Kong Stock Connect (XPeng) [50] , while US regulatory risk persists after the Hang Seng slipped as WuXi Biologics sank by a record following its addition to a US red-flag list (South China Morning Post) [51] .

New / Most Important: The new element, per the Reuters report, is that easing may be reaching developers' cash flow rather than only benchmark rates. Combined with the PBOC's stimulus stance, that improves the odds of completed-project deliveries, which is what restores buyer confidence. The counterweight is external: ASML flagged possible IP infringement by a 'little giant' in China (South China Morning Post) [52] , a reminder that technology friction can tighten while domestic policy loosens, and XPeng's first European retail store (CNBC) [53] shows the export ambition that trade disputes can derail.

Playbook ✓: Action: own Hong Kong-listed platform exposure selectively, Tencent over the US-listed KWEB basket, and avoid private-developer credit. Trigger: further property-funding steps that build on the escrow change, with the Hang Seng holding its +4.96% twenty-session gain and the Shanghai Composite ending its underperformance. What changes the call: another major developer default, or a Lithuania-style trade dispute that pulls the EU into retaliation. ⚠ Divergence: policy is easing and Hong Kong is rallying, yet the Caixin PMI showed contraction [25] and the holiday box office fell 23% [28] , so equity markets are front-running a demand recovery that activity data have not confirmed.

Activity & Currency: December exports and imports hit records by value as supply bottlenecks eased (Reuters) [59] , but record imports on surging energy prices (The Seattle Times) [60] mean the terms-of-trade loss runs through the currency: with the US 10Y at 2.03% (US Treasury, prior close 2022-02-10) and the BoJ still applying negative rates, the yen loses rate support without any BoJ move. Playbook ✓: Action: favour semiconductor-equipment exporters over domestic automakers. Trigger: the 10Y Treasury holding above 2% while Advantest and Tokyo Electron keep their February 10 gains. What changes the call: the USD-listed EWJ fell -1.59% after the CPI shock (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2022-02-10), so the next Tokyo session has to absorb it; if the equipment makers then lag Toyota, the rate shock is overwhelming the chip cycle. 🇵🇱 Poland  Mixed  Hawkish NBP, strong zloty, frontline risk

Macro Stance: Poland has moved from behind the curve to openly hawkish. The Monetary Policy Council raised rates again, taking them to their highest in almost 9 years (Rzeczpospolita) [73] , after Pekao had forecast a 50 bp move (StockWatch.pl) [74] . Governor Glapiński then cast himself as a hawk at an unusual press conference ('Ja jako jastrząb', Interia Wydarzenia) [75] and PolsatNews.pl ran the line 'I will urge tightening of monetary policy' over the question of whether loan instalments will rise further [76] . The backdrop justifies it: inflation exceeded 8% and hit a 21-year high (Bankier.pl) [77] , and the end-2021 reading rose to 8.6% (300Gospodarka.pl) [78] . Labour is tight, with registered unemployment at 5.6% in January (RadioMaryja.pl, citing the family and social policy ministry) [79] and the average wage up 9.6% in 2021 (pb.pl) [80] . Our stance is Mixed: credible tightening and a strong currency against fiscal noise and frontline security risk.

Markets: Currency and equities are sending opposite messages. Bankier.pl reported the zloty at its strongest in 8 months [81] and credited Glapiński with strengthening it [82] . Warsaw equities have not followed: the Beta ETF WIG20TR fell -0.88% to 38.15 PLN on February 10 and is -8.09% over twenty sessions, while the USD-listed EPOL fell -2.27% to 21.13 USD in the US session (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2022-02-10). Bankier.pl is already asking whether markets are still in a correction or already in a bear phase [83] . Banks are where higher rates should pay: Parkiet argues WSE bank quotations have the potential to continue their bull run [84] , but PKO Bank Polski fell -1.73% to 48.15 PLN and is -3.27% over twenty sessions, while Bank Pekao is -0.18% at 136.20 PLN over the same window (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2022-02-10). A strong currency alongside flat bank stocks says investors are discounting regulatory and geopolitical risk, not rates.

Fiscal & Politics: Fiscal policy is the weak flank. Experts quoted by Miesięcznik Finansowy BANK call Polski Ład unfixable and want an entirely new law [85] , Polityka.pl reports that Finance Minister Tadeusz Kościński was made an example of over the reform [86] , and Wyborcza.biz says the next finance minister will be a woman [87] . A Business Insider Polska poll measuring how many people understand Polski Ład [88] keeps the reform's comprehension problem in public view. Inflation flatters the debt arithmetic, which Rzeczpospolita says helps the government manage state debt [89] , but it also lifts indexation: pension indexation will be higher than forecast [90] , even as purchasing power may fall [91] . In Brussels, the EU is withholding funds from Poland over an unpaid fine (The Guardian) [92] in the coal-mine dispute (DW) [93] , Rzeczpospolita reports the government drawing lessons on whether Upper Silesian mines could become new Turów cases [94] , and Politico Europe described a half step back to cool legal conflicts with the EU [95] .

Corporate & Sectors: Energy costs dominate corporate news. Money.pl reports entrepreneurs alarmed by letters announcing gas supply restrictions [96] , Business Insider Polska says bakers facing gas price rises of up to 1000% wrote to the prime minister [97] , and WysokieNapiecie.pl notes that gas for business quietly became cheaper [98] , a sign the worst of the contract reset may be passing. Orlen raised wholesale fuel prices (INNPoland.pl) [99] , its CEO says Lotos jobs will be kept (Bankier.pl) [100] , and MOL's deputy chief says the Hungarian group will become one of the most important players (Rzeczpospolita) [101] . Orlen fell -2.78% to 71.22 PLN and is -12.55% over twenty sessions, while KGHM rose +4.63% to 152.40 PLN and JSW is +20.79% over twenty sessions at 48.80 PLN (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2022-02-10); KGHM has registered its copper cathodes on China's INE exchange (Inwestycje.pl) [102] . Consumers are the pressure point: LPP is optimistic about the year but expects cost pressure to hit margins (Bankier.pl) [103] , and its shares are -14.12% over twenty sessions, with Allegro -20.91% (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2022-02-10).

Geopolitics & Security: Poland is now NATO's frontline logistics hub. The first US reinforcement troops have arrived (Reuters) [104] , US troops in Poland are preparing for Americans potentially fleeing Ukraine (Reuters) [105] , US aircraft are heading to Poland to enhance collective defence (European Command) [106] , and the UK is sending 350 more troops to the Polish border (Politico Europe) [107] ; Prime Minister Morawiecki called Britain's move a wonderful proof of solidarity (Gov.pl) [108] . Diplomatically, Poland, as OSCE head, says political will is needed to end the standoff (Reuters) [109] . The danger is that markets treat security as binary: Russia and Belarus began military drills near Belarus's border with Ukraine (The Guardian) [110] , a survey reported by Bankier.pl finds Europeans fear Russia will attack Ukraine this year [111] , and TVN24 reports people arriving from all over Ukraine to prepare to defend the country [112] .

New / Most Important: The decisive development is hawkish NBP communication turning into currency strength, because a firmer zloty is the cheapest disinflation tool Poland has. Glapiński also intends to buy another 100 tonnes of gold (Business Insider Polska) [113] , diversifying reserves while regional risk rises. Biznes Wprost had noted first signals of falling inflation as the zloty awaited the decision [114] , and Credit Agricole's revised forecast of 7.5% inflation this year and 5.5% next year (Bankier.pl) [115] shows how long the overshoot is expected to last. Higher real-rate expectations, a firmer currency and reserve accumulation form the macro case for Polish assets; geopolitics is the reason it is not yet priced into equities.

Playbook ✓: Action: express the hawkish NBP through the currency and short-dated domestic rates rather than broad Warsaw equities, and within equities favour commodity exporters such as KGHM and JSW over consumer names. Trigger: the zloty holding its eight-month high while the WIG20TR tracker stops falling from 38.15 PLN. What changes the call: a Russian escalation that lifts regional risk premia, or a fiscal response to Polski Ład that undoes the NBP's work. ⚠ Divergence: the zloty sits at an eight-month high on NBP hawkishness, yet EPOL is -8.57% and the WIG20TR tracker -8.09% over twenty sessions (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2022-02-10), so the currency market is pricing policy credibility while equity holders are pricing war and regulatory risk.
