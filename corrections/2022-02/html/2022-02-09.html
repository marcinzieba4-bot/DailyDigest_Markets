🌍 Geographic Macro Overview

🇺🇸 United States  Mixed  Front-end repricing before CPI

Macro Stance: The US is a strong-labour, high-inflation economy that the bond market is now pricing for an imminent tightening cycle, and equities are trying to live with that rather than fight it. January payrolls rose by 467,000, which Al Jazeera framed as calming Omicron fears [1] , and Reuters described a labour market that shrugged off the surge ahead of rate hikes [2] . The Fed pointed to a rate hike coming in March, CNBC reported [3] . Reuters also reported that robust imports pushed the 2021 trade deficit to a record [4] , a reminder that domestic demand is still outrunning supply. Stance: Mixed, because growth is not the problem; the price of money is.

Rates & Curve: The 2Y Treasury closed at 1.35% and the 10Y at 1.96% (US Treasury, prior close 2022-02-08), leaving 10Y-2Y at +61 bps. Over 20 observations the 2Y is up 43 bps against 18 bps for the 10Y, so the spread has narrowed 25 bps: a bear flattening, although the curve shape is still moderately steep. The last five observations added 17 bps on the 2Y and 15 bps on the 10Y, so the long end has started to participate. NBC Connecticut flagged the 1.96% print with inflation data in focus [5] . With the 30Y at 2.25% and 10Y-3M at +171 bps, this remains a front-loaded hiking story rather than a recession signal, and Credit Suisse argued that any inversion may be a 'false positive' recession signal [6] .

New / Most Important: Risk assets rallied into the rate move rather than away from it. The S&P 500 rose 0.84% to 4,521.54 index points and the Nasdaq 100 gained 1.21% to 14,747.03 index points, while the VIX fell 1.42 points to 21.44 (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2022-02-08). The DXY added 0.25% to 95.64 index points but is down 0.36% over 20 sessions, so the dollar is not tightening conditions on top of yields. Leadership was telling: XLF gained 1.39% while XLE lost 2.15%. A Reuters poll had pencilled in three hikes this year [7] , while another Reuters analysis argued a start 'with a bang' is not likely [8] ; the next CPI print decides which framing survives.

Playbook ✓: Action: overweight US banks against long-duration growth and REITs into the CPI print. Trigger: a hot core number that lifts the 2Y above its 1.35% close faster than the 10Y would extend the bear flattening and favour lenders over duration. What changes the call: a soft print that pulls the 2Y back toward its 20-observation start of 0.92% and the VIX back below 19.40, its level 20 sessions ago, which would argue for rotating back into the Nasdaq 100. The Senate fight over Fed nominations, with Politico quoting McConnell accusing nominee Sarah Bloom Raskin of seeking to 'bully' companies on climate [9] , matters for pricing only if it delays the March move.

Trigger: TTF back above its 84.61 EUR/MWh 20-session reference, or a breakdown in the Macron channel, would favour energy over banks. 🇨🇳 China  Mixed  Easing into a soft patch

Macro Stance: China is easing into weakness while the rest of the world tightens, which makes it the one major economy where policy pushes in the direction equity investors want. The PBOC cut key lending rates, including one for the first time in nearly two years, CNBC reported [22] , and Bloomberg framed it as the central bank seizing the moment for stimulus as the Fed hikes [23] . GDP grew 8.1% in 2021, the fastest in a decade, according to Global Times [24] , but the BBC tied the rate cuts directly to slowing growth [25] . Stance: Mixed, with supportive policy set against fragile demand.

PMIs & Activity: The factory signal is soft. Reuters reported January Caixin factory activity contracted as COVID lockdowns bite [26] , and Geopolitical Futures examined how zero-COVID weighs on manufacturing [27] . Consumption is not compensating: the holiday box office plunged 23% as theaters pushed prices to record highs, CNBC reported [28] , an early holiday read that points to price-sensitive households. The bright spot is upstream: industrial profits rebounded in 2021 as production improved, per China Daily [29] , and fixed-asset investment rose 4.9% in 2021, Macau Business reported [30] . Firm upstream profits against weak downstream demand is a margin-squeeze story for consumer-facing names.

Credit Impulse: January credit data are still pending, so the impulse has to be read from policy. The cut to medium-term lending and reverse repo rates [31] lowers the floor under funding costs, and MacroMicro tracks the LPR interest-rate swaps [32] through which further cuts get priced. Regulation is still tight at the edges: Reuters reported Ant-backed MYBank was fined for violating credit-scoring rules [33] . BNP Paribas Asset Management argued China's debt vulnerability keeps defying dire predictions [34] . The read-through is that easing is incremental and targeted rather than a broad credit flood, which caps the upside for cyclical commodities.

Property: Property remains the drag that easing has not fixed. Caixin described the property market as off to a bleak start in 2022 [35] , even though new home prices fell less in December on promotions and easing curbs, Business Recorder reported [36] ; that is a slower decline, not a recovery. In Hong Kong, lived-in home valuations dropped amid the fifth Covid wave, according to the South China Morning Post [37] , and MoneyWeek argued for shorting China's largest real-estate broker [38] . Developers need sales, sales need confidence, and confidence needs either a credit push or a clear signal that deleveraging pressure is easing; neither is visible yet.

Fiscal, Policy & Trade: Trade policy is turning into a liability. The Peterson Institute found China bought none of the extra $200 billion of US exports promised under the Trump-era deal [39] , and CNN reported China missed its trade targets [40] , handing Washington a ready-made grievance. At the same time the US and Japan agreed to cut Trump-era steel tariffs, the BBC reported [41] , so tariff relief is flowing to allies, not Beijing. Diplomacy is tilting toward Moscow: Reuters reported a 30-year Russia-China gas deal via a new pipeline, settled in euros [42] , and Putin hailed $117.5 billion of China deals [43] . That alignment raises sanctions-spillover risk for Chinese banks and commodity traders.

Markets & Flows: Back from the Lunar New Year break, the Shanghai Composite rose 0.67% to 3,452.63 index points, still down 4.95% over 20 sessions (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2022-02-08). Hong Kong was weaker: the Hang Seng fell 1.02% to 24,329.49 index points as WuXi Biologics sank by a record after a US red-flag listing, the South China Morning Post reported [44] . Tencent lost 1.67% to 446.40 HKD and Alibaba's Hong Kong line fell 3.30% to 111.20 HKD, but in New York the Alibaba ADR jumped 6.17% to 121.90 USD and KWEB rose 4.08% (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2022-02-08). XPeng announced the inclusion of its shares in the Shenzhen-Hong Kong Stock Connect [45] , widening the mainland buyer base for EV names.

New / Most Important: The most important new signal is that Washington's tools are shifting from tariffs to lists. The WuXi Biologics drop after its US red-flag listing [44] shows how a single US designation can reprice a Hong Kong healthcare leader within one session, and it lands alongside the trade-deal shortfall, which adds to Washington's list of grievances. Common-prosperity pressure is also still shaping corporate behaviour: JD.com's founder donated US$2 billion to charity amid the tech crackdown, the South China Morning Post reported [46] , and the WSJ described Xi Jinping seeking to scale China's 'three big mountains' in the name of common prosperity [47] . That keeps a platform-economy discount alive even as monetary policy turns friendlier.

Playbook ✓: Action: prefer China exposure through policy-sensitive, domestically funded channels, meaning A-shares via ASHR and the NEV supply chain, over US-listed platforms and Hong Kong healthcare. Trigger: a further rate cut or a property-specific easing signal; ASHR was unchanged at 36.54 USD and is down 4.04% over 20 sessions (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2022-02-08). Caixin calls this a turning point for new-energy vehicles [48] , and CnEVPost reported China took 53% of global passenger NEV sales in 2021 [49] . What changes the call: another US listing action or a weak credit print. ⚠ Divergence: New York told the opposite story, with the Alibaba ADR and KWEB rallying while Hong Kong lines fell, so US-listed platforms are being bought on valuation even as listing risk rises.

🇯🇵 Japan  Bearish  Imported inflation, BOJ anchored

Macro Stance: Japan is the major market where rising global yields and import costs bite without a domestic-demand offset. The Nikkei 225 closed at 27,284.52 index points, up 0.13% but down 3.32% over 20 sessions, with Tokyo Electron down 11.57% and Sony Group down 13.05% over the same window (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2022-02-08). Stance: Bearish on the index, selective on exporters.

Activity & Currency: The trade channel shows the squeeze. Households are cautious: Reuters' profile of the frugal Nambu family illustrated the limits of stimulus [55] , even as a Cup Noodle price hike tests student budgets [56] . Corporate news is better: Nissan raised its profit outlook while warning chips limit growth [57] , and Washington agreed to cut tariffs on Japanese steel [58] . Toyota Motor rose 0.90% to 2,296.00 JPY, while SoftBank Group fell 0.90% to 5,302.00 JPY ahead of its results (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2022-02-08). The currency channel works against households: a BOJ holding rates negative while the Fed prepares to hike is the mechanism that would weaken the yen and amplify imported energy costs.

Playbook ✓: Action: underweight the Nikkei against Europe, but own exporters with pricing power over domestic consumer plays. Trigger: SoftBank's next step after the Arm collapse; Reuters reported it will take Arm public instead of selling it [59] , while the Asahi said the collapse leaves SoftBank's asset sales in disarray [60] . What changes the call: a BOJ shift toward normalisation messaging, or a sharp rebound in Advantest, down 11.84% over 20 sessions, signalling that chip-equipment demand is re-rating Tokyo's tech complex.

🇵🇱 Poland  Mixed  NBP hikes into fiscal disarray

Macro Stance: Poland is tightening hard into an inflation problem that its own fiscal policy keeps feeding. The Monetary Policy Council raised rates again on Tuesday, leaving them at the highest in almost nine years, Rzeczpospolita reported [69] , and Bankier.pl led with the hike [70] . December inflation rose to 8.6% on high food prices, GUS data showed, per 300Gospodarka [71] , and Credit Agricole had already lifted its 2022 inflation forecast to 7.5% [72] . Rzeczpospolita warned that the slowdown is masked by statistics, with inflation and rates already curbing appetites [73] . The individual investors' association SII read the decision as a sign the NBP has not said its last word [74] . Stance: Mixed, because monetary policy is credible on direction while politics, EU funds and security keep a premium on Polish assets.

Markets: Polish assets welcomed the hike. The Beta ETF WIG20TR tracker rose 1.98% to 38.15 PLN and EPOL gained 2.45% to 21.34 USD, with banks leading: Bank Pekao up 3.38% to 137.60 PLN, PKO Bank Polski up 2.88% to 48.97 PLN and mBank up 2.70% to 495.00 PLN (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2022-02-08). Parkiet's 'the bulls have their five minutes again' caught the mood [75] . Before the decision, Biznes Wprost wrote that the zloty was waiting for the Council and saw first signals of easing inflation [76] . Money.pl tallied almost 50 new companies debuting on the GPW [77] , evidence of a deep domestic listing pipeline. This is still a recovery inside a downtrend: the WIG20TR tracker is down 5.57% over 20 sessions, and the FT's report that emerging markets are signalling an end to aggressive rate-raising cycles [78] puts Poland on the late side of the global cycle.

Fiscal & Politics: Fiscal policy is in visible disarray. Polityka wrote that finance minister Tadeusz Kościński had been made an example of over the Polski Ład tax reform, with nothing following from it [79] ; Wyborcza.biz reported the next finance minister will be a woman [80] , and Wprost said Gertruda Uścińska received the offer [81] . Miesięcznik Finansowy BANK quoted experts calling Polski Ład unfixable and urging an entirely new law [82] . The anti-inflation shield is showing up as grosz-sized price cuts in shops, Bankier.pl reported [83] , while Money.pl had economists tallying its costs and revenues [84] . Business Insider Polska reported bakers writing to the prime minister over gas bills up as much as 1000% [85] , the energy shock the shield does not reach. Rzeczpospolita noted inflation is helping the government manage state debt [86] , convenient arithmetic that weakens the incentive to fight prices through the budget, especially with pension indexation the largest in years, per 300Gospodarka [87] .

Corporate & Sectors: Orlen rose 4.53% to 71.60 PLN; its CEO pledged that jobs at Lotos will be maintained, Bankier.pl reported [88] , and INNPoland said Orlen raised its wholesale petrol price while stations stop subsidising the pump [89] , which is margin-positive but politically sensitive. KGHM gained 2.58% to 143.10 PLN and registered its copper cathodes on China's INE exchange, Inwestycje.pl reported [90] . LPP climbed 3.83% to 15,990.00 PLN, though Bankier.pl said the retailer expects cost pressure to hit margins this year [91] . Allegro was the laggard, down 3.94% to 33.66 PLN and 13.33% over five sessions, while CRN Polska reported new managers on its management board [92] . Orange Polska jumped 5.06% to 8.09 PLN, a defensive name catching a bid. JSW is up 16.11% over 20 sessions at 45.98 PLN (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2022-02-08), the market's coking-coal hedge.

Geopolitics & Security: Poland is now NATO's front line in practice. Reuters reported the first US reinforcement troops arrived with more expected [93] , Notes From Poland described a preparatory group ahead of a deployment of 1,700 troops [94] , and Politico Europe said the UK will send 350 more troops to the Polish border [95] . Voice of America reported the Baltic states and Poland are increasingly alarmed by a revanchist Russia [96] , and Defense Express reported Poland will supply Piorun MANPADS to Ukraine [97] . Brussels is squeezing Warsaw at the same time: DW reported the EU will withhold funds over the coal-mine dispute [98] , and the Guardian tied the move to an unpaid fine [99] . Security spending is rising while EU transfers are being docked, a combination that widens the fiscal gap.

New / Most Important: The most important development is the policy mix delivered within days: a rate hike to multi-year highs, a finance minister replaced over a tax reform [79] [80] , and EU payments cut over the mine dispute, as the New York Times reported [100] . Together they argue that the NBP will keep carrying the adjustment. Pekao had flagged a 50 bp move this month, StockWatch reported [101] , and Wiadomości Onet warned loan instalments would rise again [102] . For banks that means higher net interest income now and higher provisions later; for households it means a squeeze the shield only partly offsets. Morgan Stanley had forecast dynamic rate rises in Poland even while the ECB stands pat, Strefa Inwestorów reported [103] .

Playbook ✓: Action: stay long Polish banks against the broad index, with Pekao and PKO Bank Polski the cleanest expressions of the rate path, and keep Orlen as a margin story. Trigger: further hawkish NBP signals and a stable zloty confirm the trade; Pekao's 20-session reference of 131.55 PLN is the line that must hold. What changes the call: a fiscal response to Polski Ład that adds spending, a deeper EU funds freeze, or a Russian escalation that hits the currency and forces a defensive hike. ⚠ Divergence: while Poland tightens, emerging markets elsewhere are signalling the end of aggressive hiking [78] , and Allegro's 17.56% 20-session drop shows consumer names pricing a demand squeeze that the bank rally ignores.
