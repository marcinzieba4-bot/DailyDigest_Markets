🌍 Geographic Macro Overview

🇺🇸 United States  Bullish  growth bid / bond discipline

Macro Stance: The election has removed a major source of uncertainty and replaced it with a forceful bet on American corporate earnings. AP reports that Donald Trump has won the White House; Reuters describes his economic plan as a combination of tax cuts, tariffs and an immigration crackdown. Those measures would not transmit to profits in the same way or on the same timetable. Lower taxation could lift after-tax earnings, while tariffs could raise input costs and labour restrictions could squeeze supply. Treat the first equity response as a repricing of possibilities, with domestic businesses better placed than companies that need frictionless global supply chains. [1] [2]

Rates & Curve: The S&P 500 closed at 5,929.04 index points, up 2.53% in the prior session; the Russell 2000 rose 5.84%, while VIX fell to 16.27 index points. DXY stood at 105.09 index points. The rates side is less comfortable: the Treasury 2Y was 4.27%, the 10Y 4.42% and the 10Y−2Y spread +15 bps. Over twenty Treasury observations, the 2Y rose 29 bps and the 10Y rose 38 bps: the curve is positive but very flat, with bear steepening (both up, 10Y up more). Equity enthusiasm therefore faces a higher discount-rate hurdle rather than a broad fall in financing costs. (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2024-11-06; US Treasury, prior close 2024-11-06)

New / Most Important: The economy underneath the political repricing is uneven. The October ISM services reading is 56 index points, against a manufacturing reading of 46.5. That supports exposure to domestic service activity, but it argues against describing every industrial business as a beneficiary of the same upswing. The contrast also matters for inflation: strong service activity can keep demand resilient even when factory orders disappoint. Reuters reports that investors are minimising bond bets as the election overshadows the Fed meeting. The policy decision remains a separate catalyst; an election result does not settle the central bank's reaction to activity or inflation. [3] [4] [5]

Playbook ✓: Keep a constructive equity stance, favouring profitable domestic franchises and companies whose recent guidance supports the valuation. Add after a pullback that holds the election repricing rather than assuming the first gap is a permanent floor. Pair that stance with restrained long-duration exposure: a further rise in long yields without stronger earnings would weaken the case for paying higher multiples. Reuters links the Treasury selloff to deficit concerns, so the trigger for adding duration is a stabilising bond market, not merely a benign equity tape. Reduce the cyclical tilt if the next earnings updates turn political optimism into warnings about costs, customer budgets or trade disruption. [6]

🇪🇺 European Union  Bearish  trade risk / political fracture

Macro Stance: Europe faces a double challenge: a fragile manufacturing recovery and a more uncertain trade relationship with the United States. Reuters reports that the euro-zone factory slump showed signs of stabilising in October. Stabilisation is useful, but it is a weaker proposition than an outright recovery in orders and pricing power. The market must now assess whether companies can defend margins if trade barriers rise. That makes indiscriminate exporter exposure less attractive than businesses with local demand, a strong service component or pricing power that does not depend on favourable currency arithmetic. [7]

Credit & Inflation: The inflation backdrop gives the ECB room to debate further easing, but not a blank cheque. CNBC reports that euro-zone inflation rose to 2%, weakening the case for a very large rate cut; Reuters' earlier policy report describes an ECB lowering rates as the economy sags. Slower activity and renewed price pressure can coexist. A lower policy rate helps borrowers only when lenders and customers are willing to transact. Favour companies whose demand is already visible rather than valuing a distant recovery solely from an anticipated fall in interest expense. [8] [9]

New / Most Important: Germany's governing coalition has crumbled and the country faces a snap-election prospect, Reuters reports. That creates uncertainty around the timing and shape of fiscal decisions just as exporters confront the prospect of American tariffs. BMW's quarterly profit missed expectations because of weak Chinese sales and brake issues, demonstrating that the industry's difficulties cannot be reduced to a single new political risk. Company execution, regional demand and the policy outlook now overlap. A rebound needs evidence from those channels, not just a technically oversold share price. [10] [11]

Playbook ✓: Underweight broad European autos and avoid treating lower valuations as a sufficient catalyst. Prefer selective domestic services and businesses with demonstrated cash generation. The condition for increasing cyclical exposure is improving orders alongside greater clarity on trade policy and German fiscal direction. Reconsider the defensive stance if earnings guidance improves before the macro headlines do; markets can recover ahead of activity. Until then, use exporter rallies to improve portfolio quality rather than restoring the full index weight.

🇨🇳 China  Mixed  policy support / tariff challenge

Macro Stance: China's investment case is moving from a debate about the scale of domestic support to a debate about whether that support can offset a tougher external environment. Reuters reports that China says it will work with the United States, while expecting more rivalry under Trump. CNBC says the election result and tariff threat have raised expectations for additional Chinese stimulus. That is a forecast about the policy response, not an announced package. The constructive argument is that pressure can accelerate action; the caution is that a defensive response may stabilise losses without creating a durable private-demand expansion. Choose instruments that can survive either outcome rather than paying for a perfect policy rescue. [12] [13]

PMIs & Activity: The latest growth picture remains uneven. Reuters describes third-quarter GDP as growing at the weakest pace since early 2023, while CNBC reports GDP growth of 4.6% for the third quarter. Global Times reports that October's Caixin services PMI rose to 52, a three-month high. Services are therefore providing a more constructive activity signal than the broad profit backdrop: Reuters reports a steep September decline in industrial profits. These observations point to an economy with pockets of improvement rather than a uniform acceleration. For portfolio construction, reward businesses with identifiable customer demand and operating progress; avoid extrapolating a service-survey improvement into a recovery in every industrial margin. [14] [15] [16] [17]

Credit Impulse: Interest-rate support is real, but the distinction between cheaper funding and greater demand for funding remains central. Reuters reports that China cut key lending rates to support growth. The latest September lending release carried by Forex Factory says new bank loans rose to 1.59 trillion yuan but fell short of forecasts. A larger monthly credit quantity can still disappoint the expectations embedded in asset prices. Lower borrowing costs may ease debt service, yet a household worried about property values or a business uncertain about orders may choose not to expand. The next confirmation would be broader private borrowing and activity, accompanied by evidence that financing supports new spending rather than simply refinancing existing obligations. [18] [19]

Property: The property story contains an encouraging transaction signal and a difficult balance-sheet signal. South China Morning Post reports better home sales among the largest developers while noting that other indicators remain weak. The Financial Times asks whether the market is turning a corner across several cities; that framing captures the right investment question without establishing a nationwide turnaround. More completed transactions could improve confidence and cash collection, but they do not automatically remove unfinished-project risk or restore the value of every developer's land bank. Prefer businesses with cash-flow visibility and avoid using a brief sales improvement to justify a blanket upgrade of highly indebted property equity. [20] [21]

Fiscal, Policy & Trade: Tariff risk raises the value of domestic demand support but also creates a harder test of policy effectiveness. Reuters explains why Trump's proposed tariffs could pose a larger threat to China this time and reports American suppliers and importers preparing for promised levies. Neither story means every proposed measure has become law. The relevant transmission is conditional: trade friction could bring forward orders temporarily, then weaken later demand or force companies to absorb costs. Domestic fiscal action would be more valuable if it supported household spending and project completion rather than merely offsetting an external shock. Demand-oriented measures deserve a stronger market response than headlines that only repackage existing financial support. [22] [23]

Markets & Flows: The market response is already differentiated. The Hang Seng closed at 20,538.38 index points, down 2.23% in the prior session and 1.86% over twenty sessions; Shanghai closed at 3,383.81 index points, almost unchanged in the session and up 3.83% over twenty. ASHR ended at USD 28.07 and KWEB at USD 32.60, down 2.06% and 2.01% respectively in the prior session. That divergence is a price observation, not proof of a measured international capital outflow. It does show that a broad domestic-policy story and offshore technology exposure are being valued differently. A policy trade would be more convincing if domestic and offshore expressions started confirming each other. (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2024-11-06)

New / Most Important: Reuters reports that China's markets fell as a Trump presidency loomed. The crucial change is the interaction between the external challenge and the domestic response: a stimulus announcement that looked sufficient before the election may face a different hurdle now. Do not assume a larger policy expectation guarantees a stronger equity outcome. Investors must ask whether the mechanism reaches consumers, credit demand and property completions, and whether companies can protect earnings against trade uncertainty. That puts domestic services and resilient balance sheets ahead of a simple export-rebound basket, while preserving the option to add beta when policy details and business results justify it. [24]

Playbook ✓: Maintain selective China exposure rather than chasing a countrywide rerating. ASHR offers a domestic-policy expression, while KWEB concentrates more heavily on internet-business expectations; neither removes execution or valuation risk. Add only when policy details are followed by better activity and evidence of cash conversion. Treat tariff-sensitive exporters more cautiously until the scope and timing of any American measures are clearer. Scale back if property transaction improvements fail to translate into broader confidence or if the next profit reports weaken despite financing support. ⚠ Divergence: improving service activity and better developer sales sit beside weak industrial profits and a negative offshore equity response, so a policy floor is easier to argue than a completed growth turn.
