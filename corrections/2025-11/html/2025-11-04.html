🇨🇳 China  Bearish  stimulus drip / weak demand

Macro Stance: Bearish on growth, neutral on the truce. October's official manufacturing PMI fell to 49 while the non-manufacturing index edged up to 50.1 [30]; Bloomberg calls it the longest factory slump in almost a decade [31]. Q3 GDP grew 4.8%, in line [32], but CNBC flagged a 'rare and alarming' drop in investment [33] and The Economist is writing about the mystery of China's slumping investment [34]. The Trump–Xi truce removes the worst tariff tail [35], yet the domestic engine — credit, housing, prices — is still running down, and Beijing's answer is a drip of targeted support rather than a bazooka.

PMIs & Activity: Both October surveys disappointed. Reuters said factory activity stumbled as export orders dried up [36], CNBC that the slump deepened to a six-month low and missed estimates [37], and the private gauge also slowed and missed expectations [38] — the WSJ read it as weaker growth momentum [39]. The offsets are narrow. September industrial profits jumped 21.6% [40] as officials stepped up the overcapacity crackdown [41], which is margin repair rather than demand, and September exports defied expectations [42] as shipments rotated away from the US [43]. The view changes only if the official PMI returns above 50 with new export orders recovering.

Credit Impulse: Credit is the weakest link. September bank loans rose less than expected as weak demand persisted [44], the largest banks held profits only as margins shrank [45], and consumer prices fell more than expected, keeping the economy in deflation [46]. Those September prints point to weak credit transmission. A durable consumer recovery needs stronger household borrowing alongside healthier banks, rather than another marginal reduction in lending rates. Policy nibbles at the edges — Hong Kong banks cut prime rates to as low as 5% [47] — and the next monthly credit release must show household loans turning up before any stabilisation call is credible. Until then the impulse is negative, and the yuan's push abroad, now topping the dollar in some trade, is the only expanding part of the story [48].

Property: No floor yet. New home prices fell at the fastest pace in 11 months in September [49], S&P expects the market to decline at least through 2026 [50], and Bloomberg warns of a 'lost decade' [51]. Developers are being managed, not rescued: Vanke reported a US$2.3 billion loss [52] and leans on Shenzhen Metro for loans of up to $3 billion [53], while New World offered a US$1.9 billion debt swap [54] — moves Bloomberg says are shaking up the sector [55]. A state shareholder funding the weakest developer is a liquidity bridge, not demand; the confirming signal would be stabilising resale prices in the top cities.

Fiscal, Policy & Trade: The truce is real but narrow. Trump announced a cut in fentanyl tariffs on China to 10% while Beijing agreed to delay its latest rare-earth curbs by a year [56], announced plans to suspend probes into US chip firms [57] and agreed to pause the dueling port fees [58]. Farm purchases stay vague [59] — Chinese buyers were still booking Brazilian soybeans as prices eased [60]. Technology remains contested: Trump says China cannot have Nvidia's top AI chips [61]. Any investment in China's domestic hardware chain still needs a credible commercial return and funding plan. At home Beijing signals a sharper pivot to consumption [62], its five-year plan drops EV subsidies [63], and a new 500 billion yuan financial instrument backs investment [64].

Markets & Flows: Onshore and offshore are telling different stories. The Shanghai Composite closed at 3,976.52, up 0.55% in the prior session and 3.88% over 20 sessions, while the Hang Seng sits at 26,158.36, down 4.14% over 20 (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2025-11-03). USD/CNY is pinned at 7.1169, down 0.03% over 20 sessions (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2025-11-03). Local money is the marginal buyer: net inflows into stock ETFs exceeded RMB 20 billion last week with a rare surge into the CSI 300 [65]. A steadier onshore market alongside weaker offshore platforms calls for selecting the earnings exposure rather than treating all China access channels as interchangeable.

New / Most Important: The signal that matters is the export-order drought inside the official PMI [36]: the tariff cut arrives into a demand hole, not a recovery. Exports have held up by rerouting — the NYT's 'What Trade War? China's Export Juggernaut Marches On' [66] — and that channel now runs into Europe's tolerance. The market shows where policy money goes: Victory Giant topped Asian stocks after an Nvidia link fuelled a 600% surge [67], while the consumer platforms in KWEB have kept sliding.

Playbook ✓: Underweight China beta: stay underweight KWEB and Hong Kong property, and avoid materials tied to Chinese construction. Cover the underweight if the official PMI returns above 50 or the next credit data show household borrowing turning up. ⚠ Divergence: the Shanghai Composite is up 3.88% over 20 sessions to 3,976.52 while the official factory PMI contracts and the private survey loses momentum [38] (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2025-11-03) — onshore investors are buying what the surveys are selling.

Why Retail Is Here: Hims offers a different growth story from the race to build computing capacity. Roblox / RBLX  "build the world, explain the bill"

Why Retail Is Here: Roblox gives investors a direct argument about how much spending a popular platform deserves. A believer can interpret present spending as the price of serving a larger community; a sceptic can ask why greater scale has not yet produced the margin investors expected. It also creates an emotional shortcut: liking the product can make a shareholder treat every increase in investment as evidence of ambition rather than an expense that must earn a return.

Where It Breaks: The trade breaks if the spending defence becomes permanent while operating leverage remains absent. Look for a clearer relationship between engagement, monetisation and the cost of supporting the experience, rather than merely another explanation for a lower margin. A sell-off can improve an entry price without fixing the concern that caused it. Avoid averaging down solely because the platform remains popular with users. Prefer a measured position that can wait for an operating improvement, and reduce if the business needs progressively more infrastructure and incentives to sustain the same commercial progress. The investment case strengthens when the new world becomes cheaper to serve relative to the revenue it generates.
