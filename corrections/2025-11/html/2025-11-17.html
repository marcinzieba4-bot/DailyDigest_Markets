🇨🇳 China  Bearish  weak demand / policy optionality

Macro Stance: China's investable problem is the distance between a tradable policy thaw and a domestic recovery that households can feel. Reuters described the weakest factory-output and retail-sales growth in over a year; Bloomberg separately highlighted an investment crash that puzzled economists and threatened growth. The combination argues against buying the entire economy on the strength of a diplomatic headline. Strong individual technology franchises can still win customers, but an equity index is also exposed to cautious borrowers, property wealth and the cost of persistent price competition. Stay defensive on broad domestic demand until policy changes the willingness to spend, not merely the ability to borrow. [12] [13]

The latest activity news extends the caution into production and shopping. A convincing turn would require improving new demand alongside stronger sales, rather than factories simply producing more into a difficult pricing environment. The uncomfortable implication is that another liquidity injection could support financial prices more quickly than private investment. Look for stronger borrowing demand connected to orders, household purchases or genuinely viable projects. A company that expands by drawing more credit while its selling prices weaken can increase financial risk without improving shareholder returns. Prefer balance-sheet room and internally funded growth to an apparently cheap company whose investment case requires both a credit revival and a favourable refinancing. [17] [18]
