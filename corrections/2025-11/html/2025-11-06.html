🇺🇸 United States  Mixed  easing bias / data blackout

Macro Stance: The Fed cut again by 25 basis points during the shutdown, but the committee heading into December is split: Dallas Fed's Logan says the cut was not needed and opposes another, Goolsbee is on the fence, and Reuters describes divided policymakers staking out positions. The data that still flow are mixed: the October ISM services index jumped to 52.4 against 50.5 expected, an eight-month high with weak employment; ADP counted 42,000 private jobs, more than expected; manufacturing sat at 48.7. Hiring is fading underneath, with Indeed's openings measure at its lowest since February 2021, and with the shutdown now the longest in US history, September CPI at 3.0% remains the latest official inflation read. Mixed: growth holds, but a Fed without data or consensus can surprise either way.

Rates & Curve: The 2Y closed at 3.63% and the 10Y at 4.17%, a 10Y−2Y spread of +54 bps (US Treasury, prior close 2025-11-05). Over 20 observations the 2Y rose 6 bps and the 10Y 3 bps, a bear flattening (both up, 2Y up more), on a curve that is positively sloped but still flat by historical standards (long-run average about +90 to +100 bps) (US Treasury, prior close 2025-11-05). The last five observations hit the long end hardest, the 10Y adding 9 bps (US Treasury, prior close 2025-11-05). Reuters tied the jump to upbeat data and earnings. The S&P 500 closed at 6,796.29, up 0.37% in the prior session but down 1.37% over five sessions; the VIX eased 0.99 points to 18.01 and the dollar index sits at 100.20, up 1.37% over 20 sessions (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2025-11-05). A flatter curve and a firmer dollar price fewer cuts, not a recession.

New / Most Important: Two things matter more than any print. First, the AI valuation shakeout: Goldman Sachs and Morgan Stanley CEOs predicted corrections of up to 20%, Burry's bets against Nvidia and Palantir set off a tech sell-off, per the BBC, and CNBC reports hedge funds dumping tech at the fastest pace in two years — yet Wall Street bought the dip the next day, as Bloomberg's wrap put it. Second, the Supreme Court: at oral argument the justices appeared skeptical that the IEEPA tariffs are legal, while Bessent said he was optimistic. Striking them would hand importers a margin windfall and, as Reuters notes, add to trade uncertainty.

Playbook ✓: Stay long US equities but pay for conversion, not narrative: overweight hyperscalers turning capex into revenue and bottleneck suppliers in memory and power, funded by trimming high-multiple software that is sold even on beats. Keep duration short until the official data restart, and hedge AI concentration with S&P puts while the VIX sits near 18. What changes the call: a VIX close above 20 with the S&P 500 back below its 20-session reference of 6,753.72 would mean the dip-buyers have lost (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2025-11-05). A Supreme Court ruling against the tariffs is the signal to add small-cap importers through IWM.

🇨🇳 China  Bearish  truce relief / weak demand

Macro Stance: Bearish on the domestic cycle, relieved on the tail risk. The Trump–Xi meeting produced a one-year truce, cutting fentanyl tariffs to 10% while Beijing delays its latest rare-earth curbs by a year, so the trade shock is off the table for now. Reuters calls it a tactical truce, not a major reset, and the domestic data keep deteriorating: third-quarter growth printed 4.8% with a 'rare and alarming' drop in investment, factories are in their longest slump in almost a decade, and home prices are falling faster. Policy is pivoting toward consumption and technology, not a big-bang stimulus, which caps the upside for cyclical China exposure.

PMIs & Activity: The official manufacturing PMI fell to a six-month low in October and missed estimates as export orders dried up. The private RatingDog survey also slowed and missed expectations, and services growth hit a three-month low, although the RatingDog services index at 52.6 still signals expansion. The offsets sit in profits and trade: industrial profits surged as officials stepped up the overcapacity crackdown, and September exports beat expectations while imports rose at the fastest pace since April 2024. Prices remain the weak spot: September consumer prices dropped more than expected, even as core inflation reached a 19-month high. Output and margins are doing the work; household demand is not.

Credit Impulse: The latest available credit print is September's, and it was soft: new bank loans rose less than expected as weak credit demand persisted. Liquidity is coming from the top instead — the PBOC bought 20 billion yuan of government bonds in October as it resumed trading — while the largest banks report steady profits but shrinking margins, which caps their appetite to lend. Hong Kong is easing at the margin, with HSBC and peers cutting the prime rate to as low as 5%. What would confirm a turn is October credit data showing household borrowing recovering; until then the impulse is flat, and central-bank bond buying is a floor, not a stimulus.

Property: New home prices fell at the fastest pace in 11 months in September, and Bloomberg notes the decline accelerated even as top cities expanded support. Developer stress has become a question of state backstops: Shenzhen Metro agreed to lend China Vanke up to $3 billion after the developer reported a US$2.3 billion loss on slowing sales, and Bloomberg says New World and Vanke debt moves have shaken the sector. An S&P analyst expects the market to keep declining at least through 2026, and CNBC reports Beijing prioritising tech growth over a property rescue. Piecemeal support is not a bottom; the sector remains a drag on household wealth.

Fiscal, Policy & Trade: Fiscal policy is targeted, not broad. Beijing injected 500 billion yuan to fund over 2,300 strategic projects, Caixin describes a proactive fiscal stance aimed at demand and technology, and the new planning cycle sets a 4.17% annual growth benchmark through 2035. On trade, China suspended some rare-earth curbs and probes of US chip firms, Bessent says China will buy 12 million metric tons of US soybeans this season, and Beijing lifted some tariffs on US farm goods, though soybeans remain costly. The technology front hardened at the same time: the White House says Nvidia cannot sell its most advanced AI chip to China. Supply discipline is spreading, with an industry body recommending capacity caps for copper, lead and zinc smelters.

Markets & Flows: Onshore is beating offshore. The Shanghai Composite closed at 3,969.25, up 2.23% over 20 sessions, while the Hang Seng finished at 25,935.41, down 3.79%, and KWEB fell 6.95% to 39.35 (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2025-11-05). ASHR closed at 32.97, up 1.17% in the prior session, and USD/CNY sat at 7.1295, up just 0.15% over 20 sessions (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2025-11-05). SCMP notes the yuan holding the line alongside Beijing's trade stance. Positioning explains part of the gap: Goldman says shorting Xiaomi is consensus among hedge funds, and Xiaomi's Hong Kong shares are down 19.55% over 20 sessions (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2025-11-05). Demand for Chinese paper is strong: a $4 billion dollar bond drew orders above $65 billion and priced at the same yield as Treasuries, and HKEX posted a record profit on rising turnover and IPOs.

New / Most Important: The signal that matters is the chip decoupling running underneath the truce. A week after the summit, Reuters reported that Beijing has banned foreign AI chips from state-funded data centres, the FT reports it is offering its tech giants cheap power to use domestic AI chips, and Washington reaffirmed that Nvidia's top chips stay off-limits. Tariffs were negotiated; technology was not. The truce helps exporters and soybean trades, but the durable money is in China's domestic substitution chain, and the losers are foreign vendors' China revenue lines — Micron is already stopping server-chip supply to China.

Playbook ✓: Own onshore over offshore: hold ASHR and fund it from KWEB and the Hong Kong platform names, where policy favours the state-backed hardware stack over consumer internet and Alibaba's ADR fell 8.42% over five sessions (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2025-11-05). Avoid developers and property-heavy lenders until a credit print shows households borrowing again. Express the truce through agriculture rather than tech: soybean futures are up 8.77% over 20 sessions (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2025-11-05). What changes the call: a broad consumption stimulus or a PMI back above 50 would make us add offshore consumer names. ⚠ Divergence: industrial profit growth has picked up to near a two-year high even as the PMIs slide — margins are repairing because the overcapacity crackdown is working, which is the one thing a China bear has to respect.

🇵🇱 Poland  Bullish  easing cycle / fiscal risk

Macro Stance: Rada Polityki Pieniężnej obniżyła stopy — the Monetary Policy Council cut again at its November meeting, 'the second autumn quarter-point', as Bankier put it, a move of 25 basis points per INFOR. The cut was earned: 'Inflacja znów zaskoczyła ekonomistów i utrzymała się poniżej 3%' — October flash inflation again surprised economists by staying below 3%, lower than forecast per Rzeczpospolita. Activity is turning up: the October PMI brought 'more optimism' to industry, per Business Insider Polska, Lewiatan asks whether it heralds the return of the 'green island', and September production finally delivered good data after a lethargic stretch; GUS also revised GDP, giving the government a margin of error. Labour is the soft spot — 'Bezrobocie rośnie, płace zwalniają', unemployment rising and wages slowing, with almost 100 thousand more registered jobless in a year. Bankier sums up the outlook: rates will fall 'like leaves'.

Markets: The zloty has weakened against a resurgent dollar — 'Dolar przebił 3,70 zł', the dollar broke 3.70 zloty, Bankier reported — with USD/PLN at 3.7078, up 2.20% over five sessions, while EUR/PLN held at 4.2586, up only 0.18% over 20 sessions (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2025-11-05). Equities are consolidating gains: the WIG20TR tracker closed at 59.24 zloty, up 3.51% over 20 sessions but down 2.02% over five, and EPOL at 33.51 dollars (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2025-11-05). Leadership rotated to energy, utilities and insurance — Orlen up 11.19%, PGE 11.27% and PZU 7.41% over 20 sessions, while CD Projekt lost 10.19% (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2025-11-05). FXMAG's October wrap had Budimex and Orlen up more than 12% and CCC down 19%. Sentiment is warm: the share of bulls in the INI investor-sentiment survey rose to 60%, and Parkiet sees the GPW gathering strength before year-end. In bonds, supply is the theme: the finance ministry will offer its first securities linked to the POLSTR rate after Bloomberg reported plans for record quarterly zloty-bond sales as debt grows.

Fiscal & Politics: Fiscal policy is the blemish. Forsal reports GUS and Eurostat data showing the deficit could exceed 7% of GDP while debt grows almost the fastest in the EU, and Notes from Poland says public debt is rising at the second-fastest rate in the bloc. Prime Minister Tusk insists 'wszystko jest pod kontrolą' — everything is under control — but Bankier framed the RPP and Poland's rating as the zloty's two tests, and the banks are paying for the gap after parliament approved a corporate income tax hike for them. Politics adds noise: Rzeczpospolita reported an unexpected resignation in the government, Super Biznes describes a 'war in the government over Orlen' that ended with deputy minister Robert Kropiwnicki walking out, and President Nawrocki keeps submitting bills of his own to the Sejm.

Corporate & Sectors: Orlen is the market's flagship: it overtook Bayer in valuation, is folding the PGNiG brand into a new MyOrlen brand, is committing billions to Baltic offshore energy and opened its first fast-charging hub. In finance, the PZU–Pekao merger is drawing closer, with Parkiet asking what awaits shareholders; analysts quoted by StockWatch call PZU clearly undervalued as it heads for a record, and mBank's results missed expectations although a record year is within reach. XTB is the retail barometer — more than 100 thousand new clients in October, a record — and BM mBanku raised it to buy with an 87-zloty target; the shares rose 9.02% over five sessions (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2025-11-05). Bankier's KGHM note puts gold in focus, with silver following and copper expected to rise over the long horizon. PGE has bids to build a billion-zloty energy-storage facility, and Tauron is up 21.23% over 20 sessions (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2025-11-05). Dino is adding 120 photovoltaic installations from Unimot, and Allegro launched an AI shopping assistant. In apparel, Rzeczpospolita says the third quarter at LPP and CCC revealed 'two worlds', while CD Projekt's pipeline keeps moving — Cyberpunk's creator has already seen the first fragments of the sequel.

Geopolitics & Security: The eastern flank remains on alert. Polish jets intercepted a Russian spy plane over the Baltic for a second consecutive time, Reuters reports Russia's suspected 'hybrid war' is testing European air defences, and Poland plans its own 'drone wall'. Counter-intelligence is busy: a Russian charged with spying admitted passing data to the FSB, and the General Staff warns of a Russian effort to stir anti-Ukrainian sentiment. The build-out is broadening into manpower and industry — Poland expects up to 13,500 more enlistments in 2026, Cognor wants more defence exposure as its plate output for 155 mm ammunition rises severalfold, WB Electronics is considering a GPW listing in 2026, and Boeing sweetened its F-15EX offer with local deals.

New / Most Important: The second autumn cut is the signal, because the RPP is easing into fiscal slippage while the zloty weakens against the dollar — exactly the tension Subiektywnie o finansach raises in asking whether the budget deficit should stop the council. So far investors answer no: EUR/PLN is barely changed over 20 sessions and the WIG20TR tracker is higher (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2025-11-05). If the rating test passes quietly, lower rates flow straight into domestic cyclicals and the insurers' bond books; if it does not, the zloty becomes the release valve.

Playbook ✓: Stay overweight Polish equities in local currency: own the WIG20TR tracker, PZU for the merger optionality, Orlen for cash flow and XTB as the high-beta play on retail participation. Underweight banks with heavy mortgage books until the tax hike and further cuts are priced, and hedge USD/PLN rather than EUR/PLN, because the dollar leg carries the volatility. What changes the call: a rating-outlook cut, or USD/PLN pushing well beyond its 3.70 break, would argue for trimming foreign exposure via EPOL. ⚠ Divergence: unemployment is rising and wages are slowing even as the PMI improves — if the labour market keeps loosening, consumer names such as Dino and LPP will lag the rate-cut story.
