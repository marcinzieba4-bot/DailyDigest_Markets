🇵🇱 Poland  Bearish  → on hold

Macro Stance: The NBP remains in a holding pattern, but the Council is less dovish than disinflation implies: the NBP's voting record showed who backed a hike (Tysol.pl, [Jun 22]) and Gazeta Prawna headlined Iwona Duda's comments "no quick RPP decisions?" ([Jun 17]), while the Czech National Bank hiked next door (ING Think, [Jun 18]). Inflation drew level with the EU average (Business Insider Polska, [Jun 17]) and food cost only 2.4% more than a year earlier (Forsal.pl, [Jun 22]), but producer prices are rising again after almost two years of deflation (CEO Magazyn, [Jun 22]). Activity is two-speed. May industrial output rose 4.1% y/y, above consensus (StockWatch.pl, [Jun 19]), and new orders jumped more than 140% on what experts call a "SAFE effect" (Interia Biznes, [Jun 24]). Retail sales missed again, "2026 will not be the year of the consumer" (wnp.pl, [Jun 22]), and construction slowed despite EU billions (Rzeczpospolita, [Jun 22]). Enterprise wages rose 5.8% y/y, below expectations (StockWatch.pl, [Jun 22]), fell month on month on one-off factors (TVN24, [Jun 22]) and employment fell (CEO Magazyn, [Jun 22]), though pay still outpaces prices (XYZ, [Jun 22]).

Markets: The Fed's hawkish hold set the tone: the WIG turned back from its bull-market peak (Bankier.pl, [Jun 18]) and, after a symbolic triple-witching gain (Parkiet, [Jun 19]), the slide resumed, with KGHM -6.4% against a 1.7% WIG20 drop as the dollar climbed (Bankier.pl, [Jun 23]). Wednesday was the heaviest session: WIG20 closed down 2.49% (BIZNES24, [Jun 24]), with KGHM -6.9%, Orlen -4.9%, PGE -4.6% and Allegro -4.2% (Bankier.pl, [Jun 24]) and the banks joining the drag (Rzeczpospolita, [Jun 24]). Part of KGHM's move is mechanical, as Tuesday was the last day to qualify for its dividend (Strefa Inwestorów, [Jun 23]). At Tuesday's close: KGHM 349.7 (-9.0% 1W, +2.4% 4W), Orlen 129.04 (-6.8% / -9.9%), PGE 9.73 (-1.9% / -7.6%), PKO 104.4 (-1.4% / -0.6%), Pekao 231.0 (-0.7% / -5.6%), Dino 29.1 (-1.4% / -8.7%), LPP 18,480 (-2.4% / -17.8%); only Allegro 38.95 (+3.8% / +12.7%) and CD Projekt 228.7 (+2.6% / -10.6%) rose on the week. EPOL 38.73 is -3.0% 1W and -3.1% 4W. The zloty weakened for a fifth straight day with EUR/PLN above 4.28 (wnp.pl, [Jun 23]) and the dollar was the most expensive in over a year (Comparic.pl, [Jun 24]). Tuesday's close had USD/PLN at 3.7343 (+1.9% 1W, +2.8% 4W) but EUR/PLN 4.2667 only +0.4% / +0.9%: mostly a dollar move. No POLGB yield headlines this week.

Fiscal & Politics: Ministry of Finance data showed debt up 46.8 billion zloty in one month (Gazeta Prawna, Interia Biznes, [Jun 22]); NIK flagged "operations disturbing transparency" in state finances (Money.pl, [Jun 17]), the Fiscal Council warned that delaying cuts could cost Poland dearly (Rzeczpospolita, [Jun 17]) and Zero.pl reports tax rises in preparation ([Jun 19]). The first answer is a levy: the Sejm passed a 60% windfall tax on fuel companies (TVP Info, Money.pl, [Jun 19]), applying from August (GazetaPrawna.pl, [Jun 19]) and adopted within 48 hours (wPolityce.pl, [Jun 18]); the government counts on 4 billion zloty (Money.pl, [Jun 22]). Tusk told rivals that hostility toward Germany and Ukraine serves Russia (Anadolu, [Jun 17]).

Corporate & Sectors: Energy: Orlen suspended its membership of the industry body POPiHN over the levy (Interia Biznes, [Jun 22]), and wPolityce.pl tallies about 20 billion zloty of value lost in days ([Jun 22]). Orlen booked FSRU 2 capacity, taking LNG import capability above 16 billion cubic metres a year (Bankier.pl, [Jun 23]), while PGE, Enea and Unimot want gas without Orlen (Rzeczpospolita, [Jun 23]) and PGE signed a regasification deal worth about 2.8 billion zloty (WysokieNapiecie.pl, [Jun 23]); a key agreement for an SMR in Włocławek was signed (TVP Bydgoszcz, [Jun 22]). Mining: KGHM's Pomeranian project gained MON backing (Bankier.pl, [Jun 23]), yet INNPoland says the share price is "bleeding" ([Jun 23]). Banks: a Supreme Court resolution on exchange-rate clauses hands CHF borrowers a new argument (Gazeta Prawna, [Jun 24]); a draft gives banks nine months to offer borrowers a WIBOR switch (Rzeczpospolita, [Jun 23]); investment loans are growing at a double-digit pace (pb.pl, [Jun 23]). Retail: Dino is under National Prosecutor's Office scrutiny (Bankier.pl, [Jun 18]) and LPP drew a negative recommendation (Comparic.pl, [Jun 17]). Gaming: CD Projekt is renaming itself CD Projekt RED and pays no dividend for 2025 (StockWatch.pl, [Jun 23]).

Geopolitics & Security: Latvia warned of Russian missile and drone provocations targeting the Baltics and Poland (Kyiv Post, [Jun 23]), though not of imminent full-scale war (Meduza, [Jun 23]), and Frontex flags a pressured eastern border (InfoSecurity24, [Jun 23]). Defence money is supportive: an EU military Schengen could bring Poland billions of euros (Rzeczpospolita, [Jun 23]) and a permanent US base is closer, with construction due this year (TOK FM, [Jun 18]). Relations with Kyiv soured: world media warned of a crisis after a decision by President Nawrocki (GazetaPrawna.pl, [Jun 20]) and Poles launched a citizen award for Zelensky after a state honour was revoked (TVP World, [Jun 21]).

New / Most Important: The 60% windfall tax matters most because the week's two stories meet in it. A hawkish Fed and the strongest dollar in over a year explain the direction of Polish assets; the fiscal backdrop explains the thin cushion. With debt up 46.8 billion zloty in a month and both NIK and the Fiscal Council critical, the state has shown it will tap listed champions' profits and legislate in 48 hours. Orlen's -6.8% week is the first cost; the larger one is a policy-risk premium on every state-controlled WIG20 heavyweight.

Playbook ✓: Hawkish Fed → strongest dollar in over a year → zloty slide and a commodity-led blue-chip sell-off is the textbook EM response; strong dollar and EUR weakness remain headwinds for PLN.  |  ⚠️ Divergence: The data do not justify the de-rating: output beat and orders surged. EUR/PLN +0.4% on the week against +1.9% for USD/PLN says the zloty is a passenger in the dollar regime, not pricing a Polish premium; equities are, with EPOL -3.0% in a week and Orlen -9.9% over four weeks reflecting fiscal and levy risk that FX has yet to price.
