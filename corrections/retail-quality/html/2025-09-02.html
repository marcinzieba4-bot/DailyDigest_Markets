🇺🇸 United States  Mixed  employment test / policy uncertainty

Macro Stance: The reopening of US trading brings an awkward combination of strong equity expectations and unresolved policy risk. Reuters' week-ahead preview puts jobs data directly in the path of rate-cut hopes and the stock rally. July core PCE firmed slightly but matched expectations, according to Reuters, so the inflation news has not removed the need to inspect the labour market. A modest cooling could support the easing thesis; a sharper deterioration could damage the earnings that equities are capitalising. Keep those outcomes separate. The investor's task is to own enough growth to participate while retaining the ability to respond if the apparently friendly rate story becomes a demand problem. [1] [2]

Rates & Curve: The last completed US session left the 2Y Treasury at 3.59% and the 10Y at 4.23%, with a 64 bps spread. Over 20 observations the 2Y declined 10 bps, the 10Y was unchanged and the spread widened 10 bps. The curve is moderately steep, approaching the long-run average; its move was steepening (2Y down, 10Y up/flat). The S&P 500 closed at 6,460.26, up 3.56% over 20 sessions, while VIX stood at 15.36 and DXY at 97.77. This is a market expecting relief at the front end while retaining a meaningful cost of long-term capital. It favours careful duration choices rather than assuming every asset benefits equally from easier policy. (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2025-08-29; US Treasury, prior close 2025-08-29)

New / Most Important: Treasury Secretary Bessent expects the Supreme Court to uphold the legality of Trump's tariffs but is considering a Plan B, Reuters reported. Reuters also reported the US continuing work on trade deals despite the court ruling. The practical message is that legal uncertainty has not ended the policy process. Importers should not be valued as if every prospective cost has vanished. The international credibility issue is also widening: Reuters reported ECB President Lagarde warning of risks to the world economy from interference with the Fed. Monetary independence can therefore affect the price of protection even before the next policy decision changes funding costs. [3] [4] [5]

Playbook ✓: Retain a diversified US core, hold intermediate-quality bonds and keep additions to speculative growth conditional on the employment and yield response. If jobs evidence supports gentle cooling and credit remains firm, add cyclical exposure in stages. If resilient hiring pushes yields higher, reduce the most expensive duration-sensitive shares. If weak employment begins to impair spending, favour quality rather than chasing the most rate-sensitive rebound. Treat tariff relief as a company-specific opportunity only when management can explain its actual effect on costs and demand; legal optimism should not replace earnings arithmetic.

🇨🇳 China  Mixed  private rebound / structural drag

Macro Stance: China's economy now offers a more interesting disagreement than an unbroken contraction story. Reuters reported the private factory survey showing activity growing at its quickest pace in five months, while the official manufacturing measure contracted for a fifth consecutive month in August. The difference is useful: it suggests investors should examine the composition of improvement rather than force every business into a single macro label. A private-sector rebound can create genuine opportunities even while larger domestic constraints remain. Keep an allocation to improving earnings, but reserve a broad cyclical overweight for evidence that the recovery reaches more of the economy. [10] [11]

PMIs & Activity: The August private PMI gives the growth camp a stronger argument, yet July factory-output and retail-sales growth had missed forecasts according to Reuters. Manufacturing surveys are index readings about business conditions, while production and retail releases measure different economic activity; they should complement each other rather than be treated as substitutes. The next confirmation is stronger orders that translate into actual sales without requiring more aggressive price competition. A factory can become busier while earning little incremental profit. That distinction matters in an economy where investors are also debating the pressure of low prices and excess competition. Use the improving survey as a reason to investigate, not as a shortcut around margins. [12] [13]

Credit Impulse: The July lending picture remains a constraint. Reuters reported bank loans unexpectedly contracting for the first time in 20 years, and Bloomberg linked the plunge in new loans to weaker demand. A convincing credit impulse would require willing borrowers as well as supportive lenders. When households worry about property and companies face uncertain returns on expansion, liquidity can remain in financial assets instead of becoming new consumption or investment. Watch for a borrowing recovery that is accompanied by better activity, rather than simply a larger financing total. For portfolio construction, this favours cash-generative companies that can fund their own opportunities while the broader financing transmission repairs. [14] [15]

Property: Shanghai's relaxation of home-buying rules, reported by Yahoo Finance, offers a concrete policy channel, but the latest July price evidence still showed new-home prices falling, according to Reuters. The obstacle is confidence as much as eligibility. Removing a restriction can help a willing buyer transact; it cannot compel a cautious household to take on a long commitment. Seazen's exploration of real-world asset tokenisation, reported by Reuters, is a financing innovation worth watching, but it should not be confused with a revival in underlying occupancy or demand. Require evidence of transactions, delivery and stable pricing before adding broad developer risk. Property can stabilise gradually while leaving large differences between issuers and cities. 🧮 Earnings Pattern — What the Market Pays For

A growth engine must pay its own way

Rewarded: Alibaba's rally shows that investors will forgive a blemish in the aggregate result when a business segment offers a compelling forward story. Reuters reported the revenue miss alongside the AI boost to cloud; Bloomberg linked the AI revenue surge to a large stock rally. MongoDB provides a software parallel, with CNBC linking its earnings reaction to a customer-growth boom. The common feature is an identifiable engine that investors believe can matter more in the next stage of earnings. Neither example means every business line or every industry peer deserves the same treatment. [84] [23] [53]

Punished: Dell and Marvell illustrate the punishment for a less convincing conversion. CNBC reported Dell shares falling on a soft quarterly earnings outlook, while Marvell's data-centre forecast prompted investors to sell, according to Reuters. Barron's reported Urban Outfitters falling despite an earnings beat. Across hardware and retail, the message is consistent: the latest reported result can be acceptable while the future economics fail the market's test. A lower share price does not erase that distinction. [85] [86] [87]

Pattern: Evaluate each report through the source of demand, the cost of winning it and the cash retained after delivery. A fast-growing market can attract competition that gives the economic benefit to customers. A slower industry can still produce a positive surprise when management controls inventory, pricing or distribution. The strongest earnings trade is often a change in the quality of growth rather than a dramatic change in the headline growth rate.
