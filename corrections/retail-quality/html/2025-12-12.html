🌍 Geographic Macro Overview

🇺🇸 United States  Mixed  pause after cut / data dependent

Macro Stance: Mixed: the Federal Reserve cut its policy rate by 25 basis points on December 10 in a divided vote, and its projections show one cut in 2026. The labour data are the swing factor: ADP reported a 32,000 fall in November private payrolls, and JOLTS openings rose to 7.7 million in October. Activity is soft but not breaking, with the November ISM manufacturing index at 48.2% and the services index at 52.6%. The writer’s reading is that the Fed has bought time for the labour market but has not yet seen a clear turn in it.

Rates & Curve: At the prior close the 2-year yield was 3.52%, the 10-year 4.14%, the 30-year 4.79% and the 3-month bill 3.66% (US Treasury, prior close 2025-12-11). The 10Y−2Y spread is 62 bps, and over 20 observations the 10-year is up 6 bps while the 2-year is down 4 bps, so the spread has widened 10 bps; the curve is “moderately steep, approaching the long-run average” and the move is “steepening (10Y up, 2Y down/flat)” (US Treasury, prior close 2025-12-11). Futures still see two cuts in 2026 even as the Fed projects one. Equities rallied after the cut: the S&P 500 closed at 6,901.00, up 0.73% over 20 sessions, and the Nasdaq 100 at 25,686.69, up 0.66% (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2025-12-11).

New / Most Important: The most important development is the Fed’s return to balance-sheet growth. It will resume net asset purchases of $40 billion in securities this month, and the New York Fed desk plans purchases of more than $54 billion over the next month. Leadership is the second item: the Fed is the most divided it has been in more than six years, and Trump will pick a new Fed chair while a divided board still has a say. Oracle is the third item: its forecasts missed Wall Street targets while spending rose, and its shares slid.

Playbook ✓: Stance: Mixed, with a preference for the front end: the 2-year fell 4 bps over 20 observations while the 10-year rose 6 bps and led the 10Y−2Y widening (US Treasury, prior close 2025-12-11), so own 2-year exposure while cuts remain priced at 3.52%. Avoid long duration: TLT is down 2.14% over 20 sessions and IEF down 0.44% (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2025-12-11). Hedge the AI-capex concentration in the Nasdaq 100 by trimming into strength rather than shorting the index. Trigger to add duration: weaker payrolls or claims that push the January pause odds toward a cut. Trigger to cut exposure: the 10-year rising further while the 2-year holds, which would show the steepening is term premium rather than cut expectations.

🇨🇳 China  Bearish  stimulus promised, not yet visible

Macro Stance: Bearish on domestic demand, with policy support promised but not yet visible in the data. The Shanghai Composite closed at 3,873.32, down 3.88% over 20 sessions, and the Hang Seng at 25,530.51 is down 5.7% over 20 sessions (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2025-12-11). The Politburo made boosting domestic demand the top goal for 2026, and the IMF urged bolder stimulus to rebalance the economy. Trade is the strong leg: the trade surplus topped $1 trillion for the first time. The writer’s reading is that the market is pricing the surplus as evidence of weak domestic demand rather than strength.

PMIs & Activity: The private services PMI slipped to a five-month low in November. Export figures show exports grew 5.9% in November while shipments to the US fell 29%. The IMF raised its China growth forecast to 5% for 2025, citing resilience, even as the IMF urged China to take the ‘brave choice’ of curbing exports and boosting consumption. No December PMI had been released by the cutoff, and no official November activity survey is in the record, so the activity view rests on the private services PMI, the export figures and the trade surplus.

Credit Impulse: Credit is contracting in the sense that matters for the impulse. A Reuters poll expects November bank lending to more than double from October, but the seasonal rebound is a poll, not a print. SCMP commentary describes China’s shrinking credit as a financial reset rather than a collapse, and SocGen sees Chinese bond yields possibly dropping to a record low in 2026. The writer’s reading is that falling yields alongside a low credit impulse are a demand signal, not a stimulus signal. No full-month November financing aggregate is in the record, so the impulse call rests on the poll and the yield forecast.

Property: Property is still in crisis. Beijing will stabilise the market ‘as the crisis deepens’, Vanke is seeking a one-year extension on a second local bond, and a ‘too-big-to-fail’ housing giant has lost state support. Home prices are forecast to fall 3.7% this year and extend declines into 2026, and UBS predicts 2.4 million property foreclosures as loan defaults rise. Chinese officials called for more REITs to steady the sector. The writer’s reading is that the property channel is the main drag on household balance sheets and on the banks that hold developer exposure.

Fiscal, Policy & Trade: Fiscal support is promised: Beijing pledged a fiscal boost next year and recognised a ‘prominent’ imbalance, and the Politburo put domestic demand first. Trade is the counterweight, with the surplus at a record, and Mexico approved tariffs of up to 50% on Chinese goods and others. Beijing is taking that move ‘very seriously’. The US will allow Nvidia H200 chip shipments to China, with a 25% cut to the government. The writer’s reading is that the policy mix is easing at the centre, with trade friction at the edges.

Markets & Flows: The Hang Seng closed at 25,530.51 and the Shanghai Composite at 3,873.32 (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2025-12-11). The CSI 300 A-share ETF ASHR closed at 32.94, down 0.48% over 20 sessions; the China large-cap ETF FXI at 39.19 is down 4.2% and the China internet ETF KWEB at 37.01 is down 5.87%, and USD/CNY closed at 7.0640, down 0.78% over 20 sessions, so the yuan is firmer (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2025-12-11). Headlines split on 2026: JPMorgan sees China and Hong Kong stocks gaining nearly 20% in 2026, and Hong Kong is expected to keep its IPO crown. A Hong Kong IPO debut that dipped 2.6% on a US$383 million listing is the cautionary print. Goldman says the yuan is 25% undervalued on a trade basis.

New / Most Important: The most important item is the combination of record trade and the domestic-demand agenda. The Politburo has made domestic demand the top 2026 goal while the trade surplus has reached a record. The NYT frames it as a second China shock. The writer’s reading is that the 2026 policy test is whether the promised fiscal boost reaches households and property, which has not yet shown up in the data.

Playbook ✓: Stance: Bearish on domestic-demand equities and developers; Mixed on exporters. Underweight Hong Kong and mainland property developers until a property price print turns, and keep China exposure through ASHR only with a stop below its 20-session reference of 33.10 (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2025-12-11). Trigger to turn constructive: the Hang Seng reclaiming its 20-session reference of 27,073.03 (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2025-12-11), which would confirm stabilisation. Trigger to add: a concrete fiscal transfer to households rather than the promised boost alone. ⚠ Divergence: the trade surplus is at a record while the Shanghai Composite and Hang Seng are weaker over 20 sessions, so the export engine is not lifting the equity market or the domestic cycle.

Trigger to add to banks: a hike that does not push long yields up further, which would show the BOJ is controlling the curve. Would change the call: a hike combined with a rising 2-year-equivalent yield, which would hit the Nikkei and the yen together.

The question is which risks are temporary uncertainty and which would become permanent claims on future cash generation. A lower quotation improves the entry only to the extent that it falls more than the expected value of the shareholder's eventual economic interest.

Historical Analog: The analogy is the recurring acquisition trade in which customers welcome a broader product while shareholders debate who pays for it. The first group imagines the combined service; the next group calculates purchase terms, financing and execution. A second wave of retail buying can arrive when a familiar share falls enough to look disconnected from the popularity of its brand. That sequence resembles earlier large-media combinations without requiring an identical operating outcome. The lesson is that consumer enthusiasm is useful evidence about an asset's appeal, while the investment return also depends on the amount and form of capital committed to obtaining it.

Smart Money View: The constructive professional view is that a strong distributor may create value from a broader set of content and customer relationships. The cautious view asks whether the price, debt burden and approval process leave sufficient return for the buyer's existing shareholders. Prefer a position whose valuation can be defended even if the transaction takes longer or delivers less immediate benefit than the most enthusiastic story assumes. Keep room to revise the assessment as terms become clearer. If the core business remains attractive but the acquisition case is uncertain, that argues for measured exposure and a wider range of possible outcomes rather than an all-or-nothing view of the company.

When the Narrative Breaks: The narrative weakens if the buyer's financial commitment expands faster than the plausible benefits, or if uncertainty begins damaging the underlying operating business. A rebound should eventually be supported by a clearer path to value creation, rather than only by relief that the stock has stopped falling. Add after the deal economics and entry valuation become easier to underwrite. Reduce when the position depends on winning the transaction at any price or on an immediate reversal of every recent decline. A household subscription can be valuable to the customer while the company's next capital-allocation decision remains demanding for the shareholder.
