Prefer businesses that can monetize realized demand now, without underwriting an economy-wide property rescue in the purchase price. Credit Impulse: A currency-management intervention is a price and funding measure; its effect on new private borrowing is a different question. The mechanism runs through borrower demand and lender risk appetite, not just the availability of a cheaper funding channel. A small borrower needs an order, expected cash receipts and a viable repayment schedule before another loan becomes productive growth. A bank can have capacity to lend while a developer or household chooses to reduce leverage. That makes the difference between easier financial plumbing and an actual credit impulse decisive for equity selection. Favor businesses with sales funded by the customer’s cash flow; demand a larger valuation discount when projected revenue depends on a progressively easier financing offer. The tripwire is a combination of verified new borrowing and improved repayment quality. Without both, treating every easing headline as a bank-earnings upgrade is too aggressive.

Property: The housing channel remains a separate underwriting question from either holiday spending or currency support. Property affects more than the quoted developer equity. A household weighing a long-lived purchase considers delivery confidence and future income; a supplier considers whether it will collect its receivable; a local business considers whether construction activity will create repeat customers. These are linked through confidence and cash, rather than a single asset-price signal. Avoid making the purchase of a developer share equivalent to buying a national recovery. A specific completion, collection or refinancing improvement can support a trade even while aggregate conditions remain difficult, but its relevance must be tied to that company’s obligations. Conversely, a market rally that leaves the collection schedule unchanged offers less support to the operating thesis than its headline return suggests.

Policy support is most valuable when it bridges a temporary funding problem in a viable activity; it is least useful to equity holders when it merely keeps capital tied up in an uneconomic expansion. Prefer documented customer demand to an assumption that the policy put will keep every producer’s margins intact.
