🧮 Earnings Pattern — What the Market Pays For

Earnings Pattern: the beat is priced, the guide decides

Rewarded: Beats with a forward line were paid. LSEG shares jumped as the exchange group forecast a profitability boost and promised $4 billion of buybacks, and Rolls-Royce's profit jumped nearly 40%. IonQ jumped 22% after an earnings beat, with a 2026 revenue outlook above forecasts. Shake Shack's earnings explosion sent its shares soaring. Canadian banks kept topping expectations: TD's quarterly earnings were lifted by growth across operations, and RBC's by personal banking and markets.

Punished: Beats without a forward raise were not. Nvidia sank 5% on a record quarter, Duolingo tumbled 22% on weak guidance and slowing user growth, CoreWeave slid as its quarterly revenue guidance disappointed, and Intuit's guidance missed estimates. Salesforce crushed earnings by 25% and still cannot get any respect. Duolingo's sales topped estimates, yet its stock still dropped 22.5%, which is the same lesson as the guide: the top line is not the test.

Pattern: The guide is the pricing unit, and the beat is already in the price. Names with AI-supply exposure were sold on the forward line: Nvidia fell on returns and CoreWeave on guidance. Names with a profit trajectory rose: LSEG and Rolls-Royce. Salesforce's $50 billion buyback is controversial, which shows that capital return does not rescue a weak forward line. The card's Nvidia move (down 5.46% in the prior session) is the test case for the pattern. Canadian banks show the opposite pattern: a beat that holds, because the personal-banking and wealth lines lift the outlook.

Attention into EPS: The next tests are set up. Snowflake's beat lost steam after results, although its $7.88 billion revenue backlog is impressive. Dell's AI server guide is the next hardware read on whether the forward line holds up.
