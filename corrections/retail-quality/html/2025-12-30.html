🇨🇳 China  Bearish  easing bias / weak demand

Macro Stance: Bearish on domestic growth, Mixed on markets. The domestic data is weakening: retail sales growth sharply missed estimates in November, even as industrial output grew 4.8% and retail sales rose 1.3%. Reuters says China's economy stalled in November as calls for reform grow. The headline strength is external: the trade surplus topped $1 trillion for the first time, on non-US growth.

PMIs & Activity: The latest official manufacturing reading in the feed is the November factory print, which weakened unexpectedly; the feed carries no December PMI. The private services PMI slipped to a five-month low in November. Industrial profits tumbled at the fastest pace in over a year, and growth engines sputtered as retail and investment slowed. Full-year consumption is still growing, with retail sales up 4% in the first 11 months, so the weakness is in the November run-rate rather than the annual total. A December print that stabilises the factory series would confirm the less bearish reading.

Credit Impulse: Credit is still muted. Bloomberg reports that China’s credit expansion stayed weak as demand languished, and a Reuters poll expected November bank lending to more than double from October, a forecast rather than a print. The PBOC kept its benchmark lending rate steady for a seventh straight month. Beijing is scrubbing small overdue debts from credit records to spur lending and hiding bad-debt records for pandemic-hit borrowers who repay small loans, which is policy acting on the stock of bad loans rather than new demand. Nikkei warns that ballooning debt heightens deflation risks, and consumer inflation was only 0.7% in November.

Property: Property remains the drag. Home prices slid further in November, and a Reuters poll sees home prices falling 3.7% this year and extending declines into 2026. Beijing further eased home-buying curbs and expanded its housing voucher programme amid rising inventories, with city-specific fixes promised. UBS predicts 2.4 million foreclosures as loan defaults rise, and Vanke won approval for a longer grace period on another yuan bond. The open question for 2026 is whether a recovery is possible at all.

Fiscal, Policy & Trade: Policy is incremental. Beijing vowed more efficient fiscal spending to support 2026 growth and will lower import tariffs on some products from 2026, under a green-transformation rationale. On trade, Washington pushed additional tariffs on Chinese chips out to June 2027, and Beijing vowed to retaliate against what it calls unreasonable semiconductor tariffs. Boeing and other defence names were hit by China sanctions. Capital controls tightened at the margin: China will require repatriation of funds raised overseas under new rules. The digital yuan becomes interest-bearing next year, a deposit-deepening move rather than stimulus.

Markets & Flows: Equities are the outperformer. The Shanghai Composite closed at 3,965.28, up 1.31% over 20 sessions and 1.22% over five (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2025-12-29), and Chinese stocks are on track for their best year since 2017. The Hang Seng closed at 25,635.23, down 1.2% over 20 sessions (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2025-12-29), after jumping 30% in 2025. KWEB fell 8.25% over 20 sessions and FXI fell 2.68%, while ASHR rose 0.58% (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2025-12-29). USD/CNY closed at 7.0280, down 0.68% over 20 sessions (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2025-12-29). The yuan moved past 7 per dollar onshore for the first time since 2023, and the PBOC set the fixing at a 15-month high, although it signalled a slow gain.

New / Most Important: The most important item is the yuan. The onshore move past 7 per dollar and state media’s warning against one-way bets on the yuan point to a two-sided currency debate. The PBOC’s message is slow appreciation through the fix. Beijing is also pushing yuan usage: China expanded its digital-yuan push to Singapore and ASEAN trade routes, and the yuan is becoming a funding currency in debt markets. The writer reads a steadier yuan as easing pressure on dollar-denominated debt for Chinese borrowers, which supports the flows narrative.

Playbook ✓: Underweight Chinese domestic cyclicals and property developers; keep a tactical long in the onshore market and hedge through the yuan. Shanghai strength with consumer weakness is the setup: KWEB dropped 8.25% over 20 sessions while Shanghai rose 1.31%. Change the call if the next lending print beats the poll or if retail sales data stops missing estimates. ⚠ Divergence: the onshore rally (Shanghai up 1.31% over 20 sessions, Yahoo Finance, prior close 2025-12-29) runs against the weak November retail and property data, and the Hang Seng’s 20-session decline shows the offshore market does not share the onshore optimism.
