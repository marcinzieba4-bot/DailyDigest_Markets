🇺🇸 United States  Bearish  relief rally / tighter financing

Macro Stance: Sell the assumption that a tariff concession repairs the investment climate. NPR reported that smartphones and computers were spared from Trump's reciprocal tariffs. [1] That is meaningful relief for exposed businesses, but the exception does not settle the wider confrontation. The latest March manufacturing ISM reading was 49 index points and services stood at 50.8 index points, a narrow base on which to absorb an additional policy shock. [2] [3] Reuters also reported that consumer inflation eased ahead of tariffs. [4] Backward-looking disinflation can support bonds only if investors believe the next cost shock is temporary. Businesses deciding whether to order, hire or invest have a harder problem: the tariff that matters is the one they expect to pay when goods arrive, and repeated changes reduce the value of any single announcement. A relief rally is therefore a trading opportunity before it becomes an earnings thesis.

Rates & Curve: The 2Y Treasury yield was 3.96% and the 10Y was 4.48%, with a 52 bps 10Y−2Y spread. Over 20 observations the 2Y fell 6 bps, the 10Y rose 17 bps and the spread widened 23 bps. The curve was positively sloped; its move was steepening (10Y up, 2Y down/flat). Over five observations the 10Y rose 47 bps. That combination matters more than the curve's positive slope: long financing costs are rising while the shorter maturity reflects more growth anxiety. The S&P 500 closed at 5,363.36 index points, up 5.70% over five sessions but down 4.89% over 20 sessions. VIX remained at 37.56 index points and DXY at 99.78 index points. Equity relief alongside a weak dollar and expensive long money is an unstable foundation for broad risk taking. (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2025-04-11; US Treasury, prior close 2025-04-11)

New / Most Important: CNBC described investors' growing concern about a US asset exodus as Treasuries and the dollar declined. [5] That is a warning about the required return on American assets, not just a recession forecast. JPMorgan's quarterly beat demonstrates that a strong franchise can still earn through turbulence; Dimon's warning about considerable turbulence prevents extrapolating that result into the economy. [6] The important test is whether relief in trade policy can lower financing stress as well as lift shares.

Playbook ✓: Keep equity exposure concentrated in businesses that can defend cash generation; hedge broad growth beta on sharp policy rallies and retain short-maturity liquidity. Add cyclical exposure only when credit and long Treasuries stop disagreeing with equities. A durable fall in long yields alongside a steadier dollar and improving company guidance would invalidate the defensive bias. A lower volatility reading by itself would not: protection can become cheaper before the earnings risk has passed.

🇨🇳 China  Bearish  stimulus support / trade fracture

Macro Stance: China's domestic support is improving, but the external shock is becoming harder to hedge with a simple index position. Reuters reported that China raised duties on US goods to 125% and dismissed further American tariff increases. [13] The electronics exemption reported by Reuters softens one part of the confrontation without restoring a predictable trading relationship. [14] A domestic policy response can redistribute demand toward local businesses, yet exporters still face orders, routes and pricing decisions that cannot be solved merely by offering cheaper credit. The bearish stance concerns earnings visibility and offshore risk premia; it does not require every domestic activity measure to weaken. Distinguish the economy's ability to absorb a shock from shareholders' ability to earn a satisfactory return through it.

PMIs & Activity: The March factory survey showed accelerating activity, according to the South China Morning Post. [15] That improvement is a real baseline, but the latest Reuters poll expected first-quarter GDP growth to slow to 5.1%; it was a forecast, not a released GDP result. [16] The timing of orders matters. Production can stay firm while businesses respond to uncertain trade rules, whereas the durability of domestic demand is tested later through consumption and investment. The most useful confirmation would be successive activity readings that remain resilient without relying on exporters bringing orders forward. Avoid treating a single stronger factory survey as evidence that household confidence, property demand and export margins are all healing simultaneously.

Credit Impulse: Reuters reported that March bank lending beat expectations and that more stimulus was expected. [17] More lending offers a buffer against cash-flow disruption and gives borrowers room to refinance. The investment question is where the money lands. Credit that sustains an existing balance sheet can prevent a disorderly contraction without generating new profitable demand. Credit that finances productive domestic investment could create a stronger earnings response. Banks and consumption businesses therefore need different confirmation: the former need acceptable credit quality and loan economics, the latter need customers spending rather than simply being offered borrowing capacity. Add exposure only as activity confirms that the financing impulse is being used, rather than assuming a lending surprise settles the growth argument.

Property: Property remains a separate hurdle. The South China Morning Post reported Pimco's view that China's property crisis was still a key signal for the junk-bond market. [18] Its March account also contrasted a thriving Shanghai luxury segment with deterioration in mass housing. [19] That divergence argues against extrapolating premium transactions into a national balance-sheet repair. A household considering a home purchase faces different incentives from a wealthy buyer seeking scarce prime property. For investors, a stronger luxury pocket is insufficient to validate developers, household consumption and banks at once. Watch for broader demand stability and easier refinancing accompanied by better operating performance. An equity bounce without those changes is vulnerable to another disappointment in the underlying cash cycle.

Fiscal, Policy & Trade: The trade response is increasingly about rerouting demand and securing supplies. Reuters reported that Chinese retail giants pledged to help exporters sell domestically, while rare-earth exports ground to a halt as controls took effect. [20] [21] Selling unwanted export production locally can support volumes but may intensify price competition. Export controls can increase negotiating leverage while disrupting customers and creating incentives to diversify supply. These are separate channels, with different winners. Domestic platforms could gain merchandise and attention without immediately gaining profit; restricted upstream products could become strategically important without making every producer a clean investment. Policy support is most investable when it changes company revenue or costs in a measurable way, rather than merely improving the tone of negotiations.

Shanghai stood at 3,238.23 index points, down 3.11% and 3.59% over those horizons. ASHR finished at USD 25.71, down 7.88% over 20 sessions; KWEB at USD 30.53 was down 16.99%. USD/CNY was 7.3140 yuan per dollar, up 1.06% over 20 sessions. Offshore internet exposure is carrying a much harsher repricing than the mainland composite. The currency is another independent risk, particularly for dollar-based holders assessing apparent local bargains. A stronger prior session does not erase the gap between domestic support and offshore earnings anxiety. (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2025-04-11)

New / Most Important: The consumer remains the decisive bridge between policy and earnings. The Wall Street Journal described consumer prices as continuing to signal weakness; the South China Morning Post reported luxury malls adjusting to lower consumer spending. [22] [23] Those observations complicate the proposed switch from export demand to domestic demand. More credit and more domestic distribution channels can help, but they cannot by themselves create pricing power. Prefer businesses able to retain customers without an escalating subsidy bill. A recovery that requires aggressive discounting may improve reported sales while disappointing shareholders who bought the policy headline.

Playbook ✓: Keep offshore China beta below a neutral allocation and use rebounds to improve the quality of holdings. Choose domestic cash generation over the most dramatic tariff rebound; separate a tactical KWEB trade from a durable allocation to China. Increase risk if household demand, property breadth and credit deployment reinforce each other, or if a credible trade settlement improves earnings visibility. Cut exposure if domestic redirection becomes a margin war or currency weakness overwhelms local equity gains. Do not use an intraday response to exemptions as a substitute for that process. ⚠ Divergence: stronger March lending and factory activity contradict an unqualified collapse narrative, even as trade risk justifies a higher required return.
