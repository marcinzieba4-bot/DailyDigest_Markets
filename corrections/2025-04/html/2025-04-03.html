🌍 Geographic Macro Overview

🇺🇸 United States  Bearish  tariff shock / cash discipline

Macro Stance: The US portfolio should stay defensive after the tariff framework moved from a pending risk into an announced policy shock. NPR reported a sweeping 10% tariff alongside reciprocal tariffs on many countries. [1] The investment issue is no longer simply whether a large announcement will arrive; it is how businesses and trading partners adapt to the costs and uncertainty it creates. Companies can absorb costs, raise prices, alter sourcing or reduce activity, and each route has a different consequence for margins and demand. Avoid assuming that the first response will also be the final economic outcome. The appropriate stance is to preserve flexibility while management teams establish what the new framework means for their customers and cash flows.

Rates & Curve: The 2Y Treasury yield was 3.91%, the 10Y 4.20%, and the 10Y−2Y spread +29 bps. Over 20 observations, both the 2Y and 10Y fell 8 bps, with the spread unchanged. The curve was positively sloped; its move was parallel rally (both down equally; spread unchanged). The S&P 500's completed cash close was 5,670.97 index points, up 0.67% in the prior session and down 2.94% over 20 sessions. VIX was 21.51 index points and DXY 103.81 index points. Those completed levels should anchor risk measurement without being read as a full verdict on the announced policy. (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2025-04-02; US Treasury, prior close 2025-04-02)

New / Most Important: Reuters explicitly reported Wall Street finishing higher while the tariff speech sent futures lower. [2] That sequence matters: the cash-session gain and the later policy response describe different moments. The activity backdrop was already fragile, with March's ISM manufacturing PMI at 49 index points, per PR Newswire's release. [3] A trade-cost shock applied to a contracting manufacturing environment raises the hurdle for cyclical earnings. Reuters' commentary arguing for Fed easing is a policy opinion, not a central-bank commitment. [4] A lower discount rate could help valuations, but it would not erase weaker volumes or an impaired margin structure.

Playbook ✓: Maintain liquid reserves, resilient cash-generative equities and defined-risk protection against a larger adjustment. Reduce exposure where the earnings case requires unchanged global sourcing or unquestioned customer price acceptance. Add broad equity only when companies demonstrate a credible adaptation path and prices begin holding gains across a wider set of businesses. The bearish call would weaken if policy adjustment materially reduces the burden and demand remains resilient. Conversely, weaker activity accompanied by persistent cost pressure would reinforce it. Do not use a familiar prior cash close as a guarantee of executable prices after a major announcement; stage entries and let actual trading conditions establish the next decision point.

🇪🇺 European Union  Bearish  trade shock / selective defence

The allocation should therefore avoid treating a possible ECB response as an automatic offset to weaker corporate earnings. Prefer resilience and clear funded demand over exposure that requires uninterrupted cross-border margins.

Activity & Inflation: S&P Global's March flash eurozone manufacturing improvement remains the latest activity baseline in the recovery debate. Defence and infrastructure can still supply opportunities where spending is funded and deliverable, but broad fiscal ambition should not be booked as immediate private-sector earnings.

Playbook ✓: Keep European exposure selective, underweight autos and other trade-sensitive manufacturers, and retain quality domestic businesses where demand is more visible. Add only if the response to tariffs becomes commercially workable and company guidance supports margin resilience. The bearish stance would improve if disinflation supports spending while exporters demonstrate adaptation. Until then, a low valuation should be paired with a credible earnings floor rather than assumed to provide one.

🇨🇳 China  Mixed  domestic support / export pressure

Macro Stance: China has a domestic policy backstop, but the announced US trade framework makes the external constraint more tangible. BBC summarised the tariffs affecting China, the EU and other trading partners. [9] The useful investment distinction is between businesses that can earn through domestic demand and those whose customer economics depend on cross-border delivery into a changed trade regime. Capital support and liquidity can reduce financial stress without replacing lost export profitability. Keep the stance mixed because policy capacity is meaningful, while the next earnings bridge has become harder. A company should earn a place in the portfolio through its own customers, costs and financing, not solely because the national authorities have the resources to respond.

PMIs & Activity: South China Morning Post reported faster Chinese factory activity in March, a constructive signal before judging the new trade burden. [10] Xinhua's January–February retail-sales increase of 4% provides the consumption baseline. [11] These indicators support a case that domestic measures have reached parts of the economy. They do not establish that every demand channel can absorb an export shock. Production can be supported by advanced orders while final demand remains uncertain, so inventory and revenue quality matter. Investors should look for broad spending resilience and company earnings that rise with sales. A recovery that requires continual acceleration in policy support or export front-loading deserves a lower valuation than one sustained by recurring household and business demand.

Credit Impulse: The bank-capital programme gives the system more capacity to lend and absorb losses. Reuters reported the biggest state banks planning to raise $71.6 billion, alongside an 800 billion yuan March outright-repo injection by the central bank. [12] [13] These are complementary tools, but Reuters also reported banks raising consumer-loan rates as bad-debt risks mounted. [14] That qualification remains central. A lender can be supported financially while deciding that a new loan needs a higher risk price. Watch whether stronger balance sheets produce productive credit and improved borrower cash flow, rather than merely allowing old liabilities to be rolled forward. Credit impulse becomes more useful for equities when it supports sustainable purchases and investment.

Property: Property is still an obstacle to a broad confidence recovery. Reuters described the slump persisting as stimulus struggled to stabilise the sector. [15] South China Morning Post reported developer profits continuing to fall and Shanghai's luxury market doing better while mass housing deteriorated. [16] [17] The premium segment cannot by itself repair ordinary household balance sheets. Country Garden's narrower annual loss is a tentative improvement, as the same outlet reported, but a smaller loss is not yet a durable operating model. [18] Require healthier sales, cash collection and manageable funding before treating a developer rally as proof of a repaired housing channel. The household's willingness to spend depends on more than the banking system's willingness to supply credit.

Fiscal, Policy & Trade: Trade changes are especially relevant to business models built around low-cost direct delivery. Axios reported the closing of a China tariff loophole as a blow to Temu and Shein. [19] Business Insider described the end of the de minimis exemption hurting major Chinese e-commerce sites. [20] These accounts support a concrete market-access risk without requiring an invented estimate of revenue lost. The path to adaptation could include sourcing changes, different fulfilment or altered customer prices, each with a cost. For technology, Reuters reported Chinese companies ordering $16 billion of new Nvidia chips, citing The Information. [21] That demonstrates reported demand for a specific input, not a guarantee of immediate delivery or profit for every company in the chain.

ASHR was USD 26.45, down 2.11% over 20 sessions, and KWEB USD 34.96, down 4.69%. USD/CNY was CNY 7.2697 per USD. These different exposures show why broad mainland stability should not be used to infer the same return for offshore internet shares. (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2025-04-02) Keep currency, customer demand and the price paid for growth as separate decisions. A stable domestic index does not by itself establish improved financing or profitable customer adoption for an offshore technology business.

New / Most Important: Alibaba's planned Qwen3 release is a pending domestic technology catalyst, with South China Morning Post reporting it was coming during the month, citing sources. [22] Reuters' earlier account likewise described preparation for a flagship model release. [23] Keep the distinction between a planned product and demonstrated earnings. The strongest investment outcome would be improved adoption and monetisation without spending that overwhelms the benefit. A successful launch can strengthen a franchise while competition still limits the profit retained. Investors should also distinguish Alibaba's domestic platform opportunity from the cross-border tariff exposure of other e-commerce models rather than treating the entire internet basket as one uniform business.

Playbook ✓: Hold measured mainland and internet exposure where policy support and company economics can be assessed together. Add when domestic spending and sustainable profit growth confirm the case; avoid buying an export-sensitive name solely because it has already fallen. Keep property-linked exposure at a higher hurdle until ordinary demand and cash collection improve. Reduce risk if trade-cost pressure overwhelms domestic support or if lending caution spreads into weaker household activity. The mixed stance can become more constructive when the earnings evidence broadens beyond product excitement and state-backed financing. ⚠ Divergence: stronger factory activity and substantial bank support coexist with persistent housing weakness and a more difficult external trading framework, so market stability is not yet the same as broad economic repair.
