🇺🇸 United States  Bearish  credit stress / selective defense

Macro Stance: The equity market has become steadier without becoming safer. A smaller index decline is welcome, but financing conditions are now giving a more troubling signal. Reuters reported US credit spreads continuing to widen with no new bonds announced. [1] That is a direct constraint on the corporate response to tariffs: a company needing to finance inventory, relocate production or refinance debt cannot rely on the equity market's mood alone. Reuters also reported Dimon warning about the economic effects of the trade conflict and a possible recession. [2] The bearish stance rests on this deterioration in the bridge from policy uncertainty to private cash flow, rather than on a prediction that every session must fall sharply.

Rates & Curve: The 2Y Treasury yield closed at 3.73% and the 10Y at 4.15%, leaving a 42 bps 10Y−2Y spread. Over 20 observations the 2Y fell 16 bps and the 10Y fell 7 bps; the curve is positively sloped with bull steepening (both down, 2Y down more). VIX remained elevated at 46.98 index points and DXY stood at 103.26 index points. (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2025-04-07; US Treasury, prior close 2025-04-07) The more stable equity close sits beside a less comfortable bond market. Bloomberg.com described turbulence that lifted the long Treasury yield sharply. [3] A portfolio cannot assume that duration will absorb every additional equity shock when inflation uncertainty and the need for liquidity are also moving yields.

New / Most Important: The labor cushion is still meaningful: CNBC reported March payrolls rising 228,000 while unemployment reached 4.2%. [4] Yet Powell's warning that tariffs would raise inflation and slow growth, reported by Reuters, leaves the policy response constrained. [5] The latest market swings also demonstrate the sensitivity to a possible change in trade policy. NPR reported a false social-media post about a tariff pause producing enormous price swings. [6] The event matters because the market is trying to price a policy path that remains uncertain; it does not establish that the desired pause occurred. Treat a rumor-driven rally as evidence of sensitivity, rather than evidence that earnings have improved.

Playbook ✓: Retain a defensive allocation with more emphasis on cash and shorter-duration protection than on a large unhedged long-bond position. Favor recurring demand and strong funding, and keep speculative rebounds small. Add equities when credit issuance resumes, spreads stabilize and company demand survives the trade shock. A durable agreement that improves market access would help, but require commercial detail. If equities bounce while credit and government bonds weaken, reduce gross risk rather than interpreting the index move as an all-clear. The bearish call would soften with improving financing and operating visibility together, not simply a calmer closing print.

🇨🇳 China  Bearish  state support / trade pressure

Macro Stance: China's equity shock has now produced an identifiable state support response. Reuters reported a sovereign fund stepping in as shares plunged on the trade conflict. [12] That can improve market liquidity and confidence, but it does not directly repair an exporter's order book or a household's income outlook. Keep the distinction between support for prices and support for profits central to the allocation. Reuters also reported Trump threatening further increases in tariffs on China. [13] The threat is a pending escalation, not a completed additional levy. It makes a quick normalization of trade a risky assumption and raises the value of businesses whose customers and financing are primarily domestic.

PMIs & Activity: The latest March factory survey had shown accelerating activity, according to the South China Morning Post. [14] The earlier January-February releases also showed retail sales picking up, per Reuters, and industrial production rising 5.9%, per Global Times. [15] [16] These are genuine domestic supports, but they precede the newest trade shock. Reuters's report of falling industrial profits in January-February remains a warning that higher output does not automatically mean a stronger shareholder return. [17] The next confirmation should be resilient final demand and better pricing, rather than production alone. If factories preserve volumes through discounts or inventory building, activity can look stronger than the cash-flow outlook. Keep growth analysis tied to orders, consumption and housing rather than a metal price or a policy slogan.

Credit Impulse: Credit policy is attempting to reduce friction for consumers and small businesses. Reuters reported lenders being asked to raise consumer-loan credit limits, while the State Council Information Office described improved small-business financing services. [18] [19] Easier access is useful, but demand for a loan still depends on the expected return from spending or investing. A firm facing uncertain export access may borrow to preserve liquidity rather than expand capacity. A household worried about its balance sheet may prefer saving even when credit is available. Watch whether financing creates transactions and investment with acceptable economics. A larger policy channel can stabilize conditions without producing a rapid consumption acceleration, and banks need to preserve underwriting standards while supporting that adjustment.

Property: Property remains a separate constraint on the domestic recovery. Reuters described a persistent slump despite stimulus, while the South China Morning Post reported another month of falling home prices. [20] [21] Weak housing wealth can make consumers cautious even when wage income or credit access improves. Bloomberg.com reported banks increasing disposals of troubled property loans. [22] That is a balance-sheet repair mechanism, not proof that households have regained confidence in new purchases. A durable improvement requires completed sales, reliable delivery and price stability, with developers able to fund the work. Avoid assuming a stock-market support operation can substitute for those conditions. Financial confidence can help, but the housing channel must ultimately be repaired through actual transactions and delivery.

Fiscal, Policy & Trade: The trade conflict now includes strategic inputs as well as finished goods. Reuters reported export controls on key rare earths. [23] That can increase negotiating leverage and create opportunities for alternative suppliers, while making manufacturers more cautious about sourcing and capital commitments. Reuters's earlier report on disruption to China's rare-earth trade from Myanmar adds another supply complication. [24] For domestic policy, the commercial challenge is to support demand without pushing companies into deeper price competition. Reuters described restaurants racing downward on prices in a deflation-hit economy. [25] Volume growth achieved through discounting can help consumers while leaving businesses with limited capacity to invest. Prefer firms with demonstrable customer value and financial flexibility, rather than assuming strategic importance guarantees pricing power.

The South China Morning Post reported authorities choosing currency stability over devaluation. [26] Treat that as the reported policy preference, while retaining a currency risk budget rather than assuming the exchange rate cannot move.

New / Most Important: The most important signal is the coexistence of market support and unresolved commercial risk. A sovereign-fund purchase can reduce disorderly selling, yet companies still need customers willing to commit capital under uncertain tariffs. Fitch's China rating downgrade, reported by Reuters, adds a funding-quality concern to the policy debate. [27] The practical opportunity lies in businesses able to turn domestic support into profitable receipts. A company can be important to industrial policy and still earn a poor return on heavy investment; a consumer business can gain transactions and still lose margin through competition. Require operating evidence to separate those cases before increasing broad exposure.

Playbook ✓: Keep broad China beta below neutral and prefer small, selective domestic allocations over a trade-truce forecast. Add when activity remains resilient after escalation and profits begin to validate the demand story. Market support is a helpful stabilizer, but property and household confidence need their own confirmation. Reduce if threats become broader restrictions or if easier credit fails to improve commercial demand. Use ASHR and KWEB according to their different exposures rather than treating either as a pure policy hedge. ⚠ Divergence: the state's direct support for shares and the earlier improvement in factory activity challenge the bearish stance; a durable stabilization that reaches orders and earnings would require rebuilding risk.
