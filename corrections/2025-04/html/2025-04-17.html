Credit Impulse: The credit impulse is a question of transmission and borrower willingness. Reuters reported that China had asked lenders to raise consumer-loan credit limits; the State Council Information Office described improved credit financing services for small businesses. [15] [16] Those measures improve access, but access is not the same as profitable demand. Bloomberg's account of a shadow-bank collapse and deepening wealth destruction points to the counterweight: losses in existing savings products can weaken the appetite to borrow even when official channels become more accommodating. [17] Favor businesses that receive actual customer cash over those whose bullish case rests entirely on a larger theoretical lending envelope.

Property: Property is stabilizing unevenly. Reuters reported that home prices halted their decline in March while pressures remained. [18] That is useful evidence against an uninterrupted deterioration story, but a halt in falling prices does not establish a broad revival in turnover, developer funding or household confidence. [19] Property policy matters because households' willingness to spend depends partly on whether their largest asset stops feeling like a source of losses. For equity exposure, prefer operating businesses that can benefit from stabilization without requiring a speculative developer rerating. The confirming combination would be durable transaction demand and cleaner financing, rather than a short burst of optimism around official support.

[22] [23] Treat those developments as an avenue for negotiation, not an agreement. Domestic substitution could help selected Chinese suppliers, but substitution also comes with implementation costs and a risk of restricted access to the most capable tools. A political narrative about self-sufficiency is not a substitute for customer qualification and realized revenue.
