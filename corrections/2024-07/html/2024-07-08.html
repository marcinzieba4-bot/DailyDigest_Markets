🇺🇸 United States  Bullish  Cooling labor, conditional easing

Macro Stance: The US deserves a selective bullish bias because cooling activity can lower the discount rate before it destroys the earnings base. CNBC reported that June payrolls increased by 206,000 while unemployment rose to 4.1%; Reuters described a labor market losing steam. That combination supports a gradual easing argument, but it also means revenue assumptions require more scrutiny. Employment growth remains positive; the deterioration is in the balance of risks rather than proof of a completed recession. [1] [2] The distinction matters for portfolio construction: profitable businesses can benefit from lower yields while businesses dependent on an immediate demand rebound still disappoint. Equity duration and economic cyclicality should therefore be evaluated separately. The Dallas Fed described an immigration surge supporting job growth and output, another reason payroll gains and a looser labor market can coexist. [3] Labor supply can ease wage pressure without an immediate collapse in demand. The bullish interpretation weakens if hiring loses momentum faster than improved supply can support productive capacity.

Rates & Curve: The 2Y Treasury yield is 4.60% and the 10Y is 4.28%, leaving the 10Y–2Y spread at -32 bps. Over twenty observations, the 2Y fell 12 bps and the 10Y fell 1 bp; the spread increased 11 bps. The curve remains inverted, with bull steepening (both down, 2Y down more) describing the move (US Treasury, prior close 2024-07-05). This favors some sensitivity to easing at the front end, without establishing an unrestricted long-duration trade. The long end has barely moved across that window, so fiscal supply, inflation uncertainty and term compensation can still frustrate an investor who treats every weaker release as a parallel fall in yields.

New / Most Important: The activity warning is broadening. The services ISM reading was 48.8 index points, while Reuters reported sinking service-sector orders; manufacturing ISM was 48.5 index points. These are reasons to test sales expectations rather than extrapolate index strength into the whole economy. [4] [5] [6] The S&P 500 closed at 5,567.19 index points, up +3.98% over twenty sessions, while VIX stood at 12.48 index points and DXY at 104.88 index points (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2024-07-05). Low implied equity volatility offers little reassurance about earnings breadth. It indicates the price of protection, not the absence of an economic disagreement.

Playbook ✓: Hold profitable technology and quality balance sheets, and add cyclical exposure only when order or guidance evidence improves. The trigger for a broader allocation is softer inflation accompanied by stable demand; the trigger for cutting cyclical risk is further order weakness coupled with disappointing corporate revenue. CNBC reported that the Fed still wants greater confidence in disinflation before cutting, so easing remains conditional rather than an accomplished policy change. [7] Retain a modest duration hedge, but reduce it if long yields rise despite weaker activity. That divergence would make fiscal or inflation compensation more important than the near-term policy path.

🇨🇳 China  Mixed  Targeted support, weak transmission

Macro Stance: China remains a selective company market rather than a convincing broad reflation allocation. The strongest evidence is asymmetric: exporters and technology producers can show industrial capability while domestic households and property remain less reliable sources of final demand. Reuters' trade report described solid export growth with slower imports tempering the outlook. That difference matters because external shipments can support factory cash flow without demonstrating an equally healthy domestic spending cycle. [14] A broad bullish allocation requires those channels to converge. Until then, prefer businesses with clear demand, cash generation and manageable trade exposure over companies whose appeal rests chiefly on the expectation of another policy announcement.

PMIs & Activity: Global factory surveys delivered a mixed June performance, according to Reuters. That prevents a simple claim that the world manufacturing cycle is uniformly accelerating into Chinese capacity. [15] The relevant investment question is whether production creates profitable sales or merely shifts excess supply into export markets. Bloomberg's report that Chinese beef prices were plunging as appetite for premium meat wavered gives a separate household-demand warning. [16] A lower input price may help a processor, yet it can also reflect consumers trading down. Compare consumer volumes, pricing and margins rather than assuming that cheaper goods automatically produce stronger aggregate earnings. The next activity readings need to show broader domestic participation before industrial strength becomes a complete growth thesis.

Credit Impulse: Credit policy faces a transmission problem: money can be available without households wishing to borrow or private firms wishing to invest. The Wall Street Journal reported that the central bank borrowed government bonds, while Global Times described analysts interpreting the borrowing as a move to stabilize markets and expectations. [17] [18] Bond-market management and credit demand are different channels. A policy designed to influence yields should not be described as evidence that loan growth or private investment has already improved. The important trigger is credit reaching productive borrowers and generating cash receipts. If liquidity instead remains concentrated in financial assets, lower market yields can coexist with weak domestic confidence and limited earnings revisions.

Property: The property evidence is deliberately two-sided. The South China Morning Post reported a surge in second-hand home sales following incentives; Global Times separately described signs of an uptick under policy support. [19] [20] Those reports can improve transaction confidence, but resale turnover does not automatically finance unfinished developments or restore new-home margins. The Guardian's earlier report of new-home prices falling at their fastest pace in nearly a decade remains a relevant macro baseline. [21] Higher turnover at lower prices can clear inventories while still damaging collateral values. Prefer evidence of completed homes, developer cash collection and stable household expectations before assuming that a transaction rebound will lift construction demand and local-government finances together.

Fiscal, Policy & Trade: Trade policy is becoming an obstacle to relying on exports to absorb domestic spare capacity. Reuters reported that China-built electric vehicles were hit with European duties, then described Beijing stepping up pressure through a brandy-probe hearing as those tariffs began. [22] [23] This is a cross-sector risk: an automotive dispute can reach luxury and agricultural supply chains without affecting every company equally. The South China Morning Post also reported Indonesian plans for high import tariffs on Chinese products. [24] Export diversification is therefore not a frictionless answer. The investment filter should examine destination markets, local manufacturing options and the ability to absorb duties, rather than treating overseas sales as a universally safer replacement for domestic demand.

New / Most Important: Fiscal repair is inseparable from housing. The Peterson Institute reported that local governments' reliance on land revenue was dropping as the property downturn dragged on. [26] Reduced reliance may sound like diversification, but it does not by itself show that replacement revenue has arrived. Infrastructure promises need a financing mechanism and projects need a path to payment. Meanwhile, CNBC's account of population aging and shrinkage highlights a structural constraint on extrapolating past housing demand. [27] These pressures favor productivity, services and targeted household confidence measures over an investment thesis that assumes property can simply resume its old role. For equities, the distinction is between policy beneficiaries with collectible revenue and enterprises asked to expand capacity without satisfactory returns.

Playbook ✓: Keep broad China exposure small and select cash-generative businesses with defensible domestic franchises or adaptable overseas production. Add property-sensitive materials only when transactions improve alongside pricing and developer cash collection; add exporters when tariff costs can be offset without sacrificing margins. Reuters reported SAIC seeking a European Commission hearing, underscoring that commercial outcomes remain negotiable rather than settled. [28] Trim positions if retaliation spreads or if a transaction rebound fails to stabilize collateral. ⚠ Divergence: property incentives are improving turnover, while weak pricing, household caution and expanding trade restrictions still challenge the assumption that policy support will translate into a broad profit recovery.
