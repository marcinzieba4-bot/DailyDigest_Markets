🇺🇸 United States  Mixed  Powell opens the easing path; demand remains the test

Macro Stance: Powell has supplied a clearer policy direction: Reuters reports him saying the time has come to cut rates. [1] The portfolio problem now shifts from waiting for the speech to deciding which assets can turn easier expected financing into sustained earnings. A broad risk rally is compatible with that signal, but it does not identify whether future cuts will mainly accompany successful disinflation or lost demand. CNBC’s payroll revision remains a warning about the strength of the labour-market starting point. [2] Keep equity participation, but make businesses with fragile cash generation demonstrate more than a lower discount rate. The constructive case is policy relief arriving while customers remain able to spend, rather than relief arriving after operating demand has already deteriorated.

Rates & Curve: The 2Y Treasury yield finished at 3.90% and the 10Y at 3.81%, with a -9 bps 10Y–2Y spread. Across twenty observations their changes were -46 bps and -39 bps respectively; the spread moved +7 bps. The front-end response is consistent with a stronger easing narrative, while continued inversion warns against equating that repricing with a completed cycle repair. Duration and cyclical equities can initially benefit together, yet their hedging roles diverge if employment and customer activity weaken.

New / Most Important: S&P 500 ended its completed session at 5,634.61 index points, returning +1.15% in the session, +1.45% over five sessions and +3.21% over twenty sessions (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2024-08-23). The latest gain and lower volatility favour participation, while the positive five-session VIX change cautions that uncertainty has not disappeared across the entire recent period. Reuters reports the dollar falling after Powell’s easing signal. [3] That directional currency development can help international financial conditions, but companies still need profitable demand. Nvidia’s pending report, identified by Reuters as a major AI-trade test, now competes with the Fed narrative for market attention. [4]

Playbook ✓: Use the rally to rebalance toward durable earnings rather than finance every potential beneficiary of cuts. Add domestic cyclicals when new labour and company-demand evidence remain consistent with an orderly easing path. Retain duration if weaker activity instead becomes the reason for cuts, but reduce cyclical concentration. Cut duration if price pressure prevents the expected policy follow-through. For technology, keep the pending earnings hurdle independent of the macro signal: a dovish Fed cannot establish product delivery or customer monetisation. Raise the total risk budget only after policy implementation and forward earnings begin to reinforce one another.

🇪🇺 European Union  Mixed  A services cushion with an industrial hurdle

Macro Stance: The European easing argument has a wage signal alongside a services impulse. Paperjam.lu reports slower euro-area wage growth and stronger expectations of an ECB cut; The Guardian reports an Olympics-related economic boost. [5] [6] The combination is potentially favourable for domestic demand, but the two developments have different persistence. Slower wage growth can ease inflation concern while also limiting the growth of household income; event spending can raise activity without becoming a recurring source of sales. A regional allocation should therefore favour companies with ordinary repeat business rather than capitalise an exceptional event indefinitely.

Activity & Inflation: Lower inflation pressure would help the financing case, yet manufacturing and autos still need a customer-demand test. CNBC reports Ford defending a costly change in EV strategy, while Reuters describes BMW taking a European EV-sales lead over Tesla. [7] [8] These contrasting company developments show that product positioning and capital discipline remain decisive inside a common policy environment. An easier rate outlook may support vehicle affordability, but does not settle the return on an individual manufacturer’s investment.

New / Most Important: DW.com describes Germany’s regional elections as a threat to the governing coalition. [9] This is a pending political-risk channel, not an election outcome. For investors, the issue is whether uncertainty delays decisions or changes confidence in implementation. It should sit beside earnings evidence rather than replace it. Do not assume either coalition stability or a specific eventual policy response when valuing future public or private projects.

Playbook ✓: Prefer repeat domestic revenue and selective industrial exposure. Add exporters when customer orders validate improving financing conditions; trim when a production or investment plan lacks profitable demand. Broader wage disinflation with resilient consumption would justify widening the regional allocation. Renewed price pressure or slower demand without adequate cost flexibility would invalidate the constructive easing case.

🇨🇳 China  Mixed  Market access improves before the domestic repair is complete

Macro Stance: Alibaba’s listing-status announcement offers an access catalyst while the domestic economy still needs a demand catalyst. South China Morning Post reports the company gaining as it changes its Hong Kong listing status to seek mainland funds. TrendForce reports unchanged loan prime rates despite continuing July activity pressure. [10] [11] Those developments should not be merged into one broad recovery claim. A company becoming easier for a particular investor base to own can improve its valuation opportunity without raising household spending. Keeping lending benchmarks steady also leaves the existing transmission problem in place. Policy expectations abroad can help the risk premium without repairing every Chinese balance sheet.

PMIs & Activity: Reuters’ weak factory-PMI account remains a useful activity baseline. AFR now reports China halting new steel plants as the industry faces a crisis. [12] [13] A restriction on additional capacity can eventually improve supply discipline, but it does not itself prove that existing output has found more profitable customers. Reducing overcapacity differs from restoring customer demand. For steel-linked businesses, stronger cash generation would need a better balance of capacity, pricing and orders; for the wider economy, services and household purchases supply different evidence. Prefer direct customer indicators to inferring growth from a commodity bounce. A capacity response is constructive when it prevents further deterioration, yet the equity upgrade still needs a credible return on existing assets.

Credit Impulse: Reuters reports Chinese bank profit growth slowing as loan rates fall, and central-bank warnings about long-dated bonds aimed at limiting systemic risk. [14] [15] The policy constraint is therefore not simply the availability of a cheaper benchmark. Bank earnings can come under pressure while borrowers receive relief, and demand for safe duration can create its own concentration problem. A stronger credit thesis requires viable borrowers willing to invest, rather than only lenders willing to refinance. Distinguish preserved financial stability from an accelerating private investment cycle. For bank exposure, require evidence that asset quality and margins can tolerate the adjustment; for borrowers, require projects whose operating returns exceed financing and execution costs. Easier global conditions do not erase these local trade-offs.

Property: Fortune reports Kaisa reaching a deal for partial repayment while facing a liquidation threat. South China Morning Post describes luxury-property demand resisting the wider home-sales slump. [16] [17] A distressed liability agreement and a resilient high-end niche supply different kinds of support. Neither alone establishes an ordinary household purchase recovery. The housing thesis should therefore follow completion, customer cash collection and solvency rather than infer a broad rebound from the strongest segment. A developer’s equity may remain sensitive to the terms of restructuring even when transactions improve. A supplier with diversified customers can offer a less binary exposure, provided its own receivables and working capital remain manageable. Broader private buying with less dependence on repeated incentives would be the more persuasive invalidation of this cautious view.

Fiscal, Policy & Trade: Reuters reports China’s dairy investigation in response to European EV tariffs and discussions with automakers about potential large-car import tariffs. [18] [19] The widening product scope makes geographic revenue exposure more useful than a narrow sector label. A company may have a strong product yet face an earnings risk from market access; a domestic competitor may gain relative position but still lack pricing power. Analyse what an implemented measure would do to realised margins and required investment. Negotiation and retaliation intentions are relevant to valuation before becoming a completed trade cost. Avoid treating either the first tariff headline or a company-specific exception as the final settlement. Profitable customer diversification would be a stronger reason to add exposure.

Access-sensitive exposure still depends on potential buyers choosing to allocate; an easier route to ownership does not guarantee immediate demand. Add domestic cyclicals when orders and collections improve alongside capacity discipline. Cut leveraged property risk if restructuring merely extends obligations without better operating cash. For trade-sensitive firms, require customer diversification that preserves margins. A broadening across ordinary housing, household spending and private investment would overturn the cautious stance; a stronger offshore benchmark alone would not. Preserve liquidity for policy-driven repricing while keeping company underwriting separate. ⚠ Divergence: Alibaba’s access change and successful consumer launches can support selected assets while onshore weakness, steel adjustment, developer distress and bank-margin pressure still point to an incomplete domestic repair. Keep supplier and lender exposure separate when the same housing environment can create different earnings outcomes.

Keep security-sensitive exposure liquid enough to absorb a risk-premium shock.
