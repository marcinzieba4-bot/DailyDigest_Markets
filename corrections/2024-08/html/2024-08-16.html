🇺🇸 United States  Bullish  disinflation / resilient spending

Macro Stance: The investable combination is improving inflation with a consumer who is still willing to spend. CNBC reports that annual inflation slowed to 2.9% in July and that retail sales rose 1%, beating expectations. [1] [2] That argues for selective equity exposure rather than a wholesale recession retreat. Cooling prices can improve households' purchasing power without requiring companies to surrender every source of growth. The qualification is composition: spending at a successful value retailer does not establish an equally healthy market for expensive renovations or discretionary services. Own businesses that can win customers while protecting the cash generated by those sales, and avoid using the aggregate spending result as permission to ignore their individual demand signals.

Rates & Curve: The official Treasury curve still points to policy restraint. The 2Y closed at 4.08% and the 10Y at 3.92%, leaving the 10Y−2Y spread at −16 bps. Over twenty observations, the 2Y fell 38 bps and the 10Y fell 28 bps: the curve is inverted, with bull steepening (both down, 2Y down more). The S&P 500 closed at 5,543.22 index points, VIX at 15.23 index points and DXY at 102.98 index points (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2024-08-15).

New / Most Important: The strongest confirmation comes from the interaction of consumption and credit. Reuters reports that US corporate bond spreads recovered on promising economic data, while CNBC says Walmart beat estimates and raised its outlook. [3] [4] Better confidence in borrowers alongside a better sales outlook is more useful than an equity rally alone. Yet Reuters' July employment report described a sharp slowdown in job growth and unemployment at 4.3%. [5] The economy can remain resilient while the margin for error narrows. Treat the spending result as evidence against immediate collapse, not a guarantee that employment will stop weakening or that every consumer business will recover together.

Playbook ✓: Keep a profitable equity core, favour businesses with visible demand and retain a measured Treasury hedge. Add to cyclical exposure when subsequent orders and hiring support the resilience suggested by spending; reduce that exposure if weaker employment begins to show up in revenues and credit quality together. The New York Times reports that the Fed opened the door to a September rate cut if inflation stays cool, which leaves easing conditional rather than promised. [6] Do not require an emergency-sized policy response for the portfolio to work. The better position is one that benefits from gradually easier financing while retaining enough cash generation to withstand a less generous demand environment.

🇨🇳 China  Bearish  weak demand / selective earnings

Macro Stance: China's broad demand recovery remains a difficult equity thesis, even where individual companies can deliver. WSJ describes fresh data showing the economy stuck in the doldrums, while Reuters reports that faltering growth has revived discussion of cash vouchers. [13] [14] That debate matters because it concerns the recipient of policy support. Cheaper funding can help a lender or a producer without persuading a household to buy an expensive product. Direct support for consumption would address a different part of the transmission chain. Until demand becomes more dependable, prefer businesses whose earnings can improve through their own product cycle, distribution or cost discipline rather than a blanket assumption of nationwide reflation.

PMIs & Activity: Reuters' latest factory-output reporting describes slowing production and dashed hopes of a speedy recovery; the South China Morning Post characterises July activity as mixed. [15] [16] Mixed activity should not be flattened into a single bullish or bearish number. A manufacturer can increase physical output while competing more aggressively on price, leaving shareholders with less profit than the volume suggests. A service business can grow without repairing the economics of a property developer. The earlier Reuters assessment of a weak factory PMI identifies pain points even within the export engine. [17] Read the next improvement through orders, pricing and customer demand, rather than treating production alone as proof that excess capacity has cleared.

Credit Impulse: New borrowing is a more demanding test of confidence than an announcement of available liquidity. Reuters reports that new loans fell to a fifteen-year low in July; CNBC describes bond-market intervention as revealing financial-stability worries. [18] [19] Weak loan creation can coexist with abundant interest in relatively defensive government bonds. It does not follow that a lower bond yield is translating into more productive private investment. If borrowers remain cautious, banks can compete for the same pool of acceptable assets rather than create a new spending cycle. The investment trigger is stronger demand for credit alongside credible repayment capacity, not simply a more forceful attempt to influence the yield on existing securities.

Property: Housing still interrupts the route from policy support to household confidence. Reuters says the home-price slump has deepened despite stimulus; Bloomberg describes a large stock of unbuilt homes threatening to prolong the crisis. [20] [21] Falling collateral values can make buyers cautious even when mortgage financing becomes cheaper. Incomplete homes raise a separate question about delivery and confidence in the developer. A rescue mechanism needs to improve those practical outcomes before it becomes an earnings catalyst for the wider economy. The South China Morning Post reports millionaire buyers upgrading to premium homes in Shanghai despite the slump. [22] That pocket of activity is a useful counterpoint, but it cannot carry a nationwide housing-recovery argument by itself.

Fiscal, Policy & Trade: Policy is becoming more selective at the same time that trade restrictions are becoming more intrusive. Reuters reports additional funding for disaster-hit agricultural regions and planned restrictions on antimony exports. [23] [24] The former addresses a particular economic disruption; the latter concerns control over a critical mineral. Neither should be translated mechanically into a broad corporate-profit forecast. For an exporter, access to customers and inputs can matter as much as domestic financing. For a household-facing business, targeted public spending is helpful only if it reaches spending power and confidence. Prefer a company with an identifiable customer and a credible route to market over an undifferentiated policy-beneficiary basket whose members face very different commercial constraints.

CNBC reports that Michael Burry made Chinese internet companies his largest bets last quarter. [25] A named investor's disclosed portfolio is a specific observation, not evidence that all institutional capital shares the view or that domestic demand has recovered.

New / Most Important: The earnings contrast is more useful than a broad country slogan. Bloomberg.com says Tencent's earnings beat has encouraged hopes for a gaming rebound; Reuters says weak Chinese spending weighed on Alibaba as it missed estimates. [26] [27] These businesses need different operating tests. A gaming or advertising improvement can reward product execution even when consumers remain cautious elsewhere. An e-commerce platform needs spending, merchant economics and competitive intensity to work together. Do not infer that strength in a digital franchise makes all China internet exposure interchangeable. The better opportunity may be a particular earnings stream, while the larger domestic-consumption thesis still requires confirmation from a broader set of companies.

Playbook ✓: Keep broad China exposure restrained and make additions through companies with evidence of their own earnings improvement. A convincing turn would combine better household demand, more willing borrowing and a less damaging property adjustment. Reduce exposure if a price rebound runs ahead of those operating signals or if trade restrictions impair the route to customers. Reuters' report that Carlsberg shares hit a prolonged low on a gloomy Chinese beer outlook adds an external company's demand warning to the domestic picture.
