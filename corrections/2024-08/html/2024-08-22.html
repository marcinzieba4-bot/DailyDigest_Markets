🇨🇳 China  Bearish  Bank transmission weakens as product demand stays selective

Macro Stance: Bearish on the broad domestic cycle, selective where a concrete product wins demand. The new policy-market tension is more explicit: Reuters reports the regulator saying bank profit growth is slowing as loan interest rates fall. [13] Cheaper financing for a customer is not automatically better earnings for the lender. If lower pricing is not accompanied by healthier loan demand or improved credit quality, the bank gives up income without receiving enough compensating growth. Reuters also reports warnings on long-dated bonds aimed at systemic risk. [14] Those warnings concern financial stability, not a promise of a particular yield path. A policy framework can seek to support activity while limiting the market's most aggressive expression of pessimism about it.

PMIs & Activity: The most useful activity question is where the marginal customer resides. ABC reports BlueScope warning that large amounts of Chinese steel are entering Australia and other markets; the South China Morning Post describes cement producers looking to African demand to offset problems at home. [15] [16] These are operating responses to a difficult domestic environment. Export volume may help utilisation without preserving the same margin once freight and competition are considered. A foreign market's willingness to buy at a discounted price is different from a strong domestic order book. It is therefore important to inspect selling terms, payment and working capital, rather than assume that production redirected abroad represents a clean improvement in the cycle. The output and the residual cash can move differently.

Credit Impulse: TrendForce reports unchanged loan prime rates despite continuing pressure in July economic data. [17] Combine that with the regulator's warning about bank profit growth, rather than assuming a new price cut must immediately solve demand. The investment question is which borrower has a viable use for credit and which lender can supply it on terms that preserve its own resilience. Reuters' report of discussion of cash vouchers identifies a possible more direct consumption channel, not an implemented national transfer. [18] The distinction is causal: a lower borrowing cost changes the price of an obligation, while household support can change spending capacity. Neither can be assumed to overcome uncertainty about employment or property wealth without evidence that customers actually transact.

Property: Fortune reports Kaisa agreeing to repay part of its debt while confronting a liquidation threat. [19] That is progress within a specific restructuring, not proof of a functioning national developer-financing model. Bloomberg describes faster new-home price declines after relaxed curbs, and the South China Morning Post reports luxury-property resilience against the broader sales slump. [20] [21] Those observations can coexist: affluent demand can be active while the mass market finds a lower clearing price. For a developer or creditor, the relevant test is whether receipts support completion, debt service and a viable residual claim. For a household, cheaper entry does not necessarily offset weaker employment confidence or lower proceeds from an existing property. Avoid treating a selected premium sale as the balance sheet of the representative buyer.

Fiscal, Policy & Trade: China has opened an anti-subsidy investigation into EU dairy imports, Reuters reports; CNBC says Beijing calls the EV tariffs unfair and promises necessary measures to defend its industry. [8] [22] The investigation has begun, while its findings and any eventual remedy remain unresolved. That distinction should govern position size. The New York Times describes businesses preparing for another phase of US–China trade conflict. [23] Preparation may shift sourcing and inventories before a new policy outcome. A producer can gain one market while encountering a barrier in another, so geographical diversification needs to be profitable as well as broad. The latest measures do not justify assuming that every exporter receives state protection on commercially favourable terms, or that a policy dispute produces the same burden for producers and consumers.
