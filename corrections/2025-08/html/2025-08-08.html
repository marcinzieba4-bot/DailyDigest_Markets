🇺🇸 United States  Mixed  easing support / tariff uncertainty

Macro Stance: The US market is trying to turn a weaker labour market into a lower discount rate without paying for the accompanying earnings risk. July payroll growth was just 73,000 and earlier months were revised sharply lower, CNBC reported. That changes the balance of risks: a central bank worried about tariff inflation must now weigh a less comfortable employment picture. Reuters reports policymakers expressing increasing concern about the cooling economy, while Daly says cuts will probably be needed in coming months. The constructive interpretation is a policy response early enough to protect demand. The less comfortable interpretation is that hiring has already lost more momentum than corporate forecasts assume. Own companies whose orders can survive that uncertainty rather than buying every rate-sensitive share as an equivalent easing beneficiary. [1] [2] [3]

Rates & Curve: The 2Y Treasury yield is 3.72% and the 10Y 4.23%, leaving a positive 51 bps spread. Over 20 observations the front end fell 14 bps and the long end 12 bps: bull steepening (both down, 2Y down more). The curve is positively sloped but still flat by historical standards. Over five observations, the 2Y fell 22 bps against 14 bps for the 10Y. That recent front-end adjustment is consistent with a stronger easing argument, but a positive curve does not establish that lending or investment is accelerating. The S&P 500 closed at 6,340.00, up 0.95% over 20 sessions; Nasdaq 100 gained 2.45% while Russell 2000 fell 2.15%. VIX at 16.57 describes a market accepting considerable uncertainty at a modest volatility price. (US Treasury, prior close 2025-08-07; Yahoo Finance, prior close 2025-08-07)

New / Most Important: The policy story is becoming company-specific. Reuters reports a proposed 100% tariff on imported chips with exemptions for some firms; separately, Apple is leading global technology gains after tariff relief. A headline tariff rate and an individual company's effective exposure can move in very different directions. CNBC also reports the proposed nomination of Stephen Miran to the Fed board. Appointment headlines can change perceived future policy preferences, but they do not constitute a current rate decision. Treat the tariff exemptions as an operating-cost issue and Fed speculation as a valuation issue. Neither eliminates the need for customers to buy the products that underpin earnings expectations. [4] [5] [6]

Playbook ✓: Keep a selective growth allocation, financed by trimming businesses with weak demand and little pricing flexibility. Lower yields can support duration, but the Russell's lag warns against calling this a broad cyclical recovery. Add smaller companies only when their earnings evidence improves alongside their relative prices. Maintain a liquidity reserve for a renewed inflation scare or a further deterioration in hiring. ⚠ Divergence: the bond market is moving toward easier policy while strong equity performance remains concentrated. That is compatible with a soft landing, but it is also compatible with investors crowding into a limited set of earnings shelters. The practical test is broader participation supported by better results, rather than a lower yield alone.
