Macro Stance: China's stock market is telling a more optimistic story than its domestic financing system. [14] [15] The stance is selective participation, with a strict separation between a reform or technology premium and a general recovery in domestic demand. A rising index should not become the justification for assuming that weak credit has stopped mattering. The key question is whether profits improve through stronger final demand or only through hopes of less destructive competition. The former can sustain a broader rerating; the latter leaves investors dependent on enforcement and producer discipline.

[17] The next confirmation should be sequentially broader orders, firmer household spending and company commentary that describes demand rather than discounts. In a market pursuing anti-price-war policies, higher selling prices can come from restrained supply; that is different from a vigorous consumer. Position in companies that can survive either route, and require stronger evidence before paying for an economy-wide acceleration.

[19] Do not treat a prospective funding measure as equivalent to loan demand.
