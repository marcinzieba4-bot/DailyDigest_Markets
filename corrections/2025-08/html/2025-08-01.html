🇺🇸 United States  Mixed  earnings strength / rate restraint

Macro Stance: The US starts August with an unusually sharp distinction between corporate execution and the price of money. Reuters reported that the Federal Reserve left rates unchanged and offered no hint of a September cut; its account of June inflation said tariffs were lifting some goods prices. Those developments make it expensive to buy an equity merely because a future easing cycle might improve its valuation. Microsoft and Meta have supplied a stronger argument: earnings and demand that justify spending today. The portfolio should distinguish those operating results from an assumption that the whole economy is accelerating. A trade-driven rebound in activity can improve a headline without establishing a lasting acceleration in domestic demand. Maintain equity exposure through companies demonstrating revenue conversion, while keeping room for a less forgiving rates environment. [1] [2]

New / Most Important: The market's opportunity is becoming more selective even inside its strongest theme. Reuters reported Microsoft reaching a much larger valuation after solid results, while Amazon's cloud performance failed to impress and its shares fell. The useful lesson is not simply that AI spending is good. Investors are distinguishing spending supported by an operating payoff from spending that still needs a more persuasive revenue bridge. Reuters' description of the Fed's reluctance forcing a rethink of the rates outlook adds discipline to that distinction: an earnings disappointment cannot safely rely on policy relief to repair the valuation. The next labour and inflation evidence must improve that policy argument on its own merits. [3] [4] [5]

Playbook ✓: Keep a core allocation to profitable growth, with Microsoft and Meta favoured over an indiscriminate long-duration basket when their earnings strength survives the broader market's hesitation. Hold some short-duration liquidity to finance additions after price confirmation. Add small-company or property sensitivity only when borrowing costs and operating demand improve together; a softer yield alone would be insufficient if it reflected deteriorating spending. Reduce speculative exposure if the dollar and front-end yields strengthen while earnings breadth narrows. The contrary signal would be resilient demand outside the technology leaders alongside a less restrictive rates path. That combination would justify broadening the portfolio rather than increasing concentration in the same winners.

🇨🇳 China  Mixed  policy support / domestic friction

Macro Stance: China's strongest investment argument is policy-directed restructuring, while its weakest remains a broad claim that household demand has already repaired itself. Reuters reported leaders promising support for the economy and a crackdown on disorderly competition; Bloomberg read the Politburo as signalling confidence despite the trade war. Those are compatible positions. Officials can believe the economy is resilient while recognising that companies are destroying margins through competition and that particular channels need support. Investors should distinguish a policy floor from a demand upswing. The first can reduce the probability of a severe outcome; the second would allow earnings forecasts to rise across a wider set of businesses. Selective technology exposure can work before that broader confirmation arrives, but property and highly leveraged domestic cyclicals require a higher standard of proof. [11] [12]

PMIs & Activity: The July official manufacturing PMI edged down, according to the NBS account carried by Global Times. The earlier quarterly GDP release showed growth of 5.2%, above the market forecast, according to Reuters. A quarterly growth rate and a weakening factory survey measure different things: the former summarises activity already recorded, while the latter warns about the direction in which businesses are experiencing orders. The portfolio should respect both. Avoid extrapolating a stronger quarterly number into every subsequent month; require better order evidence before raising exposure to businesses with substantial fixed costs and limited pricing power. Credit Impulse: Credit quality is a constraint on the transmission of easier policy. HSBC's profit fell as China losses mounted, Reuters reported; Nikkei described deeper credit losses at HSBC and Hang Seng associated with Hong Kong property. The lesson is not that every mainland borrower has the same problem. It is that cheaper funding cannot by itself make an impaired asset worth its original carrying value, or persuade a cautious household to buy a home. Banks must balance new business against the stock of risk already on their books. Prefer borrowers with operating cash and lenders with credible loss absorption. A productive credit recovery would show up in borrowers using financing to expand viable activity, rather than in repeated extensions that postpone recognition of losses. [16] [17]

Property: Bloomberg reported a deepening slump in home sales as falling prices deterred buyers. Reuters described Evergrande's debt restructuring dragging on as delisting approached. That combination identifies a confidence problem as well as a financing problem. A household deciding whether to buy a home cares about delivery, resale value and the stability of its own income; a lower mortgage cost is only one input. Restructuring can clarify the claims of creditors without creating a buyer for the next development. Do not treat a rally in a developer's equity as proof that transactions have turned. A more durable improvement would combine steadier sales with evidence that completed homes and cash collection support the remaining construction programme. Until then, avoid making property the principal expression of a general China recovery. [18] [19]

A household transfer can improve spending capacity, but the investment effect depends on whether households spend rather than rebuild savings. Debt management can ease financing pressure without adding the same amount to new demand. Meanwhile, Reuters said the US–China tariff truce held for now with Trump retaining the final say. Treat the truce as a reduction in immediate disruption, not as a completed removal of the trade barrier. Position for companies able to operate under imperfect terms. [20] [21] [22]
