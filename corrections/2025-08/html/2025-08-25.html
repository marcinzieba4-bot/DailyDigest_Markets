The portfolio should move toward a broader recovery while retaining a bias toward companies able to finance themselves. 🇪🇺 European Union  Mixed  policy support / trade friction

Macro Stance: Bloomberg reported that ECB officials judged they could hold rates steady in September. [3] Europe offers a valuation and financing argument, but the earnings transmission is uneven. Lower borrowing costs help domestic services, property financing and household demand before they necessarily revive export manufacturing. A trade-exposed manufacturer needs orders and pricing power as well as cheaper money. Prefer companies with a clear domestic earnings engine over businesses whose entire investment case rests on a single tariff negotiation. A regional index can advance while exporters remain under pressure, so distinguish the broad financial conditions from the particular demand faced by each sector.

Conversely, a sharp currency appreciation can dilute the foreign earnings of companies that otherwise appear well positioned. Keep the domestic and exporter books separate when setting risk.

New / Most Important: Bloomberg reported Ursula von der Leyen defending the European Union’s trade agreement as strong, if imperfect. [5] The temptation is to price legal or political language as an immediate restoration of margins. Actual purchase orders, delivery terms and inventory decisions are slower. Negotiation headlines should therefore change scenarios and sizing before they change long-run earnings assumptions.

Playbook ✓: Hold a selective European allocation through diversified exposure and businesses with visible cash generation. Add export beta only when order commentary corroborates the policy narrative; use domestic services and well-capitalised financials to express a steadier recovery. Hedge a trade-sensitive position if currency strength and weak customer orders arrive together. The mixed stance can turn more constructive when improved activity is matched by earnings revisions, or more defensive if the financing benefit is consumed by margin pressure.

🇨🇳 China  Mixed  market momentum / demand test

Macro Stance: Bloomberg’s assessment of growing bubble risks framed China’s bull run against continuing anxiety about the economy. [6] China is a selection market: a rise in listed equities can coexist with cautious households and companies protecting cash. The important distinction is between improved willingness to own financial assets and a broad willingness to borrow, invest and spend. Those channels can reinforce each other, but they should not be assumed to move together. Prefer liquid companies with identifiable revenue engines and strong balance sheets to a blanket bet on reflation. A market supported by policy expectations can make substantial progress before demand confirms the move; that is an opportunity for staged exposure and a warning against leverage.

PMIs & Activity: Global Times reported a lower July manufacturing PMI, citing the National Bureau of Statistics. [7] CNBC reported that China’s July retail-sales and industrial-output growth missed forecasts. [8] The latest monthly evidence should be read as a description of the underlying economy, not as a mechanical timing indicator for the equity index. Manufacturing surveys reflect the balance between order books, production and inventories, while consumption and services determine whether a household recovery is taking hold. A factory can increase output into weak final demand and create an inventory problem rather than better profits. For equity selection, the useful confirmation would be firmer orders alongside better pricing and collection of receivables. Until those signals align, concentrate on businesses that can defend margins without relying on a wholesale recovery in producer prices.

Credit Impulse: The Wall Street Journal reported an unexpected fall in China’s new yuan loans. [9] Credit transmission is the central test of policy effectiveness. More available bank funding is helpful only when viable borrowers want to use it and when the money reaches productive activity. Refinancing an old liability can lower immediate stress without creating a new source of revenue. A convincing improvement would involve private firms funding expansion, households taking on obligations with confidence and lenders accepting risk on commercial grounds. For investors, that argues for distinguishing liquidity relief from a durable credit impulse. Financial conditions can support equities first, but companies with sound cash conversion remain the cleaner exposure while demand for new borrowing is uncertain.

Property: The South China Morning Post reported that home prices continued sliding while support measures failed to shift sentiment. [10] Housing matters through household wealth, local-government financing, construction employment and the collateral available to lenders. A measure that reduces a developer’s immediate cash pressure can protect completion of homes without restoring enthusiasm for new purchases. The investable distinction is between stabilising the system and reviving the old development model. Avoid treating a property headline as a uniform benefit to every bank, builder and supplier. Better sales collections, completed projects and less pressure to discount would make the stabilisation case more credible. Until then, balance-sheet resilience matters more than the cheapest valuation or the most dramatic rebound from a depressed share price.

Fiscal, Policy & Trade: Axios reported Scott Bessent describing the US–China trade truce as “working pretty well”. [11] Policy can improve the distribution of outcomes even before it improves aggregate activity. A more predictable trade environment allows firms to plan shipments and sourcing with less precautionary cash, while targeted domestic spending can sustain particular order books. Neither channel removes competitive pressure or guarantees an attractive return on capital. The relevant beneficiaries are companies selling into funded projects, exporters with flexible supply chains and domestic leaders able to gain share without sacrificing profitability. Keep tariff-sensitive positions small enough to survive a change in negotiating language. The better expression is a collection of identifiable cash-flow opportunities rather than a single bet that every dispute is about to disappear.

Playbook: Buy demonstrated resilience at a valuation that leaves room for ordinary execution. Keep earnings-event positions smaller when targets have risen faster than the company’s demonstrated cash generation. Use hedges around concentrated binary outcomes rather than assuming a strong company must produce a strong immediate return. Add after a result when the future profit path improves and the stock retains that improvement; reduce when management’s outlook requires a recovery that current customer behaviour does not yet support.

💰 Capital Flows & Liquidity Framework

These are linked channels, but they do not have identical consequences for shareholders. The constructive market view requires financing to support activity without allowing weak projects to consume too much capital. Where operating cash remains distant, use a stricter hurdle for both valuation and position size.
