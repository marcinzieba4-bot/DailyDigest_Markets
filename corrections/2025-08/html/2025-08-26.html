🇺🇸 United States  Mixed  policy opening / credibility risk

Macro Stance: CNBC reported that markets were confident about a September rate cut but much less certain about the path after it. [1] That is a more demanding setup than a simple policy-relief rally. The immediate benefit of cheaper money has to be balanced against weaker hiring, tariff costs and uncertainty about the institutions setting the price of money. Keep a constructive core allocation, but stop short of treating every pullback as an automatic invitation to add risk. A broad recovery needs companies to keep earning, banks to keep lending and investors to trust that inflation will be contained. 🇨🇳 China  Mixed  savings rotate / real demand lags

Macro Stance: CNBC described Chinese household savings helping to fuel a stock-market boom. [6] The important investment question is where that money goes after it leaves a bank account. Buying existing shares can improve sentiment and lower the cost of equity without immediately creating household consumption or business investment. That distinction explains how a strong market can coexist with a subdued economy. Participate through liquid businesses with credible earnings, but resist the temptation to treat rising stock prices as proof that every domestic-demand problem has been solved. A rally funded by a reallocation of savings needs a second stage in which companies deliver more cash to shareholders.

PMIs & Activity: Global Times reported the July manufacturing PMI edging lower, citing the National Bureau of Statistics. [7] CNBC reported that July retail-sales and industrial-output growth fell short of expectations. [8] Those earlier monthly releases still frame the demand question. Production, retail activity and purchasing intentions should eventually reinforce one another in a durable recovery. Until they do, companies must be assessed on the particular customer, product and pricing environment they face. A strong technology franchise can grow in a weak aggregate economy, while a commodity producer or discretionary retailer may still struggle. Avoid using a single macro label to erase these differences.

Credit Impulse: The Wall Street Journal reported an unexpected decline in new yuan loans. [9] Cash moving into equities is not equivalent to a sustained increase in productive borrowing. A borrower must see a return worth pursuing before lower financing costs can create new investment. Refinancing can improve survival, but it may leave demand unchanged. The stronger confirmation would involve private companies committing to commercially attractive expansion, households showing greater confidence in future income and lenders seeing viable opportunities rather than only policy targets. Equity exposure should remain tilted toward companies that can fund themselves while that transmission is incomplete.

Property: The South China Morning Post reported Shanghai easing property rules to support its sluggish housing market.
