# Writing brief — February 2026 retrospective Market Intelligence Briefings

You write the daily **Market Intelligence Briefing** for a weekday in February 2026 **as it would have been written at
the report's information cutoff: 00:00 UTC on the report date**. The page goes into the public report archive next to
the March 2026 briefings and must read like an ordinary report of that day.

Paths below are relative to `corrections/2026-02/` (absolute: `/home/user/DailyDigest_Markets/corrections/2026-02/`).

## The one rule that matters most: nothing after the cutoff
You know a lot about 2026 from training — including what happened *after* each report date. **None of it may enter the
report.** The only admissible facts are:

1. **The date's feed** `feeds/<date>.json` — Google News headlines whose publication time is strictly before the cutoff
   (news ≤ 7 days old, monthly data releases ≤ 35 days, earnings reactions ≤ 14 days). You only have the
   **headline**, not the article: claim nothing the headline does not say.
2. **The date's market card** `cards/<date>.json` — completed closes before the cutoff (Yahoo Finance, exchange-local
   session dates; crypto UTC days) and the official US Treasury par curve at the prior close, with 1-, 5- and
   20-session changes.

General economic reasoning (how a supply shock transmits, why a flat curve squeezes bank margins) is fine. Facts — an
event, a data print, a company result, a policy decision, a price, a date, a schedule — must come from (1) or (2).
Never mention a later outcome, a later price, a later decision, or "what happened next". If you notice yourself
"knowing" how something turned out, leave it out. Do not read feeds, cards or specs of later dates.

Headline hygiene (Google's dates are approximate):
- A headline that describes an event which, per other headlines, had **not happened yet** by the cutoff is misdated —
  drop it (e.g. a "January payrolls beat" headline dated before the payrolls release, or a central-bank "decision"
  headline dated before the meeting). Real example in these feeds: "Preview: February non-farm payrolls … A Good
  Friday report" dated Feb 4 is about an April release — drop it.
- Do not cite live blogs / rolling pages ("live updates", "LIVE", "as it happened", "stock market today" pages): their
  titles change after publication.
- Monthly releases can be weeks old: call them "the January ISM print", never "this week's".
- Calendar claims ("Nvidia reports on Wednesday", "jobs data Friday") need a feed headline that says so (a preview / week
  ahead headline dated before the cutoff). Otherwise write "the next print" without a date.
- When headlines conflict (one says shares rose, another fell), give no direction.

## Numbers
Every number you write must be in the card, or in a headline cited **in the same paragraph** (verify.py enforces this).
- Market data sentences end with a source tag: `(Yahoo Finance, prior close 2026-02-06)` using the card's `session`
  date of that instrument, or `(US Treasury, prior close 2026-02-06)` using `treasury.prior_close_date`. Both may be
  combined: `(Yahoo Finance, prior close 2026-02-06; US Treasury, prior close 2026-02-06)`. For crypto use the
  crypto session date (weekends included) and say "prior completed daily close".
- Yields with two decimals and %; **yield changes and spreads in bps, never in % of the yield**. Use the card's
  `curve_shape` and `curve_move_20obs` wording for the curve ("positively sloped but still flat by historical
  standards", "bull flattening"). Never call a +60 bps 2s10s "steep" or "normal".
- Price changes: use the card's `chg_1s_pct` / `chg_5s_pct` / `chg_20s_pct` (you may round to one decimal). Say
  "over 20 sessions" / "over five sessions" / "in the prior session". Direction words must match the sign.
- Allowed without a source: small counts (≤ 12), "20 sessions", years, and names such as S&P 500, Russell 2000,
  Nasdaq 100, STOXX 600, 10Y, 2Y, Q4.
- Do not compute new ratios or derived figures.

## Citations
Write `[[n0123]]` (the feed item id) immediately after the sentence it supports. The renderer turns it into
`Reuters [Feb 05]` linking to the article. Prefer Reuters, Bloomberg, CNBC, FT, WSJ, AP, Barron's, MarketWatch,
Nikkei Asia, SCMP, Caixin, central-bank and statistics-office sources, PR Newswire (ISM), Bankier / Money.pl /
Business Insider Polska / Parkiet / PAP / Notes from Poland for Poland, CoinDesk / The Block for crypto, Stocktwits /
24/7 Wall St. / Yahoo Finance for retail attention. Avoid autogenerated ticker spam (MarketBeat, Defense World, ETF
Daily News …). Aim for **40–70 distinct sources** per report; one citation may be reused, but not more than four times.

## Style — useful, specific, honest
The reader is a professional investor who wants to *act* on the page. Be concrete: name the print, the level, the
company, the move, and say what it means for positioning. Each paragraph: 2–5 sentences. One caveat where it matters
is good; boilerplate disclaimers in every paragraph ("this does not establish…") are not — keep them rare.
- Take a view. Stances are **Bullish / Bearish / Mixed**; use Mixed only when the evidence really is balanced.
- Every **Playbook** states an action with its trigger: what to own / avoid / hedge, and what would change the call.
- Past events in past tense. No hindsight wording ("turned out", "subsequently", "retrospective"), no mention that this
  is a reconstruction, no notes to the reader about methodology.
- Copper is an asset (AI data-centre / grid capex metal), never a gauge of China or global growth; no gold/copper or
  oil/copper ratios. Read growth from PMIs, credit and housing.
- No Polish companies in the Sector Deep Dive (they belong in the Poland block).
- Company accuracy: describe a company's role exactly as the headline does; for M&A say what the target does.

### Section guide (word counts are per block; the whole report ≈ 4,500–6,500 words)
**🌍 Geographic Macro Overview** — six blocks, `label` = 2–6 word policy/stance tag (e.g. "hold / data dependent",
"easing bias / weak demand").
- **US** (150–250 words): Macro Stance · Rates & Curve (2Y, 10Y, 10Y−2Y spread at the prior close, 20-observation bps
  changes, curve shape and move labels, plus S&P 500 / VIX / DXY context if useful) · New / Most Important · Playbook ✓.
- **EU** (120–200): Macro Stance · Credit & Inflation (or Activity & Inflation) · New / Most Important · Playbook ✓.
- **CN** (450–700, exactly these eight parts): Macro Stance · PMIs & Activity · Credit Impulse · Property ·
  Fiscal, Policy & Trade · Markets & Flows (Hang Seng / Shanghai / ASHR / KWEB / USD/CNY from the card) ·
  New / Most Important · Playbook ✓ (end with "⚠ Divergence: …" — the signal that contradicts the main story).
  If the feed has no fresh print for a part, say what the latest available print was (with its month) or that the
  feed carries none, and what would confirm the view.
- **JP** (100–180): Macro Stance · Activity & Currency (USD/JPY, Nikkei from card) · Playbook ✓.
- **AU** (100–180): Macro Stance · Demand & Financing (AUD/USD, ASX 200 from card) · Playbook ✓.
- **PL** (180–300): Monetary & Activity · FX & Markets (USD/PLN, EUR/PLN, EPOL, WIG20TR ETF, named Polish stocks from
  the card) · Companies · Security & New Development · Playbook ✓ (end with "⚠ Divergence: …"). Polish-language
  headlines are welcome; translate them in the sentence.

**📊 Sector Deep Dive** — 13 blocks, 90–160 words each, four parts: **Narrative** (consensus + the driver that matters
now, may use the sector ETF's 20-session change from the card) · **Reported** (who reported in the feed window: beat /
miss, guidance, share reaction — only as the headline says) · **EPS Upside Watch** (who is set up for an earnings
surprise and why, from previews / estimate revisions / demand headlines) · **⚡ Breaking Convention** (the signal that
cuts against the consensus narrative). SXAP = European autos; SOXX = US–Asia semis incl. ASML / TSMC.

**🧮 Earnings Pattern — What the Market Pays For** — one block, 180–320 words, read from the last two weeks of results
and share reactions: Rewarded · Punished · Pattern · Attention into EPS · Playbook.

**🔍 Underowned Themes** — two themes, each Attention Gap · Exposure & Risk (name instruments). Do not claim measured
positioning data unless a headline gives it.

**₿ Crypto & Digital Assets** — Market Context (BTC / ETH / SOL closes and changes from the card, IBIT / COIN / MSTR
if useful) · New / Most Important · Playbook.

**💰 Capital Flows & Liquidity Framework** — Rates & Dollar (DXY, curve, HYG / LQD / TLT from the card) · Credit
Check · Portfolio Implication.

**🔮 Anticipatory Positioning** — two scenarios the market is pricing (only events the feed shows as pending):
What Is Priced · Conditional Expression (the trade if it goes either way) · Risk.

**⚡ High-Conviction Stock Catalysts** — three single names with a stance; title "$TICKER — short thesis".
Catalyst & Thesis (the dated catalyst from the feed + the thesis) · Price Hurdle & Risk (use the card's close /
20-session change if the ticker is there; otherwise no price).

**🔥 What Retail Is Playing** — four names/themes that retail attention headlines (Reddit, Stocktwits, meme / most-active
lists, retail flow stories) show; title "Name / TICKER — the hook". Observed Attention · Motivation & Bias ·
Instrument & Risk.

## Procedure per date
1. `python3 -I tools/digest.py <date>` (prioritised headlines per topic) and `python3 -I tools/digest.py <date> --grep
   "regex"` to search all items. `cat cards/<date>.json` for market data.
2. Write `specs/<date>.json`.
3. `python3 -I tools/verify.py <date>` — fix every FAIL; review every WARN (fix it or be sure it is right).
4. `python3 -I tools/render.py <date>` — must render without error.
5. Keep a 3–5 line note: overall tone, the stances you chose, the main stories, anything you deliberately excluded.
