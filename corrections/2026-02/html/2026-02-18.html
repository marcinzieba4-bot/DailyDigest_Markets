🧮 Earnings Pattern — What the Market Pays For

Guidance beats the quarter: buy physical-AI bottlenecks, sell beats without raises

Rewarded: Guidance raisers in physical bottlenecks were rewarded: Siemens jumped on a lifted outlook WSJ [Feb 12], Applied Materials gave a rosy AI and memory forecast Bloomberg.com [Feb 13], and NAB hit a record on robust cash profit Reuters [Feb 17]. McDonald's value-led sales beat Reuters [Feb 11] showed that consumers reward price and investors reward the companies that capture it.

Punished: Beats with flat or light guidance were sold: Medtronic fell on an unchanged outlook Reuters [Feb 17], Palo Alto slid after cutting its annual profit forecast Reuters [Feb 17], and Pinterest plunged on a downbeat revenue forecast Reuters [Feb 12]. Microsoft is still being sold despite the largest earnings beat in its history Yahoo Finance [Feb 17].

Pattern: The market is paying for the durability of earnings, not the quarter. It discounts any business whose terminal value AI could compress — software seats, ad networks, brokers — and pays for physical scarcity in memory, equipment, power and metals. Beats are necessary but not sufficient; the raise is what moves the stock.

Attention into EPS: Attention now concentrates on Walmart, the focus of the Reuters week-ahead Reuters [Feb 15], and on Nvidia's results on Feb. 25 The Motley Fool [Feb 17]. In Europe, Stellantis's full-year results on February 26 Stellantis.com [Feb 16] test whether the autos rally has substance.

Playbook: Buy post-print dips in guidance raisers tied to physical AI supply chains, and fade beats that come without a raise, especially in software and ad-tech. Into prints, use defined-risk option spreads where 'beat and fall' has been the rule. Revisit if two large software names beat and rally in a row, which would mark the end of the AI-disruption discount.
