🧮 Earnings Pattern — What the Market Pays For

Guidance beats the quarter — and pricing power is paid

Rewarded: Pricing power and capex leverage: Cava jumped 26% on same-store sales growth driven by menu prices CNBC [Feb 24], Constellation topped estimates and CEG rose 6.41% (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2026-02-24) Investor's Business Daily [Feb 24], and Home Depot beat for the first time in a year CNBC [Feb 24].

Punished: Weak guidance and growth disappointments: Workday sank on weak revenue guidance CNBC [Feb 24], Lucid widely missed and forecast slowing EV growth CNBC [Feb 24], Kratos fell even after a beat Barron's [Feb 24] and Fresenius Medical Care dropped on an underwhelming outlook WSJ [Feb 24].

Pattern: The reaction function is unchanged: forward guidance first, AI exposure second, the reported quarter third. Beats without guidance support (Kratos) are sold; companies that can raise prices or ride AI-power demand are paid. The tape also rewards visibility — utilities with multi-year capex plans and restaurants with proven traffic trade at a premium to companies whose demand depends on discretionary software or EV budgets.

Attention into EPS: Nvidia’s report on Feb. 25 The Motley Fool [Feb 24] collides with Wall Street skepticism over AI spending CNBC [Feb 24]; the bar is guidance on hyperscaler demand and margins.

Playbook: Buy post-earnings strength only where guidance rose or pricing power is explicit; fade beats with flat outlooks. Into Nvidia, use call spreads; after the print, follow guidance, not the headline beat.
