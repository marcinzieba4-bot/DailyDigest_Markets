🧮 Earnings Pattern — What the Market Pays For

Cash today beats AI promises tomorrow

Rewarded: The market paid for visible demand and pricing power: CBA's record half sent the shares higher, Spotify jumped on earnings and subscriber growth, and McDonald's beat as value drew traffic. TSMC's January revenue jump kept the AI hardware chain bid.

Punished: Beats without guidance were sold: Cisco fell 7% despite topping estimates. Weak outlooks were punished hard — Molina sank 28% and Dassault Systèmes 18% — and Amazon fell on a miss paired with a $200 billion capex plan.

Pattern: Two filters explain the reactions. Guidance beats the quarter: companies that raise or hold credible outlooks get paid, those that do not are sold regardless of the beat. And AI is a cost line until proven a revenue line — software and capex-heavy platforms are discounted, while hardware where AI already shows up in revenue is rewarded.

Attention into EPS: Applied Materials reports with traders positioned for a large move, the first test of whether TSMC's capex vote converts into tool orders. Coinbase is also on deck, with JPMorgan cutting its target ahead of earnings — a test of how much crypto weakness the stock already discounts.

Playbook: Buy beat-and-raise in hardware, banks and staples retail on the first pullback; sell beat-and-hold in software on the first bounce. Avoid owning capex-heavy platforms into prints unless they quantify AI revenue. Change the call when a guidance-cut stock rallies on the day — that would mark capitulation in the punished cohort.

Conditional Expression: Hot core print: sell 2Y Treasuries, rotate from homebuilders and small caps into energy and staples retail, and buy the dollar against the euro. Soft print: add duration via TLT, buy homebuilders and IWM, and sell the dollar against the yen. IWM is up only 1.38% over 20 sessions at 264.95 (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2026-02-11), so a soft print has the most room to run there.

Risk: The release was delayed, and data collected around the shutdown carry larger revision risk, so a single month can mislead. The bigger risk is a mixed print — firm services, soft goods — that leaves the pause intact and markets range-bound, with VIX at 17.65 (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2026-02-11) offering little cushion for a disappointment.

US–Iran: talks or a second carrier

What Is Priced: Oil carries a modest risk premium: Brent closed at 69.40, up 6.0% over 20 sessions, and WTI at 64.63, up 5.69% (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2026-02-11). The Pentagon is preparing a second aircraft carrier to deploy to the Middle East while diplomacy continues; the market prices a standoff, not a supply disruption.

Conditional Expression: Escalation: own XLE and Brent call spreads, short airlines. De-escalation: the premium deflates toward the EIA's lower-price path — sell energy into strength and rotate into airlines and refiners. Use options rather than adding to energy equity, which is already up 16.98% over 20 sessions (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2026-02-11).

Risk: The asymmetry is timing: a carrier deployment is a signal, not a decision, and oil can drop sharply on any sign of talks. Gold, up 10.86% over 20 sessions (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2026-02-11), is the cleaner hedge if escalation coincides with weaker risk appetite.

$MCD  value wins the K-shaped consumer  ↑

Catalyst & Thesis: McDonald's earnings beat estimates as the chain's value push paid off. In a consumer sector where Amazon sold off on capex and December retail sales stalled, a restaurant gaining traffic with value is a share-taker, not a victim.

Price Hurdle & Risk: No card price. The risk is that a firmer labour market — unemployment fell to 4.3% — lifts wage costs and squeezes franchisee margins. Own on post-results pullbacks; exit if traffic gains fade in the next quarter.

🔥 What Retail Is Playing

Micron & SanDisk / MU, SNDK  "the memory squeeze"

Observed Attention: Memory is retail's AI trade of the month: 24/7 Wall St. says Micron stock can't stop going up and Goldman Sachs knows exactly why. MU rose 9.94% in the prior session to 410.34 and 21.36% over 20 sessions (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2026-02-11).

Motivation & Bias: A simple shortage story plus visible momentum — recency bias treats peak-cycle pricing as permanent, even though memory is the most cyclical corner of semis.

Instrument & Risk: Prefer MU stock or call spreads to leveraged single-stock products. The risk is a supply response from rivals — the classic end of a memory upcycle — and Micron's 20-session reference of 338.13 (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2026-02-11) shows how far a reversal could run.

Gold & Silver / GLD, SLV  "the safe haven trading like a meme"

Observed Attention: Yahoo Finance describes gold as a safe haven that is trading like a meme stock. SLV jumped 4.29% in the prior session to 76.56 but is down 2.6% over 20 sessions, while GLD is up 10.91% (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2026-02-11).

Motivation & Bias: Debasement fear and FOMO after record prices; retail treats a hedge as a momentum trade, which is how hedges become crowded positions.

Instrument & Risk: Use unlevered GLD or SLV and size as a hedge, not a trade. Silver's 20-session decline despite gold's rally shows how quickly the speculative layer reverses, and leveraged products decay in that kind of chop.

Bitcoin dip-buyers / IBIT, MSTR  "buying the winter"

Observed Attention: Hong Kong retail investors snapped up bitcoin amid the crypto crash even as prices kept sliding — bitcoin is down 5.05% over five sessions (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2026-02-11).

Motivation & Bias: Anchoring on prior highs and averaging down; the dip-buyer trusts that the four-year cycle guarantees a recovery.

Instrument & Risk: Use IBIT rather than MSTR, which is down 27.12% over 20 sessions at 126.07 (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2026-02-11) and adds balance-sheet leverage. Size small and wait for the credit stress shown by BlockFills' withdrawal halt to clear.

Reddit / RDDT  "buyback versus drawdown"

Observed Attention: Forbes asks whether Reddit's 36% drop is an overreaction — a platform retail traders use daily has become one they debate buying.

Motivation & Bias: Familiarity bias and the anchor of the old high; holders treat the drawdown as a discount on a business they see every day.

Instrument & Risk: Use stock rather than short-dated options and add only if the shares hold their post-earnings lows. The risk is AI search eroding traffic and the broader de-rating of internet platforms, which has not ended.
