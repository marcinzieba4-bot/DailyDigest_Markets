Macro Stance: China is choosing restraint: Beijing has signalled a trimmed GDP growth target that requires less stimulus Bloomberg.com [Feb 05]. Exports carry the headline numbers, but credit, property and household demand are not turning, and a lower target means policy will not force them. We stay Bearish on the domestic cycle and on Hong Kong-listed platforms, where tax and AI fears now compound.

PMIs & Activity: The official January manufacturing PMI dropped to 49.3 Reuters [Jan 30], back below the 50 line. The private gauge disagreed: factory activity grew at the fastest pace since October, beating the official reading CNBC [Feb 01]. The gap between the two surveys is what an economy carried by external demand looks like — exporters holding up, domestic orders soft — rather than a broad recovery.

Credit Impulse: The latest credit data are weak: new bank loans in 2025 were the lowest in seven years Reuters [Jan 15], and the feed carries no January lending print yet. Marginal rate relief cannot create an impulse without willing borrowers, and the property slump removes the main collateral channel. A January new-loan figure above last year's pace would be the first confirmation that demand is responding.

Property: Housing is still sliding: Shanghai is piloting state home purchases in three districts to halt the market slide Caixin Global [Feb 05], which makes the city government a buyer of last resort. State buying can put a floor under prices in one city, but it does not restore developer cash flow or land-sale revenue for local governments. What would change the view: stabilising tier-one sales after the Shanghai pilot, followed by other cities copying it.

Fiscal, Policy & Trade: Fiscal room is tight: China logged its first fiscal revenue drop since 2020 on the property slump and weak consumption Reuters [Jan 30], which explains both the restraint on stimulus and the search for new tax revenue. On trade, the US proposed a critical-minerals trade bloc aimed at countering China Reuters [Feb 04], a direct challenge to Beijing's main supply-chain lever. 🧮 Earnings Pattern — What the Market Pays For

Paid for AI revenue, punished for AI cost

Rewarded: Companies that showed AI turning into revenue now: Meta soared after proving its AI spend CNBC [Jan 29], and Reddit rose on strong guidance and a $1 billion buyback CNBC [Feb 05]. Niche guidance raisers also won — Tapestry lifted annual forecasts after a blowout holiday quarter Reuters [Feb 05].

Punished: Beats did not help where guidance or AI exposure disappointed: AMD sank 17% after earnings CNBC [Feb 04], Microsoft sat about 10% lower after its call The Motley Fool [Feb 02], and Amazon fell on a miss plus $200 billion of capex guidance CNBC [Feb 05]. Lilly's 46% Q4 revenue growth BioSpace [Feb 04] did not prevent a 7.79% one-day drop (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2026-02-05).

Pattern: The market pays for AI that shows up in this quarter's revenue and punishes two things: AI capex without visible monetisation (Amazon, Microsoft) and business models AI can replicate (software, information services). Beats are table stakes; guidance and the capex-to-revenue ratio decide the reaction.
