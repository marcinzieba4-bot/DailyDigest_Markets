🧮 Earnings Pattern — What the Market Pays For

Beats are table stakes; guidance and capital return get paid

Rewarded: Companies that paired a beat with a raised outlook or visible demand: Axon jumped 17% Livemint [Feb 25], Deere boosted its outlook WSJ [Feb 19], Constellation topped estimates Investor's Business Daily [Feb 24], and HSBC's results lifted European shares to a record Reuters [Feb 25]. Nvidia's earnings-and-guidance beat CNBC [Feb 25] extended the pattern into the AI supply chain.

Punished: Beats with soft guides were sold: Workday hit a five-year low on a sluggish sales forecast Reuters [Feb 25], Lowe's dipped 4.2% and GoDaddy slumped 16% Livemint [Feb 25], TJX's muted forecast weighed Reuters [Feb 25], and Diageo fell nearly 13% after cutting its dividend CNBC [Feb 25].

Pattern: The market pays for forward visibility and penalises two things: subscription models seen as exposed to AI substitution, and consumer companies guiding cautiously. Hard-asset and AI-infrastructure exposure is rewarded even at high valuations, while optically cheap consumer and software names keep de-rating on in-line numbers.

Attention into EPS: Attention shifts to the AI-infrastructure tail of the season: CoreWeave, where options traders expect a 15% move on its Q4 results tastylive [Feb 24]. Nvidia's forecast of accelerating growth CNBC [Feb 25] raises the bar for every AI-supply-chain report that follows, because in-line guides will now read as deceleration.

Playbook: Buy beats only with raised guides; fade beats paired with flat outlooks on the first bounce.
