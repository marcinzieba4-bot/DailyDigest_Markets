🧮 Earnings Pattern — What the Market Pays For

Beats are table stakes; guidance and capital return get paid

Rewarded: Companies that paired a beat with a raised outlook or visible demand: Axon jumped 17%, Deere boosted its outlook, Constellation topped estimates, and HSBC's results lifted European shares to a record. Nvidia's earnings-and-guidance beat extended the pattern into the AI supply chain.

Punished: Beats with soft guides were sold: Workday hit a five-year low on a sluggish sales forecast, Lowe's dipped 4.2% and GoDaddy slumped 16%, TJX's muted forecast weighed, and Diageo fell nearly 13% after cutting its dividend.

Pattern: The market pays for forward visibility and penalises two things: subscription models seen as exposed to AI substitution, and consumer companies guiding cautiously. Hard-asset and AI-infrastructure exposure is rewarded even at high valuations, while optically cheap consumer and software names keep de-rating on in-line numbers.

Attention into EPS: Attention shifts to the AI-infrastructure tail of the season: CoreWeave, where options traders expect a 15% move on its Q4 results. Nvidia's forecast of accelerating growth raises the bar for every AI-supply-chain report that follows, because in-line guides will now read as deceleration.

Playbook: Buy beats only with raised guides; fade beats paired with flat outlooks on the first bounce. Into prints, prefer companies with buyback capacity — Salesforce committed $50 billion — and physical AI exposure over seat-based software. Change the call if two or more software names rally on in-line guides, which would signal the de-rating is exhausted.
