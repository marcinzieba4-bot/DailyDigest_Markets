🧮 Earnings Pattern — What the Market Pays For

AI proof and cash returns get paid; beats alone do not

Rewarded: Results that disproved the AI-disruption thesis or raised the outlook: RingCentral and Five9 rallied as earnings quelled AI concerns CNBC [Feb 20], and Deere jumped on a higher outlook WSJ [Feb 19].

Punished: Beats without a better outlook were sold: Walmart's cautious guidance overshadowed steady sales Reuters [Feb 19], and Grail has plunged 50% despite beating Barron's [Feb 20]. Consumer-facing and AI-exposed services needed a guidance raise, not just a beat, to hold their gains.

Pattern: The market pays for forward visibility in the physical economy and in AI hardware, and for software that can show AI adds revenue. It discounts beats in businesses exposed to AI substitution or to cautious consumers, and it rewards capital discipline over volume growth.

Attention into EPS: Nvidia on Feb. 25 is the event The Motley Fool [Feb 17], but Bloomberg argues the stock is so stuck that even blowout earnings may not lift it Bloomberg.com [Feb 22]; NVDA closed at 189.82, up 2.69% over 20 sessions (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2026-02-20). Home Depot, banks and Berkshire also report in the week ahead Investopedia [Feb 22].

Playbook: Into reports, buy names where guidance risk is skewed up — equipment, memory, industrial capex — and sell beats in seat-based software and consumer platforms that lack guidance raises. Treat a beat-and-hold as a sell signal in crowded defensives.
