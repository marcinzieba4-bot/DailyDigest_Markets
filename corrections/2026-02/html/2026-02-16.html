🧮 Earnings Pattern — What the Market Pays For

Physical AI gets paid; seat-based software and intermediaries get fined

Rewarded: Results that show AI as demand rather than threat: Applied Materials jumped and Siemens rose on a lifted outlook. Self-help with credible guidance also paid — Airbnb rose on a revenue beat and rosy guidance.

Punished: Beats did not protect anyone whose outlook is clouded by AI or tariffs: Cisco fell 7% despite topping estimates, Dassault Systèmes plunged 18% and Pinterest sank nearly 17%. Payout cuts were punished even in the market's strongest sector — BP fell 7% after suspending its buyback.

Pattern: The market pays for capacity and pricing power (memory, semicap, automation, grid) and fines revenue earned per seat, per transaction or per ad click. Guidance outranks the quarter, and a capital-return cut outranks both. Over 20 sessions the gap is stark: Micron up 22.29% and Caterpillar 19.63%, against Microsoft down 12.12% and Amazon 16.54% (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2026-02-13).

Attention into EPS: Walmart and fresh economic data lead the week ahead; Hims & Hers reports on February 23; and Nvidia's Feb. 25 date has Wall Street giving a nearly unanimous answer on buying beforehand. In Australia, 80 companies prepare to report — the first large sample of how a higher cash rate and a strong AUD hit guidance.

Playbook: Buy beats with raised guides in physical-AI, grid and memory; fade beats with flat guides in seat-based software, payments and ad platforms until the AI-proof baskets outperform. Use Walmart as the test of the defensive rotation: a beat that still sells off means staples are exhausted and the next leg is a rotation back toward beaten-down growth.
