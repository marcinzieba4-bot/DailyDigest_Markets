We stay bearish on domestic demand and selective on exporters.

PMIs & Activity: The January official manufacturing PMI fell to 49.3, while the private survey showed factory activity growing at its fastest pace since October and beating the official reading. Credit Impulse: The latest credit print (December) was weak: 2025 new bank loans were the lowest in seven years, and Reuters notes policy support is needed. Lower funding costs have not created borrowers, so we read the credit impulse as still negative until household lending turns. A January credit print with rising household loans would change the call; the feed carries none yet.

Property: Property is still the drag: sales declines accelerated in a 'bleak start' to 2026 for the sector. Policy has shifted to supporting developer funding and prices rather than demand, which leaves property-linked lenders and building materials as funding sources, not holdings. A stabilisation in new-home sales is the signal to revisit.

What Is Priced: US-Iran nuclear talks are set for Oman, and oil rose after Trump said Iran's supreme leader 'should be very worried'. Brent at $69.46 is up 14.4% over 20 sessions and WTI at $65.14 is up 14.0% (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2026-02-04) — a rising geopolitical premium.

Conditional Expression: If talks open constructively, the premium deflates fast: own refiners and airlines and overwrite upstream positions. If talks stall or incidents escalate, own XLE and Brent call spreads; the oil-equity hedge remains cheaper than outright crude.

Risk: Incidents at sea or in the air can override diplomacy at any moment, so keep sizes small and use options rather than futures; a de-escalation surprise would erase the 20-session gain quickly.

Japan's election: fiscal push without a BOJ backstop

What Is Priced: Markets are positioned for a strong Takaichi mandate: the dollar firmed and the yen came under pressure ahead of the vote, with USD/JPY up 2.2% over five sessions to 155.80 (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2026-02-04).

Risk: The BOJ won't rescue a Takaichi-driven bond rout, so a fiscal splurge could push JGB yields high enough to hit equities and force intervention; size the short-JGB leg conservatively.

🔥 What Retail Is Playing

Figma / FIG  "retail buys the software crash"

Observed Attention: Stocktwits reports Figma is down over 40% this year and retail traders feel it's a steal. That dip-buying runs into a sector where nearly $1 trillion of value has been wiped out.

Motivation & Bias: Anchoring on earlier prices and the belief that design software is AI-proof; the bias is treating a lower price as a margin of safety while the market re-rates the whole category.

Instrument & Risk: Shares only and small size; avoid short-dated calls in a sector where hedge funds are adding to shorts. A squeeze is possible, but a guide confirming AI pressure would extend the drawdown.

GameStop / GME  "Burry's bet revives the meme"

Observed Attention: 24/7 Wall St. reports Michael Burry is betting GameStop will 'level up', reviving attention on the original meme stock while megacap tech sells off.

Motivation & Bias: Authority bias and nostalgia: a famous contrarian's position lends credibility, and the meme playbook offers a lottery-ticket payoff when the index leaders are falling.

Instrument & Risk: GME options carry very high implied volatility; if participating, use defined-risk spreads and keep size small. The investment case is separate from the meme, and momentum can reverse as fast as it builds.

BitMine / BMNR  "Ark keeps buying the ether proxy"

Instrument & Risk: BMNR is an equity proxy for ether; with spot bitcoin ETFs bleeding $2.9B, crypto-treasury premiums can compress further. Prefer spot ETFs and hedge any proxy position with puts.

SpaceX IPO / early index entry  "the access trade"

Observed Attention: SpaceX is seeking early index entry as it prepares a massive IPO, turning a private company into a retail talking point well before any listing.

Motivation & Bias: Retail treats index inclusion as guaranteed demand; the bias is assuming forced passive buying can only push prices up.

Instrument & Risk: Until a listing exists, proxies carry premiums that can collapse on timing news. If early index entry is granted, index funds would need to sell other holdings to make room — a hidden cost for existing megacap holders.
