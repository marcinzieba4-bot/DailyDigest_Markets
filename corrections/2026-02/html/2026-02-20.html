🧮 Earnings Pattern — What the Market Pays For

Guidance and balance sheets beat the beat

Rewarded: Companies that paired results with raised guidance, growth or capital returns: Deere jumped on a higher outlook WSJ [Feb 19], Occidental on a debt cut and dividend hike 24/7 Wall St. [Feb 19], DoorDash on a stronger growth forecast Reuters [Feb 19], and Applied Materials on an AI and memory-driven forecast Bloomberg.com [Feb 13].

Punished: Beats with soft guidance were sold: Walmart's cautious outlook overshadowed steady sales Reuters [Feb 19], EPAM plunged despite beating Barron's [Feb 19] and Medtronic fell on an unchanged outlook Reuters [Feb 17]. Tariff-hit results drew the harshest reactions, with Pinterest down nearly 17% CNBC [Feb 13], while Mercedes' earnings more than halved Automotive News [Feb 12].

Pattern: The market pays for forward visibility in the physical economy and in AI hardware, and discounts beats in services exposed to AI substitution or tariff-squeezed customers. Capital discipline — debt reduction, buybacks, dividends — is rewarded more than volume growth, consistent with energy and industrials leading.

Attention into EPS: Nvidia on Feb. 25 is the dominant event The Motley Fool [Feb 17]. With the stock up only 2.50% over 20 sessions at 187.90 (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2026-02-19), the bar is guidance on data-centre demand and supply, not the headline beat.

Playbook: Into reports, buy names where guidance risk is skewed up — equipment, memory, industrial capex — and sell beats in software and consumer platforms that lack guidance raises. Fade post-earnings pops that are not backed by higher full-year guidance, and treat a beat-and-hold as a sell signal in crowded defensives.
