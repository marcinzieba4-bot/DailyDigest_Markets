🇺🇸 United States  Mixed  hold / data dependent

Macro Stance: The Fed is on hold with a hawkish edge, and the market has stopped pricing cuts. The minutes of the latest FOMC meeting, published on February 18, showed a committee that is confident in labour-market stability but unsure about inflation and increasingly divided on the next move, and the Financial Times-style reading in the headlines is that some members are open to a hike rather than a cut. Fortune’s headline ‘Warsh’s Fed headache’ puts it plainly: some members are open to hiking, not cutting. The WSJ read of the same minutes is that there is little appetite for rate cuts at all. Axios advises against counting on another cut soon. The Fed also confirmed that its unusual ‘rate check’ in the dollar was made at the Treasury’s request, a detail that matters for anyone who treats the dollar as a policy tool. Weekly jobless claims fell by more than expected to 206,000, the largest drop since November, which supports the view that the labour market is stable rather than cracking.

Rates & Curve: The Treasury par curve at the prior close shows a 2-year yield of 3.47% and a 10-year yield of 4.08%, a 10Y−2Y spread of 61 bps and a 10Y−3M spread of 39 bps (US Treasury, prior close 2026-02-19). Over 20 observations the 2-year is down 13 bps and the 10-year is down 18 bps, so the 10Y−2Y spread has narrowed by 5 bps. Over five observations the 2-year is down 5 bps and the 10-year is down 10 bps (US Treasury, prior close 2026-02-19). The curve is described as moderately steep, approaching the long-run average, and the move over 20 sessions is a bull flattening, with both yields lower and the 10-year falling more. The bond rally is recent and shallow: the headlines show Treasuries had their worst run in a month on inflation fears on February 19, and yields were little changed after a strong economic data print. The S&P 500 closed at 6,861.89 and the VIX at 20.23, up 19.7% over 20 sessions (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2026-02-19). The dollar index stands at 97.93, down 0.84% over 20 sessions (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2026-02-19).

New / Most Important: The most important US story is the combination of a Fed that will not cut soon and a bond market that has priced some of that out. Reuters reported that the dollar advanced after data pointed to a stable labour market. Foreign investors bought $720.1B of US stocks in 2025, up 134% on the year, and Bloomberg reports that foreigners bought a net $1.6 trillion of assets, which is a direct rebuttal of the ‘sell America’ narrative. Mortgage rates have fallen to their lowest level in more than three years, which helps housing demand even while pending home sales fell again in January. Morningstar flags a K-shaped economy as the risk to watch. The equity market has been less generous than the flows. The S&P 500 closed flat to lower after mixed data, and the Nasdaq 100 is down 2.09% over 20 sessions (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2026-02-19).

Playbook ✓: Stance Mixed. Own the front end and keep duration modest: with the 2-year at 3.47% and the Fed hawkish, the carry on 2-year paper is adequate, but the 20-session rally in long bonds has already happened, so do not chase TLT on the strength of one print. Hedge equity beta with the VIX, which has risen 3.33 points over 20 sessions, rather than selling the index outright. The trigger to get more constructive is a cooler inflation print and a Fed that stops talking about hikes, both of which would validate the bull flattening. The trigger to get defensive is a renewed rise in long yields that pushes the 30-year back above its recent level, which would damage the housing recovery that mortgage rates are now supporting. A second caution: the foreign-buying story is the reason US equities have held up, so any sign of reversal in those flows would remove the main support under the index.

🇪🇺 European Union  Mixed  ECB succession / stuck growth

Macro Stance: Europe is caught between an ECB leadership change and a growth outlook that is not improving. The Bundesbank expects a weak first quarter for Germany, with a rebound still to come, and a German chamber of commerce forecast a growth rate of just 1% for 2026. The Financial Times reported that Christine Lagarde may leave the ECB before the end of her term, and the Bloomberg and WSJ follow-ups carried her own line that her baseline is still to finish the term. Reuters quoted her signalling no imminent exit. The succession question is therefore live but not settled, and it has already produced commentary about political meddling and the independence of the central bank. Europe’s equity market has been strong in the meantime: the STOXX 600 is up 2.71% over 20 sessions and the Euro Stoxx 50 is up 1.74%, and the earnings season has so far been better than feared.

Credit & Inflation: Euro-area credit is not the problem; the risks are on the bank-regulation and supervision side. The ECB fined JPMorgan’s European arm 12.2 million euros for misreporting capital requirements, and the ECB estimates that the digital euro would cost EU banks 4 to 6 billion euros over four years. Those are structural costs rather than credit signals. On the market side, EUR/USD closed at 1.1789 and is up 0.99% over 20 sessions, while EUR/PLN is at 4.2139 and up 0.1% over 20 sessions (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2026-02-19). Inflation in Europe is less of a headline than in the UK, where CPI fell to 3.0% in January and the Bank of England is now debated in terms of cuts. The UK jobless rate climbed to a five-year high, which adds to the case for easing. The euro area itself is not seeing that inflation drop yet, and the German economy remains stuck.

New / Most Important: The most important European item is the ECB leadership question, because it sets the path for policy after the next meeting and for the euro. Bloomberg reported that European governments may want to fast-track the Lagarde succession, and the candidate list is being discussed in detail. A premature exit would narrow the field and shift the debate from policy to politics, which is the risk the commentary highlights. Separately, the German debate about China has changed. Merz is seeking strategic partnerships with China while the US tariff picture is uncertain, and The Economist argues that Germany has fallen out of love with China. The DW headline on the China shock says the rivalry is testing Merz and the German economy. For the car sector this is a direct read-across: BMW’s chief executive said German carmakers need China to compete globally, ahead of the Merz trip.

Playbook ✓: Stance Mixed. Keep European exposure through the broad STOXX 600 rather than through single-country calls, because the succession story and the growth outlook pull in different directions. Buy the pullbacks in European banks only if the ECB headlines stay about process rather than independence; that is the trigger to add. Hedge the euro against a sharp move in the dollar if the Lagarde story turns into an early exit, because the market will reprice the path of rates on that headline alone. The signal that would change the view is a clear ECB statement that the term is being finished, which would take the succession premium out of the euro and make European financials a plain beta trade. ⚠ Divergence: UK rate expectations are moving toward cuts, with traders betting on two Bank of England cuts this year, while the euro-area debate is about who runs the ECB rather than about the rate path.

🇨🇳 China  Bearish  weak demand / stimulus tilt

Macro Stance: The Chinese story is weak domestic demand that the authorities are trying to lift through services and tech rather than through credit or property. Xi’s stated priorities are ‘AI-plus’ and consumer spending as a counter to economic risks, and Caixin reports he is prioritising domestic demand and tech independence in the 2026 agenda. The IMF warns that China’s economic policies are causing damage to others, and its newest report finds the yuan undervalued. The consumer data is soft: January CPI rose 0.2% and by less than expected, and producer deflation persisted. Factory deflation eased more than forecast at the start of the year, but Reuters judged the battle against deflation to be only a small dent.

PMIs & Activity: The feed carries no fresh China manufacturing PMI in the window: the only PMI headline is an S&P Global China general services PMI dated February 4 with no figure in the headline, so the latest hard activity read is the January data. Exports look weak: a US port leader called exports to China ‘dismal’, and freight at the nation’s biggest port is in a slump. Japanese exports to China surged, which is a reminder that the China trade is still moving even as domestic demand is soft. The confirmation would be a January-to-February activity print that beats the consensus, which the feed does not yet contain. Until then the activity picture is a weak trend with the services economy as the supporting leg.

Credit Impulse: January new loans jumped but missed forecasts, and Reuters described weak demand as persisting. Economists are urging looser capital controls as the dollar dips, which tells you the authorities are thinking about the external channel rather than domestic credit. Provinces have cut revenue outlooks and analysts warn of debt control, which limits the fiscal impulse. Meituan’s warning of a 3.5 billion dollar loss amid a food-delivery price war is a sign that competition, not credit, is what is squeezing corporate profits. The credit impulse is therefore modest and mostly administrative. The confirmation that would change the view is a sustained acceleration in new loans that beats forecasts for two months running.

Property: Property is still the largest drag. The headlines say home prices kept falling with no floor yet despite policy support. The Caixin headline on the slump easing after the stimulus push is the bull case, while used-home prices fell at a slower pace in relief to the market. State firms are buying foreclosed properties to slow the downturn. The S&P Global view is that the property slump will be worse than expected this year. The green shoots story is not yet credible: property mega-deals returning to Asia-Pacific and a Chinese developer’s revival through US ventures are the exceptions, not the trend. The trigger that would change the stance is a sustained turn in home prices, not a single month of slower declines.

Fiscal, Policy & Trade: Fiscal policy is about tilting spending to consumers and services. The authorities plan to build a 1.5 trillion yuan tea industry by 2030 and to stage a consumer push around the Spring Festival, which is the policy language of a demand push rather than a credit push. China’s commerce ministry wants more adequate trade-in programme funding to support domestic consumption. On trade, the US trade deficit with China fell to a 21-year low, which the headlines attribute to tariffs, and Bloomberg reports that US imports from Taiwan now exceed those from China for the first time in decades. Zero-tariff access for 53 African nations is a trade pivot the Chinese side has announced. Japan’s Takaichi is pursuing a rare-earth partnership with the US, which is a further sign of supply-chain re-routing.

Markets & Flows: The Shanghai Composite closed at 4,082.07 on February 13, down 0.48% over 20 sessions (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2026-02-13). The Hang Seng closed at 26,705.94 on February 16 and is up 0.53% over 20 sessions (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2026-02-16), and Hong Kong stocks finished the lunar year with their best performance in eight years. The CSI 300 ETF ASHR closed at 33.88 and is up 0.44% over 20 sessions, while the China large-cap ETF FXI closed at 38.61, down 1.86% over 20 sessions, and the China internet ETF KWEB closed at 32.75, down 7.95% over 20 sessions (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2026-02-19). USD/CNY is at 6.9080 and has moved down 0.8% over 20 sessions, which is a yuan that is strengthening slowly rather than collapsing. Chinese retail is actively buying gold, a theme the headlines link to the lunar new year.

New / Most Important: The most important China headline is the IMF’s finding that the yuan is undervalued, paired with its warning that China’s policies are hurting others. That is a bullish signal for the currency and a bearish one for the growth story, which is the divergence the stance is built on. Policymakers are also tightening supervision of banks: a large bank has been put under stricter watch and Zheshang Bank was added to the systemically important lenders list. The equity side is mixed. A Bloomberg piece says the stock bull run is faltering with earnings set to underwhelm, and Hong Kong stocks declined after the US AI-driven selloff. Playbook ✓: Stance Bearish on domestic demand, and neutral on the currency. Stay underweight the China internet complex, because KWEB is down 7.95% over 20 sessions and the consumer spending push has not shown up in the data, and do not chase the Hang Seng on the best-year headline. Prefer the currency as the expression of an undervalued yuan rather than equity beta, and wait for a credit beat before adding to the CSI 300 through ASHR. The trigger to get more positive is two months of new-loan beats together with a turn in home prices, and the trigger to get more negative is a renewed rise in the deflation prints. ⚠ Divergence: the IMF’s undervaluation finding and the strong Hong Kong year are pointing to a stronger external position even as domestic demand is weak, which is why the currency and the equity market are telling different stories.

🧮 Earnings Pattern — What the Market Pays For

Pattern: Guidance and the quality of the forward line are dominating the reaction, and a headline beat is not enough. Playbook: Buy the guidance raisers and avoid the beats with soft outlooks. The trigger for the trade is a forward raise in the next print, not the headline EPS number. Into Nvidia, the setup favours owning the chip supply chain that benefits from the spend while hedging the software names that have been punished for capex.
