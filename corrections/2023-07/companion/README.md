# July 2023 Market Intelligence Research Companion

**Research companion · Monday, July 31, 2023 · 00:00 UTC**

The US economy gave investors something they had spent much of the year doubting they could have together. Consumer spending rose in real terms as inflation eased. The Fed still raised rates. Intel could rally on a quarter in which its sales fell, and a company warning about its next interest payment could become one of the market’s most discussed stocks. What joined those trades was the starting expectation. Intel had to show the PC slump was easing. Tupperware needed another willing buyer before it needed another customer. Those are very different ways to make money. # Geographic Macro Overview

## United States · Mixed

The most important detail in Friday’s inflation release was what Americans were doing while inflation fell. Real consumption rose 0.4% in June. Headline PCE inflation eased to 3.0% year over year, with core at 4.1% and up 0.2% on the month. Thursday’s advance estimate put second-quarter real GDP growth at a 2.4% annual rate. A business planning its inventory could reasonably take heart from those numbers. A bondholder counting on an imminent rate cut still had to contend with core inflation and a Fed that had just raised its target to 5.25–5.50%. [BEA personal income and outlays, July 28](https://www.bea.gov/news/2023/personal-income-and-outlays-june-2023) · [BEA advance GDP estimate, July 27](https://www.bea.gov/news/2023/gross-domestic-product-second-quarter-2023-advance-estimate) · [Federal Reserve, July 26](https://www.federalreserve.gov/newsevents/pressreleases/monetary20230726a.htm)

**Rates and curve.** Friday’s two-year Treasury yielded 4.87% and the ten-year 3.96%. The ten-year sat 91 basis points below the two-year. Twenty Treasury observations earlier, on June 29, the short yield was the same and the ten-year was 3.85%. All 11 basis points of steepening came from the long end selling off. An equity investor interpreting that move as cheaper money would have the direction of the financing change backwards. The S&P 500 finished Friday at 4,582.23 and the Nasdaq Composite at 14,316.66. For expensive growth shares to hold those prices as long yields rise, actual earnings must take over some of the work previously done by valuation. [US Treasury, July par yields](https://home.treasury.gov/resource-center/data-chart-center/interest-rates/TextView?type=daily_treasury_yield_curve&field_tdr_date_value_month=202307) · [US Treasury, June par yields](https://home.treasury.gov/resource-center/data-chart-center/interest-rates/TextView?type=daily_treasury_yield_curve&field_tdr_date_value_month=202306) · [July 28 cross-market close](https://en.stockq.org/stock/history/2023/07/20230728_en.php)

**Playbook.** Look for businesses that can keep unit volumes and gross margins together as inflation cools. A resilient consumer helps a retailer move inventory. It does much less for a company whose earnings depend on passing through another round of price increases. A weaker consumption print alongside persistent core inflation would damage both sides of that distinction. [BEA personal income and outlays, July 28](https://www.bea.gov/news/2023/personal-income-and-outlays-june-2023) · [Procter & Gamble results, July 28](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/80424/000008042423000059/fy2223q4amj8-kexhibit991.htm)

## Euro area · Bearish

The ECB announced another quarter-point increase on July 27. The deposit rate was set to reach 3.75% on August 2. The effective date matters to a treasury desk, but loan officers were already feeling the tightening. The ECB’s bank lending survey showed tighter credit standards and weaker business demand for loans. A manufacturer can pay a higher rate if its order book justifies expansion. When the same manufacturer scraps the investment altogether, the loss falls on its equipment suppliers as well as on its bank. Watch the next loan survey for businesses returning to the market and lenders willing to fund them. A rate pause by itself would leave that second condition unresolved. [ECB decision, July 27](https://www.ecb.europa.eu/press/pr/date/2023/html/ecb.mp230727~da80cfcf24.en.html) · [ECB bank lending survey, July 25](https://www.ecb.europa.eu/press/pr/date/2023/html/ecb.pr230725~8358d3939d.en.html)

## China · Mixed

The headline recovery looked stronger over a year than it did over a quarter. Second-quarter GDP grew 6.3% year over year and 0.8% from the first quarter. June’s manufacturing PMI was 49.0. Over the first half, property development investment fell 7.9% and the floor area of commercial buildings sold fell 5.3%. A producer supplying export factories or unfinished developments could experience weak orders even as headline GDP accelerated against a depressed comparison. [China National Bureau of Statistics, July 17](https://www.stats.gov.cn/english/PressRelease/202307/t20230715_1941276.html)

**Credit and property.** New yuan loans reached RMB 3.05 trillion in June. A lender refinancing an existing obligation and a buyer borrowing for a newly sold home can both add to credit. They have very different implications for cement orders, developer cash receipts and consumer confidence. On July 24 the Politburo called for property policy to reflect changed conditions. The housing minister subsequently urged implementation of lower down payments and mortgage rates for eligible first-time buyers, along with delivery of presold homes. Completing a home already paid for can restore trust. A lower down payment is useful only when a household feels confident enough to sign. [China lending statistics, July 11](https://english.www.gov.cn/archive/statistics/202307/11/content_WS64ad48bcc6d0868f4e8ddafb.html) · [Politburo meeting, July 24](https://english.www.gov.cn/news/202307/24/content_WS64be6483c6d0868f4e8de104.html) · [Chinese housing minister’s remarks, July 28](https://english.www.gov.cn/news/202307/28/content_WS64c2fcf5c6d0868f4e8de250.html)

**Playbook.** Treat policy-sensitive shares as a bet on implementation until local rules change and contracts are signed. Watch sales area and completions alongside credit volumes. Stronger borrowing while sales remain weak would help explain support for lenders without providing a reason to raise building-material volume forecasts. [China National Bureau of Statistics, July 17](https://www.stats.gov.cn/english/PressRelease/202307/t20230715_1941276.html) · [Politburo meeting, July 24](https://english.www.gov.cn/news/202307/24/content_WS64be6483c6d0868f4e8de104.html)

## Japan · Mixed

The Bank of Japan kept its short policy rate at −0.1% and its ten-year target around zero. The change was in how it would defend that target. The previous half-percentage-point bounds became references, with daily fixed-rate purchases offered at 1.0%. The distinction matters to a bank holding a large JGB book. The overnight funding rate can stay low while longer bonds lose value. Whether better reinvestment yields compensate depends on how quickly liabilities reprice and when bonds mature. [Bank of Japan, July 28](https://www.boj.or.jp/en/mopo/mpmdeci/mpr_2023/k230728a.pdf)

Japan’s preliminary June industrial production rose 2.0% month over month. The release arrived at 08:50 in Tokyo on July 31, ten minutes before this briefing’s UTC cutoff. That was genuine evidence of stronger factory activity. It gave exporters a useful counterweight to uncertainty over the yield curve. Watch whether orders sustain the rise before assuming that one month of production translates into a durable increase in profits. [Japan METI preliminary release](https://www.meti.go.jp/english/statistics/tyo/iip/b2020_202306se.html) · [Bank of Japan, July 28](https://www.boj.or.jp/en/mopo/mpmdeci/mpr_2023/k230728a.pdf)

## Australia · Mixed

The RBA held its cash rate at 4.10% early in July. Later that month, the statistics bureau reported consumer prices up 0.8% in the June quarter and 6.0% over a year. June retail turnover fell 0.8% even in nominal terms. A retailer usually hopes an end-of-financial-year promotion will bring enough extra customers through the door to pay for the discount. A fall in receipts makes the margin question harder. The pause gave the bank time to see how much tightening was still moving through household budgets. Another soft retail reading would strengthen that argument, particularly if quarterly inflation slowed again. [Reserve Bank of Australia, July 4](https://www.rba.gov.au/media-releases/2023/mr-23-16.html) · [ABS quarterly CPI, July 26](https://www.abs.gov.au/media-centre/media-releases/cpi-rose-08-cent-june-2023-quarter) · [ABS retail turnover, July 28](https://www.abs.gov.au/media-centre/media-releases/less-spending-mid-year-sales-sees-retail-turnover-fall)

## Poland · Mixed

The NBP held its reference rate at 6.75%. June industrial production fell 1.4% year over year and inflation-adjusted retail sales fell 4.7%. A bank and a retailer could read the same interest-rate decision quite differently. Santander Bank Polska reported PLN 6.3 billion of first-half net interest income. Its deposits were up 9% year over year while gross customer receivables grew 3%. High interest income can support the current quarter while sluggish credit demand and competition for deposits weigh on the next one. Friday’s shares fell 2.61%. The upgrade gave shareholders a better estimate of full-year earnings while leaving a harder question about the earnings power of the next six months. On the household side, the Senate passed the increase in the monthly child benefit from PLN 500 to PLN 800. The measure awaited the president’s signature and envisaged payments from January 2024. July shop takings could tell investors about current purchasing power. They could tell them little about spending out of a future benefit. A statement about a movement on the Belarusian side establishes a political and security concern. A forecast for a Polish freight operator requires evidence about delays, restrictions or actual routes. For domestic equities, track margins and real activity first and identify which firms have direct border exposure before assigning an earnings cost. # Sector Deep Dive

**Technology · XLK.** Microsoft’s Azure and other cloud services grew 26% year over year, or 27% in constant currency. That shows customers still bought more computing capacity while companies scrutinised their budgets. For the AI trade the harder question is how much extra capacity customers will pay for once initial trials end. Cloud growth bundles established workloads and new products together. A better signal would be sustained growth alongside evidence that incremental sales cover the cost of new infrastructure. [Microsoft results, July 25](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/investor/earnings/fy-2023-q4/press-release-webcast)

**Communication services · XLC.** Meta delivered 34% more ad impressions than a year earlier while the average price per ad fell 16%. More inventory and better distribution can keep revenue growing while advertisers still pay less for each placement. If pricing stabilises while impression growth persists, there is room for operating leverage. If volume growth slows first, the same figures reveal how much recovery the stock already demands. [Meta results, July 26](https://investor.atmeta.com/investor-news/press-release-details/2023/Meta-Reports-Second-Quarter-2023-Results/default.aspx)

**Financials · XLF.** U.S. Bancorp’s quarter-end deposits grew 3.2% sequentially while its average deposits fell 2.6%. Taxable-equivalent net interest income fell 4.7% from the prior quarter. The bank could enter July with more deposits and still have earned less on the funding it carried through spring. For the next quarter, follow average balances and what it costs to retain them. A stabilising account base may take time to repair the spread on a loan book. [U.S. Bancorp results, July 20](https://www.usbank.com/about-us-bank/company-blog/article-library/us-bancorp-reports-second-quarter-2023-results.html)

**Energy · XLE.** WTI settled at $80.58 and Brent at $84.99 on Friday, the fifth straight weekly advance. Chevron nevertheless reported second-quarter earnings of $6.0 billion against $11.6 billion a year earlier. The crude screen shows the marginal price today. The earnings release reflects volumes and realised prices over an entire quarter against an unusually profitable comparison. A bullish case needs prices to persist long enough to reach cash flow. A five-week rally and a year-over-year profit decline can comfortably coexist. [Reuters oil close, July 28](https://www.investing.com/news/commodities-news/oil-prices-open-lower-on-demand-concerns-remain-on-track-for-weekly-gain-3136817) · [Chevron results, July 28](https://chevroncorp.gcs-web.com/node/35026)

**Industrials · XLI.** GE booked $8.3 billion of renewable-energy orders in the quarter. A large turbine order provides work, but signing it also locks in obligations for materials, labour, transport and performance. Track the conversion of those contracts into margin and cash. Aerospace service demand has a different clock, tied to engines returning for work. Looking at the two businesses separately gives a clearer earnings signal than treating a bigger group order book as cash already in hand. [GE results, July 25](https://www.ge.com/news/press-releases/ge-announces-second-quarter-2023-results)

**Consumer discretionary · XLY.** Ford Pro earned $2.4 billion of quarterly EBIT, while the Model e electric-vehicle business lost about $1.1 billion. The commercial division can help finance a ramp that still loses money. That transfer matters to a Ford shareholder in a way the industry’s EV delivery growth alone cannot capture. Watch whether improving electric-model volumes lower the loss per vehicle without requiring further discounts. A healthy commercial order book buys time to find out. [Ford results, July 27](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/38009/000003800923000044/exhibit99tojuly272023for.htm)

**Consumer staples · XLP.** Procter & Gamble’s 8% organic sales growth included seven percentage points from pricing, two from mix and a one-point volume decline. A household continuing to buy detergent at a higher price protects revenue. Buying a little less with each shopping trip eventually matters to factories and retailers. As inflation cools, compare unit volumes with the slowing price contribution. Flat prices with recovering units would be a stronger sign of underlying demand than another quarter of price-led nominal growth. [Procter & Gamble results, July 28](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/80424/000008042423000059/fy2223q4amj8-kexhibit991.htm)

**Healthcare · XLV.** Roche’s diagnostics division is a useful read-through for test and equipment suppliers. Its first-half sales fell 23% at constant exchange rates while sales excluding COVID products rose 6%. The headline fall combines a disappearing pandemic market with growth elsewhere. For peer comparisons, ask how much of the extraordinary test base remains in the prior-year numbers and whether routine diagnostics can support the installed equipment. [Roche half-year results, July 27](https://www.roche.com/investors/updates/inv-update-2023-07-27)

**Materials · XLB.** Dow reported sales of $11.4 billion, down 27% year over year. Local prices fell 18% and volumes fell 8%. Cheaper energy or feedstock can improve a producer’s spread, but operating a chemical plant below capacity still spreads fixed costs over fewer tonnes. For a turn in the cycle, watch shipment volumes and the difference between product and input prices together. A rebound driven solely by input inflation gives shareholders a very different margin outlook. [Dow results, July 25](https://investors.dow.com/en/news/news-details/2023/Dow-reports-second-quarter-2023-results/default.aspx)

**Real estate · XLRE.** Prologis reported 97.5% average occupancy and a 48.1% cash rent increase on leases that commenced in the quarter. That increase describes the leases turning over. Existing contracts elsewhere in the portfolio still reset on their own schedules. As long yields rose, the offset for a warehouse owner was the ability to renew leases at higher rents. New leasing activity, occupancy and the cost of the next refinancing tell investors how much of the headline uplift accrues to equity. [Prologis results, July 18](https://ir.prologis.com/financials/sec-filings/content/0000950170-23-033263/pld-ex99_2.htm) · [US Treasury, July par yields](https://home.treasury.gov/resource-center/data-chart-center/interest-rates/TextView?type=daily_treasury_yield_curve&field_tdr_date_value_month=202307)

**Utilities · XLU.** NextEra’s adjusted earnings reached $0.88 per share against $0.81 a year earlier. Its renewables and storage backlog added roughly 1,665 MW since the previous earnings update. The margin between a project’s contracted revenue and its financing and construction costs matters more than the nameplate capacity. If financing costs stay high, investors should look for projects reaching service on time and at the economics assumed when contracts were signed. [NextEra results, July 25](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/753308/000075330823000042/neeq22023exhibit99.htm)

**European autos · SXAP.** Mercedes-Benz Cars reported a 13.5% adjusted return on sales against 14.2% a year earlier. Premium mix can defend margins while mass-market manufacturers compete on price. Volkswagen’s XPeng agreement shows a different response to the Chinese market, buying access to local technology and development speed. Both strategies take time. Pricing and order mix are the near-term evidence for Mercedes. Delivery milestones and contractual progress are the better tests for the partnership. [Mercedes-Benz results, July 27](https://group.mercedes-benz.com/investors/reports-news/interim-reports/q2-2023/) · [Volkswagen and XPeng, July 26](https://www.volkswagen-group.com/en/press-releases/more-e-models-for-fast-growing-e-mobility-market-in-china-vw-brand-and-audi-agree-strategic-cooperations-with-local-automakers-17497)

**US and Asian semiconductors · SOXX.** TSMC’s dollar revenue fell 13.7% year over year to $15.68 billion, while guidance of $16.7–17.5 billion pointed to a sequential improvement. Intel’s data-centre and AI segment revenue was down 15% year over year. ASML booked €4.5 billion of orders, including €1.6 billion of EUV. A purchaser of equipment, a foundry and a chip designer record revenue at different points in the same investment. AI demand can be real while clients still draw down PC inventory or delay equipment. Look for the recovery to show up in each company’s own orders and utilisation before projecting it across the whole chain. # Earnings Pattern

**What the market rewarded.** By July 28, 51% of S&P 500 companies had reported. Four in five beat EPS estimates, but the aggregate surprise was 5.9%, below FactSet’s five-year average of 8.4%. Its blended estimate still put earnings 7.3% below the prior year. Forecasts had fallen far enough that a company could clear the hurdle while reporting less profit than last summer. Intel illustrates the trade. It closed Friday at $36.83, up 6.6%, after delivering positive adjusted EPS on revenue down 15% year over year. An investor buying that reaction was paying for a change in the direction of expectations. Sustained earnings growth still had to follow. [FactSet earnings update, July 28](https://insight.factset.com/sp-500-earnings-season-update-july-28-2023) · [Intel results, July 27](https://www.intc.com/news-events/press-releases/detail/1637/intel-reports-second-quarter-2023-financial-results) · [July 28 closing prices](https://www.investing.com/news/stock-market-news/us-stocks-higher-at-close-of-trade-dow-jones-industrial-average-up-050-3137966)

**What investors could distinguish.** Alphabet’s Cloud business earned $395 million in operating income on $8.031 billion of revenue, after an operating loss of $590 million a year earlier. Unlike a press release about generative AI, a change in reported segment profit can be followed through to group earnings. Even here the source of improvement matters. Better use of existing capacity can lift margins until another round of investment is needed. P&G faced the opposite question. How much of the margin it gained from pricing would it retain as inflation eased and customers reconsidered quantities? The common discipline is to identify which reported number could repeat and which benefited from an unusually low prior comparison. [Alphabet results, July 25](https://s206.q4cdn.com/479360582/files/doc_financials/2023/q2/2023q2-alphabet-earnings-release.pdf) · [Procter & Gamble results, July 28](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/80424/000008042423000059/fy2223q4amj8-kexhibit991.htm)

**Into the next reports.** Ahead of AMD, distinguish server demand from the PC inventory cycle. Ahead of Apple, compare the services contribution with demand for replacement hardware. A rally on better-than-feared sales is vulnerable if the next quarter still requires discounts or heavy investment to sustain them. # Underowned Themes

**Warehouse lease renewals.** Investors looking at industrial property through the ten-year yield alone can miss the rent already embedded in older leases. Prologis gives that idea an observable test. Compare the cash rent increase on leases as they roll with occupancy and renewal volumes. It is possible to report very high spreads on the best properties while tenants become harder to find elsewhere. [Prologis results, July 18](https://ir.prologis.com/financials/sec-filings/content/0000950170-23-033263/pld-ex99_2.htm)

**Commercial fleets.** Ford Pro’s profit gives the commercial franchise room to finance investment across the group. Watch operating earnings from vans, trucks and services alongside the Model e loss. The discount on an electric SUV makes news immediately. Changes in a fleet customer’s replacement schedule can matter more to near-term group cash generation. A downturn in commercial demand would remove part of the financial cushion. [Ford results, July 27](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/38009/000003800923000044/exhibit99tojuly272023for.htm)

**Routine diagnostics.** Roche’s underlying diagnostics growth shows how the decline in COVID testing can obscure expansion in ordinary medical tests. The investment question is whether persistent demand for reagents and services can support earnings as emergency orders disappear. A weaker routine business would remove this source of resilience even if the headline comparison eventually looks easier. [Roche half-year results, July 27](https://www.roche.com/investors/updates/inv-update-2023-07-27)

**Polish corporate margins.** Kęty’s forecast offers a way to distinguish a lasting improvement in a manufacturer’s economics from a favourable half year. Reported revenue was never the strongest part of the upgrade. The question is how much of the forecast earnings improvement survives the margin pressure management itself expected. The term “underowned” here identifies avenues for research. Direct ownership estimates would be needed to claim a crowded or neglected position. # Capital Flows and Liquidity

**Where the cash went.** Lipper recorded $35.5 billion of net inflows across its US fund universe in the week ended July 26. Money-market funds took in $28.8 billion, equity funds $3.2 billion and taxable bond funds $2.9 billion. A week in which equity prices rise and investors add to equities can also be a week in which the much larger cash allocation earns a competitive return. It would be a mistake to treat the entire balance in money funds as a pending equity order. Some holders need cash for expenses, some have short investment horizons and some may prefer the available yield. [LSEG Lipper, July 27](https://lipperalpha.refinitiv.com/2023/07/u-s-weekly-fundflows-insight-report-despite-fed-interest-rate-hike-conventional-fund-and-etf-investors-pad-the-coffers-of-fixed-income-funds-for-the-fund-flows-week/) · [Federal Reserve, July 26](https://www.federalreserve.gov/newsevents/pressreleases/monetary20230726a.htm)

**What retail added.** Reuters reported Vanda’s estimate of roughly $1.27 billion a day in US retail equity inflows month to date as of July 25. The same report identified an eleven-month high in retail net purchases of AMC on July 24. The broad estimate describes a category of investor, the AMC number one security and day, and Lipper the fund vehicles in its sample.
