🇺🇸 United States  Mixed  hold / data dependent

Macro Stance: The US tape is caught between a softening consumer and a Fed that is not yet ready to lean on the cut button. Consumer sentiment slumped in March to its lowest level since 2022 as tariffs fed inflation worries [1], and retail sales came in weaker than expected, another bad sign for the economy [2]. Wholesale prices were flat in February against an expected rise [3], and headline CPI eased to 2.8% in February, below forecasts [4]. The labour and price backdrop is therefore not the problem; the problem is demand. J.P. Morgan’s economist put the odds of a US recession at 40% [5], and Goldman Sachs cut its US growth forecasts as tariff effects grew “considerably more adverse” [6]. Bessent insists a “detox” period for the economy does not have to become a recession [7], but the market is not yet buying the reassurance. The S&P 500 fell into correction territory as the trade war escalated [8], and US equities lost $5 trillion in value over three weeks [9].

Rates & Curve: The 2Y closed at 4.06% and the 10Y at 4.31%, with the 10Y−2Y spread at +25 bps (US Treasury, prior close 2025-03-17). The curve is positively sloped, and the 20-observation move is bull steepening: the 2Y is down 20 bps from 4.26% and the 10Y is down 16 bps, so the front end is doing most of the repricing, which is what a market pricing rate cuts looks like. The 10Y−2Y spread has widened 4 bps over the same window (US Treasury, prior close 2025-03-17). Over five sessions the 2Y is up 17 bps and the 10Y up 9 bps, so the rally has already partly reversed. The S&P 500 closed at 5,675.12, up 0.64% in the prior session but down 7.19% over 20 sessions (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2025-03-17). The VIX finished at 20.51, down 5.79% in the prior session but up 38.86% over 20 sessions (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2025-03-17). The US Dollar Index stood at 103.37 index points, down 3.00% over 20 sessions (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2025-03-17).

New / Most Important: The Federal Reserve meets on March 19, and that is the date rates desks are likely working toward [10]. Bloomberg reported that the Fed was expected to cut rates twice this year, starting in September [11], and an economist quoted by Barron’s sees slowing growth and multiple cuts ahead [12]. Morningstar’s preview is pitched as “What to Expect Wednesday” [13]. Institutional tone matters as much as the rate call: the Fed’s top bank supervisor seat is going to Michelle Bowman, who was nominated as vice chair for supervision [14], and the nomination replaces Michael Barr in that post [15]. Fiscal risk has been pushed out for six months after Trump signed the bill to fund the government [16], which removes a shutdown event from the front of the calendar. The tariff channel is the open question: the White House said there will be no exemptions from steel and aluminum tariffs [17], and tariffs on lumber and appliances set the stage for higher new-home and remodelling costs [18].

Playbook ✓: Stay underweight the cyclical and high-beta leaders into the Fed decision and keep index exposure at reduced size rather than chasing the five-session bounce. Own the front end of the curve on any pullback that does not lift the 2Y back toward the 4.26% level of 20 sessions ago, because that is where a cut path gets priced first. Hedge equity risk with a modest VIX position, since the index is still 7.19% below its level of 20 sessions ago and implied volatility has already risen 38.86% over the same window (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2025-03-17). What changes the call: a Fed statement that pushes the first cut out of September, a 10Y that breaks higher while equities hold, or tariff exemptions that reverse the no-exemptions line. Until one of those happens, the risk is a sharp dip-and-recover pattern, not a trend.

The ECB’s own wage tracker still points to negotiated wage pressures easing [24], which is the reason the easing bias survives. A poll now shows the ECB cutting by less than before as Europe ramps up spending [25].

That is a direct hit to European spirits and wine exporters, and it lands just as the German spending plan is meant to lift domestic demand. The DAX closed at 23,154.57, up 2.36% over five sessions and 1.56% over 20 sessions, which shows investors have bought the fiscal story rather than the trade story so far (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2025-03-17). Playbook ✓: Keep a modest overweight in German domestic cyclicals and the DAX, which benefit from the fiscal impulse, and avoid the export-heavy car names until US tariff terms are clear. Treat the euro as a funding currency against the dollar only while the ECB keeps its easing bias; if the 2.4% inflation print proves sticky, the trade reverses. Hedge the trade-war tail by holding protection on the auto complex, because the alcohol tariff threat shows the US is willing to target European sectors by name. The divergence to watch: the defence bid is strong, but it is funded by debt that Europe’s growth momentum has not yet absorbed. ⚠ Divergence: the rally in defence and the European auto ETF, down 5.86% over 20 sessions, are pulling the index in opposite directions (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2025-03-17).

🇨🇳 China  Mixed  consumption push / credit lag

Macro Stance: China is trying to replace export demand with household demand, and the latest evidence says the push is working at the margin but not yet in prices. Beijing announced a plan to “vigorously boost consumption”, and the plan was described as the most comprehensive since the 1970s [35][36]. Its 30-point toolbox is the detailed version [37], and the trade-in subsidies are being doubled down on to lift sluggish spending [38]. The leadership still anchors on a growth target of around 5% for 2025 [39]. Reuters reported that retail sales picked up as Beijing turned to consumers to ease the US trade pressure [40]. The stance is Mixed because the demand impulse is real while the credit and price data are not confirming it.

PMIs & Activity: The most recent factory survey in the window was February: the official manufacturing reading hit a three-month high, but the US tariff war clouds the outlook [41]. Factory activity picked up while trade uncertainty weighed [42]. The non-manufacturing gauge stood at 50.4 in February [43], which is only just expansionary. For January and February combined, industrial output rose 5.9%, beating market expectations [44], and Bloomberg reported that the economic pickup topped forecasts before tariff pain deepens [45]. Retail sales of consumer goods increased 4.0% over the same two months [46]. Those are the most recent activity prints, and as two-month figures they carry the seasonal distortion of the Lunar New Year.

Credit Impulse: Credit is the weak link in the consumption story. Bank lending in January hit a record high on policy stimulus [47], which looks strong until the composition is checked; the property-loan contraction eased after government support [48]. The central bank has said it will create “more tools” to shore up the economy [49], and Bank of China set up a $7 billion fund to support technology development [50]. Those are targeted instruments, not a broad credit push. A credit impulse that lifts household borrowing would confirm the consumption thesis; the absence of one is why the activity beat should be treated as a pickup rather than a turn.

Property: Property is still deflating while the policy response is being tested. Home prices fell at a faster pace even as the authorities tried to revive the housing market [51], and Reuters described the slump as persisting while stimulus struggles to stabilise the sector [52]. Developers remain the stress point: Sunac China warned of a wider loss for 2024 [53], and ICBC (Asia) is backing Hong Kong developers with loan support as the sector struggles [54]. The state is pushing quality rather than volume, and analysts warn that the push for better homes risks further developer stress [55]. Country Garden’s unit, meanwhile, forecast higher annual earnings [56]. Property is the sector where a bottom would show up first in credit, and it has not shown one yet.

Fiscal, Policy & Trade: Policy is leaning on consumers and on the central bank at the same time. The 30-point toolbox and the trade-in programme are fiscal in character [37][38], while the PBOC promise of new tools is monetary [49]. The trade channel is noisy: China renewed export registrations for US pork and poultry plants, a concession to farmers [57], and Xi may visit the US at some point, Trump said [58]. A Trump–Xi summit for June is being discussed [59]. The tariff dispute is not closed. China is taking issue with Trump’s move to link tariffs to fentanyl [60], and its agricultural tariffs on US goods took effect [61]. Trade policy is therefore a two-way event risk rather than a settled backdrop.

Markets & Flows: Equity markets have been leading the rebound. The Hang Seng closed at 24,145.57, up 0.77% in the prior session and up 6.76% over 20 sessions; the Shanghai Composite closed at 3,426.13 index points, up 2.09% over 20 sessions (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2025-03-17). The ASHR China A-share ETF finished at 27.74, up 2.06% over 20 sessions, while KWEB, the internet ETF, rose 7.41% over the same window to 38.29 (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2025-03-17). USD/CNY finished at 7.2371, down 0.22% over 20 sessions, a stable currency (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2025-03-17). Foreign money has noticed: Chinese stocks and EM debt saw large inflows in February, according to the IIF [62], and Reuters said Donald Trump had made Chinese stocks somewhat great again [63]. Foreign investors are also being drawn to high-yielding Chinese bank debt [64].

New / Most Important: The most important signal is the divergence between the activity beat and the flat price level. Consumer prices fell in February and remain flat in a sluggish economy [65], and an earlier warning that falling prices signal bigger troubles ahead stands [66]. A consumption plan can lift volumes while deflation keeps eroding margins, which is the bind Beijing is in. The growth target of around 5% [67] was set as the country reels from Trump’s tariffs, so the next step will show whether the tools move credit or only spending. Investors have pushed Hong Kong and China internet names higher in the meantime, which makes the rally more about policy expectation than about earnings.

Playbook ✓: Own the consumption and internet beneficiaries on dips, using KWEB and the Hang Seng as the vehicles, because the policy signal is the most direct it has been since the plan was announced. Keep the property exposure to the developer names that are already distressed out of the book, and treat any rally there as a trade rather than a recovery. Hedge the trade tail with USD/CNY at current levels, since the currency has been stable and there is no need to pay up for protection. What would change the call: a credit impulse that fails to confirm the consumption push, a new round of tariff linkage, or a home-price decline that accelerates. ⚠ Divergence: retail sales and output are up while consumer prices are flat, so the price line is the signal that contradicts the main story.
