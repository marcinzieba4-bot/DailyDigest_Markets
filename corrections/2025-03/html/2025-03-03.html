🌍 Geographic Macro Overview

🇺🇸 United States  Mixed  soft data, tariffs due

Macro Stance: Growth data softened into the report date while markets priced a slower path rather than a recession. The January PCE index rose 2.5%, in line with expectations [1], and the Fed’s preferred core gauge hit 2.6% [2], so inflation is not what is breaking the tape. Activity is. The Atlanta Fed indicator put first-quarter growth on track to be negative [3], and its nowcast saw the economy shrinking after Friday’s data [4]. Households are pulling back: consumer confidence fell by the most since 2021 [5], spending posted its first drop in almost two years [6], and weekly jobless claims jumped to 242,000 [7]. Fourth-quarter GDP was still 2.3% annualized [8], so the slowdown is a late-winter story, not a finished one.

Rates & Curve: The 10-year Treasury closed February at 4.24% and the 2-year at 3.99%, a 10Y−2Y spread of 25 bps (US Treasury, prior close 2025-02-28). Over 20 observations the 2-year fell 19 bps and the 10-year 28 bps, which leaves the spread 9 bps narrower. The curve is positively sloped and the move is a bull flattening, with the long end rallying more than the front. The five-observation move (2Y −20 bps, 10Y −18 bps; US Treasury, prior close 2025-02-28) says the bid is recent and still running, and Bloomberg reported a Treasury rally that sent yields back below 4% as inflation cooled [9]. Equities did not follow the bonds. The S&P 500 closed at 5,954.50, down 1.9% over 20 sessions and 1.0% over five (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2025-02-28). The Nasdaq 100 is down 2.9% over 20 sessions, and the Russell 2000, at 2,163.07, is down 6.3%, the sharpest drawdown of the three. The VIX stands at 19.63, up 3.79 points over 20 sessions (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2025-02-28), and the dollar index at 107.61 is down 0.2% over 20 sessions but up 0.9% over five (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2025-02-28).

New / Most Important: The tariff clock is the event to price. The administration has set March 4 for tariffs on Mexico and Canada, with the exact levels still to be set [10], and Trump has vowed an extra 10% on China over fentanyl in the same package [11]. Bessent says tariffs will not raise inflation [12], and that is the argument the bond market is buying: duties read as a growth tax rather than a price shock. Bessent has also promised an ‘affordability czar’ to deal with the cost effects [13]. For equities the threat already sits in the Russell 2000’s underperformance, which is where import-dependent and credit-sensitive companies trade.

Playbook ✓: Own duration into the jobs data and keep equity beta light. The bull flattening, with the 10-year down 28 bps over 20 sessions, says the bond market is pricing a softer labour market before the data confirm it. Reuters reports that investor angst about the economy will be tested by the next US jobs data [14], so the bid can extend if the print is weak. Add equity risk only when the Nasdaq 100 reclaims 21,508.12, its 20-session reference close, and the 10-year closes back above 4.52%, the level it held on January 30, which would be the sign the bond rally has run its course. Stay away from small caps until the tariff date has passed.

New / Most Important: The tariff is the event. Trump said 25% tariffs on the EU would be announced soon [22], and the Guardian warned the threat could trigger economic turmoil [23]. Playbook ✓: Own the defence bid as the hedge and treat a tariff announcement as the sell trigger for German cyclicals. Rheinmetall closed at EUR 1,006.50 after gaining 33.24% over 20 sessions and 12.56% over five (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2025-02-28), so the trade is already crowded and the risk is a peace headline that removes the premium quickly. The DAX’s 3.8% gain over 20 sessions discounts a deal, which makes a 25% announcement the test [22]. Avoid French banks and autos until the S&P outlook cut is absorbed, and treat a close back below 1.0395 on the euro as the sign that the tariff pass-through has begun.

🇨🇳 China  Mixed  stimulus wait, tariff squeeze

Macro Stance: China is stabilizing in the data but not yet recovering, and the tariff threat dominates. The Economist called it a moment of truth for Xi Jinping on deflation [28], and the early recovery signals come from factories rather than households. Washington has added an extra 10% on China alongside the Mexico and Canada duties [11], Beijing vowed all necessary measures against the new tariffs [29] and accused Washington of blackmail over fentanyl [30]. Consumers are cautious: sentiment lags even as blueberry sales boom [31].

PMIs & Activity: The official manufacturing gauge reached a three-month high in February [32], and the non-manufacturing PMI printed 50.4 for the month [33]. Reuters put the factory picture as activity picking up while trade uncertainties weigh [34]. The base is low: January’s official manufacturing PMI fell to 49.1 from 50.1 in December [35], and the Caixin reading showed growth slowing that month [36]. Activity is improving from a weak start, which is not the same as a recovery. A reading just above the 50 line is the one that matters for the credit cycle, since January’s print shows how quickly the index can slip back under it.

Credit Impulse: Policy is leaning on the banks rather than on large fiscal numbers. Reuters relayed a Bloomberg report of a $55 billion bank recapitalization [37], and it also reported that Chinese banks heeded a PBOC call to cut dollar deposit rates [38]. The yuan is the pressure valve: USD/CNY rose 1.45% over 20 sessions to 7.2853 (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2025-02-28), and regulators stepped up scrutiny of capital flows as the currency weakened [39]. A weaker yuan gives exporters room, and it gives the PBOC a reason to keep the banks cutting. The test is whether the recapitalization reaches the loan book, which is the next thing to watch.

Property: Property is still a drag, and the bottom is being argued over. Reuters said second-hand home prices fell for a seventh straight month, with the decline narrowing [40]: a narrowing, not a turn. Hedge funds are positioning for a turnaround in the unloved sector [41], while the sale of Evergrande’s remains is being fought over in pieces [42]. Developer credit is priced for the worst, and the equity bounce is in the offshore names rather than the onshore developers. Hong Kong’s bourse logged a 10% profit jump as trading turnover and IPOs surged [43], which is the offshore channel doing the work.

Fiscal, Policy & Trade: The trade lever is larger than the fiscal one. AP reported that Trump plans tariffs on Mexico and Canada while doubling the existing 10% tariffs on China [44], and Treasury’s Bessent urged Canada and Mexico to match US tariffs on China [45]. Mexico has proposed matching them [46]. Beijing is answering with trade and regulatory moves, targeting US agriculture [47] and requiring approvals for autonomous-driving software upgrades [48]. It also opened its telecoms sector to 13 foreign companies [49]: a reform signal aimed at the tech trade, not at the consumer.

Markets & Flows: The onshore and offshore markets disagree. On February 28 the Hang Seng closed at 22,941.32, up 13.4% over 20 sessions but down 3.3% in the prior session (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2025-02-28). The Shanghai Composite at 3,320.90 gained only 2.8% over 20 sessions, and the CSI 300 ETF (ASHR) 0.8%. KWEB, the China internet ETF, is down 7.4% over five sessions at 33.93. Alibaba’s US shares rose 29.0% over 20 sessions to 132.51 (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2025-02-28). Retail money is there for the new listings: Mixue’s debut popped 47% as Hong Kong IPO volumes rebounded [50], after a retail subscription book of nearly US$206 billion [51].

New / Most Important: The most important signal is the AI capex, which is not waiting for stimulus. Chinese buyers are ordering Nvidia’s newest AI chips in defiance of US curbs [52], and Alibaba has set a US$52 billion capex plan that it sees as a catalyst for China’s big tech in the AI race [53]. Alibaba made its AI video model free to use globally [54], and Jack Ma is back in public view [55]. The pricing signal is in Hong Kong, where the AI trade sits and the listing pipeline is busy: Chinese tech firms are eyeing Hong Kong IPOs amid the DeepSeek-triggered AI boom [56].

Playbook ✓: Own the AI capex names through the offshore listings and keep China-domestic cyclicals underweight until the credit impulse turns. Alibaba’s 29.0% gain over 20 sessions shows the market is paying for the AI spend, and the Hang Seng’s 13.4% lift is the same trade in an index. Fade rallies in the onshore Shanghai index while the banks are being recapitalized rather than rewarded. ⚠ Divergence: the Hang Seng is up 13.4% over 20 sessions while the onshore Shanghai Composite is up only 2.8%, so the market is paying for AI and not for a broad recovery.

Corporate & Sectors: Orlen is the dividend story. It proposed a record PLN 7 billion dividend for 2024 and the shares rose [93], and Bankier called it the national champion’s record cash stream [94]. The stock is up 16.6% over 20 sessions at PLN 62.70 (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2025-02-28). KGHM is the risk name. PZU named Andrzej Klesyk its new chief executive [99].

Geopolitics & Security: Security spending is a structural bid. The president has vowed to spend 4.7% of GDP on defence this year [100], Poland leased eight Apache gunships as a warm-up for a larger delivery [101], and Poland and Slovakia are teaming up on ammunition and weapons purchases [102]. Russian missiles hitting western Ukraine prompted Polish fighters to scramble [103], and a report found Russia paid operatives to paint anti-government graffiti in Poland [104]. The defence budget is also a fiscal argument, which is why the Balcerowicz critique [86] matters: the spending is political as well as military.

New / Most Important: The one signal that matters is the zloty. A currency at a seven-year high tightens conditions for exporters and squeezes bank margins, and Santander’s warning that it could weigh on GDP [83] puts the question directly to the MPC: is the strong zloty doing the disinflation work for it? The equity market has already decided that growth wins, as the Pekao and Dino moves show. The pace of the currency move, not its level, is what would change the call.

Playbook ✓: Own the domestic growth names, with banks and Dino as the core, and hedge the currency only above its reference. A EUR/PLN close above 4.1976, the reference from January 31, would mean the zloty has given back its 20-session gain, and the bank trade would need to be re-examined. Pekao is up 6.0% over 20 sessions at PLN 170.00 and Dino up 10.1% at PLN 495.60 (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2025-02-28). ⚠ Divergence: the zloty’s strength and the bank rally argue for growth, but KGH is down 6.0% over five sessions, which says the metals and export-earnings side is breaking the other way.
