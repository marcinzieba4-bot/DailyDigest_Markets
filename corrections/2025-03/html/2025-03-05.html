Equities confirm the growth worry: the S&P 500 closed at 5,778.15, down 1.2 percent in the prior session and 3.6 percent over 20 sessions (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2025-03-04).

Over 20 observations the 2-year is down 30 bps from 4.26 percent and the 10-year is down 32 bps from 4.54 percent, so the spread has narrowed by only 2 bps. The curve is positively sloped and the 20-observation move is a bull flattening. The 10Y-3M spread is still -12 bps against a 4.34 percent three-month bill, and the 30-year at 4.53 percent keeps the long end 31 bps above the 10-year. This is a growth-led rally, not a dovish Fed: Reuters reports that parts of the Treasury market are worried about a pause in rate cuts [12], and Cleveland's Hammack is urging patience on further cuts [13]. The dollar index is at 105.74 index points, down 2.98 percent over 20 sessions, and the VIX rose to 23.51, up 26.3 percent over 20 sessions and 21.0 percent over five (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2025-03-04; US Treasury, prior close 2025-03-04).

New / Most Important: The most important item is a split in the headlines that the market is hedging rather than resolving. Commerce Secretary Lutnick said the Canada tariffs are just getting started [14], while CNBC reported that Trump could scale back the Canada and Mexico tariffs on Wednesday [15]. A second risk sits inside government: the administration disbanded two expert panels on economic data [16], and the Commerce Secretary's comments on federal data raised fears of interference [17]. Democrats are serious about a shutdown [18], and CNN reports they rejected the GOP funding strategy [19]. Data credibility and shutdown risk arrive together, which is why the bond market is buying duration and equities are not.

Playbook ✓: Stay light on high-beta US growth into the Wednesday tariff decision. Own the 10-year as the hedge while it trades below the 4.54 percent level of 3 February, and use VIX calls rather than outright shorts while the VIX sits at 23.51. Change the call if the 10-year closes back above 4.54 percent with the S&P 500 holding 5,778.15: that would mean the bond bid is fading and the stagflation trade has won the argument. A shutdown that drags on pushes the same logic toward defensive staples.
