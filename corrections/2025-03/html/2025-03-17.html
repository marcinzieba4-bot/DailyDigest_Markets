🇺🇸 United States  Mixed  hold / data dependent

Macro Stance: The S&P 500 closed at 5,638.94 (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2025-03-14), down 7.79% over 20 sessions even after a 2.13% bounce in the latest session. The index has slipped into correction, and CNN put it bluntly: “Dow slides and S&P 500 closes in correction as Trump’s trade war escalates” [1]. The pressure is coming through the household. Consumer sentiment fell to its lowest since 2022 on tariff worries [2], and inflation expectations jumped [3]. The 25% tariffs on steel and aluminum imports took effect and Europe retaliated [4]. The data cut the other way: February CPI eased to 2.8%, below forecasts [5], and the wholesale price measure was flat [6]. Washington removed one risk as well, with the spending bill signed and the shutdown averted [7] [8]. The Fed decides on Wednesday, and the pre-meeting framing is simply what to expect [9]. JPMorgan’s economist puts the odds of a US recession at 40% [10]. The data support holding risk while the tariff shock works through household budgets.

Rates & Curve: At the prior close the Treasury par curve had the 2Y at 4.02%, the 10Y at 4.31% and the 30Y at 4.62%, with the 10Y−2Y spread at +29 bps (US Treasury, prior close 2025-03-14). Over 20 observations the 2Y fell 29 bps from 4.31% and the 10Y fell 21 bps from 4.52%, so the spread widened by 8 bps. The curve is positively sloped, and the 20-observation move is bull steepening (both down, 2Y down more) (US Treasury, prior close 2025-03-14). The market is paying for earlier easing, not for a sharper cycle. Equities are pricing something else: the Nasdaq 100 is down 10.56% over 20 sessions, and the VIX, at 21.77, is up 44.17% over the same span (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2025-03-14). The dollar index sits at 103.72 index points, down 3.35% over 20 sessions (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2025-03-14), and Bloomberg reports Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent calling the currency’s decline a natural adjustment [11]. Lower front-end yields with a softer dollar is the mix that rescues long-duration assets, but only if volatility stops rising.

New / Most Important: The signal that matters is what the household squeeze does to demand. Reuters reported consumer confidence sinking on tariff fears as inflation expectations jumped [3], and CNBC put March’s reading at its lowest since 2022 [2]. Retailers are saying the same thing. Dollar General’s chief executive warned that consumers are cash-strapped and that 2025 will not be better [12], and Business Insider quotes her saying shoppers only have enough money for ‘basic essentials’ [13]. The Fed is the other half of the trade. Bloomberg reports markets expect two cuts this year, starting in September [14], which is why the front end has rallied while equities fell. If the Wednesday decision keeps that path, the bond rally can carry. If it pushes back, the steepening stalls and the high-beta growth names that have been hit hardest face another leg down.

Playbook ✓: Stay at neutral equity exposure and rotate inside it rather than adding beta. Keep duration on through the Wednesday decision: the 2Y has already fallen 29 bps over 20 observations, and long Treasuries (TLT) are up 1.68% over 20 sessions (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2025-03-14), so the market leans that way. Buy quality on down days rather than chasing the 2.13% bounce, and keep a volatility hedge while the VIX sits well above its February reference of 15.10 (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2025-03-14). The call changes in two directions. A VIX close back under 15.10 with the 2Y still falling would make the rally broad and justify adding cyclicals. A 10Y yield back above its February level of 4.52%, with tariff escalation in the headlines, would argue for cutting duration and staying defensive.

🇨🇳 China  Mixed  stimulus promised / property still falling

Macro Stance: Beijing is talking about demand, and equity markets are listening. Beijing has said it will “vigorously boost consumption” [32], and the plan arrives as a 30-point toolbox [33]. The central bank said it will create “more tools” to shore up the economy [34]. The 5% growth target leaves most of the work to policy [35], and Fortune argues China has only one real way to hit it [36]. The official data are mixed rather than weak. The non-manufacturing PMI was 50.4 in February [37], but Bloomberg’s headline says China is still struggling to escape a cycle of deflation [38]. The verdict is stimulus-driven stabilisation in markets, with an unresolved balance-sheet problem underneath.

PMIs & Activity: The most recent official survey is February’s non-manufacturing PMI at 50.4 [37], which says services are holding. Activity indicators are positive at the margin. Express delivery posted fast growth in the first two months [39], and railway cargo volume rose in January and February [40]. Households are the soft spot. Food and beverage operators report slowing consumer spending [41], and Chinese lawmakers are targeting the job-market bias against workers over 35 [42]. Taken together, services and logistics hold while household demand and goods do the damage.

Credit Impulse: Credit is where the policy has to show up, and the evidence is still intent rather than loan growth. The central bank’s pledge of more tools [34] is the clearest signal, alongside a $7 billion state-backed fund that Bank of China set up to support technology development [43]. The bigger headline is a US$1 trillion reserves fund for Hong Kong, which Yahoo Finance describes as a secret plan [44]. Foreign money moved first: the IIF reports large February inflows into Chinese stocks and EM debt [45]. Until new loan figures show household borrowing turning up, the impulse is a bet on Beijing’s execution rather than on bank balance sheets.

Property: Property is where the stimulus story still runs into trouble. The developer plumbing is still under strain. China Evergrande and others were ordered to repay $1.8 billion of deposit pledges to a property unit [46], and some Hong Kong banks are refusing home loans for a Vanke project in Tai Po [47]. Official policy is pushing quality over volume, and SCMP’s analysts warn that the push for better homes adds stress for developers [48]. Some developers are less stretched: Country Garden’s unit forecast higher annual earnings [49].

Fiscal, Policy & Trade: Trade is the second drag. China’s tariffs on US agricultural products took effect [50], and AP reports Beijing is taking issue with tying tariffs to fentanyl [51]. US meat exporters are days away from being shut out of China [52]. Beijing is also watching US retailers, and Business Insider reads China’s warning to Walmart on tariffs as a sign it is worried about local businesses [53]. The Guardian’s argument is that China can live with Trump’s tariffs [54]. On the fiscal side, the 5% target leaves the mix open, and the state is promising consumption a larger role [55].

Markets & Flows: Chinese equities are doing the work. The Hang Seng closed at 23,959.98, up 2.12% in the latest session and 5.92% over 20 sessions (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2025-03-14). The Shanghai Composite is at 3,419.56 index points, up 2.18% over 20 sessions, and the CSI 300 ETF (ASHR) at 27.91 USD is up 4.73% (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2025-03-14). FXI at 37.66 USD is up 10.25% over 20 sessions, and the internet ETF KWEB at 36.78 USD is up 7.11% (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2025-03-14). The yuan is steady, with USD/CNY at 7.2371, down 0.71% over 20 sessions (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2025-03-14). Flows agree. Reuters headlines that Trump has made Chinese stocks “somewhat” great again [56], and Bloomberg reports Hong Kong’s stock market booming while Wall Street is in turmoil [57].

New / Most Important: The most useful signal is that the consumption push is now a written, numbered plan. The 30-point toolbox [33] turns a vague promise into a checklist, and that is the kind of signal equities tend to pay for. The test is whether it reaches household income, and developer stress is the counterweight [48]. The debate over where the 5% growth will come from is still open [35]. A plan that does not reach incomes is a financing exercise for the banks, not a demand story.

Playbook ✓: Own China exposure through the liquid large caps and the internet ETFs on pullbacks rather than chasing the 20-session move. Keep the yuan position light because USD/CNY is steady, and avoid developers and mainland property names until developer balance sheets stabilise. What changes the call: a consumption plan that shows up in household income data would extend the rally into consumer names, while a new US tariff tied to fentanyl [51] would hit the export names first. ⚠ Divergence: the Hang Seng is up 5.92% over 20 sessions while Evergrande and others were ordered to repay $1.8 billion of deposit pledges to a property unit [46], so the equity rally is pricing a policy the developer balance sheets have not yet confirmed.
