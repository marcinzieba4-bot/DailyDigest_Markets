🌍 Geographic Macro Overview

🇺🇸 United States  Mixed  → rates on hold (hawkish hold)

Macro Stance: The Fed held rates steady at its late-July meeting but the decision was far from unanimous — three officials dissented in favor of a hike, the first three-way hawkish dissent in a decade. Chair Warsh stated the Fed "won't hesitate to stop inflation," yet the bond market is openly questioning the Fed's inflation credibility. The 10Y yield sits at 4.63% and is climbing (+6 bps vs 4W ago), while the 2Y at 4.20% (prior close, +1 bps vs 4W) is barely moving, producing a +43 bps curve — positively sloped but still flat by historical standards — that points to term premium re-pricing rather than imminent easing. Fiscal posture remains stimulative — the economy is being described as "overstimulated" by Reuters, and corporate earnings continue to pile up (Palantir, Disney, EMCOR/Quanta all beating). The Iran/Hormuz deal chatter is providing a modest geopolitical relief valve today.

New / Most Important: The three hawkish dissents at the July FOMC meeting are structurally significant — this is the clearest signal in years that the Committee's reaction function has shifted. The bond market's response has been to push long yields higher while keeping the front end anchored, a classic "credibility gap" pattern. Two regional Fed banks announced a pilot survey of the private credit market, acknowledging the shadow banking system's macro relevance for the first time operationally. Amazon's announcement that it will share $600M in Trump tariff refunds with customers is a novel deflationary micro-impulse but underscores how tariff policy remains embedded in the corporate cost structure. Trump's comments that a Hormuz deal is close moved oil modestly but the bigger signal is stocks hitting record highs — S&P at 7738 (↑3.4% vs 4W).

Playbook ✓: Equities grinding higher on earnings momentum despite elevated yields — the classic late-cycle "profits trump rates" trade is intact. VIX at 16.0 (↓5.4%) confirms complacency regime.  |  ⚠️ Divergence: The three hawkish dissents fundamentally change the Fed's forward guidance credibility. For the past cycle, the market could assume the next move was a cut — now it must price a non-trivial probability of a hike. This is structurally new. The bond market is already voting with its feet: 10Y yields climbing while the curve steepens is not "growth optimism" — it is inflation risk premium being re-inserted into duration. The private credit pilot survey is the Fed admitting it is flying partially blind on credit conditions, which means the transmission mechanism of monetary policy is even more uncertain than models suggest. If the three dissenters hold firm, a September hike becomes a live risk, and the equity market's complacent VIX is mispriced against that tail.

Macro Stance: Eurozone inflation surprised to the upside in July, rising to 2.9%, reinforcing the case for a September ECB rate hike. Money supply growth is rising and lending is steady — this is not the credit contraction the doves were hoping for. The euro is advancing on tightening expectations.

New / Most Important: The July inflation print materially changed the ECB trajectory. A September hike is now the base case per Morningstar. Eurozone business borrowing costs are rising while mortgage rates stabilize — a divergence that pressures corporates disproportionately. This is a reversal from earlier 2026 when the ECB was expected to be done tightening.

Playbook ✓: Euro strengthening on hawkish ECB repricing is textbook.  |  ⚠️ Divergence: The structural divergence is that European inflation is re-accelerating while the US is debating whether to hike — meaning both major DM central banks may tighten simultaneously. This is a global liquidity drain scenario that has not been priced into risk assets. If ECB hikes in September while the Fed holds but signals hawkishness, the combined effect on global dollar liquidity is contractionary even without a Fed move. European equities, particularly rate-sensitive sectors, are not pricing this double-tightening risk.

🇨🇳 China  Mixed  → easing bias, selective stimulus

Macro Stance: Easing is moving from words to liquidity. The PBOC injected CNY 500 billion (BigGo Finance, [Aug 4]) and signalled continued monetary support in H2 (Fibre2Fashion, [Aug 4]), after pledging timely adjustment of its policy tools (Reuters, [Aug 2]); China Daily now writes that interest rate and RRR cuts may be on the table ([Aug 4]). The yuan is the counterweight: it hit its strongest in 3-1/2 years as trade tensions grew (NST Online, [Aug 4]) even as it edged lower when the PBOC set the central parity at 6.7 (BusinessToday Malaysia, [Aug 4]), and offshore long-dated yuan bonds sold at record low yields (Bloomberg, [Aug 4]). June CPI rose only 1% (Global Times, [Jul 9]) while PPI climbed 4.1% (finance.biggo.com, [Jul 8]), a margin squeeze rather than reflation.

PMIs & Activity: The July surveys are now complete, and every gauge slowed. The official manufacturing PMI dropped to 49.2 (The State Council Information Office, [Jul 31]) and non-manufacturing to 49 (Macau Business, [Jul 31]); the private manufacturing PMI eased to 50.9 (Fibre2Fashion, [Aug 3]) and private services growth hit its slowest since September 2024 (Reuters, [Aug 4]). Reconciled: the export-tilted private samples are still expanding but decelerating, the broader official ones are contracting. This is the "two-speed economy" Deutsche Bank describes, with tech and AI accelerating while property and consumption lag. Q2 GDP grew 4.3%, below the target range (Bloomberg, [Jul 15]), and first-half industrial profits rose 18.7% as a tech boom masks the property slump (Caixin Global, [Jul 27]). Autos are the exception, with CPCA seeing July NEV wholesale up 23% as fuel prices climb (CnEVPost, [Aug 4]); Tesla's China wholesale rose 38% (Yahoo Finance, [Aug 4]) even as its domestic sales crash and exports surge (Electrek, [Aug 4]).

Credit Impulse: June new bank loans rose less than expected (The Star, [Jul 15]) and credit growth missed forecasts as the PBOC flagged a "new normal" (Caixin Global, [Jul 16]); household loans fell by 366.8 billion yuan in the first half (36Kr, [Jul 17]). That is why a CNY 500 billion injection changes little on its own: liquidity is ample, credit demand is not. Local debt remains fragile, with one LGFV insisting it can repay even as a bond sale hit a snag (The Edge Malaysia, [Aug 3]). The impulse reads flat, with households negative.

Property: July housing prices fell 0.5% and sales volume weakened (Investing.com, [Aug 3]), and real home prices have sunk to their lowest level in 21 years (the deep dive, [Aug 3]). June's official data showed property investment down 18 percent y/y in January–June (The Standard (HK), [Jul 15]), and July was the worst building month since Covid (Wood Central, [Aug 1]). Breakingviews argues the chips lottery is no cure for the property bust (Reuters, [Aug 4]), and Xi's Shanghai tour offered no relief for the housing crisis (The Diplomat, [Aug 4]).

Fiscal, Policy & Trade: The Politburo settled on incremental policy support (Caixin Global, [Jul 31]), and leaders pledged support without big new stimulus plans (Reuters, [Jul 30]). Trade friction is rising on the AI and solar engine: the US plans to ban imports of Chinese data-centre components (NST Online, [Aug 4]) and weighs a polysilicon price floor and tariffs (Reuters, [Aug 4]), while China tightened exit rules to guard rare earth and battery secrets (Asia Times, [Aug 4]). A surprise tax on offshore trusts has left the super-rich hunting for cash (CNBC, [Aug 4]). Against that, GM and SAIC extending their JV through 2047 is a contrarian vote of foreign capital confidence.

Markets & Flows: The ban report split the tech complex: optical module makers slumped (Reuters, [Aug 4]) and Chinese AI darlings slid (Financial Times, [Aug 4]), yet onshore stocks rose as AI and chip shares rebounded while Hong Kong slipped (Business Recorder, [Aug 4]), with the Hang Seng back below 26,000 (The Standard (HK), [Aug 4]). Reconciled: names exposed to US data-centre demand sold off, domestic chip substitution was bought. Beijing is courting global investors with bond market tweaks (Financial Times, [Aug 4]), and Hong Kong retail sales rose 4.6% in June (South China Morning Post, [Aug 4]).

New / Most Important: Two things landed together. Private services growth at its slowest since September 2024 completes a July picture in which every PMI slowed, and the US moved against the one engine that was offsetting domestic weakness, AI hardware exports. The PBOC's CNY 500 billion injection and talk of rate and RRR cuts are the response, but they cushion liquidity, not demand.

Playbook ✓: Selective stock-picking in China outperforming broad indices, plus incremental easing, is on script.  |  ⚠️ Divergence: The two-speed economy is now visible in commodities: iron ore hit a year low (Tech Times, [Aug 4]) and BHP remains a top mining pick despite a China growth downgrade (TheBull.com.au, [Aug 4]), while the Australian dollar came under pressure as the US dollar regained ground (www.tmgm.com, [Aug 3]). The demand that is left is external, with Matson expecting strong US demand for China exports through Q3 (Journal of Commerce, [Aug 4]), so EM Asia and European exporters compete with Chinese goods rather than sell into China, and oil at $74.86 trims the terms-of-trade tax without fixing domestic demand.

🇯🇵 Japan  Bearish (for JGBs)  ↑ rates — hike imminent

Macro Stance: The BoJ held at its July meeting but explicitly nodded to the chance of an early rate hike as price pressures build. The yen remains under severe pressure, prompting what appears to have been a coordinated US-Japan intervention — Bessent publicly backed Japan's stabilization efforts and signaled the US expects BoJ to act. This is a major shift.

New / Most Important: The Bessent "nudge" is the week's most important development in global macro. The US Treasury Secretary explicitly backing BoJ tightening and joint yen intervention is structurally unprecedented in the modern era — the US is effectively outsourcing dollar weakness to Japan. This collapses the traditional "carry trade" framework: if the US is actively promoting yen strength, the massive yen-funded carry positions in global risk assets (estimated in the trillions) face an existential unwind catalyst. The NYT article connecting the stock market's record rally to "rescuing the yen" reveals the mechanism — coordinated intervention provides yen stability that prevents a disorderly carry unwind, allowing equities to rally. But this is inherently unstable: if the BoJ actually hikes in September, the orderly carry adjustment becomes a forced liquidation. The CNN interview on joint intervention and multiple Reuters/CNBC pieces confirm this is not rumor — it is active policy coordination. Positioning for a September BoJ hike should be a high-conviction trade.

Playbook ✓: Yen weakness forcing BoJ's hand is the consensus script playing out.  |  ⚠️ Divergence: The US actively encouraging BoJ tightening is the divergence — historically the US preferred Japanese accommodation to fund Treasury demand. Bessent's stance suggests the US prioritizes dollar weakness over cheap JGB-funded Treasury buying. This is a regime change in global capital flow architecture.
