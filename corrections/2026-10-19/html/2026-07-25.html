🇺🇸 United States  Mixed  → hold / possible hike

Macro Stance: The Fed is poised to hold rates at next week's FOMC meeting, but the conversation has shifted dramatically — from "when do they cut?" to "could they actually hike?" The 10Y yield has surged to 4.70% (+31 bps vs 4 weeks ago), the 2Y sits at 4.33% (prior close, +24 bps vs 4W), and the curve at +36 bps is 5 bps steeper than four weeks ago but still flat by historical standards, reflecting a market repricing growth resilience and renewed inflation risk from the US-Iran war and oil at 89.31 (↑24.2%). Federal debt interest hit $857B in nine months. Fiscal stance remains expansionary with no credible consolidation path.

New / Most Important: The dominant new development is the oil-driven inflation scare colliding with the Fed's credibility. CNBC reported surging odds of a Fed rate hike as oil ripped higher. Multiple outlets framed the July meeting as genuinely uncertain — USA Today called the Fed chair "an enigma." Markets triggered a sell signal per Yahoo Finance, with Apple earnings and the Fed meeting front and center for next week. Mortgage rates hit their highest level this year, tightening the housing channel even as the Fed hasn't moved. This is structurally important: the US is experiencing a supply-side inflation shock (energy) layered on top of AI-driven capex and fiscal profligacy. The Fed's reaction function is being stress-tested — if they hike into a geopolitical oil spike, it signals prioritizing inflation credibility over growth; if they hold, they risk unanchoring expectations while long-end yields do the tightening for them. The market is pricing equities flat (S&P 7408, ↑0.7%) but punishing duration and rate-sensitive names — classic late-cycle stagflationary positioning.

Playbook ✓: AI concentration driving index-level returns while breadth narrows — Nasdaq 100 (↓2.6%) underperforming Russell 2000 (↓1.6%), though both are down, consistent with rising rate pressure hitting long-duration growth hardest. Gold at 4067 (↑0.9%) holding as a geopolitical hedge. VIX at 18.70 in elevated regime, reflecting uncertainty without panic.  |  ⚠️ Divergence: The truly surprising signal is that S&P 500 is still marginally positive (↑0.7%) in the face of 10Y yields surging 31 bps in four weeks and oil up 24%. This resilience is not normal. It suggests either massive passive flows and buyback support are overriding macro signals, or the market is pricing the oil spike as transitory and the Iran conflict as containable. The growth pulse says this is not a healthy reflationary impulse — it's a stagflationary one: the July flash PMIs are still expanding (US manufacturing at 53.8), so the oil spike is a supply shock layered on positive activity, while the 30-year mortgage rate at 6.58%, the highest in nearly a year, is already choking the rate-sensitive end. The divergence between equity calm and bond market stress is the single most important cross-asset signal right now.

New / Most Important: The ECB hold was well-flagged but the hawkish pivot is structurally significant. The euro's reaction to the hold was muted, suggesting the FX market had already priced hawkish risk. This is a central bank that cut its way into a corner and now faces a supply shock with limited room to maneuver — classic European policy trap.

Playbook ✓: ECB data-dependent hold amid geopolitical uncertainty is textbook. Energy-driven inflation pass-through fears dominating the Governing Council's rhetoric as expected.  |  ⚠️ Divergence: The speed of the pivot from "easing cycle" to "September hike firmly in play" is extreme. If oil stays above 85, the ECB may be forced to hike into weakness — a policy error that could tip peripheral economies.

Prices are split: June CPI rose only 1% (Global Times, [Jul 9]) while PPI climbed 4.1% (finance.biggo.com, [Jul 8]), a near four-year high that squeezes manufacturers (Reuters, [Jul 8]).

🇯🇵 Japan  Bearish (bonds) / Bullish (JPY eventually)  ↑ rate hikes coming

Macro Stance: The BOJ is at a historic inflection point. The yen hit a 40-year low (near 160) even as rates reached a 31-year high — a paradox that reflects the massive Fed-BOJ rate differential still favoring carry into USD. The BOJ is reportedly open to a faster rate hike pace, with a Reuters poll pointing to another hike by December.

New / Most Important: This is the most structurally important story in global macro right now and deserves extended treatment. The yen is simultaneously at a 40-year low and rates at a 31-year high because the absolute level of Japanese rates remains far below the US and the carry trade incentive overwhelms the marginal tightening. Bloomberg reported the BOJ is "said open to faster rate hike pace as yen adds risks" — this is the BOJ telegraphing that they will use rates more aggressively to defend the currency, a fundamental shift in their reaction function. The intervention talk is back, meaning the MOF/BOJ toolkit now explicitly includes FX intervention alongside rate hikes — dual-barreled defense. The global risk here is enormous: a BOJ rate hike into this environment could trigger another wave of yen carry trade unwinds, which in August 2024 caused a global equity flash crash. With US yields surging simultaneously, the cross-border capital flow implications are massive — Japanese institutions are the world's largest foreign bond holders, and higher domestic rates could accelerate repatriation flows out of Treasuries and European govvies, adding further upward pressure to global yields. This is a reflexive doom loop: higher US yields → wider rate gap → weaker yen → BOJ forced to hike → carry unwind → risk-off globally → flight to... where exactly?

Playbook ✓: BOJ hawkish drift and yen weakness are consensus. Carry trade remaining attractive at current differentials is textbook.  |  ⚠️ Divergence: The yen is NOT strengthening despite the hawkish rhetoric — this suggests the market does not believe the BOJ will move fast enough, or that US rate dynamics are simply overwhelming. If the BOJ surprises with a larger-than-expected hike, the carry unwind could be violent and global.
