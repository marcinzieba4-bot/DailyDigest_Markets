🇯🇵 Japan  Bearish  ↑ rates rising, more hikes expected

Macro Stance: The BOJ is in an active tightening cycle — the first in a generation — with interest rates at a 31-year high and the yen simultaneously hitting a 40-year low near 163 against the dollar. A Reuters poll shows expectations for another rate hike by December. Sources indicate the BOJ is open to faster rate hikes as yen weakness feeds back into import-price inflation. Intervention talk is intensifying.

New / Most Important: This is a genuinely unprecedented macro configuration: the BOJ is hiking into yen weakness, which is the opposite of what textbooks predict. Normally, rate hikes attract capital inflows and strengthen the currency. The yen's 40-year low despite a 31-year high in rates exposes a structural carry-trade unwind problem — global investors are borrowing yen to fund higher-yielding assets elsewhere, and the positive carry differential with US rates at current levels overwhelms any BOJ tightening. The oil shock makes this exponentially worse: Japan imports virtually all its energy, and $100 Brent priced in 163 USDJPY means the yen-denominated oil import bill has roughly doubled in two years. The BOJ faces an impossible trilemma — tighten to defend the yen (crushing domestic demand), intervene in FX markets (burning reserves), or accept inflation passthrough (politically toxic). The next BOJ meeting is the single most important central bank event globally right now. If they hike aggressively and the yen still doesn't respond, the credibility damage could trigger a disorderly unwind of the carry trade — with systemic implications reminiscent of August 2024's mini-crash.

Playbook ✓: Weak yen → import inflation → BOJ forced to hike — standard.  |  ⚠️ Divergence: The failure of rate hikes to strengthen the yen is a structural break in the monetary transmission mechanism that has no modern precedent in a G7 economy.
