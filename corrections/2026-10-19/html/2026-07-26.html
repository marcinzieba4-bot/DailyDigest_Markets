🌍 Geographic Macro Overview

🇺🇸 United States  Mixed  → rates on hold (hike risk rising)

Macro Stance: The Fed enters its July meeting this week with policy in a genuine no-man's-land. The 10Y yield has surged to 4.70% (+31 bps vs 4W ago), the 2Y at 4.33% (prior close, +24 bps vs 4W), and the curve at +36 bps is 5 bps steeper than four weeks ago — still flat by historical standards, with both ends repricing inflation risk rather than easing expectations. WTI at $89.31 (↑24.2% vs 4W ago) is the elephant in the room — a genuine supply-shock input that complicates any dovish pivot. CME FedWatch shows 62.1% probability of no rate change at the July FOMC meeting, but headlines indicate odds of a rate hike have surged alongside oil.

New / Most Important: The US-Iran war escalation dominated cross-asset pricing this week. Oil's 24% surge in four weeks is the single most important macro variable globally right now — it is transmitting stagflationary impulse into every economy simultaneously. The Fed meeting on July 29 is now a genuine coin-flip event in terms of forward guidance. Multiple outlets framed this week as "all about the cash" with Mag 7 earnings (Apple, Microsoft, Meta, Amazon) arriving alongside the rate decision — a rare double catalyst. Google already reported and burned $5.9 billion in cash, its first negative free cash flow quarter since IPO, a canary for AI capex sustainability. The "Trump Trade" is reportedly souring, adding political risk premium.

Playbook ✓: Equities hanging in (S&P 500 at 7408, ↑0.7%) despite rising yields and oil — the AI narrative is doing heavy lifting as expected. Gold at $4071 (↑1.0%) acting as the expected geopolitical hedge. VIX at 18.7 (elevated but not panicking) — classic pre-event vol compression.  |  ⚠️ Divergence: The massive divergence is the Nasdaq 100 falling 2.6% while the S&P is up 0.7% and the Russell/Nasdaq ratio is showing small-caps outperforming. This is NOT supposed to happen in a risk-on environment — it signals that the mega-cap AI trade is cracking under its own capex weight while the broader economy (proxied by small-caps and industrials) is actually absorbing the oil shock better than expected. Google's negative FCF quarter is structural: the hyperscalers are now in a capex arms race that is consuming cash faster than AI revenue is materializing, and the market is beginning to price this asymmetry. If Meta or Microsoft echo this pattern in their reports this week, we could see a violent rotation out of concentration risk and into breadth — the Russell outperformance is the leading edge of this. The growth pulse confirms supply-shock rather than demand-led commodity strength — July flash PMIs are expanding (US manufacturing at 53.8, euro-zone activity back in growth) while China's Q2 GDP slowed to 4.3% — which historically favors value/energy over growth/tech.

🇪🇺 European Union  Bearish  → rates on hold, hike risk building

Macro Stance: The ECB held rates at 2.25% on July 23, confirming a hawkish pause driven by the Iran conflict's inflationary transmission through energy prices. The easing cycle that defined late 2024/early 2025 is definitively over. Fiscal policy remains expansionary across the bloc (defense spending, green transition), creating a push-pull with monetary tightening bias.

New / Most Important: The ECB's hold was widely expected, but the key development is that traders now see a September rate hike as firmly in play. This is a regime change — the ECB was in cutting mode just months ago and is now debating whether to re-tighten. The Iran war's impact on European energy prices is the proximate cause, but structurally this reflects Europe's unresolved energy vulnerability three years after the Russia shock.

Playbook ✓: Euro area bank lending survey (July 21) and the hold decision played out as consensus expected — cautious, data-dependent, hawkish tilt.  |  ⚠️ Divergence: The structural break here is that Europe may be forced to hike rates into an already fragile growth environment — a genuine policy error scenario. Unlike the US, Europe has no energy self-sufficiency buffer. Every dollar increase in oil is a direct terms-of-trade hit to the eurozone. If the ECB hikes in September while manufacturing PMIs remain contractionary, it would be the clearest stagflationary policy trap since 2008. Capital flows should favor exiting European duration and European equities ex-energy. The euro's path becomes binary: it strengthens on rate differentials but weakens on growth destruction — net-net likely weaker as growth concerns dominate.

🇨🇳 China  Mixed  → easing bias

Macro Stance: The PBOC is easing through volume, not price. Loan prime rates were left unchanged for a 14th consecutive month (The Business Times, [Jul 20]) despite the Q2 growth miss, with the market eyeing a cut window near the end of the third quarter (finance.biggo.com, [Jul 21]). Instead the central bank added the most liquidity in five months (Bloomberg, [Jul 23]) and is running overnight reverse repos alongside a 500b-yuan MLF operation, injecting a total of 2.1 trillion yuan (Reuters, [Jul 24]; China Daily, [Jul 24]). The yuan is not the release valve: UOB sees an upside bias against the dollar below 6.7820 (www.tmgm.com, [Jul 20]) as Europe scrutinises its value (Bloomberg, [Jul 21]). Prices are split: June CPI rose only 1% (Global Times, [Jul 9]) while PPI climbed 4.1% (finance.biggo.com, [Jul 8]), a near four-year high that squeezes manufacturers (Reuters, [Jul 8]).

PMIs & Activity: The last official read is June: manufacturing PMI 50.3 and non-manufacturing 50.2 (Reuters, [Jun 29]); July's numbers are due in the coming week (ING Think, [Jul 23]). The hard data show two speeds: Q2 GDP grew 4.3%, below the official target range (Bloomberg, [Jul 15]); June industrial output rose 5.3% y/y and retail sales returned to growth (BusinessLine, [Jul 15]), but first-half retail sales were up just 1.3% (Ecns.cn, [Jul 15]) and the investment slide deepened as infrastructure joined property (Caixin Global, [Jul 15]), while exports jumped 27% y/y (Reuters, [Jul 13]). The consumer signals are weak: trade-in sales are dropping as the stimulus effect fades (Caixin Global, [Jul 24]) and car sales plunge 20% toward the worst year since 2021 (CNBC, [Jul 20]).

Credit Impulse: June new bank loans rose less than expected (The Star, [Jul 15]) and credit growth missed as the PBOC flagged a "new normal" (Caixin Global, [Jul 16]). Household loans fell by 366.8 billion yuan in the first half (36Kr, [Jul 17]). The finance minister's claim that fiscal-financial synergy leveraged over 17 trillion yuan in new loans (finance.biggo.com, [Jul 22]) is not a contradiction: the volume is policy-directed corporate and state-linked lending while households pay down. Banks breaking with the lending benchmark in pricing new credit (Bloomberg, [Jul 24]) is quiet price easing, and a bond rally before the Politburo (Bloomberg, [Jul 22]) says the market expects more. Read: headline impulse flat, household impulse negative.

Property: June new home prices fell at a slower pace (Reuters, [Jul 14]) and first-tier prices extended a four-month rebound (South China Morning Post, [Jul 15]), but resale prices fell faster (Reuters, [Jun 30]) and property investment slumped 18 percent y/y in January–June (The Standard (HK), [Jul 15]). The capital side is in retreat: KKR and AEW seek to sell China assets at steep losses (Bloomberg, [Jul 20]), and regulators curbed a loan structure used by distressed developers (Bloomberg, [Jul 22]) and offshore loans for local real estate firms (The Standard (HK), [Jul 23]). Vanke completed extensions on its 2026 public bonds (Caixin Global, [Jul 23]) with a RMB 519 million loan from Shenzhen Metro at 2.29% (BigGo Finance, [Jul 21]): state shareholders, not buyers, hold it together.

Fiscal, Policy & Trade: Beijing vowed to achieve the annual growth target (Bloomberg, [Jul 20]), yet clings to austerity despite the slowdown (Bloomberg, [Jul 22]); fiscal revenue expanded 4.7% in the first half (Reuters, [Jul 22]). The July Politburo meeting, still ahead, is the marker for stock-stimulus clues (South China Morning Post, [Jul 22]) and the growth commitment (Bloomberg, [Jul 23]). On trade, Washington will impose new tariffs of 10 to 12.5 percent on over 60 countries (China Daily Global Edition, [Jul 24]); Beijing is set to look past them before Xi's US trip (Bloomberg, [Jul 24]) and is consulting on tariff cuts on $30 billion in trade (Reuters, [Jul 23]). The New York Times judges that China has come through the trade war in a good position ([Jul 25]). Toward Europe it is harder: 14 EU entities went onto its export control list (Reuters, [Jul 24]).

Markets & Flows: The Kimi K3 scare sent chip stocks tumbling on China AI fears (TradingView, [Jul 19]); the "national team" answered by buying shares worth $9bn (Financial Times, [Jul 20]) with record inflows into a tech ETF (Bloomberg, [Jul 21]). The bounce is fading: Hong Kong sank back below 25,000 as oil and tariff fears bit (BusinessToday Malaysia, [Jul 25]) after chip stocks dived on overvaluation (Asia Times, [Jul 24]). BCA tips a three-month reversion trade into China away from South Korea (Yahoo Finance UK, [Jul 25]).

New / Most Important: The consensus has now priced the absence of a bazooka: growth is set to slow in the second half as Beijing avoids broad stimulus (Yahoo Finance Australia, [Jul 25]). That is the bar the Politburo has to clear. Liquidity, equity support and quiet loan-rate cuts are in place; what is missing is a fiscal transfer to households, and Hong Kong slipping back below 25,000 shows the state-buying rally cannot carry itself without it.

Playbook ✓: LPR on hold, liquidity easing, export strength, property drag and a calm tariff response before Xi's trip are on script.  |  ⚠️ Divergence: The rest of the world is tilting hawkish while China eases, yet the WEF's call to "rethink the developed-emerging market divide" is landing on an economy whose credit and fiscal data still point down. With property investment down 18 percent and infrastructure contracting, construction-linked bulk demand keeps fading, a drag on AUD and iron ore that 27% export growth does not offset. The oil spike is a terms-of-trade negative for China as a massive importer; discounted Russian and Iranian crude only partly buffers it. European exporters absorb the surplus as the yuan draws scrutiny, and EM inflows ride state buying rather than a PMI or credit upturn.

🇯🇵 Japan  Bearish  ↑ rates rising — hike to 1.25% in focus

Macro Stance: The BOJ is in active tightening mode — the only major DM central bank unambiguously hiking. The yen remains pinned near 40-year lows despite rate increases, creating a monetary policy paradox. Fiscal policy under the current PM involves a "giant spending spree" that directly conflicts with the BOJ's tightening, drawing explicit Liz Truss comparisons from The Guardian.

New / Most Important: This is the most structurally important story in global macro right now. The BOJ is reportedly open to faster rate hike pacing, with a Reuters poll showing consensus for another hike by December, and FXEmpire flagging a move to 1.25%. The yen hit 40-year lows even as rates hit 31-year highs — this is a historic dislocation. The "Liz Truss" framing (Jul 25) is significant because it signals that international capital is now pricing Japan's fiscal-monetary contradiction as a potential crisis catalyst rather than a manageable transition. Two central banks decide rates next week (the Fed and BOJ), and the interaction between the two decisions is the key cross-border capital flow event. If the BOJ hikes while the Fed holds or even hints dovish, we could see a violent yen carry trade unwind — multiple outlets flagged this exact risk. The BOJ's conundrum is that hiking doesn't strengthen the yen because the fiscal impulse overwhelms it, but NOT hiking allows inflation expectations to de-anchor further. Japan is approaching a regime break where the traditional playbook of "BOJ hike = yen strength = Nikkei weakness" may not hold because the fiscal dominance channel is overriding monetary transmission.

Playbook ✓: BOJ hawkish signaling is consistent with yen weakness forcing their hand.  |  ⚠️ Divergence: The Liz Truss parallel is the divergence — Japan's fiscal-monetary schizophrenia is now being priced as a tail risk by global capital, not just a curiosity.
