🌍 Geographic Macro Overview

🇺🇸 United States  Bearish  ↑ rate direction (hike risk)

Macro Stance: The Fed is in a hawkish hold that has now escalated. FOMC minutes released this week revealed officials saw the need for a rate hike if inflation doesn't cool — a material rhetorical escalation from prior meetings. Fiscal dominance is the story: US debt crossed $40 trillion, the 30-year yield topped 5.31% (highest in 19 years as of last weekend), and Bessent intervened to "break bond market fever." The 10Y sits at 4.70% while the 2Y is at 4.19% (prior close, −12 bps vs 4W), producing a +46 bps curve — positively sloped but still flat by historical standards, and this is a bull steepener led by the front end, not growth optimism. The 2Y falling 12 bps vs 4W ago while the 10Y is roughly flat (unchanged vs 4W) means the market is still pricing eventual easing at the front end while the long end refuses to follow it lower on term premium and fiscal risk. VIX at 15.64 and equities at highs (S&P 7675) reflect equity complacency that is structurally disconnected from the stress in sovereign credit.

New / Most Important: Three things happened simultaneously this week that constitute a regime signal. First, FOMC minutes explicitly flagged rate hike optionality — the first time since 2023 this language surfaced. Second, Bessent conducted Treasury intervention to stabilize the long end, echoing the Yellen bill-shift playbook but now under far more strained conditions. Third, NPR reported margin debt at all-time highs. This is the classic late-cycle configuration: fiscal authorities fighting the bond vigilantes while equity leverage peaks. The Telegraph's comparison of US government credit risk to AI speculation risk is not hyperbole — CDS-implied sovereign risk has structurally re-rated. The fact that equities rallied 3.6% over the past month while this bond crisis unfolded tells you liquidity is still finding a bid for risk assets, but the source of that liquidity (Treasury intervention, not organic flows) makes it fragile.

Playbook ✓: Front-end-led bull steepening under fiscal stress is textbook; gold surging +12.8% in 4 weeks confirms the sovereign debasement hedge is working exactly as capital flow models predict.  |  ⚠️ Divergence: The divergence is that equities are NOT repricing for the hike scenario the bond market is screaming about. Normally, hawkish FOMC minutes with hike language would produce a 2-3% equity drawdown — instead the S&P made new highs. The Russell/Nasdaq ratio continuing to fall (↓0.8% vs 4W) shows this rally is entirely mega-cap driven, meaning narrow breadth is masking a market that should be distributing. Oil falling 6.6% simultaneously suggests the real economy is weakening even as financial assets levitate. This is the most dangerous macro configuration: policy tightening risk + fiscal blowout + equity complacency + record leverage.

Playbook ✓: European equity outperformance on valuation gap and earnings delivery is consensus and playing out.  |  ⚠️ Divergence: The divergence is that European bond stress is running hotter than the ECB's rhetoric — borrowing costs at record highs are a fiscal sustainability issue for peripheral nations even before the ECB has hiked. If the ECB is forced to hike into a geopolitical supply shock, the periphery-core spread could blow out in a way that tests the TPI framework for the first time under real stress.

🇨🇳 China  Mixed  → easing bias continues

Macro Stance: The PBOC held rates and keeps easing through liquidity instead. Liquidity has moved to the overnight tool: after the first mid-month overnight reverse repo boosted bonds (Bloomberg, [Aug 13]), 7-day reverse repos have been at zero for seven straight sessions (BigGo Finance, [Aug 19]), and July corporate loan rates fell below 3% (finance.biggo.com, [Aug 14]). On the currency the bank is leaning against strength, slowing yuan gains with a weaker-than-expected fixing (Bloomberg, [Aug 19]) as the offshore yuan hit its strongest since 2023 (TradingView, [Aug 19]). Prices leave room to ease: July CPI rose 0.5% and PPI growth slowed to 3.5%, both missing expectations (BigGo Finance, [Aug 8]).

PMIs & Activity: July's official manufacturing PMI fell to 49.2 and non-manufacturing to 49.0 (FXStreet, [Jul 31]); the private factory gauge eased to 50.9 (Fibre2Fashion, [Aug 3]), a four-month low (Reuters, [Aug 2]), and private services growth was the slowest since September 2024 (Reuters, [Aug 4]). The hard data confirmed the slide: industrial output grew 4.5% y/y and retail sales just 0.6% (Reuters, [Aug 17]), output, retail sales and investment all missed forecasts (The Business Times, [Aug 17]), and fixed-asset investment fell 6.7% in the first seven months (Xinhua, [Aug 17]). Reconciled: headline financing, which includes bond issuance, beat; bank lending to households and firms shrank, leaving cumulative yuan loans up 10.38 trillion yuan in January–July (Xinhua, [Aug 14]). An unchanged LPR does nothing for that demand gap. The bond market has priced it: China's bonds bucked the global selloff on a policy divide with peers (CNBC TV18, [Aug 19]) and the 10-year yield lingers near a one-year low (TradingView, [Aug 19]).

Property: July new home prices were stagnant as demand stayed weak (Reuters, [Aug 17]) and first-tier prices were flat, ending a four-month rebound (South China Morning Post, [Aug 17]). Bloomberg says prices fell at a faster pace ([Aug 17]), while the 70-city index fell 3.2% y/y, the smallest drop in five months (BigGo Finance, [Aug 17]): the annual comparison eased as the monthly decline deepened, and Caixin notes the recovery in smaller cities faltered ([Aug 18]). The response is city by city: Shanghai started a new round of property easing after the economy slowed (Bloomberg, [Aug 19]), following wider use of a $1.6 trillion housing fund (Bloomberg, [Aug 18]). Evergrande founder Hui Ka Yan was sentenced to life (Nation Thailand, [Aug 19]).

Fiscal, Policy & Trade: Premier Li Qiang called for stabilising external demand as growth sputters (Reuters, [Aug 17]), and the State Council urged faster rollout of the "Six Networks" to boost domestic demand (BigGo Finance, [Aug 17]), a programme that could exceed 26 trillion yuan in the 15th Five-Year Plan with power grid and computing power first in line (BigGo Finance, [Aug 18]). Stimulus calls are growing (Bloomberg, [Aug 18]) and Caixin wants the fiscal taps opened ([Aug 18]), but China Daily still favours tailored easing over broad stimulus ([Aug 14]). On trade, the White House says more than 40 nations help China dodge tariffs (The Washington Post, [Aug 13]), Mexico weighs tougher trade rules for China (South China Morning Post, [Aug 19]) and Beijing blocked firms from aiding the EU's JD.com probe (South China Morning Post, [Aug 19]).

Markets & Flows: A-shares plunged on heavy volume, with ChiNext down 6.26% as over 5,000 stocks fell (BigGo Finance, [Aug 19]) and the SSE Composite off 2.4% in a semiconductor rout (BBN Times, [Aug 19]), even as Unitree soared 460% on its IPO (Investor's Business Daily, [Aug 19]); the PBOC had already warned of a stock frenzy (The Standard (HK), [Aug 19]), and stocks rebounded as it stood pat (TradingView, [Aug 19]). New / Most Important: The LPR hold is the answer to July's broad miss: no rate cut, liquidity through the overnight window, targeted property easing in Shanghai and a weaker fixing to slow the yuan. Beijing is choosing to protect bank margins and the currency's pace rather than cut the cost of credit, while loans contract and the equity market swings between a ChiNext rout and IPO euphoria.

Playbook ✓: Rates on hold, liquidity over cuts, city-level property easing and a managed yuan are on script.  |  ⚠️ Divergence: The consensus read a policy pivot into July's miss; the LPR says otherwise, so the transmission runs through weak domestic demand and strong exports. 🇯🇵 Japan  Bullish (JPY)  ↑ rate direction (hiking cycle accelerating)

Macro Stance: The BoJ hiking cycle is accelerating in rhetoric and market expectations. Multiple feed sources confirm: ex-currency chief Watanabe called for rate hikes at every meeting, Mizuho's markets head expects hikes soon and more often, and Takaichi's 2027 BoJ appointments loom over the pace of normalization. This is structurally the most important central bank story in global macro right now.

New / Most Important: This week's signal cluster is the densest Japan hawk-signal we've seen. The structural story is that Japan's exit from ZIRP/NIRP is no longer a tentative experiment — it is becoming a consensus acceleration. This matters for global capital flows because Japan is the world's largest net creditor. As JGB yields rise and the BoJ tightens, the repatriation of Japanese capital from US Treasuries, European bonds, and carry trades intensifies. The yen strengthened this week as hike expectations rose. If the BoJ moves to hiking at every meeting, the $3+ trillion of Japanese overseas investment book begins to reprice in a way that amplifies the global bond selloff already underway. The weak-yen era that subsidized global carry is ending. More yen intervention is also on the table from the MoF, adding a second channel of JPY support. This is structurally bearish for UST, EUR govvies, and AUD — anywhere Japanese capital has been the marginal buyer.

Playbook ✓: Yen appreciation on hike expectations is textbook.  |  ⚠️ Divergence: The yen remains weak against the dollar despite the hawk shift — this likely reflects the sheer magnitude of the US yield advantage and suggests the BoJ needs to actually deliver multiple hikes before the carry unwind becomes disorderly. The gap between rhetoric and action is the trade.

🇦🇺 Australia  Bearish  ↑ rate direction (hike threat explicit)

Macro Stance: The RBA held rates at its recent meeting but the hawkish rhetoric escalated dramatically. Deputy Governor Hauser explicitly warned the bank will raise rates again if inflation doesn't come down. The RBA also flagged AI mania as an inflation driver — a novel framing that links tech investment directly to monetary policy.

New / Most Important: The RBA's identification of AI investment as an inflationary force is a structurally new argument that no other major central bank has made this explicitly. The logic: AI capex is driving energy demand, wages in tech, and asset price inflation, all of which feed into CPI. This framing, combined with Hauser's explicit hike threat and employment data running too hot, makes Australia the developed market most likely to surprise with a hike this cycle. Bendigo Bank tipped a November hike. Yet the AUD is declining despite this hawkish tone — the feed directly flags this contradiction. The explanation is likely terms-of-trade deterioration: oil falling (energy export revenue down), iron ore under pressure from weak China demand, creating a current account drag that overwhelms rate differentials. This is a classic small-open-economy trap: the central bank needs to tighten but the currency refuses to cooperate because commodity export revenues are falling.

Playbook ✓: Hawkish hold with hike risk is consensus.  |  ⚠️ Divergence: AUD weakness despite hawkish RBA is a genuine divergence — normally hike talk strengthens the currency. The commodity terms-of-trade channel is dominating the rate differential channel, which is a bearish signal for AUD-denominated assets.
