Playbook ✓: Rising yields compressing equity multiples, small-caps underperforming large-caps (Russell/Nasdaq ratio ↓2.7%), gold selling off as real rates surge (↓3.1%), Bitcoin weak (↓3.7%) — all textbook rate-hike playbook.  |  ⚠️ Divergence: The massive divergence is VIX at 17.5 in a "complacency" regime while the macro backdrop is genuinely threatening. The growth pulse is flashing amber — China's August retail sales rose only 0.4% and its property investment slump is the steepest on record — while equity vol says "nothing to see here." This is a classic late-cycle setup where cross-asset signals diverge before equities capitulate. With China's imports missing estimates in August and its retail sales up only 0.4%, this is a supply shock, not a demand boom. Historically, the Fed hiking into a supply shock with equities already declining and complacent vol has ended badly. I would be aggressively buying VIX calls and S&P puts here — the vol surface is mispriced relative to the macro risk distribution.

🇪🇺 European Union  Bearish  ↑ Rates Rising

Macro Stance: The ECB hiked rates to 2.5% on September 10, driven by persistent energy-cost-driven inflation, while simultaneously raising both inflation and growth forecasts. The eurozone is caught in the same oil supply shock as the US but with greater structural vulnerability given energy import dependency.

New / Most Important: The ECB's decision to hike while raising growth forecasts is noteworthy — it signals the ECB sees this as an inflation problem it must fight rather than a growth problem it can accommodate. Policymakers agreed inflation will "remain high amid energy crisis." The succession battle for Lagarde's replacement has begun, per the FT, which introduces medium-term policy uncertainty. The euro declining against CAD despite the hike suggests the market views the ECB as behind the curve relative to energy-driven inflation — the rate hike isn't enough to attract flows.

Playbook ✓: ECB hiking into energy shock, euro weakening despite hikes — classic European stagflation dynamics.  |  ⚠️ Divergence: The fact that the ECB raised growth forecasts alongside inflation forecasts is unusual for a supply-shock environment. Either they're seeing fiscal impulse or defense spending effects that offset the energy drag, or they're making a forecast error that will force a harder pivot later. Watch whether the raised growth forecast holds — if it doesn't, the ECB will be trapped having hiked into a recession, which would be structurally devastating for peripheral spreads.

🇯🇵 Japan  Mixed  ↑ Rates Rising

Macro Stance: The BOJ is set to deliver what headlines call the "fastest hike of the cycle," expected to bring rates to their highest level since 1995. The yen is near a 7-month high as markets price the hike, but Reuters warns the BOJ "risks disappointing markets" — suggesting the bar for hawkish surprise is very high. Scott Bessent has directly warned yen traders as hike bets intensify, an extraordinary level of US Treasury Secretary jawboning of a foreign central bank.

New / Most Important: This is structurally significant: we have three major central banks (Fed, ECB, BOJ) all hiking simultaneously into a global oil supply shock. The last time anything like this happened was arguably 2008, and the coordination of tightening amplifies the liquidity withdrawal effect on global risk assets. The BOJ hike is being framed partly as responding to "external pressure" (the Bessent Put concept from Moomoo), which means Japan is being forced to tighten to defend the yen rather than because domestic conditions warrant it. This creates a fragile equilibrium — if the yen strengthens too much post-hike, Japanese exporters and the Nikkei get crushed; if it doesn't strengthen enough, the BOJ faces pressure for more. Bitcoin "isn't flinching yet" at this BOJ hike, per Yahoo Finance, which contrasts with the August 2024 carry-trade unwind. The yen carry trade is smaller now, but the global liquidity impact of synchronized tightening is larger.

Playbook ✓: Yen strengthening into hike, Japanese equities under pressure from strong yen expectations.  |  ⚠️ Divergence: The Bessent intervention is genuinely unusual — the US Treasury Secretary publicly warning traders about a foreign central bank's rate decision suggests geopolitical coordination on currency levels that markets haven't fully priced. If the BOJ hike is seen as a "Bessent Put" for the yen, it reframes BOJ policy as partially a tool of US macro management, which has profound implications for JGB positioning and cross-border capital flows out of Japan.
