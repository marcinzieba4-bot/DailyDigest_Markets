🌍 Geographic Macro Overview

🇺🇸 United States  Mixed  ↑ rate risk rising

Macro Stance: The Fed is in a hawkish hold with a clear bias toward further tightening. Minutes released last week showed many officials said rate hikes may be needed if inflation doesn't cool. The 10Y yield has climbed to 4.64% (+4 bps vs 4W), while the 2Y has actually fallen to 4.24% (prior close, −7 bps), producing a +46 bps curve (positively sloped but still flat by historical standards, 12 bps steeper than four weeks ago) that hints at term premium repricing — the bond market is pricing persistent deficits and sticky inflation at the long end while the front end prices delayed hikes. Consumer confidence dipped. New home sales fell short of expectations.

New / Most Important: The FOMC minutes were the week's anchor event — they revealed a genuine debate about hiking, not just holding. This is structurally important: the market had been pricing the next move as a cut throughout most of 2025, and now the distribution has shifted. The widening Iran sanctions under Bessent target 60 entities but are designed to avoid pushing oil higher — a novel "surgical sanctions" playbook. US-Canada trade tensions heated up again. Cramer flagged a "jarring gulf" between stocks and the economy, and Invesco urged investors not to let debt fears derail market perspective — the fact that this needs saying is itself a signal.

Playbook ✓: Mega-cap tech leadership continues (Nasdaq 100 ↑5.0% vs 4W, Russell/Nasdaq ratio ↓3.1%), VIX at 15.49 in complacency regime, S&P grinding higher at 7668 — all consistent with the liquidity-driven narrow-market script.  |  ⚠️ Divergence: The curve steepening dynamic is the real story and it breaks the playbook badly. A 10Y-2Y spread steepening to +46 bps with the 10Y rising and the 2Y falling is NOT what you see in a normal tightening cycle — it signals that the bond market is repricing US fiscal risk and term premium while front-end traders bet the Fed ultimately can't hike without crashing housing (already soft) and the consumer (confidence already dipping). This is a fiscal dominance signature: the Fed talks hawk but the long end is moving independently, driven by supply dynamics — Treasury issuance, buyback program mechanics (headlines about buybacks redirecting capital to Asia), and foreign buyer fatigue. The DXY holding near 99 despite hawkish Fed minutes is the smoking gun — the dollar should be ripping on these minutes, and it isn't. Capital is flowing OUT of dollar-denominated long-duration assets even as rates rise, which is the textbook "selling your bonds AND your currency" dynamic last seen in the UK's 2022 gilt crisis. Watch this closely.

🇯🇵 Japan  Bearish (bonds) / Bullish (JPY)  ↑ hiking cycle imminent

Macro Stance: The BOJ is on the cusp of a historic tightening cycle. Markets are pricing 80% odds of a September hike. Japanese inflation has risen, and the yen is near the 160 level that has previously triggered intervention. Ex-BOJ officials see back-to-back rate hikes ahead.

New / Most Important: This is the most structurally important story in global macro right now. The BOJ is about to execute what headlines call its "first rate hike in 31 years" with momentum — ex-officials and market pricing both point to sequential hikes, not a one-and-done. The implications for global capital flows are enormous: Japan's ¥4+ trillion in overseas assets — the world's largest creditor nation position — faces repatriation pressure as domestic yields rise and the carry trade economics shift. MUFG notes yen risk is "skewed weaker despite 80% hike odds," which is a fascinating contrarian signal — it suggests the market thinks even a hike won't close the rate differential enough to matter. But if the BOJ delivers back-to-back hikes, the carry trade unwind could be non-linear. The EUR/JPY cross near 185.70 with both the ECB and BOJ tightening creates a rare scenario where both sides of a major pair are hiking — watch for vol compression followed by a violent directional break. The global liquidity impact of Japanese repatriation flows would tighten dollar funding conditions precisely when the US fiscal situation demands more foreign buyers, not fewer.

Playbook ✓: Yen strengthening on hike bets, JGB yields rising — as expected.  |  ⚠️ Divergence: MUFG's view that yen risk is skewed weaker despite 80% hike odds is the divergence. The carry trade community is fighting the BOJ, betting the differential remains wide enough to sustain short-yen positions. If they're wrong, the unwind echoes of August 2024 will look like a gentle rehearsal.

New / Most Important: The minutes revealed a genuinely divided board, with hike probability rising — this is hawkish relative to market expectations of a prolonged hold. The RBA's speech on shifting toward a "demand-driven liquidity regime" is a structural change in how Australian money markets will function, potentially tightening interbank conditions even without a formal rate hike. The AUD edged up on the hawkish minutes.

Playbook ✓: Sticky inflation keeping the RBA hawkish — consistent with Australia's commodity exposure and tight labor market.  |  ⚠️ Divergence: The liquidity regime shift is underappreciated. Moving from an ample-reserves to a demand-driven system while simultaneously considering rate hikes is a double tightening — one explicit (rates), one structural (liquidity). This mirrors elements of what the Fed did with QT, but Australia's smaller, more commodity-dependent economy is more vulnerable to liquidity accidents.
