But the bid is narrow — AI, chips and new listings — and the PMI upturn behind it rests on tech exports, while property, private credit and car sales still deteriorate. An export-driven PMI at 50.3, a loan book households will not use and resale prices falling faster in June mean the rally prices an AI premium, not a domestic recovery, and the transmission follows that split. Until loan growth and property sales outside the top tier turn, this is selective positioning, not macro confidence.

🇯🇵 Japan  Bearish  ↑ hiking cycle finally beginning

Macro Stance: The yen crisis is intensifying. USD/JPY at 162 — the market is calling Tokyo's bluff. The BOJ is under acute pressure from multiple directions: a sinking yen, robust economic data, government panel members calling for moderate hikes, and QT already underway (BOJ shed 15.6% of its massive assets per Wolf Street). Yet the BOJ's slow, dovish leadership revamp casts doubt on whether hikes will actually materialize at pace.

New / Most Important: This is the most structurally important story in global macro right now. USD/JPY at 162 represents a yen that has blown through every level the market assumed would trigger intervention or accelerated tightening. The BOJ is attempting QT as a substitute for rate hikes — shedding balance sheet rather than raising the policy rate — but the market is not accepting this as sufficient. Japan wage growth missed, clouding the hike outlook and keeping the yen under pressure. This is a carry-trade supercycle feedback loop: weak yen → capital outflows → weaker yen → BOJ credibility erodes → more carry selling. The headline "BOJ's First Rate Hike in 31 Years Gains Momentum" tells you how far behind the curve they are. Korean stocks flirting with bear territory are collateral damage from the yen — competitive devaluation pressure spreading across Asia. The Nikkei dropped on semiconductor earnings jitters, adding an equity-side headwind to the currency crisis. If the BOJ actually hikes into a semi downturn, the carry unwind could be violent and globally systemic.

Playbook ✓: Yen weakness forcing BOJ's hand — this has been the base case for a year.  |  ⚠️ Divergence: The BOJ choosing QT over rate hikes is the divergence — no major central bank has tried to defend its currency primarily through balance sheet reduction while keeping rates near zero. It isn't working.

🇦🇺 Australia  Bearish  ↑ rate hike risk rising

Macro Stance: Australia now tops inflation rankings among all major developed economies. Economists are calling for no rate cuts until 2027 and flagging a fourth hike as a live possibility. The RBA has been described as having "failed" on inflation.

New / Most Important: This is a structural break from the "synchronized global easing" narrative. Australia being the worst inflation performer in the developed world while simultaneously facing housing affordability conditions worse than the 17% rate era is a policy trap. If the RBA hikes again, it risks a housing crash in a market where household leverage is among the highest globally. If it doesn't, inflation persistence damages credibility and the AUD. This is a no-win setup — the kind of environment where you short the currency and short the banks simultaneously.

Playbook ✓: Sticky services inflation keeping the RBA hawkish — on script.  |  ⚠️ Divergence: The market pricing no cuts until 2027 is the divergence — this was supposed to be a 2025 easing story. Australia is now an outlier in the developed world, and capital flows will reflect that via AUD weakness and bank equity underperformance.
