Macro Stance: The Fed hiked rates on Sep 16, its first increase in three years, with a unanimous vote and signaled one more hike this year. Markets are now pricing two hikes. The 10Y yield has surged to 5.10% (+46 bps vs 4W ago), the highest since 2007. The 2Y at 4.71% (prior close, +47 bps) confirms the front end is repricing aggressively. New / Most Important: The Fed delivered its hike on Sep 16, defying Trump's demands for a cut — headlines explicitly frame this as a test of Fed independence. This is structurally important: the combination of a hiking Fed, rising oil (Iran's president vowed to "never surrender"), and a Treasury secretary who won't jawbone rates lower represents a genuine tightening of the financial conditions impulse. Capital flow implications are massive — higher US rates are already accelerating capital flight from EM (per ET Markets), and the cost-of-capital piece from J.P. Morgan on Sep 18 asks whether equities have caught up. They haven't — that's the risk.

Playbook ✓: Mega-cap AI names absorbing all the flow in a rising-rate environment — classic duration-indifferent momentum crowding. VIX at 15.14 in complacency regime while yields scream higher is textbook late-cycle "nothing can hurt us" positioning.  |  ⚠️ Divergence: The massive divergence is that the Fed is hiking INTO a market that is pricing complacency. Gold is down 7.9% in 4 weeks despite geopolitical risk surging — this is NOT a fear market, it's a liquidity-withdrawal market where real rates are crushing non-yielding assets. Gold's direction is down even as the growth pulse softens — US housing starts fell 2.6% in August — meaning the fear premium is eroding as rate differentials dominate. Most critically, Bessent's refusal to fight rising yields breaks the post-2020 playbook where Treasury would coordinate with Fed to cap term premium. We may be entering a regime where fiscal dominance concerns push term premium structurally higher regardless of the hiking cycle's endpoint.

🇯🇵 Japan  Bearish (for JPY)  ↑ hiking aggressively

Macro Stance: The BOJ hiked to its highest level since 1995 on Sep 18, the fastest pace of tightening since 1990. This is a regime shift — preemptive inflation fighting rather than reactive, per Bloomberg. Yet the yen continues to weaken, nearing 155 against the dollar.

New / Most Important: This is structurally the most important story in global macro right now. The BOJ is hiking at its fastest pace in 35 years and the yen is STILL falling — Chosun reports 170/dollar is "still in sight." This completely breaks the standard central bank playbook where rate hikes support the currency. The mechanism is clear: Japan's rate differential vs. the US remains massively negative (BOJ at ~1.25% vs. US rates far higher), so the carry trade unwind that briefly spooked markets in mid-2024 has reversed. Japanese institutional investors (lifers, pension funds) continue to recycle savings into higher-yielding US assets, creating a self-reinforcing loop where BOJ hikes → flatter JGB curve → more incentive to buy USTs → weaker yen. The BOJ dissent noted in the vote clouds the path forward and introduces policy uncertainty that further undermines yen confidence. For global macro, this means Japan remains the world's largest source of exported liquidity — the carry factory is still open despite rate hikes.

Playbook ✓: BOJ tightening was widely expected after persistent inflation overshoots.  |  ⚠️ Divergence: The yen weakening INTO a hiking cycle is the divergence. Historically unprecedented at this scale. It means real Japanese yields are still deeply negative and the structural capital outflow from Japan is not being arrested by policy rate changes alone — the BOJ would need quantitative tightening (balance sheet reduction) to meaningfully alter the flow dynamic.
