🇺🇸 United States  Mixed  → rates on hold

Macro Stance: The Warsh-led Fed held rates steady at its July 29 meeting, but three dissents — all in favor of a hike — signal a deeply fractured committee. The 10Y yield is surging at 4.69% (+21 bps vs 4W), while the 2Y at 4.28% (prior close, +14 bps vs 4W) has risen less, producing a +47bps curve that is positively sloped but still flat by historical standards. Oil at 79.47 (↑15.9% vs 4W) is a new inflation input. The Fed explicitly cited the Iran war oil shock as context for the hold. Fiscal stance remains expansive; no new consolidation signals.

New / Most Important: The three-dissent hold was the most hawkish "pause" in years. Warsh stated the Fed "won't hesitate to stop inflation," yet the bond market is skeptical — Reuters notes the market is "scratching its head." Big Tech earnings (Amazon, Meta, Microsoft) surged, but the Nasdaq 100 at 28,720 is actually ↓3.3% vs 4W, suggesting the broader mega-cap complex is under distribution even as individual beats occur. The Leopold Aschenbrenner AI fund blowup — a 4x-leveraged vehicle losing 67% in a month after being up 439% — is a microcosm of the speculative froth now unwinding in AI-adjacent levered strategies. The S&P at 7,587 (↑0.7%) is diverging sharply from the Nasdaq, which is structurally new: this is a rotation out of duration-sensitive growth into value/cyclical, confirmed by Russell/Nasdaq ratio ↑2.1%.

Playbook ✓: Fed hold amid conflicting data; oil shock providing cover for hawkish inaction; curve steepening as long-end reprices term premium.  |  ⚠️ Divergence: The three-dissent hawkish hold is structurally unusual under the new Warsh Fed. This is the first time in the modern era that three FOMC members voted for a hike while the chair held — it suggests the committee's reaction function has shifted materially. The market is not pricing a hike (VIX at 15.6 screams complacency), but the option space is mispriced if Warsh himself rotates toward the dissenters on the next inflation print. The fact that the 10Y is surging while equities are broadly flat means the bond market is doing the tightening the Fed won't — a classic late-cycle dynamic where the term premium becomes the policy tool. The Iran war oil spike layered onto this creates a stagflationary whiff that consensus has not yet internalized.

If the ECB hikes while the Fed holds, the EUR strengthening becomes self-defeating — a tighter financial condition overlay on already weak industrial activity.

PMIs & Activity: The two July surveys diverge in level but agree in direction. The official manufacturing PMI dropped to 49.2 (The State Council Information Office, [Jul 31]) and non-manufacturing to 49 (Macau Business, [Jul 31]), as factories and construction slumped together (The Edge Malaysia, [Jul 31]); the private survey still shows factory growth, but at a four-month low (Reuters, [Aug 2]). Reconciled: the export-oriented private sample is slowing, the broader official sample with its construction and domestic weight is contracting, and Bloomberg reads it as a slump that reinforces signs of slowdown ([Aug 2]). Against June's 50.3 (Reuters, [Jun 29]) the break is clear. Q2 GDP grew 4.3%, below the target range (Bloomberg, [Jul 15]), first-half industrial profits rose 18.7% as a tech boom masks the property slump (Caixin Global, [Jul 27]), and US-bound shipments fell in July (CNBC, [Jul 30]).

A dip in the debt ratio may not bode well for the economy (South China Morning Post, [Jul 31]), even as the local government debt clean-up makes progress (ISI Markets, [Aug 1]). The impulse reads flat, with households negative.

CXMT surged 466% on its Shanghai debut (Reuters, [Jul 27]), though The Business Times calls the US$10 billion IPO a flop ([Jul 28]): the first-day pop measured domestic capital's appetite for chip self-sufficiency, not post-listing returns. With the official PMI in contraction and the private gauge at a four-month low, that reads as a door opened to RRR and rate cuts. Playbook ✓: Domestic capital channelled into strategic tech, incremental easing and weak property are on script.  |  ⚠️ Divergence: Asia's other factories are firming, with South Korea's activity quickening on export demand (Reuters, [Aug 2]), while China's PMIs slide, so the drag is Chinese domestic demand rather than global trade. Playbook ✓: BOJ gradually normalizing; yen intervention when weakness threatens imported inflation.  |  ⚠️ Divergence: The bilateral US-Japan intervention pact is the divergence — this is NOT in any standard playbook. This has massive implications for the carry trade, for US Treasury demand (Japan is the largest foreign holder), and for the dollar's role as the unilateral FX anchor. If the US is now willing to co-intervene for allied currencies, the entire G10 FX regime is shifting.
