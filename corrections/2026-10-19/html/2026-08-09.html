🇺🇸 United States  Mixed  → rates on hold

Macro Stance: Fed under new Chair Warsh has signaled a smaller footprint in markets, holding rates steady amid conflicting data. Fiscal spending remains expansionary. The 10Y yield has risen to 4.66% (+9 bps vs 4W ago) while the 2Y sits at 4.19% (prior close, −2 bps vs 4W), producing a +46 bps curve — positively sloped but still flat by historical standards — with the steepening reflecting term premium re-pricing rather than imminent easing. VIX at 14.90 screams complacency.

New / Most Important: July payrolls shocked with a loss of 23,000 jobs — the first negative print in years — yet equities rallied (S&P 500 at 7757, ↑2.4% vs 4W). This is a structurally important divergence. The market is treating bad labor data as good news for eventual rate cuts, but Warsh's philosophical shift toward less market hand-holding means the "Fed put" strike price is much lower than the market assumes. The US also actively sold euros and intervened jointly with Japan to prop up the yen — an extraordinary and historically rare move for the Treasury to intervene against its own currency's trading partners. Bessent's comments nudging BOJ toward a September hike confirm this is a coordinated Plaza Accord-lite framework. CPI data due this week will be the critical test of whether Warsh can maintain the hold posture.

Playbook ✓: Equity markets making new highs on breadth expansion (Russell ↑1.9%, R/N ratio ↑2.3%) fits the late-cycle rotation playbook; gold surging (4399, ↑7.2%) on fiscal concerns and real-rate anxiety is textbook.  |  ⚠️ Divergence: The combination of negative payrolls, rising long-end yields, AND equities at all-time highs is genuinely anomalous. This is not a "bad news is good news" rate-cut trade — long yields are rising, not falling. What's happening is a liquidity-driven melt-up where capital flows into US equities as a default allocation even as the macro deteriorates. Gold at 4399 (↑7.2%) alongside S&P records suggests the equity rally is narrow in its macro conviction — big money is hedging with gold even as it buys stocks. If CPI comes in hot this week, the contradiction resolves violently to the downside. If CPI is cool, Warsh faces enormous pressure to cut into negative payrolls, but his stated philosophy resists that. Either outcome is explosive.

Playbook ✓: EUR strengthening on relative rate differentials narrowing is consensus.  |  ⚠️ Divergence: The US unilaterally selling euros to buy yen is a structural break in the post-GFC central bank coordination framework. The ECB being "blindsided" suggests this was not a consensual G7 operation but a bilateral US-Japan move with collateral damage to Europe. European policymakers may need to respond with their own intervention toolkit or accept euro appreciation that tightens financial conditions beyond what the ECB intends. This is a new regime for EUR crosses.

🇯🇵 Japan  Bullish  ↑ rates rising

Macro Stance: BOJ on a clear tightening path with September hike expectations firmly embedded. Fiscal stance neutral. The yen intervention has been the dominant macro story globally.

New / Most Important: This week's developments around the coordinated US-Japan yen intervention represent the most significant FX policy event since the Plaza Accord. The US actively supported yen strength — Reuters and CNBC detailed how Bessent and Japanese officials structured the bilateral pact to squeeze yen shorts. Bessent publicly stated confidence that BOJ's Ueda will "do what is best," which is diplomatic code for "hike in September or we'll have a problem." PM Takaichi is being pressured to align with BOJ's tightening — a notable political shift since she was previously seen as a dove. BOJ has lined up additional speaking events ahead of the September meeting, telegraphing intent. This is structurally new: the US has never in the modern era actively intervened to weaken its own currency against the yen while simultaneously pressuring the partner central bank to tighten. The implication is that the US views yen weakness as a financial stability threat (carry trade unwinds, Treasury market dysfunction) serious enough to warrant sacrificing dollar strength. For capital flows, this redirects Japanese institutional money back home — the largest pool of overseas fixed-income capital in the world begins to repatriate.

Playbook ✓: Yen strengthening and BOJ hawkishness are consensus.  |  ⚠️ Divergence: The US actively helping is NOT consensus — most desks had modeled unilateral Japan intervention with US tacit approval at best. Active US euro selling to fund yen support is a completely different animal with implications across every G10 cross.

The feed shows US drillers adding rigs in the Permian, but retail is focused on the demand side — rising oil into a weakening labor market screams stagflation to them, and energy stocks become the "inflation hedge that also pays dividends." China's official PMIs in contraction confirm a supply-shock narrative rather than a healthy demand-led move. The risk: if recession fears deepen from soft data and global demand rolls over, oil's supply-shock premium evaporates fast, as it did repeatedly in 2022–2023 corrections.

The Dow Jones futures coverage heading into Monday confirms retail is positioned long. The risk is a classic complacency trap: a hot CPI print this week could reprice rate cut expectations violently, and the 10Y yield already climbing to 4.66% is tightening financial conditions under the surface while nobody is hedging.

Bitcoin — Stalled but Stalked  MicroStrategy endgame + halving lag

Bitcoin at $65,175 is essentially flat over four weeks (↓0.1%), but it's simmering in retail consciousness. Yahoo Finance highlighted that "Michael Saylor's Bitcoin sales are about to end" — a narrative trigger for supply squeeze hopium — while crypto prices moved higher after the jobs report miss. The Gold/BTC ratio shows gold massively outperforming (+7.3pp divergence), which BTC bulls frame as "gold moved first, Bitcoin catches up next." The risk: with yields rising and gold absorbing all the fear-trade flows, Bitcoin may remain stuck in no-man's-land — too correlated to risk assets to benefit from recession fear, too speculative to attract the safety bid that's currently flooding into gold.
