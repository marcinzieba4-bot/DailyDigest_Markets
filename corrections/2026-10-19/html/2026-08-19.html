🇺🇸 United States  Mixed  → rates on hold / hawkish tilt

Macro Stance: The Fed remains in a restrictive hold. The 10Y yield sits at 4.66% (unchanged over 4 weeks), but the 30Y topped 5.31% — its highest in 19 years — signaling term-premium is the story, not the front end. The 2Y at 4.19% (prior close, −7 bps vs 4W) implies the market still prices eventual easing but the curve (10Y-2Y at +52 bps, positive but still flat by historical standards) reflects a regime where fiscal dominance, not Fed guidance, drives duration. Equity markets are pushing higher (S&P at 7715, +3.5% over 4 weeks) alongside record margin balances, a textbook late-cycle complacency signal with VIX at 15.24.

New / Most Important: Treasury Secretary Bessent doubled long-bond buybacks to contain the surge in long-end yields — this is the single most important policy development this week. This is structurally significant: the Treasury is now actively managing the yield curve through liability management, effectively short-circuiting the price discovery function of the long bond. This echoes Japan's YCC playbook but through buybacks rather than caps. It tells you the administration views 30Y yields above 5.3% as a systemic threat — to housing, to CRE refinancing, to the federal interest expense trajectory. If buybacks become a recurring tool, long-duration Treasuries become a policy-managed asset rather than a market-priced one, which paradoxically may reduce their efficacy as portfolio hedges. Watch whether this steepens or flattens the belly — my read is it compresses the 10s30s spread while doing nothing for the fundamental deficit problem, storing energy for a bigger move later. Meanwhile, FOMC minutes loom today and markets are watching for any signal on the bar for future cuts.

Playbook ✓: Equities grinding higher on AI momentum and buyback support, bond market stress contained by active Treasury intervention, gold surging as the "debasement hedge" ($4,552, +9.8% in 4 weeks) — all consistent with the fiscal dominance / financial repression playbook.  |  ⚠️ Divergence: Record margin debt alongside a VIX in "complacency" territory while the bond market is sounding alarms — this is the kind of cross-asset divergence that historically doesn't resolve gently. The Washington Post and Barron's both flagged the bond-equity disconnect this week. NPR reported margin balances at all-time highs. The ECB's own economists warned that AI-driven equity concentration makes a US tech correction "likely." When vol is suppressed, leverage is maxed, and the sovereign bond market is requiring emergency buybacks to stay orderly, the risk is non-linear. The equity market is pricing perfection while the bond market is pricing dysfunction — one of them is wrong.
