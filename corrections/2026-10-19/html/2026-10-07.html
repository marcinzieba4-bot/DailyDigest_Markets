🌍 Geographic Macro Overview

🇺🇸 United States  Mixed  ↑ rates rising / hike cycle

Macro Stance: The Fed is in a tightening or hold-at-peak posture — FOMC minutes due today could detail the rate-hike decision and forward policy path. Fiscal deficits remain structurally large, with 30-year Treasury yields hitting levels not seen since 2002. The 10Y yield at 5.32% is surging (+48 bps vs 4 weeks ago), the 2Y at 4.79% (prior close, +40 bps vs 4W), and the 10Y-2Y spread at +48 bps (prior close, 7 bps steeper than four weeks ago) is positive but still flat by historical standards — a bear steepening driven by term premium expansion, not benign growth expectations.

New / Most Important: S&P 500 hit all-time highs (7800.57, +2.2% vs 4W) even as bond yields surge violently — the most important divergence in global markets right now. Wall Street posted a record $45.9B profit in H1. Goldman warned that higher rates will pull down consumer spending. Michael Burry warned the stock market is in "Stage 1" of a crash with 6-9 months until Stage 2. The equity market is ignoring the bond market's distress signal — this is the textbook late-cycle setup where equity vol (VIX 15.37, -6.6%) compresses while rate vol explodes.

Playbook ✓: Mega-cap AI leadership continues to dominate — Nasdaq 100 +5.8% vs Russell 2000 -4.4% (Russell/Nasdaq ratio ↓9.6%), confirming the narrowest market breadth of this cycle. Liquidity is concentrating, not broadening.  |  ⚠️ Divergence: The structural break here is that equities are making new highs while the 10Y yield surges to 5.32% — historically, equities cannot sustainably rally through a 5%+ 10Y for more than a quarter or two without something cracking (housing, credit, or multiples). Mortgage rates sitting at 7.5% and an 8.5% drop in newly pending home sales in September tell you the real economy senses stress even as equity indices don't. Small caps (Russell -4.4%) are already pricing the pain from higher rates — they are the canary. The FOMC minutes today could reveal whether the committee discussed further hikes, which would widen this equity-bond chasm further. Capital flow dynamics are extraordinary: foreign buyers appear to be on a buyer's strike in long-duration Treasuries (hence the term premium surge), while equity inflows concentrate in a handful of AI mega-caps. This is a "narrow bridge over troubled water" market — structurally fragile.

🇪🇺 European Union  Bearish  ↑ rates rising / hiking pressure

Macro Stance: Eurozone inflation surged to 3.8%, its highest in three years, keeping maximum pressure on the ECB to hike again. ECB's Rehn warned that high long-term rates are contributing to a growth slowdown — the classic stagflationary bind. ECB's Lane flagged economic growth concerns. The monetary transmission mechanism is tightening aggressively via bank lending channels.

New / Most Important: The bond market selloff rocked Europe — investors are picking "new darlings and duds" as the repricing forces active rotation. French fiscal concerns eased slightly (per JPY cross headlines) but remain a structural overhang. The EU trade chief headed to China this week, signaling Brussels is trying to manage the tariff/trade relationship while unveiling pre-enlargement reforms.

Playbook ✓: ECB trapped between above-target inflation and slowing growth — textbook policy dilemma for a current-account-surplus bloc facing imported energy inflation and weak domestic demand.  |  ⚠️ Divergence: The ECB's diagnostic paper published this week acknowledged "diagnostic challenges" — this is unusually candid institutional uncertainty about the transmission mechanism. With inflation at 3.8% and rising while growth slows, the ECB may be forced into a hike that actively damages the periphery. European bond market stress is a capital-flow story: as US yields surge to 5.32%, the gravitational pull on global capital toward Treasuries (despite their own problems) drains liquidity from European sovereign and credit markets. This is the reflexive loop: European yields rise not because of European fundamentals, but because US term premium is repricing globally.

🇨🇳 China  Mixed  → easing bias but constrained

Macro Stance: The PBOC is easing while most major central banks hike or hold, but through structural tools: it cut the PSL rate to 1.5% and expanded relending quotas (Fibre2Fashion, [Sep 30]), a 25 bp cut to a targeted rate rather than to the LPR. The constraint is the rate gap, with US 10Y yields at 5.32% against Chinese lending at 1.7% (Modern Diplomacy, [Oct 6]). So far the yuan absorbs it: the fixing was set at a 3.5-year high before the National Day holiday (The Standard (HK), [Sep 30]), the currency was set for a seventh straight quarterly gain as exporters sold dollars (Business Recorder, [Sep 30]), and UOB sees gradual upside within defined bands (www.tmgm.com, [Oct 6]). Reserves tell the other half: FX reserves shrank by $38.1 billion while gold holdings rose for a record 23rd month (BigGo Finance, [Oct 6]). August CPI rose 0.8% and PPI 3.8% (BigGo Finance, [Sep 8]), so deflation no longer forces a broad cut.

PMIs & Activity: September brought the first official expansion in three months: manufacturing at 50.1, exactly the consensus, and non-manufacturing at 50.2 (investingLive, [Sep 29]), with the private RatingDog gauges stronger at 52.1 and 51.6 (investingLive, [Sep 30]). The rebound is AI-led (Reuters, [Sep 29]) and export-led after a 25% August jump (AP News, [Sep 7]); the domestic side lags, with August retail sales up 0.4% against 0.6% expected (investingLive, [Sep 14]) and industrial profits up just 4.2%, the weakest since November (Bloomberg, [Sep 27]). The first holiday reads are firmer: National Day retail sales rose as travel volumes surged (www.citynewsservice.cn, [Oct 6]). The quarter still looks soft, with Q3 growth estimated below target at 4.4% despite the AI boom (Nikkei Asia, [Oct 6]).

Credit Impulse: August bank loans rose less than expected (Reuters, [Sep 14]) and credit expansion missed forecasts (Bloomberg, [Sep 14]), with bond and equity financing exceeding loans for the first time (BigGo Finance, [Sep 14]): both measures missed, and government bonds carried what growth there was. The banking system is being shrunk, not levered: China closed hundreds of banks to bolster the financial system (Financial Times, [Oct 3]), 670 in 2025 (Yahoo Finance, [Oct 5]), and is accelerating the small-bank purge to contain systemic risks (CHOSUNBIZ - Chosunbiz, [Oct 6]), a clean-up that adds no lending capacity in the near term.

Property: The mortgage subsidy of 1 percentage point for first-time buyers (bloomingbit, [Sep 30]) is meant to unlock housing demand (China.org.cn, [Sep 30]), and the holiday gives the first test: Shenzhen and Guangzhou property markets picked up steam during National Day (chinadailyhk, [Oct 6]), after Beijing's September new-home sales surged nearly 50% month-on-month (BigGo Finance, [Oct 1]). The base is weak: August home prices fell across 70 cities (BigGo Finance, [Sep 14]) and property investment fell 19.9% in eight months (Business Recorder, [Sep 15]). Developers did not rally: property shares slid after the stimulus (Prop News Time, [Oct 2]), and the growth target is under scrutiny as stimulus struggles to lift housing (Traders Union, [Oct 6]).

Fiscal, Policy & Trade: Bloomberg judges the stimulus underwhelming, a sign the two-speed economy will persist (Bloomberg, [Sep 30]), and Beijing is seen doing 'just enough' to defend GDP growth (South China Morning Post, [Oct 2]). The EU-China talks are the live wire: they zero in on cars (POLITICO.eu, [Oct 6]), Franco-German unity paves the way for a tougher EU line (Bloomberg, [Oct 6]), and China opened an anti-dumping probe into European chemical exports beforehand (Financial Times, [Oct 3]). The UK weighs following the EU with EV tariffs (Bloomberg, [Oct 5]), and Washington has no timeline for the cuts on $60 billion of goods (Yahoo Finance, [Oct 1]).

Markets & Flows: The holiday thinned the tape: the SSE Composite stayed shut for Golden Week (BBN Times, [Oct 5]) and Hong Kong reopened without Stock Connect (BBN Times, [Oct 1]). The Hang Seng dropped 3%, Asia's worst, after the stimulus disappointed (NewsBytes, [Oct 2]) and the Hang Seng Tech index fell into a bear market on tax fears (Gotrade, [Oct 5]), before the Hang Seng closed 1 percent higher on Tuesday (The Standard (HK), [Oct 6]).

New / Most Important: The first holiday data and the first quarterly estimate point in different directions. Holiday retail and travel rose and Shenzhen and Guangzhou housing picked up, the first sign the mortgage subsidy reaches buyers; yet Q3 growth is estimated at 4.4%, below target, with exports and AI hardware doing the work. A holiday bounce in services and showrooms is not yet a credit impulse, and the reserve data show the yuan's strength is being managed rather than bought.

Playbook ✓: The deflationary domestic impulse, with weak consumption, property stress and cautious lending, continues on script, as does the drip-feed of targeted stimulus; trade partners push back on an export machine that keeps growing.  |  ⚠️ Divergence: The Seeking Alpha 'global liquidity regime shift' has a China leg: with the yuan firm and reserves shrinking, China is not recycling surpluses into Treasuries, while its growth stays export-led. Transmission to global assets therefore runs through exports, not demand. An AI-led PMI above 50 helps Asian tech supply chains, but with credit missing, property investment falling and August imports below estimates (CNBC, [Sep 7]), iron ore and the AUD get no property-led bid; the Australian dollar struggled despite the solid PMIs (FXStreet, [Sep 30]) and ASX stocks most exposed to the Chinese consumer are sinking fast (AFR, [Oct 6]). European luxury and auto exporters face a weak Chinese consumer and now a trade fight centred on cars, so the reflation trade in EM and European exporters stays impaired until credit and housing turn.

🇯🇵 Japan  Mixed  ↑ slow normalization, QT accelerating

Macro Stance: The BoJ is "pussyfooting around on rate hikes" while pushing QT to prop up the plunging yen — a split-personality policy that cannot persist. The yen weakened to the lower 158 range against the dollar as markets scaled back BoJ rate-hike bets. A former reflationist BoJ policymaker called an end to the era of low rates and big spending — a significant philosophical shift from Japan's old guard.

New / Most Important: BoJ rate-hike expectations receded materially this week, pushing the yen weaker. The BoJ is substituting QT (balance sheet reduction) for rate hikes as its primary tool to support the currency — this is structurally novel and potentially destabilizing for JGB markets.

Playbook ✓: Yen weakness continues as US-Japan rate differentials widen with US 10Y surging to 5.32%.  |  ⚠️ Divergence: The BoJ's QT-instead-of-hikes approach is a structural experiment. By reducing JGB purchases while keeping rates low, they're attempting to steepen the JGB curve and withdraw liquidity without the blunt instrument of rate hikes. The risk is a disorderly JGB selloff — Japan's government debt-to-GDP means even small yield increases have massive fiscal implications. The yen at 158 is approaching intervention territory, and if the BoJ cannot credibly signal hikes, verbal intervention loses potency. Japanese institutional investors (lifers, pension funds) repatriating capital to capture higher domestic yields would be a structural global capital flow event — it would drain liquidity from US Treasuries, European bonds, and credit markets simultaneously.

Headlines like "surging yields could shake things up" and Europe's bond market "selloff" reinforce the conviction. The risk is a sudden flight-to-safety event (geopolitical shock, equity crash, credit event) that triggers a violent bond rally and crushes the crowded short, exactly as happened in past cycles when everyone was convinced rates could only go one way.
