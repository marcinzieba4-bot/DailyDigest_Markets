🇺🇸 United States  Mixed  ↑ rate direction (hawkish pivot)

Macro Stance: Fed Chair Warsh delivered his Jackson Hole debut this past Friday with what the market read as a hawkish turn. The 10Y yield sits at 4.67% (−7 bps vs 4W), the 2Y at 4.34% (prior close, +6 bps vs 4W), and the curve at +39 bps is positively sloped but still flat by historical standards, 8 bps flatter than four weeks ago. The VIX at 14.51 (↓15.1% vs 4W) screams complacency even as rate-hike expectations repriced. Fiscal policy remains expansionary — the Venezuela oil deal expanding U.S. control to 7.1% of proven global reserves is a structural energy-security shift.

New / Most Important: Warsh's Jackson Hole speech was the week's defining event. Reuters flagged rising rate-hike expectations on the back of it, WSJ called it a hawkish turn, and Harvard's Rogoff praised Warsh for "defying political pressure." This is structurally new: we have a Fed chair who appears willing to tighten or at minimum hold firm in an election-adjacent environment, breaking the post-2020 pattern of dovish accommodation. The market reaction was telling — equities barely flinched (S&P 7,731, ↑3.9% vs 4W) while bonds sold off, meaning equity bulls are treating hawkishness as a credibility signal rather than a growth threat. This sets up a dangerous divergence: if September data (jobs week ahead Aug 31–Sep 4) comes in hot, equities may finally have to price what bonds already reflect. The AI capex narrative (WSJ: "AI investment craze keeping the global economy afloat") is providing air cover for now, but a Warsh hike would be the first under his chairmanship and would reset the entire global rate calculus.

Playbook ✓: Mega-cap tech leadership continues — Nasdaq 100 up 5.5% vs 4W, Russell/Nasdaq ratio ↓3.0% confirming narrow breadth. Microsoft, Palantir leading software bounce exactly as the quality-over-cyclicals script dictates.  |  ⚠️ Divergence: Gold at 4,609.70 (↑12.4% vs 4W) is screaming while VIX is at complacency levels — this Gold/VIX combination is deeply unusual and suggests someone large is hedging a tail risk that equity vol markets aren't pricing. Gold's surge alongside a hawkish Fed is particularly anomalous: typically gold underperforms in rate-hike cycles. The buyer here is likely sovereign — central bank reserve diversification and geopolitical hedging (Iran sanctions intensifying, Venezuela deal reshuffling petro alliances) are overwhelming the rate-differential argument against gold. Bitcoin at 78,043 (↑20.2% vs 4W) moving in sync with gold (divergence only -7.8pp) confirms this is a monetary-regime hedge, not a simple risk-on trade.

🇪🇺 European Union  Mixed  ↑ rate direction (hike likely)

Macro Stance: The ECB is on a tightening trajectory. July meeting accounts revealed hawks were ready to hike, and eurozone inflation is set to rise further. Lending growth picked up in July after the ECB's rate hike, though business lending fell — a classic split where mortgage/consumer credit responds before corporate demand.

New / Most Important: ECB's Kocher stated the European economy shows momentum but flagged threats to price stability, effectively pre-announcing a September hike. Eurozone lending growth accelerating post-hike is the exact opposite of what the transmission mechanism is supposed to deliver — credit should be contracting, not expanding. This is the structural problem: the ECB hiked, and the economy absorbed it, which means the terminal rate is higher than the market had priced. A September ECB hike now looks near-certain, synchronized with a potentially hawkish Fed and a hiking BOJ — we haven't had a true triple-tightening across the three largest DM central banks since pre-GFC. The euro trimming intraday losses as traders weighed relative Fed-ECB paths suggests FX markets see rate convergence, not divergence.

Playbook ✓: Inflation persistence forcing continued tightening — textbook for a services-heavy economy with tight labor markets.  |  ⚠️ Divergence: Business lending falling while consumer lending rises creates a toxic mix — it means households are leveraging while corporates pull back. This is a late-cycle signal that typically precedes credit stress by 6-12 months.

New / Most Important: The property package is a stronger-than-expected policy move, and it is structural: curbing presales and stretching mortgages to 40 years lowers buyers' delivery and payment risk, but shifting away from presales also removes a funding channel weaker developers depend on, days after Vanke's loss. It stabilises confidence without adding the fiscal demand that profits, autos and a contracting PMI say is missing.

🇯🇵 Japan  Bearish (bonds) / Bullish (JPY)  ↑ rate direction (hike imminent)

Macro Stance: The BOJ is accelerating its tightening campaign. Deputy chief Himino drove home inflation risks, keeping the door open for a September rate increase. Tokyo core CPI inflation came in hot, bolstering the case.

New / Most Important: Reuters reported the BOJ plans to speed up tightening and raise rates in September — this is structurally the most important development in Japanese macro in a generation's worth of policy. Commerzbank warned "anything else would put the yen under significant pressure," effectively framing the hike as a necessity rather than a choice. The Takaichi political dynamic adds complexity — the yen slide is putting the BOJ in a bind where domestic politics and monetary orthodoxy point in the same direction for once. This is the end of the carry-trade paradigm that has defined global FX flows for two decades. If the BOJ hikes to the levels being discussed, the repatriation of Japanese capital from foreign bond markets (particularly USTs and European govies) will be a structural headwind for global duration assets. The August 2024 unwind was a preview — but this time the BOJ is signaling clearly, so the move will be more orderly but more persistent. Watch Japanese life insurer hedging ratios as the leading indicator.

Playbook ✓: Inflation persistence forcing normalization — Japan joining the global tightening club as expected.  |  ⚠️ Divergence: The speed is the surprise. Moving to a September hike with forward guidance for further tightening is far more aggressive than the gradual normalization consensus had expected even six months ago.

🇦🇺 Australia  Bearish (rates/housing)  ↑ rate direction (hike bets rising)

Macro Stance: The RBA remains hawkish with the board divided — it debated a rate hike at the August meeting. July inflation ran hotter than expected, and three of four major banks now forecast another hike this year.

New / Most Important: This is a market where the housing slump and rate hikes are on a collision course. The AFR explicitly argued the RBA "must lift rates in September despite the housing slump" — a remarkable editorial position that shows how embedded inflation anxiety has become in Australian policy discourse. The Aussie dollar strengthened on hike repricing, which creates a secondary tightening channel through the trade-weighted index. Australia is the canary for what happens when a heavily indebted household sector meets persistent inflation: the RBA is being forced to choose between financial stability and price stability, and it's choosing price stability. This has echoes of 2008-era UK policy errors.

Playbook ✓: Hot inflation → hawkish repricing → AUD strength. Textbook.  |  ⚠️ Divergence: Housing slump deepening while rates may still rise — the RBA is tightening into asset deflation, which is unusual for a central bank so historically attuned to property markets.

🌏 EM Spotlight: Venezuela  Mixed  → geopolitical repricing

Macro Stance: Venezuela is trending on X and in headlines following Trump's oil deal that gives the U.S. control of 7.1% of proven global reserves.
