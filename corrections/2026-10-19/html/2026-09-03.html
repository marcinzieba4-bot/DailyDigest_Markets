🌍 Geographic Macro Overview

🇺🇸 United States  Mixed  → hold / pause

Macro Stance: The Fed remains in a hawkish-hold posture. The 10Y yield is elevated at 4.75% (+8 bps vs 4W ago), while the 2Y sits at 4.39% (prior close, +21 bps vs 4W), leaving a +40 bps curve — positively sloped but still flat by historical standards, a bear flattening that reflects front-end hike repricing rather than imminent easing. Mortgage rates hit their highest level in over a year per today's headlines. Fiscal policy remains expansionary by default, with no consolidation in sight.

New / Most Important: Fed Governor Waller signaled a September hold, explicitly asking markets to "give disinflation a chance." This triggered a major risk-on response: the Dow surged ~600 points, bond yields retreated intraday, and traders pared rate-hike bets. Bitcoin pumped 28.6% over 4 weeks as $415M in shorts were liquidated. The Waller speech is structurally important: it confirms the Fed's bar for HIKING further is very high, but the bar for CUTTING is also high — a genuine plateau regime. This is the first time in this cycle a senior Fed official has explicitly framed disinflation as a process requiring patience rather than policy action, which functionally caps the rate ceiling while keeping the floor elevated. The market's euphoric response to a mere "hold" signal reveals just how much latent hike fear was embedded in positioning.

Playbook ✓: Large-cap tech continues to lead — S&P 500 at 7741 (↑0.4%), Nasdaq 100 at 29450 (↑0.3%), while Russell 2000 at 2966 (↓1.2%). Narrow mega-cap dominance is textbook late-cycle. VIX at 14.6 in complacency regime confirms hedging demand is anemic.  |  ⚠️ Divergence: Gold at $4534 (↑6.9%) is pricing fear — a striking contradiction to VIX complacency. This divergence is structurally significant: equity vol markets are pricing serenity, but the real-asset market is pricing tail risk, and macro hedgers in commodity space see something equity options markets do not. Oil surging 18.5% to $91.60 while US factory growth slowed (ISM manufacturing at 54.6 in August) and China's official factory PMI stayed below 50 at 49.8 confirms this is a supply-shock narrative, NOT a demand-led commodity cycle. This configuration — complacent VIX, a fear bid in gold, supply-driven oil — typically precedes an equity volatility repricing event within 4-8 weeks.

🇪🇺 European Union  Bearish  ↑ hiking

Macro Stance: The ECB is in an active tightening stance, with further rate hikes signaled. Eurozone inflation rose to 3.3% in August, driven primarily by energy costs. The euro remains weak against the dollar, trading in a narrow range at depressed levels.

New / Most Important: This week's inflation print confirmed energy prices are re-accelerating eurozone CPI above 3%, forcing the ECB into what ING characterizes as a "dovish hike path" — reluctant tightening driven by headline inflation they cannot ignore. Nomura flagged that further ECB hikes could boost the euro in Q4. This is a structurally painful setup: the ECB is hiking into what is almost certainly a weakening growth backdrop, compelled by energy-driven inflation that monetary policy cannot address. The eurozone is importing a supply shock via oil (WTI ↑18.5%) and converting it into a policy tightening error. Capital flow implications are negative: higher rates attract some carry inflows, but growth deterioration will dominate portfolio allocation decisions. The euro's failure to rally on hike expectations is the tell — FX markets are pricing the growth damage ahead of the rate differential advantage.

Playbook ✓: Energy-driven inflation forcing reluctant ECB hikes is textbook stagflationary dynamics for the bloc.  |  ⚠️ Divergence: The euro's persistent weakness despite hawkish ECB rhetoric and widening rate differentials vs. some peers is anomalous. Markets are voting that the ECB's hiking cycle is self-defeating.

🇨🇳 China  Bearish  ↓ easing bias

Macro Stance: China remains in an easing posture amid persistent deflationary pressure, and the PBOC is now easing through its balance sheet: the central bank bought a net 50 billion yuan of sovereign bonds in August (TradingView, [Sep 2]). Governor Pan told the G20 that China never deliberately pursues a trade surplus (Reuters, [Sep 2]) and set out a four-pronged growth push (BigGo Finance, [Sep 2]). The currency is being managed down: China is reining in the rising yuan as weak domestic demand clouds the outlook (Reuters, [Aug 31]), and the yuan eased as rising oil prices and higher US yields weigh (Business Recorder, [Sep 2]). July CPI slowed to 0.5%, more than expected (BigGo Finance, [Aug 9]), and PPI growth to 3.5%, missing expectations (BigGo Finance, [Aug 8]).

PMIs & Activity: The August surveys improved on every private measure. The official manufacturing PMI rebounded to 49.8 with the new orders index back in expansion (BigGo Finance, [Aug 30]) after July's 49.2 (FXStreet, [Jul 31]); the private manufacturing PMI rose to 51.5, its longest expansion streak in five years (BigGo Finance, [Aug 31]); and the private services PMI rebounded to 51.4 (BigGo Finance, [Sep 2]), quickening on stronger domestic demand (Yahoo Finance, [Sep 2]). The headlines conflict on services: Reuters read the official survey as weak services signalling an uneven recovery (Reuters, [Aug 30]), and FocusEconomics sees both official PMIs staying soft (FocusEconomics, [Sep 2]). Reconciled: the private surveys, tilted to export-facing and coastal firms, are in expansion, while the official gauges, broader and more domestic, remain soft — the rebound still depends on exports (EU Today, [Aug 31]). Industrial profit growth hit a seven-month low in July (Anadolu Ajansı, [Aug 27]) and auto sales plunged 20% (BigGo Finance, [Aug 28]).

Credit Impulse: This is the bearish leg. July bank loans posted a record contraction (Reuters, [Aug 14]) even as credit growth exceeded forecasts (Bloomberg, [Aug 14]); reconciled, the aggregate holds only because the state borrows while households and firms do not. The slowdown is now a global risk-asset theme: a China indicator that supports risk-taking in stocks and bitcoin is flashing red (CoinDesk, [Sep 2]). The bond leg will grow as China speeds up issuance after a national meeting on investment (South China Morning Post, [Aug 31]), and the five largest banks posted their strongest first-half profit since the height of the property crash (Reuters, [Aug 28]), so the problem is demand for credit, not supply.

Property: New home prices were stagnant in July as demand stayed weak (Reuters, [Aug 17]). China is curbing presales to reshape property finance after Evergrande (Chosunbiz, [Sep 2]), calling time on the home-sales system that worsened its meltdown (Bloomberg.com, [Sep 1]) after extending mortgage terms to 40 years (www.briefasia.com, [Aug 30]). The reform helps buyers, not developers: property stocks fell as the new rules upend the presale funding playbook (Reuters, [Aug 31]), Vanke's first-half loss widened as sales slumped (Caixin Global, [Aug 29]), and a Reuters poll sees the investment slump deepening (Reuters, [Aug 27]). The one bright spot is the resale segment, with Shanghai's used housing market showing vitality (chinadailyhk, [Sep 2]).

Fiscal, Policy & Trade: G20 finance chiefs except China backed action on distorted trade (Reuters, [Sep 2]), and Europe is moving from words to deadlines: EU trade enforcers descended on Beijing demanding China fix untenable imbalances (South China Morning Post, [Sep 2]), and Šefčovič says China must deliver by October or face harsher measures (Euronews.com, [Sep 2]). With Washington the tone is transactional: the US and China are debating nonsensitive tariff cuts ahead of the Trump-Xi summit (Nikkei Asia, [Sep 2]) even as the US slaps 100% tariffs on drones (Yahoo! Finance Canada, [Sep 2]). At home fiscal policy extracts rather than supports: China removed the tax exemption on foreigners' dividend income (The Edge Singapore, [Sep 2]) and a local surtax of 11%–13% has entered legislation (BigGo Finance, [Aug 28]), while hidden local government debt has been cut by half in two years (Caixin Global, [Sep 1]).

Markets & Flows: China escapes the global bond rout, but for the wrong reasons (Reuters, [Sep 2]): Chinese bond yields fell below 1.7% (Mezha.net, [Sep 2]) and the US-China 10-year gap widened to 312 basis points, near a record (bloomingbit, [Sep 2]). Equities are torn: the Hang Seng closed down 0.23% as Shanghai dropped nearly 1% (24/7 Wall St., [Sep 2]), while stocks rebounded on the upbeat PMI data (TradingView, [Sep 2]). China widened access to overseas stocks with a QDII quota handout (South China Morning Post, [Sep 2]), another outflow valve.

New / Most Important: The private services PMI at 51.4, quickening on domestic demand, is the first August signal that is not purely export-led. The bond market is not buying it: yields below 1.7% while US, European and Japanese yields surge price deflation, not recovery, and record negative loans support that read. Until credit demand turns, the PMIs are a stabilisation, not a cycle.

Playbook ✓: Sub-1.7% bond yields and a record loan contraction are consistent with subdued Chinese domestic demand.  |  ⚠️ Divergence: The surveys improved while bonds and credit deteriorated, and the transmission runs through both. Better new orders and services support iron ore expectations and the AUD at the margin, but a 312 basis-point yield gap and a managed-down yuan push Chinese savings abroad and keep EM Asia currencies under dollar pressure. An October EU deadline puts European exporters on the front line of any retaliation cycle, and with WTI at $91.60 China's terms of trade are deteriorating just as its import demand stays soft.
