Macro Stance: The Fed is expected to leave rates unchanged at the September meeting after recent soft inflation data caused rate-hike odds to plunge. New / Most Important: The 30-year Treasury yield hit levels not seen since 2007, per Chase. Retail sales surprised to the downside, pushing the dollar lower mid-week. Michigan consumer sentiment dipped below expectations. WSJ flagged a "Jobless Boom" — rising unemployment even as equities make new highs. Markets flipped expectations on Fed Chair Warsh's posture. The S&P hit 7786 (↑4.4% vs 4W) in a classic narrow-leadership rally — Russell/Nasdaq ratio fell 1.4%, confirming mega-cap dominance.

Playbook ✓: Equities ripping higher on AI earnings beats (Amazon, Alphabet circular capex boom), VIX crushed to 14.25 (↓24.1%), gold surging as dual safe-haven/debasement play.  |  ⚠️ Divergence: This is a structurally novel setup that deserves attention. We have a bear steepener with equities at all-time highs — historically these regimes don't coexist for long. The long end is repricing for fiscal dominance: $39T debt stock, no credible consolidation path, and the Bessent "K-shaped economy is over" narrative colliding with data showing 50% of Americans own no stocks. The VIX at 14.25 in a complacency regime while gold surges 9.2% and China's July bank loans post a record contraction is a genuine divergence — equity vol is underpricing something that credit and commodities are already sniffing out. The "Jobless Boom" dynamic — rising unemployment with robust corporate profits — is the kind of late-cycle bifurcation that historically precedes a non-linear equity repricing. Watch the long bond: if 30Y yields keep pushing higher, the equity risk premium compression that justified this rally becomes untenable.

The yuan heads for a 7th weekly gain on strong fixing and waning US rate hike hopes (The Standard (HK), [Aug 14]), and the five-year plan rules out devaluation (FXStreet, [Aug 10]). July CPI rose 0.5% (CGTN, [Aug 9]), a six-month low (TradingView, [Aug 8]), with PPI up 3.5% (China Daily, [Aug 9]).

PMIs & Activity: July's official PMIs both sit below 50, manufacturing at 49.2 and non-manufacturing at 49.0 (FXStreet, [Jul 31]). The private manufacturing PMI eased to 50.9 (Fibre2Fashion, [Aug 3]), a 4-month low (Reuters, [Aug 2]), and services growth hit its slowest since September 2024 (Reuters, [Aug 4]). Exports rose 23.9% as high-tech shipments strengthened (The Asian Banker, [Aug 14]), but the high-tech boom is seen failing to halt the slowdown (Bloomberg, [Aug 13]). Chinese car sales are booming almost everywhere except at home (Reuters, [Aug 14]): July new car sales fell 21% (finance.biggo.com, [Aug 11]) and NEV retail dropped 17% in the first week of August (CnEVPost, [Aug 13]). Goldman sees retail sales growth staying weak (Investing.com, [Aug 14]).

Credit Impulse: The July print confirms the demand problem and splits the headline. New bank loans posted a record contraction as credit demand faltered (Reuters, [Aug 14]), slipping below zero for the second time this year (The Standard (HK), [Aug 14]), yet credit growth exceeded forecasts despite the rare loan slump (Bloomberg, [Aug 14]). Reconciled: aggregate financing was carried by channels outside bank lending while the loan book itself shrank; cumulative yuan loans still rose 10.38 trln yuan in January–July (Xinhua, [Aug 14]). Price is falling faster than volume: corporate loan rates fell below 3% as the PBOC signals a shift from volume to price (finance.biggo.com, [Aug 14]), and banks are testing repo-linked corporate loans (South China Morning Post, [Aug 14]). Cheaper credit with fewer borrowers is the textbook liquidity-trap signature, and banks also look to unload US$8.2b in bad credit-card loans (South China Morning Post, [Aug 11]).

Property: Easing continues at the city level: top cities are relaxing rules for home purchases (chinadailyhk, [Aug 12]) after Beijing eased home-buying curbs (Bloomberg, [Aug 10]). Prices have not responded: a private survey shows housing prices fell 0.5% in July as sales volume weakened (Investing.com, [Aug 3]), and real home prices sank to their lowest level in 21 years (the deep dive, [Aug 3]). The official June data showed new home prices falling at a slower pace (Reuters, [Jul 14]) but property investment down 18 percent y/y in January–June (The Standard (HK), [Jul 15]). Expiring land leases are a $148 billion threat (Bloomberg, [Aug 11]) that Beijing is moving to defuse (Sri Lanka Guardian, [Aug 13]). The official July price survey is still to come.

Fiscal, Policy & Trade: Fiscal stress shows up in collection: Breakingviews says China's emboldened taxman is just getting started (Reuters, [Aug 14]), authorities are mounting a tax-collection crackdown in a sign of fiscal worry (Bloomberg, [Aug 12]), and social security checks are tightening as fiscal pressures mount (ANI News, [Aug 14]). Trade frictions are building into the summit: Trump imposed 100% tariffs on some drones (The Japan Times, [Aug 14]), the US accused dozens of countries of helping China dodge tariffs (BBC, [Aug 14]), an ECB blog warns China's aid for key sectors can feed trade tensions (Bloomberg, [Aug 14]), and a US-China "Board of Investment" has stalled before the expected Trump-Xi meeting (South China Morning Post, [Aug 13]). Agriculture is the bargaining chip, with China's US deal gaining soybean traction (World Grain, [Aug 14]) while US and Mexican pecans face anti-dumping tariffs of up to 54.3% (Global Times, [Aug 10]).

Markets & Flows: Equities are not buying the easing: China and Hong Kong stocks fell as liquidity tightened ahead of the Unitree IPO (The Economic Times, [Aug 14]), JD.com slid after a rare revenue plunge (Financial Times, [Aug 13]), and regulators are clamping down on margin debt after the AI stock frenzy (Bloomberg, [Aug 10]). The safe-asset bid is in bonds and gold, with the World Gold Council reporting strong official sector buying in July (World Gold Council, [Aug 14]).

New / Most Important: July new loans contracted at a record pace even as the PBOC cut the price of money through overnight operations and corporate loan rates fell below 3%. That is monetary transmission failing at the borrower, and the official message of tailored easing over broad stimulus says no fiscal bazooka is coming to fill the gap. The headline credit beat rests on non-loan financing, not private demand, which keeps the stance bearish into the July activity and home-price data.

Playbook ✓: Weak domestic demand, an easing bias and targeted rather than broad support are on script.  |  ⚠️ Divergence: A 10-year yield at an over 1-year low and a yuan on a 7th weekly gain look like confidence but are the signature of savers chasing safe assets while loans shrink. For global assets the transmission runs through credit and construction: Fortescue's Super Special Fines iron ore stocks at China ports are at a five-week high (Reuters, [Aug 14]), so iron ore and AUD carry the downside; EM Asia competes with exports up 23.9% that China cannot absorb at home; and WTI at $82.40 is a terms-of-trade tax on margins with PPI easing.

Faster BoJ hikes → higher JGB yields → reduced Japanese appetite for USTs → US term premium expansion → bear steepener. This is not a one-week story; it's a regime change in the plumbing of the global fixed income market. The yen rebounding from two-week lows on softer USD and hike bets suggests the FX channel is already transmitting. If the BoJ delivers in September, expect a second wave of JGB yield normalization that reverberates into US and European sovereign curves.
