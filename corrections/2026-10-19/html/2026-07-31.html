🌍 Geographic Macro Overview

🇺🇸 United States  Bearish  → rates on hold (hawkish hold)

Macro Stance: The Warsh-led Fed held rates steady on July 29 for the fifth consecutive meeting this year, but the meeting revealed a deeply fractured committee — three officials voted for a hike. Fiscal stance remains loose with the SPR at a 43-year low signaling continued drawdown. The 10Y yield is surging at 4.74% (+25 bps vs 4W), the 2s10s curve is positively sloped at +45bps (still flat by historical standards), and the bond market is repricing higher-for-much-longer. Q2 GDP growth came in weaker than expected but the composition showed elevated labor costs, creating a stagflationary flavor. WTI crude oil is ripping (↑23.3% vs 4W), which feeds directly into the inflation problem the Fed can't solve with rate holds.

New / Most Important: The three-dissent hawkish hold is structurally significant. This is the first time under Warsh's chairmanship that internal pressure for a hike has been this visible. The bond market's reaction — yields surging, Dow dropping 1,153 points on Fed day — suggests markets read the hold as insufficient given the oil shock feeding through to inflation expectations. The US Treasury reportedly informed banks it may intervene in Japan's yen, which is a new cross-border capital flow signal: if the US is coordinating FX intervention with Japan, it implies dollar strength is becoming a policy problem, not just a market outcome. Private sector wage gains lifted Q2 labor costs even as the "underlying trend" was called benign — a split that will keep the hawks agitated. Consumer distress signals (retirees turning to credit cards for groceries) suggest the rate level is already restrictive for the real economy even as financial conditions remain loose enough to sustain equities near highs.

Playbook ✓: Equities grinding sideways-to-down (S&P ↓0.2%, Nasdaq ↓3.7%) as rising yields compete with mega-cap earnings — textbook late-cycle compression. VIX at 16.85 in "complacency" territory even as macro risks accumulate is classic pre-correction positioning.  |  ⚠️ Divergence: The oil/yields co-surge is the divergence that matters. WTI up 23% in four weeks while the Fed is on hold creates a feedback loop the playbook doesn't price: energy costs flow into CPI with a lag, meaning the August and September prints will likely re-accelerate, potentially forcing the Fed's hand toward the hike the three dissenters wanted. This is NOT a demand-driven oil move — China's July factory activity unexpectedly contracted and the US 30-year mortgage rate rose to its highest level in a year, confirming a supply-shock narrative. The market is priced for a soft landing that requires oil to cooperate, and oil is not cooperating. If Warsh is forced into a hike by September, the repricing in duration will be violent given how much complacency is embedded in VIX at sub-17.

🇪🇺 European Union  Mixed  ↑ rates likely rising (hike expected Sep)

Macro Stance: The ECB is now on course for a September rate hike after eurozone inflation surprised to the upside, rising to 2.9% in July. Bund yields are holding firm. The macro data is running hot enough to embolden hawks, even as the ECB's own wage tracker detected no pickup in wage pressures — a contradiction the market hasn't fully digested.

New / Most Important: July eurozone HICP at 2.9% — reported today — is the key fresh data point. This is structurally important because it reverses the disinflationary narrative that had been building in H1 2026. Higher energy prices (WTI ↑23%) are the primary driver, and the ECB faces the same oil-pass-through dilemma as the Fed. The euro rose on strong data and hike bets, which tightens financial conditions further. The wage tracker showing no pickup is being ignored by the market, but it matters: if the inflation is purely supply-side (energy), a rate hike is a policy error that compresses demand without addressing the cause. The ECB hiking into a supply shock while the US holds would create a notable transatlantic monetary divergence for the first time in this cycle — bullish EUR, bearish European risk assets, and a potential capital flow catalyst out of European equities into European fixed income.

Playbook ✓: Data-dependent ECB following CPI higher — standard reaction function.  |  ⚠️ Divergence: The wage/inflation disconnect — wages calm, CPI rising — means the ECB may be hiking into a growth slowdown masked by energy-driven headline inflation. Watch European credit spreads for the first signs of stress.

🇯🇵 Japan  Bearish (for JGBs)  ↑ rates rising (hike telegraphed)

Macro Stance: The BOJ held rates at 1.00% on July 31 as expected, but the communication was unambiguously hawkish — Governor Ueda warned core inflation is exceeding the 2% target and explicitly kept a September hike in play. The BOJ revised its inflation forecast down but this is a technicality; the forward guidance is tightening-biased.

New / Most Important: The suspected yen intervention ahead of the BOJ meeting, combined with the US Treasury reportedly informing banks it may intervene in the yen, is a major cross-border capital flow event. This is structurally new: coordinated or parallel FX intervention between the US and Japan hasn't been on the table since the 2022-era yen weakness. The signal is that both governments now view yen weakness as destabilizing — for Japan because of imported inflation, for the US because dollar strength is becoming politically toxic. If the BOJ hikes in September AND intervention continues, the unwind of yen-funded carry trades could be a systemic liquidity event. Global portfolios are still heavily short yen via carry, and the combination of a hawkish BOJ + active intervention is the exact catalyst that forces those positions to close. The last time this dynamic played out (mid-2024), it produced a global risk-off event concentrated in tech and high-beta. Positioning for yen strength via long JPY against high-carry EM currencies is a high-conviction trade here.

Playbook ✓: BOJ gradual normalization, hold-then-signal pattern — entirely on script.  |  ⚠️ Divergence: The US Treasury involvement in yen intervention is NOT in the playbook. This converts a unilateral Japanese FX defense into a bilateral capital flow coordination that changes the game for USD/JPY positioning globally.
