🇺🇸 United States  Mixed  → rates on hold / genuinely conflicted

Macro Stance: The Fed under new Chair Kevin Warsh remains on hold, described as "genuinely conflicted" on rates. June CPI slowed to 3.5%, which weakened the case for further hikes but is still well above target. The 10Y yield sits at 4.55 (+8 bps vs 4W ago) and the 2Y at 4.18 (prior close, +9 bps), so the curve is shifting up in parallel in a rising-yield environment — the market is pricing in higher-for-even-longer with a +40 bps slope that is positive but still flat by historical standards. Fiscal stance remains expansionary with no deficit discipline on the horizon.

New / Most Important: The soft June CPI print landed this week and was received positively — headlines called it "good news for Warsh and the stock market." However, Warsh's first rate decision at the July meeting looms as a defining moment. US-Iran escalation is simultaneously firming the dollar on safe-haven demand and threatening to re-accelerate energy-driven inflation, creating a genuine policy bind. 372 large US bankruptcies drew an "unexpected" credit market response — spreads apparently not blowing out despite stress, suggesting excess liquidity is still papering over micro credit deterioration. SpaceX shares fell below IPO price for the first time, a notable sentiment marker for the private-to-public tech liquidity narrative.

Playbook ✓: Breadth rotation from mega-cap tech into small caps is proceeding textbook — Russell 2000 (2970, ↑0.2%) outperforming Nasdaq 100 (29349, ↓3.9%), with the Russell/Nasdaq ratio up 4.2% in four weeks. VIX at 16.26 in complacency regime despite all the noise.  |  ⚠️ Divergence: The CPI-to-yield disconnect is the structural story. CPI decelerated but 10Y yields are still climbing. This is NOT a classic "good inflation = lower yields" setup. The bond market is telling you something different — either term premium is being repriced higher on fiscal sustainability fears (issuance flood), or the Hormuz supply shock is being priced into forward breakevens even as backward-looking CPI cools. The credit market's blasé response to 372 large bankruptcies is equally anomalous — in prior cycles that volume of filings would trigger spread widening. Either CLO/private credit structures are absorbing losses quietly or we're seeing a dangerous complacency that ends abruptly. Watch the gap between bankruptcies and spreads — when it closes, it closes fast.

🇪🇺 European Union  Bearish  ↑ rates — ECB just hiked

Macro Stance: The ECB delivered its first rate hike in nearly three years, a pivotal shift back to tightening driven by the energy supply shock from renewed Hormuz hostilities. The Bundesbank chief publicly argued the ECB should hold despite the oil surge, but was overruled. Fiscal policy across the bloc remains fragmented — defense spending pressures persist alongside austerity-leaning northern members.

New / Most Important: This ECB hike is structurally significant and breaks the easing consensus that prevailed through most of 2025-26. The central bank is now caught in a classic supply-shock trap: hiking into what was already tepid growth to contain energy-pass-through inflation. The "extremely volatile" outlook language from ECB officials signals they have low conviction in their own forward guidance. The Bundesbank dissent being made public is unusual and reveals deep internal division. The digital euro pilot moved forward with 36 payment providers selected, but this is a multi-year plumbing story, not a market mover today. The euro edged higher against GBP on the hike, but the narrowing rate gap with the BOJ is creating cross-currency complications — yen buying pressure could siphon flows from European fixed income. Rising oil prices are pushing Eurozone bond yields higher, tightening financial conditions beyond what the policy rate alone implies.

Playbook ✓: Energy shock → ECB forced to tighten → euro strengthens short-term → growth outlook deteriorates. Classic stagflationary playbook.  |  ⚠️ Divergence: The Bundesbank publicly breaking ranks is unusual institutional signaling. If the hawks dissented against a hike, it means the ECB's reaction function is being driven more by headline oil than by underlying demand-pull inflation. This makes the hike potentially a policy error if Hormuz tensions de-escalate quickly — they'd have tightened into a growth downturn for a transient supply shock.

🇯🇵 Japan  Bearish  ↑ rates — BOJ tightening gaining momentum

Macro Stance: The BOJ is building toward what headlines describe as its first rate hike in 31 years, with tightening "gaining momentum." Japan spent $73.7 billion defending the yen and "it's barely working." PM Takaichi's draft economic blueprint triggered a bond market rout, creating contradictory signals between fiscal expansion desires and monetary tightening.

New / Most Important: The massive pension shift toward domestic assets is the structurally transformative story here. Japan signaled a reallocation of its enormous pension pools (GPIF and related funds) toward domestic equities and bonds, which already sparked a yen and JGB rally. This is a capital flow event of the first order — Japan's pension system is one of the largest pools of capital on the planet, and any meaningful repatriation would drain liquidity from foreign bond markets (particularly US Treasuries and European sovereigns) at exactly the wrong time. The carry trade unwind fears are now colliding with easing oil prices (which should help Japan as an energy importer), creating a confusing macro signal. The Takaichi government wants fiscal expansion while the BOJ wants to tighten — this policy contradiction is rattling both bond and currency markets simultaneously. The $73.7B in yen intervention barely moving the needle tells you the structural flow forces are overwhelming the MOF's firepower. Watch for the pension repatriation to become the dominant flow story of H2 2026.

Playbook ✓: BOJ normalization → yen carry unwind → JGB volatility. Textbook.  |  ⚠️ Divergence: The pension repatriation is NOT in the consensus playbook. Most are focused on rate differentials; the pension flow channel is a structural supply/demand shift in global fixed income that could amplify Treasury yield rises far beyond what US domestic factors alone would justify.
