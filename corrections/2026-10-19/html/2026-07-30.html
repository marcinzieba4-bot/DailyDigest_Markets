🌍 Geographic Macro Overview

🇺🇸 United States  Bearish  → rates on hold, hawkish tilt

Macro Stance: The Warsh-led Fed held rates steady on Jul 29, but the decision was fractured — three members voted for a hike. This is the most hawkish hold in years. GDP growth came in below expectations, yet PCE inflation showed some cooling with underlying growth still firm. The 10Y yield is surging at 4.66% (+19 bps vs 4W), the curve is positively sloped at +45bps (still flat by historical standards), and VIX has risen to 18.10 (↑9.1%). The S&P 500 is down modestly (↓0.8%) but Nasdaq has been hit hard (↓5.9%), signaling a clear rotation away from mega-cap tech.

New / Most Important: The three-way FOMC split is structurally new and deserves attention. Warsh publicly vowed to fight inflation and won't hesitate to act, yet three dissenters pushed for an outright hike — this is the first meaningful internal revolt under Warsh's chairmanship. The bond market's response was telling: the long end sold off (10Y +23 bps vs 4W) while the 2Y rose far less (4.22% at the prior close, +8 bps), steepening the curve by 15 bps. This pattern suggests the market reads the Fed as behind the curve on inflation but unlikely to follow through with a hike, creating a credibility gap. The "hawkish hold" leaves risk assets in limbo — not tight enough to kill inflation expectations, not dovish enough to support multiples. Capital flow implications are significant: the dollar dropped sharply (headlines note the biggest dollar decline since 2022), which is unusual for a hawkish hold and points to eroding confidence in U.S. policy coherence. Oil surging 22.5% in four weeks adds a stagflationary overlay that makes the Fed's job harder.

Playbook ✓: GDP miss + sticky inflation = textbook late-cycle bind. Rotation from growth to value/small-caps fits the rising-rate, steepening-curve template (Russell/Nasdaq ratio ↑3.4%).  |  ⚠️ Divergence: The dollar weakening on a hawkish hold is a major break from the playbook. Normally, hawkish Fed rhetoric supports the greenback. The fact that the dollar dropped the most since 2022 immediately after the decision — compounded by suspected Japanese intervention — suggests global capital is beginning to question whether the U.S. can credibly tighten while running massive fiscal deficits. If this dollar weakness persists alongside rising long-end yields, it implies a sovereign risk premium is creeping into Treasuries. This is the kind of dynamic you see in EM economies, not the reserve currency issuer, and it changes the entire cross-border flow calculus. Watch for foreign central bank reserve diversification accelerating — gold at 4,169 (↑2.5%) is already sniffing this out.

🇪🇺 European Union  Bullish  ↑ rates — ECB hiking cycle resuming

Macro Stance: The ECB is on course to raise rates again in September, supported by strong Eurozone GDP data and firm inflation expectations. ECB's Kazimir explicitly stated one more hike is needed. Euro is strengthening (near 1.1450 vs USD), and Bund yields are holding firm. The wage tracker showed no pickup in wage pressures, which gives the ECB some comfort, but higher energy prices (oil ↑22.5%) are a clear upside risk to headline CPI.

New / Most Important: Strong GDP prints and inflation concerns have solidified the September hike consensus. The euro's strength against the weakening dollar represents a significant tightening of financial conditions for European exporters, yet markets are interpreting this as a positive signal for European asset quality. This is a structurally interesting divergence — Europe is hiking into strength rather than defensively, which is rare and supports equity inflows.

Playbook ✓: ECB hiking on strong data, euro appreciating, Bunds selling off — all textbook.  |  ⚠️ Divergence: The absence of wage pressure acceleration despite strong GDP is genuinely unusual for a late-cycle hiking economy. If this holds, the ECB may be able to engineer a "one and done" September hike without triggering a recession — a soft landing narrative that would be very bullish for European equities relative to U.S. The geopolitical wildcard is the Spain/Morocco crisis trending heavily on X — if the Ceuta situation escalates, it could trigger migration-related fiscal spending and political instability in the southern periphery, which is not priced into sovereign spreads at all.

🇨🇳 China  Mixed  → easing bias, stimulus signaled

Macro Stance: The PBOC is easing through volume while firming the currency. It set the overnight reverse repo rate steady at 1.25% (Reuters, [Jul 28]) and is maintaining liquidity support before month-end (TradingView, [Jul 28]), after injecting a total of 2.1 trillion yuan (Reuters, [Jul 24]). The fixing has turned into a signal: the yuan was steady as the PBOC set the fix at a 3-1/2-year high (Business Recorder, [Jul 29]), then rose to a 1-1/2-month high on the strong fixing and dollar softness as the Fed held (NST Online, [Jul 29]). A stronger yuan is the opposite of the export-led devaluation Europe fears. Prices are split: June CPI rose only 1% (Global Times, [Jul 9]) while PPI climbed 4.1% (finance.biggo.com, [Jul 8]), a near four-year high (Reuters, [Jul 8]).

PMIs & Activity: June's official manufacturing PMI was 50.3 and non-manufacturing 50.2 (Reuters, [Jun 29]); for July, factory activity is expected to have stalled (Reuters, [Jul 29]), so the official print is now the key test. Q2 GDP grew 4.3%, below the official target range (Bloomberg, [Jul 15]); June industrial output rose 5.3% y/y (BusinessLine, [Jul 15]) and exports surged 27% (AP News, [Jul 14]), but first-half retail sales were up just 1.3% (Ecns.cn, [Jul 15]). First-half industrial profits rose 18.7% as a tech boom masks the property slump (Caixin Global, [Jul 27]), at the slowest growth this year (Financial Times, [Jul 26]). Fortune describes goods consumption slumping even as exports and services boom ([Jul 29]); NEV retail sales dipped 2% in the first 26 days of July (CnEVPost, [Jul 29]), and Toyota's sales fell for a fifth month on a China slump (Yahoo Finance Singapore, [Jul 29]).

Credit Impulse: June new bank loans rose less than expected (The Star, [Jul 15]) and credit growth missed as the PBOC flagged a "new normal" (Caixin Global, [Jul 16]); household loans fell by 366.8 billion yuan in the first half (36Kr, [Jul 17]). The cleanest tell is that cash at Chinese banks has swelled to a record as borrowing slows (Nikkei Asia, [Jul 28]): liquidity is not the constraint, demand for credit is. Pricing is loosening quietly, with banks breaking with the lending benchmark (Bloomberg, [Jul 24]) and launching the first repo-linked loans (Yahoo Finance, [Jul 27]). Read: impulse flat at best, household impulse negative.

Property: June new home prices fell at a slower pace (Reuters, [Jul 14]) and first-tier prices extended a four-month rebound (South China Morning Post, [Jul 15]), but resale prices fell faster (Reuters, [Jun 30]) and property investment slumped 18 percent y/y in January–June (The Standard (HK), [Jul 15]). Prime land still clears for state-linked names: Poly Developments and China Resources Land spent 23.2 billion yuan in a day in Shanghai's inner ring (finance.biggo.com, [Jul 28]), and property stocks led gains on hopes for supportive policies (Moomoo, [Jul 28]). Regulators curbed offshore loans for local real estate firms (The Standard (HK), [Jul 23]), and Vanke completed its bond extensions with a Shenzhen Metro loan (China Economic Review, [Jul 23]).

Fiscal, Policy & Trade: Messaging before the meeting leans to consumption: China Daily calls boosting H2 consumption a leading priority ([Jul 29]), while Caixin's analysis is that Beijing will likely forgo major stimulus as the growth target remains in reach ([Jul 28]). Stocks were mixed as the Politburo meeting looms (TradingView, [Jul 29]), so its outcome is still ahead. On trade, China says the US pledged to cap replacement tariffs at 20% (Bloomberg, [Jul 27]) and it held off on retaliation over Section 301 (South China Morning Post, [Jul 27]), but it is gearing up to fight overcapacity claims (Bloomberg, [Jul 28]) and has put EU entities on an export control watchlist targeting rare-earth supply chains (Shanghai Metals Market, [Jul 29]).

Markets & Flows: The chip shock keeps spreading: ASML slid on a report of China starting DUV tool production (Bloomberg, [Jul 27]) and the KOSPI plunged 11% on China chip competition fears (Nikkei Asia, [Jul 28]), while chip stocks dragged mainland shares as Hong Kong rose (Business Recorder, [Jul 29]). The Hang Seng surged nearly 500 points to reclaim 25,000 (BigGo Finance, [Jul 29]), and Chinese firms are readying the most buybacks since the trade war to stem the rout (Bloomberg, [Jul 29]).

New / Most Important: Two signals land on the eve of the data and the Politburo. A Reuters poll expects July factory activity to have stalled, and the PBOC set the yuan fix at a 3-1/2-year high. Beijing is accepting a stronger currency while manufacturing loses momentum, which raises the bar for the Politburo: without a consumption push that offsets slowing factories, the strong-yuan, tight-budget mix tightens conditions.

Playbook ✓: Steady policy rate, ample liquidity, tech- and export-led profits, weak property and a measured tariff response are on script.  |  ⚠️ Divergence: China's tech wins are a shock to others: Korean memory names fell on CXMT and its DUV push. A stalling factory PMI and an 18 percent property-investment slump keep construction-linked bulk demand soft, a drag on iron ore and AUD that hopes for Politburo support have not reversed. European exporters face weak China luxury and auto demand, though a firmer yuan eases their price pressure at the margin. EM inflows follow AI and buybacks, not a PMI or credit upturn, and oil near $84 remains a terms-of-trade hit for China.

🇯🇵 Japan  Bullish  ↑ rates — BOJ signaling further hikes

Macro Stance: The BOJ is signaling more rate hikes as price pressures build, with the market looking for hints on timing. The yen surged on what analysts suspect was official intervention, and the dollar dropped the most against the yen since 2022. BOJ rate rise bets have increased as the yen had reached multidecade lows prior to intervention.

New / Most Important: The suspected intervention on Jul 30 is the headline event. This is structurally important: Japan intervening into a weak-dollar environment (post-Fed) amplifies the effect dramatically. The BOJ is now on a collision course with global liquidity — every hike and every intervention drains yen carry trade funding. A former S&P Global executive publicly stated the policy rate needs to reach 1.5%, which would represent a massive regime shift. The carry trade unwind risk is the single largest tail risk in global macro right now. If the BOJ delivers a hawkish signal at its upcoming meeting, the repatriation flows could be violent — we saw a preview of this in mid-2024. Japanese life insurers and pension funds hold trillions in foreign bonds; even a modest shift in hedging behavior sends shockwaves through UST and European sovereign markets.

Playbook ✓: Intervention after yen weakness, hawkish BOJ signaling — follows the 2024 pattern.  |  ⚠️ Divergence: The timing of intervention coinciding with a Fed hawkish hold and dollar weakness is unusual — Japan typically intervenes against dollar strength. Intervening here suggests they want to accelerate yen appreciation, not just arrest depreciation. That's a more aggressive posture than markets are pricing.

Softening demand signals — China's July factory activity is expected to have stalled — confirm a supply-shock narrative rather than broad demand strength. Retail is piling into energy names as both an inflation hedge and a geopolitical fear trade. The narrative breaks if the Ceuta situation de-escalates rapidly, if OPEC signals increased output, or if the GDP weakness visible in today's data translates into genuine demand destruction — the same weak growth that fuels the gold trade could eventually cap oil if recession fears overtake supply fears.

Microsoft ($MSFT) & "Non-Nvidia AI"  The AI rotation trade

$MSFT is trending on Twitter and leading a Nasdaq rebound today after earnings, with headlines highlighting Microsoft leading tech gains while Nvidia stumbles. Retail is rotating within AI — the narrative has shifted from "buy the GPU shovel-seller" to "buy the companies deploying AI at scale." The chip ETF pulling off a "historic 2026 while Nvidia stumbled" headline captures a broadening-out thesis: AI is real but the winners are rotating. AMD falling 8% on China chip news and Nvidia's potential $250B OpenAI backstop are creating a complex reshuffling. The risk is that the Nasdaq is still down 5.9% over four weeks and the hawkish Fed is compressing multiples on exactly the high-duration growth stocks retail is rotating into — a classic value trap if rates stay elevated.

Small Caps (Russell 2000)  Breadth expansion = "the real economy" trade

The Russell/Nasdaq ratio is up 3.4% over four weeks, signaling small-cap outperformance even as both indices are down in absolute terms. Retail is latching onto the "breadth is expanding" narrative — the idea that money is rotating out of mega-cap tech into domestically-oriented companies that benefit from reshoring and a weaker Nasdaq. Headlines about semiconductors correcting and restaurants booming reinforce the "real economy over AI hype" angle. The risk is stark: the Russell 2000 is still down 2.7%, the VIX is elevated and rising, and the hawkish Fed with a surging 10Y yield is the worst possible macro backdrop for rate-sensitive small caps that need cheap financing to grow.
