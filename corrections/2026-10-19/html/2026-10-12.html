🇺🇸 United States  Mixed  ↑ rates rising / hike debate live

Macro Stance: The Fed is in a deeply contested holding pattern. Economists are publicly divided on whether the Fed will raise rates again this year, per ABC News [Oct 10]. The 10Y yield at 5.24% (+28 bps vs 4W) and 2Y at 4.80% (prior close, +17 bps vs 4W) confirm the market is repricing higher-for-even-longer. The 10Y-2Y spread is +44 bps (prior close, 11 bps steeper than four weeks ago) — positively sloped but still flat by historical standards, with the steepening reflecting term premium re-pricing rather than easing expectations. Fiscal concerns and persistent inflation are the twin drivers. UBS and Invesco are both publishing frameworks around "a higher-rate reality" as the new structural regime.

New / Most Important: The most consequential signal this week is the convergence of multiple outlets flagging "bad news" for the economy (Yahoo Finance, Motley Fool [Oct 10]) while the S&P 500 sits at 7804.82 (↑2.4% vs 4W), having hit all-time highs earlier this month [Oct 7]. JPMorgan Chase reports earnings tomorrow [Oct 13] — the feed explicitly warns of downside risk from surging yields hitting bank earnings. The "stock market is the economy" vs "isn't the economy" debate is running hot across Seeking Alpha, FT, and Marketplace — this reflexivity matters because the wealth effect from equities is colliding with a price shock (Reuters [Oct 12]). Big bank Q3 earnings season is now the immediate catalyst; the market needs financials to demonstrate that 5%+ long rates aren't breaking credit quality.

Playbook ✓: Mega-cap AI leadership continues — Nasdaq 100 at 30,666 (↑5.3% vs 4W) while Russell 2000 at 2,799 (↓3.2% vs 4W). The Russell/Nasdaq ratio is down 8.1% in 4 weeks, confirming extremely narrow market breadth. This is textbook late-cycle quality flight. VIX at 15.27 (↓10.7% vs 4W) in "COMPLACENCY" regime — low hedging demand despite yields surging and small caps rolling over.  |  ⚠️ Divergence: The structural divergence is the 10Y at 5.24% with the S&P at all-time highs — this combination has almost no historical precedent in the post-GFC era. The traditional playbook says 5%+ long yields should compress equity multiples, particularly for duration-sensitive growth. Instead, mega-cap tech is ripping higher as if rate-insensitive, while rate-sensitive small caps and real estate are getting crushed. This is a two-economy market: companies with pricing power and cash-flow self-sufficiency are detaching from the cost-of-capital cycle, while leveraged, bank-dependent businesses are living in a restrictive monetary regime. The wealth boom from the top-heavy equity market is sustaining consumption even as borrowing costs punish new entrants and smaller firms. November elections (Morningstar [Oct 12]) add a fiscal wildcard — both parties may promise more spending, which would further pressure the long end. If bank earnings this week confirm credit deterioration in commercial real estate or consumer lending, the narrow breadth could snap violently into a broader repricing.

PMIs & Activity: September's official manufacturing PMI returned to growth at 50.1, exactly at consensus and up from 49.8, with non-manufacturing at 50.2 (investingLive, [Sep 29]); the private RatingDog gauges printed 52.1 and 51.6 (investingLive, [Sep 30]), with Reuters tying the factory expansion to the AI boom (Reuters, [Sep 29]). Property: The mortgage subsidy reached demand over the holiday. New-home subscriptions in 50 key cities rose over 15% year on year (Moomoo, [Oct 8]), Shanghai new-home sales doubled and second-hand transactions in 11 cities jumped over 40% (BigGo Finance, [Oct 9]), and Beijing new-home sales have picked up since October began (巴士的報, [Oct 11]). The base is August's, with prices down across 70 cities and investment down 19.9% in eight months (BigGo Finance, [Sep 14]).

Fiscal, Policy & Trade: The fiscal push is real but rationed. Local governments may tap 550 billion yuan of unused debt quotas (Caixin Global, [Oct 9]) and the NDRC pledges more policy tools (Bloomberg, [Oct 10]), yet debt interest costs are growing faster than any other budget category (Fortune, [Oct 11]), Michael Pettis notes the debt pile constrains new stimulus (Traders Union, [Oct 11]) and the SCMP argues Beijing is right to reserve its fiscal firepower (South China Morning Post, [Oct 11]). On trade, tensions with Europe came to a head (The New York Times, [Oct 7]) and ended in a deal capping Chinese hybrid imports at 350,000 a year (Bloomberg, [Oct 11]), with the EU now agreeing to discuss curbs on Chinese inverters (Bloomberg, [Oct 11]). Britain is set to levy tariffs on Chinese EVs (CNA, [Oct 5]). September export data is due this week (bloomingbit, [Oct 11]) after August's 25% jump (AP News, [Sep 7]).

Markets & Flows: China's securities watchdog is seeking investor ideas as the selloff deepens (Business Standard, [Oct 11]); blue chips hit an over one-year low on an AI supply-chain selloff (Zawya, [Oct 8]) and the Hang Seng a three-month low (The Standard (HK), [Oct 8]), with Alibaba rising as China limits sell orders (Benzinga, [Oct 9]). Breakingviews argues the slow bull is poised to take off (Reuters, [Oct 8]); the consumer side disagrees, with luxury's China hopes facing a reality check (The Business of Fashion, [Oct 11]).

New / Most Important: The weekend made the firepower limit explicit: debt interest at 19.2% of central outlays and rising fastest of any budget line, with Pettis and the SCMP both arguing for restraint. That is why the response to a weak consumer is quota releases and sell-order limits rather than a broad package, and why the bearish read holds despite the housing bounce.

Playbook ✓: Property developer stress, weak household confidence and local fiscal strain follow the balance-sheet-recession script; exports compensate for domestic demand, and announcements outpace delivered stimulus.  |  ⚠️ Divergence: Both export corridors are narrowing at once: US tariffs and now an EU cap on hybrids with inverters next and UK EV tariffs coming. A China that cannot export its way to growth and will not stimulate at scale transmits weakness through PMIs and property rather than through prices. The AUD and iron ore depend on a property recovery that has reached sales but not the 19.9% investment slump; European luxury and industrial exporters lose a consumer whose holiday spending disappointed; EM Asia competes with Chinese goods diverted from Europe. Beijing's toolkit is pinned not by the yuan but by fiscal arithmetic, so the policy floor is real while the ceiling on reflation is low.

Playbook ✓: Yen weakness continues as the BOJ telegraphs glacial normalization against a US regime of 5%+ yields. This creates a fragile equilibrium where any exogenous shock (a further US yield spike, an energy price reversal upward) could force a disorderly policy shift.
