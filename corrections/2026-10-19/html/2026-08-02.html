🇺🇸 United States  Mixed  → rates on hold (hawkish hold)

Macro Stance: The Warsh-led Fed held rates steady at its July 29 meeting but delivered a hawkish hold — three officials dissented in favor of a hike. Fiscal policy remains expansionary under Trump. The 10Y yield is surging at 4.74% (+25 bps vs 4W ago) while the 2Y has risen less, to 4.28% (prior close, +14 bps vs 4W), steepening the curve to +47bps — positively sloped but still flat by historical standards, and one that now reflects term-premium repricing rather than easing expectations. Oil ripping +23.3% in four weeks is an unambiguous stagflationary input.

New / Most Important: The three-dissent hawkish hold is structurally significant. This is the first time under Warsh's chairmanship that the committee has been this visibly split. Warsh stated the Fed "won't hesitate to stop inflation," but the bond market has doubts — hence yields climbing on term premium rather than short-rate expectations. The headline that "GDP growth is stronger than it looks" combined with the Washington Post flagging that "the biggest gamble in the U.S. economy is starting to look riskier" paints a picture of above-trend nominal growth masking deteriorating real fundamentals. Meanwhile, Amazon nearly doubling its debt to $129B in six months to fund $220B in data center capex is the single most important corporate capital-flow signal of the week — this is fiscal-scale private-sector spending being financed in a rising-rate environment. Friday's jobs report looms as the week's key catalyst.

Playbook ✓: S&P 500 at 7489 (↑0.1%) is flatlined at highs — mega-cap resilience masking breadth stress is textbook late-cycle. VIX at 16.0 screams complacency.  |  ⚠️ Divergence: The Nasdaq 100 is DOWN 3.6% over four weeks while the Russell 2000 is only down 2.2% — small-caps are actually outperforming large-cap tech in a rising-rate, rising-oil environment. This is not the playbook. Rising long-end yields should punish duration-sensitive small-caps more, yet the Russell/Nasdaq ratio is ↑1.5%. This suggests the Nasdaq selloff is AI-specific (valuation compression on capex concerns) rather than macro-driven. The rotation is telling us that the market is starting to price "real economy over AI narrative" — a theme that has legs if oil stays elevated and the Fed stays hawkish. Watch this ratio closely as it may signal a regime shift in equity leadership.

🇪🇺 European Union  Bearish  ↑ rates — hike likely September

Macro Stance: The ECB is now on course to raise rates again in September after eurozone inflation surprised to the upside at 2.9% in July. Higher energy prices are the proximate cause. Despite the ECB wage tracker detecting no pickup in wage pressures, headline inflation re-accelerating is forcing the Governing Council's hand.

New / Most Important: This is a structurally important moment for the eurozone. The ECB is being forced into a hiking posture at a time when the underlying economy is fragile and the inflation impulse is largely exogenous (energy, not wages). This is a classic policy trap: tightening into a cost-push shock that monetary policy cannot resolve. The euro is rising on rate-hike bets, which will tighten financial conditions further via the exchange rate channel. For capital flows, a hawkish ECB plus a hawkish-hold Fed compresses the transatlantic rate differential — this could pull capital toward European fixed income at the margin, but the equity implication is unambiguously negative for cyclicals. The absence of wage pressures is the ECB's own data telling it not to hike, yet headline CPI politics will override. This sets up a potential policy error that could show up in peripheral spreads by Q4.

Playbook ✓: Euro strengthening on rate expectations is textbook.  |  ⚠️ Divergence: Hiking into an energy cost shock without wage confirmation is fighting the wrong war. If oil stays elevated (↑23.3% in 4 weeks per quant snapshot), the ECB will be tightening into the precise headwind it cannot control.

🇯🇵 Japan  Bearish (bonds)  ↑ rates — hike signaled for September

Macro Stance: The BOJ held rates at 1% on July 31 but explicitly warned that core inflation is exceeding its 2% target and signaled commitment to further hikes. Suspected yen intervention occurred ahead of the meeting, confirming the currency remains a policy priority. The BOJ is now the most hawkishly-signaling it has been in decades.

New / Most Important: The BOJ's simultaneous rate hold + hawkish forward guidance + yen intervention is a three-pronged signal that September is live for a hike. This is structurally important for global capital flows: a BOJ hike would narrow the USD-JPY carry trade spread further, potentially triggering another wave of carry unwind. Last year's carry unwind in August 2024 caused significant global market dislocations. The fact that the BOJ is telegraphing this so clearly suggests they want to avoid a disorderly market reaction, but the carry trade community is larger and more leveraged than official communication can fully manage. Any September hike combined with a weakening dollar would create a JPY rally that forces cross-asset deleveraging. The MUFG note flagging "suspected JPY intervention" confirms authorities are already actively managing the transition.

Playbook ✓: Gradual normalization path is consensus.  |  ⚠️ Divergence: The speed of signaling — moving from "chance of early hike" to "committed to more hikes" in a single meeting — is faster than markets expected. Watch JGB yields and JPY crosses for stress signals.
