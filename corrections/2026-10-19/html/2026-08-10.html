🇺🇸 United States  Mixed  ↑ rate direction (hike expected)

Macro Stance: The Fed held rates at its July meeting and market pricing has shifted toward a 25bp hike in September — a dramatic pivot from the easing expectations that dominated earlier this year. The economy is shedding jobs, but MarketWatch framed this as positive for equities, suggesting markets interpret softening labor as reducing the urgency for further tightening. The 10Y yield has climbed to 4.70% (+9 bps vs 4W ago), while the 2Y sits at 4.19% (prior close, −2 bps vs 4W), producing a +46 bps term spread — positively sloped but still flat by historical standards — and the curve is steepening in a way that points to fiscal premium, not recession.

New / Most Important: The Wall Street–Nvidia $500B AI financing deal reported by the FT is structurally significant. This is the financialization of AI infrastructure — major banks are now underwriting capex cycles directly, bypassing traditional corporate balance sheet constraints. This converts what was a tech P&L story into a credit creation story, meaning AI capex can persist even if tech earnings decelerate, so long as credit conditions remain accommodative. The DXY holding near 99.50 despite a potential hike path signals the dollar is caught between safe-haven bids (Middle East tensions) and structural weakness from twin deficits — notably, the US sold euros to prop up the yen, which blindsided the ECB per the FT, suggesting active coordinated FX intervention is back on the table. Meanwhile, headline dot-com bubble comparisons are circulating as a market indicator flashed a warning, but S&P at 7753 (↑3.2%) and VIX at 15.32 (↓10.7%) show no stress — classic late-cycle complacency.

Playbook ✓: Tech inflows remain strong despite Nvidia's Monday sell-off; breadth is expanding with Russell 2000 (↑2.2%) outperforming Nasdaq 100 (↑1.4%). Inflation data this week (July CPI) will be the key catalyst.  |  ⚠️ Divergence: The potential September hike is a total script-breaker. For most of 2026, consensus expected cuts. The fact that the labor market is weakening while the Fed debates hiking creates a stagflationary micro-environment that the equity market is completely ignoring at VIX 15.3. The gold/BTC divergence of +14.4pp over 4 weeks confirms this is a sovereign-risk bid, not a speculative risk-on move. If CPI prints hot, the hike path hardens and this equity complacency unwinds violently.

Macro Stance: The ECB remains in a data-dependent posture. European equity markets are generating buzz among global investors per Bloomberg, with capital flows rotating toward EU assets amid relative valuation appeal and dollar weakness.

New / Most Important: This is the most signal-dense geography this week and represents a structural regime shift. The BOJ summary published this week flagged upside inflation risks and the possibility of faster hikes — this is new hawkish language that goes beyond the incremental normalization narrative. Bessent's nudge supporting yen strength, combined with the coordinated US euro-sale for yen intervention (confirmed by OMFIF and FT), means this is no longer just a BOJ story — it's a US-Japan coordinated policy axis to manage yen weakness. If the BOJ delivers in September and the Fed also hikes, the rate differential narrows from both sides simultaneously — this is the scenario that could trigger a violent unwind of the $3T+ yen carry trade. JGB yields surging on hike expectations will stress Japanese life insurers' duration portfolios and could force repatriation flows that amplify the move.

Playbook ✓: BOJ normalization proceeding; yen intervention as expected.  |  ⚠️ Divergence: The speed of the pivot is the divergence. Markets priced gradual normalization — "faster hike pace" language from the BOJ is not in the playbook. Cross-border capital flow implications are enormous: a rapid BOJ tightening cycle is the single biggest liquidity risk to global risk assets because it reverses the funding currency dynamic that has suppressed global volatility for years.
