🌍 Geographic Macro Overview

🇺🇸 United States  Mixed  → rates on hold

Macro Stance: The Fed held rates steady at its latest meeting under Chair Warsh, who explicitly stated "prices are too high," signaling no imminent pivot. The 10Y yield has surged to 4.60% (+15 bps vs 4W ago) while the 2Y sits at 4.18% (prior close, −2 bps vs 4W), producing a +37 bps curve — positive but still flat by historical standards, 8 bps steeper than four weeks ago — this is a term premium story, not a Fed pricing story. Fiscal concerns around $39.5T in outstanding federal debt are driving the long end. Labor market data reportedly hit a new negative record, suggesting deterioration beneath the surface even as the Fed stays hawkish on prices.

New / Most Important: The US-Iran exchange of fresh strikes pushed Brent above $90 and WTI to $81.95 (↑7.0% vs 4W ago), injecting a genuine supply-shock into an economy already running hot on prices. This is the central tension: Warsh faces a stagflationary impulse — weakening labor, sticky inflation, now an energy shock — with no good rate move available. The "Mag 7" rotation narrative accelerated as WSJ reported everyday investors souring on mega-cap tech and rotating into newer AI plays; Nasdaq 100 is down 5.4% over 4 weeks, the worst-performing major equity index. Big Tech earnings this week (Alphabet, Tesla, Intel) will test whether this rotation is a tremor or a regime change.

Playbook ✓: VIX at 17.84 (↑8.8%) edging up into earnings is textbook seasonal. The Russell/Nasdaq ratio rising 4.9% confirms the broadening-out rotation that consensus called for.  |  ⚠️ Divergence: Gold is DOWN 5.0% vs 4W ago despite an active shooting war between the US and Iran — this is deeply anomalous. In prior geopolitical escalations (2020, 2022), gold spiked reflexively. The Gold/BTC ratio at 0.0614 with a -16.7pp divergence vs BTC suggests that the marginal "fear bid" is flowing into Bitcoin (↑11.7%) rather than gold. This may mark a structural shift in the macro hedging hierarchy. If sovereign creditors (China, GCC) are diversifying into BTC as a reserve-adjacent asset alongside gold, the traditional gold-as-geopolitical-hedge playbook is broken. Watch whether gold's weakness persists even as oil/geopolitical risk escalates — if it does, this is a secular reallocation signal, not a temporary divergence. Softening growth data — US pending home sales fell 5.4% in June and China's second-quarter GDP growth slowed to 4.3% — tell us risk aversion is real but choosing different outlets.

🇪🇺 European Union  Bearish  → rates on hold, hike risk rising

Macro Stance: The ECB is expected to hold rates at its July 23 decision, but the consensus is shifting toward a September hike as Hormuz-related energy shocks feed through to eurozone inflation, which printed at 2.8%. The energy price resurgence has forced a rates rethink amid what the ECB itself called an "extremely volatile" outlook. The ECB survey signals easing wage growth, but that's cold comfort when imported energy costs are re-accelerating.

New / Most Important: The Hormuz hostilities (US-Iran strikes) have directly reshaped ECB rate expectations — this is the first time since 2022 that a geopolitical supply shock has forced the ECB to pivot from a dovish glide path toward considering tightening. The ECB's digital euro pilot advanced with 36 payment providers selected, a quiet structural development. Andy Burnham reportedly could surprise UK markets (spillover relevance for EUR/GBP).

Playbook ✓: ECB holding this week is fully priced and consensus. Euro holding early recovery vs USD as rate differentials narrow modestly.  |  ⚠️ Divergence: The September hike signal from Reuters polling is a genuine break from the six-month narrative of "ECB cuts toward neutral." If Brent sustains above $90 and Hormuz remains contested, the ECB faces a policy trap: hiking into a manufacturing recession (German PMIs still weak) to fight imported energy inflation. This is the nightmare scenario that capital flows analysis flags — European capital will flee to the US or EM if the ECB tightens into weakness, amplifying EUR weakness despite higher rates. The traditional "higher rates = stronger currency" link breaks when the hiking is perceived as a policy error.

🇨🇳 China  Mixed  → easing bias

Macro Stance: Loan prime rates were left unchanged (chinadailyhk, [Jul 19]), the 14th month on hold that Reuters had flagged ([Jul 17]), so the PBOC keeps easing through liquidity rather than price. It boosted liquidity to smooth tax payments and debt issuance (Bloomberg, [Jul 14]) and told some banks not to re-discount bills at rates below 0.5% (Reuters, [Jul 14]), a floor under a bill market that banks use to pad loan books when genuine demand is missing. The PBOC signalled comfort with yuan levels and expects two-way moves (Bloomberg, [Jul 15]); the yuan touched a 4-week high (TradingView, [Jul 15]) before weakening slightly on the fixing (BusinessToday Malaysia, [Jul 17]). Prices are the wrong mix: June CPI rose only 1% (chinadailyhk, [Jul 8]) and slowed more than forecast (Bloomberg, [Jul 8]), while PPI climbed 4.1% (BigGo Finance, [Jul 8]) to a near four-year high that squeezes manufacturers (Reuters, [Jul 8]).

PMIs & Activity: The surveys and the hard data point in different directions. The June official manufacturing PMI rose to 50.3 and the non-manufacturing PMI to 50.2 (Reuters, [Jun 29]); the private gauge capped the strongest factory quarter since late 2020 (Reuters, [Jun 30]). Yet second-quarter GDP growth weakened to 4.3%, below the official target range (Bloomberg, [Jul 15]), the slowest since 2022 as investment slumped (CNBC, [Jul 14]). June industrial output rose 5.3% y/y and retail sales returned to growth (BusinessLine, [Jul 15]), but retail sales of consumer goods were up just 1.3% in the first half (Ecns.cn, [Jul 15]) and first-half infrastructure investment turned negative (BigGo Finance, [Jul 19]). The engine is external: June exports surged 27% y/y with imports up 36% (Reuters, [Jul 13]).

Credit Impulse: Credit is the weak link. June new loans missed forecasts (Bloomberg, [Jul 15]) and overall credit growth undershot as the PBOC flagged a "new normal" (Caixin, [Jul 16]). Household loans decreased by 366.8 billion yuan in the first half (36Kr, [Jul 17]), and record consumer defaults undermine the push to boost spending (Reuters, [Jul 15]). Read: with households repaying and private firms not borrowing, the impulse rests on state-directed financing, and the bill-rate floor shows banks scrambling for assets.

Property: Prices are stabilising at the top while capital keeps leaving. June new home prices fell at a slower pace, though Reuters doubts a recovery ([Jul 14]), and first-tier prices extended a 4-month rebound (SCMP, [Jul 15]). But property investment slumped 18% y/y in January–June (The Standard, [Jul 15]), developers face a fresh liquidity crunch (Nikkei Asia, [Jul 13]), and KKR and AEW reportedly seek to sell Beijing and Shanghai assets at 50-60% discounts (BigGo Finance, [Jul 19]). Beijing is also curbing new bond sales by indebted LGFVs (Bloomberg, [Jul 15]), so the land-finance drag on local governments persists.

Fiscal, Policy & Trade: Beijing's response so far is plans, not money. The State Council approved the first five-year plan to boost consumption (China Daily, [Jul 13]) with a 60 trillion yuan retail-sales target for 2030 (Caixin, [Jul 14]), but Reuters reports a bet on faster state-backed projects to avoid broad stimulus ([Jul 16]); the FT says leaders are zeroing in on the need for stimulus ([Jul 18]) as the July Politburo meeting is prepared (Sinocism, [Jul 13]). A phased battery consumption tax (BigGo Finance, [Jul 19]) lifted solar stocks (Bloomberg, [Jul 19]). Trade friction is building: the surplus with the EU hit a record (Bloomberg, [Jul 14]) and Brussels signalled emergency import curbs (SCMP, [Jul 14]), the US Senate backed a 100% tariff bill targeting buyers of Russian oil, China included (The New Indian Express, [Jul 17]), and China is exporting 20% fewer magnets to the US despite the truce (Bloomberg, [Jul 19]).

Markets & Flows: The state has stepped in. After an AI-driven rout in which CXMT's $8.6 billion IPO stirred liquidity concerns (TradingView, [Jul 17]), ChiNext plunged over 7% (BigGo Finance, [Jul 16]) and the Hang Seng slumped 446 points (BusinessToday Malaysia, [Jul 18]), Beijing moved to steady markets with a regulator stability meeting and state buyers (TradingView, [Jul 19]): China Reform Holdings has deployed over 50 billion yuan and China Chengtong nearly 10 billion yuan (BigGo Finance, [Jul 19]). The Hang Seng rose 2.07% by midday on semiconductors (Moomoo, [Jul 19]), and JPMorgan calls the A-share slide a healthy "AI deleveraging", not a bubble bursting (36Kr, [Jul 19]).

New / Most Important: China's state-owned funds bought nearly $9 billion in shares to support the market, and the CSRC's Wu vowed a "stable market" as A-shares rebounded on concerted buying. Beijing has effectively put a floor under equities and turned the market into a policy tool. That truncates the downside but does not fix the constraint one of China's top investors named: finance, not AI, is the biggest bottleneck (CNBC, [Jul 16]). With June loans missing, household debt shrinking and property investment still down 18%, this is a liquidity-driven stabilisation, not a fundamentals-driven one, and state-bought rallies fade unless the Politburo converts them into household and private credit.

Playbook ✓: State intervention to stabilise equities, LPR on hold, liquidity-led easing and a managed yuan are fully on script.  |  ⚠️ Divergence: The size of the rescue signals more stress underneath than headline A-share levels suggest, and it targets the wrong market. The growth pulse is split between a 50.3 manufacturing PMI with 27% export growth and negative household credit with falling property investment. That mix transmits abroad as supply rather than demand: European exporters face record Chinese surpluses as Brussels prepares curbs; crude imports at the lowest in nearly a decade (Bloomberg, [Jul 13]) and the construction slump weigh on oil, iron ore and AUD; and Vietnam's VN-Index posting its strongest daily fall in nearly four months hints at outflows from Asia ex-Japan that China's backstop cannot contain. Only a credit turn would change those channels.

🇯🇵 Japan  Bearish  ↑ rates rising

Macro Stance: The BOJ has been hiking into a rising-rate regime that is now causing real pain — nearly half of Japanese firms report being hurt by rate hikes, per Reuters. The yen hit a 40-year low even as interest rates hit a 31-year high, creating what Fortune calls Japan's "monetary conundrum." BOJ expectations remain skewed to the upside for further tightening.

New / Most Important: The coexistence of 31-year-high rates and a 40-year-low yen is a genuinely unprecedented configuration for Japan. This is structurally NEW: the traditional carry trade unwind thesis assumed that BOJ hikes would strengthen the yen, but instead the rate differential with the US (where 10Y is at 4.60%) remains so wide that capital continues to flee JPY. The Takaichi government sending contradictory signals vs the BOJ is rattling both bond and currency markets simultaneously. A BOJ bond-buying pause has left the yen exposed to intervention risk — the question is whether intervention can work when the fundamental flow dynamics (Japanese institutions still chasing offshore yield) remain intact. This is arguably the most dangerous macro setup globally: if the BOJ hikes aggressively enough to actually attract capital home, it risks crashing the JGB market and the domestic firms already struggling; if it doesn't, the yen keeps weakening, importing more inflation. No good exit.

Playbook ✓: AUD/JPY rising on RBA-BOJ rate differential is textbook.  |  ⚠️ Divergence: The BOJ is the only major central bank where rate hikes are failing to strengthen the currency — a complete inversion of standard monetary transmission.
