Something has to give: either the PMIs re-accelerate, or equities are late to price a material slowdown. 🇨🇳 China  Bearish  ↓ easing cycle

Macro Stance: The PBOC is easing the price of money while rationing duration. Overnight reverse repos debuted at 1.25% (Reuters, [Jun 28]) and the injection was doubled at an unchanged rate (Reuters, [Jun 29]); TradingView reports liquidity added [Jun 30] even as BigGo Finance tallies a drain of over 1.16 trillion yuan [Jun 30], daily top-ups against larger maturities. The new signal is at the long end: the PBOC cut bond purchases in a sign of wariness over yield declines (Bloomberg, [Jul 2]) and the 10-year yield rebounded on the cutback (TradingView, [Jul 2]). A central bank leaning against its own bond rally is telling you the market is pricing deflation. The yuan steadied after the PMI (Yahoo Finance Australia, [Jun 29]), but options traders have turned bullish on the dollar (Bloomberg, [Jun 30]). May CPI rose only 1.2% (China Daily, [Jun 10]) while factory-gate inflation hit a near four-year high (Reuters, [Jun 9]), and Caixin warns deflation risks are squeezing profits [Jun 30].

PMIs & Activity: The June upturn is already fading at the margin. The official manufacturing PMI rose to 50.3 and non-manufacturing to 50.2 (Reuters, [Jun 29]), and the private factory gauge grew for a seventh month (China Daily, [Jul 2]), but it grew less than expected (Bloomberg, [Jun 30]) and private services activity slowed slightly (Reuters, [Jul 2]). Nikkei Asia reports the subsidy pullback is hitting auto and appliance sales, a policy test [Jul 2]. Citigroup calls it a K-shaped economy cut by an AI supercycle [Jul 2]. May's hard data define the split: output growth quickened to 4.5% (China Daily, [Jun 16]) and exports jumped 19.4% (AP News, [Jun 9]), but retail sales fell 0.6% (Il Sole 24 Ore, [Jun 15]), fixed-asset investment fell 4.1% in five months (Macau Business, [Jun 16]), and profit growth slowed for the first time in six months (Times of India, [Jun 27]).

Credit Impulse: Total social financing grew 7.7% in May (CGTN, [Jun 12]) and topped forecasts (Bloomberg, [Jun 12]), but new loans missed as the property slump curbed demand (Reuters, [Jun 12]): the beat is government bonds, the miss is private demand. The PBOC is urging banks to lend because demand remains weak (Reuters, [Jun 26]). Beijing is now also capping LGFV bond yields (IFR, [Jul 2]), administering credit prices rather than reviving demand; with debt above 300% of GDP (UPI, [Jun 29]), the private impulse is negative.

Property: Volumes and prices diverge. The top 100 developers sold nearly RMB 1.6 trillion in the first half, with June up 11.8% month on month (Moomoo, [Jul 1]), but resale home prices fell faster, a private survey shows (Reuters, [Jun 30]): a half-year-end sales push, not pricing power. May new home prices fell at a faster pace (Reuters, [Jun 15]) and real-estate investment fell 16.2% y/y in January–May (GMK Center, [Jun 18]). Resolution is state-led: Vanke ended a 17-month CEO vacuum by naming a former Shenzhen financial official to lead risk resolution (BigGo Finance, [Jul 2]), and Country Garden cleared its first offshore coupon after its overhaul (Caixin, [Jul 2]).

Fiscal, Policy & Trade: Fiscal support is receding: the 62.5 billion yuan trade-in batch (Global Times, [Jun 26]) is small, SCMP argues China must balance as it tightens the fiscal net [Jul 1], and a tax crackdown is hitting A-share and Hong Kong-listed firms (SCMP, [Jul 2]). Control is rising: curbs on foreign investments (Times of India, [Jul 1]) and a landmark law over offshore tech transfers (SCMP, [Jul 1]). On trade, China and the US target tariff cuts on farm goods (Bloomberg, [Jul 2]), with Beijing pledging $17 billion in annual purchases (BigGo Finance, [Jul 2]). The EU flinched in its reset, fearing retaliation (Bloomberg, [Jul 2]), as Goldman says Europe's growth is hit more by Chinese exports than by the trade gap (Reuters, [Jul 2]).

Markets & Flows: Mainland stocks hit a three-week low as chipmakers tumbled while Hong Kong rebounded (The Star, [Jul 2]), after a brief rise on the factory data and Xi's speech (The Business Times, [Jul 1]); financial stocks slumped despite a massive dividend payout (Caixin, [Jul 1]). Adam Tooze's Chartbook calls this China's "walled-in wealth" [Jul 1].

New / Most Important: The PBOC cutting bond purchases because yields are falling is the clearest bearish signal of the week. It means the domestic bond market is pricing deflation faster than Beijing is comfortable with, on the same day private services slowed and the trade-in subsidy fade showed up in auto and appliance sales. The 50.3 PMI is an export print; at home, overnight money is cheap and private borrowers still do not want it.

Playbook ✓: Stimulus-lite, a slow grind lower in domestic activity and a managed yuan are on script.  |  ⚠️ Divergence: The consensus reads June's PMI as a turn; the bond market and the PBOC's own operations say otherwise. Europe: Goldman's point is that Chinese export volumes, not the deficit, are the growth drag on European industry. Oil: TotalEnergies offering Iraqi crude to Asian buyers and Bloomberg's report of fuel export curbs being lifted [Jun 30] both point to supply chasing soft Asian demand, with WTI down 26.1% in four weeks. Until private loans turn, every Hong Kong bounce is a flow.

🇯🇵 Japan  Bearish (for JGBs)  ↑ hiking cycle

Macro Stance: The BOJ is under intensifying pressure to hike rates as the yen sank to a 40-year low. Japan spent $74 billion on FX intervention to prop up the currency, but the market views this as fighting the fundamental rate differential with the Fed. A robust domestic economy and persistent yen weakness are building a strong case for an earlier rate hike.

New / Most Important: This week's signal density on Japan is the highest of any geography — a government panel member explicitly called for "moderate BOJ rate hikes," while multiple outlets reported that the sinking yen and robust economy give the BOJ "more grounds for early hike." This is a notable shift from the BOJ's traditionally glacial communication. However, Reuters reported that the BOJ's "slow, dovish revamp casts doubt over long-term rate-hike plans," highlighting a structural tension between market urgency and institutional inertia. Japan is now at the center of a global capital flow story: the yen carry trade is the single largest source of global liquidity, and its unwinding — even partial — would trigger cross-asset volatility far beyond Japanese borders. The $74 billion intervention war chest is being depleted against a structural problem that only rate hikes can solve. If the BOJ moves before the Fed cuts, the yen carry trade unwind could be the catalyst that breaks the VIX complacency regime we're sitting in at 15.81.

Playbook ✓: Yen weakness forcing intervention is on-script for a country with a deeply negative real rate differential.  |  ⚠️ Divergence: The gap between explicit government calls for rate hikes and Reuters' reporting on the BOJ's dovish institutional revamp is a structural conflict. The BOJ may be losing its hawkish members faster than the macro warrants — meaning the institution could fall further behind the curve even as the economy and FX scream for tightening.
