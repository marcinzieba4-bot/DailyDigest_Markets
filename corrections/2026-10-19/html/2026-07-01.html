Macro Stance: The PBOC's overnight framework is in its first week: reverse repos debuted at 1.25% (Reuters, [Jun 28]), below the 1.35% a Bloomberg survey expected ([Jun 25]), and the cash injection was then doubled at an unchanged rate (Reuters, [Jun 29]). Prices stay split: May CPI rose only 1.2% (China Daily, [Jun 10]) while factory-gate inflation hit a near four-year high (Reuters, [Jun 9]).

PMIs & Activity: The growth pulse turned up in June, narrowly. The official manufacturing PMI rose to 50.3 (Reuters, [Jun 29]) from a flat 50 in May, on tech export demand (CNBC, [Jun 29]); non-manufacturing rose to 50.2 (Reuters, [Jun 29]). The private gauge capped its strongest quarter since late 2020 (Reuters, [Jun 30]), yet June grew less than expected (Bloomberg, [Jun 30]): a high average with momentum fading. Credit Impulse: Total social financing grew 7.7% in May (CGTN, [Jun 12]) and topped forecasts (Bloomberg, [Jun 12]), but new loans missed as the property slump curbed demand (Reuters, [Jun 12]): the beat is government bonds, the miss is private demand. The PBOC is urging banks to lend because demand remains weak (Reuters, [Jun 26]). With debt above 300% of GDP (UPI, [Jun 29]), the household and private-firm impulse is still negative.

Property: June's first datapoint is poor: resale home prices fell faster, a private survey shows (Reuters, [Jun 30]), after new home prices fell at a faster pace in May (Reuters, [Jun 15]). Real-estate investment fell 16.2% y/y in January–May (GMK Center, [Jun 18]). State capital is replacing private balance sheets: Shenzhen Metro's chairman now also chairs Vanke (BigGo Finance, [Jun 30]) and state developers are powering Shenzhen construction (Nikkei Asia, [Jun 29]), while Country Garden paid over 2.8 billion yuan in the half-year after its offshore restructuring (BigGo Finance, [Jun 30]). Fiscal, Policy & Trade: The 62.5 billion yuan third batch of trade-in funds (Global Times, [Jun 26]) is small against a retail contraction, and the State Council reviewed AI, trade and carbon plans (Dimsum Daily, [Jun 29]) with no demand package. Trade is where policy moves: the EU and China set an October deadline to reset ties (Bloomberg, [Jun 29]) over a €360bn deficit (The Guardian, [Jun 29]); Beijing and Washington agreed a trade council on tariffs (Global Times, [Jun 25]); and China imposed a 73.5% preliminary tariff on Canadian pea starch (Global News, [Jun 30]) and listed 20 Japanese entities for export controls (Reuters, [Jun 29]). Adam Tooze's Chartbook calls this China's "walled-in wealth", cut off from the AI boom.

Beijing's response is stealth easing, a 1.25% overnight rate it would not even publish (Bloomberg, [Jun 29]), not a demand package, and walled-in savings go to bonds and gold rather than spending.
