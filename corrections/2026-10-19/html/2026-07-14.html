🌍 Geographic Macro Overview

🇺🇸 United States  Mixed  → on hold

Macro Stance: Fed remains on hold under new Chair Kevin Warsh, who testified today reaffirming commitment to the 2% inflation goal. June CPI came in at 3.5% YoY — cooler than expected but still well above target, leaving the Fed "genuinely conflicted." Fiscal remains expansionary. The 10Y yield is at 4.58 (+9 bps vs 4W ago) and the 2Y at 4.26 (prior close, +21 bps vs 4W ago), with the curve at +36 bps — positively sloped but still flat by historical standards in a high-nominal environment.

New / Most Important: Three converging events today reshape the near-term picture. First, June CPI printed 3.5% YoY, described as relief partly attributable to a "brief US-Iran peace deal" dampening energy pass-through — but 3.5% is still nowhere near 2%. Second, Warsh's first Congressional testimony as Chair projected hawkish credibility ("committed to 2%"), which markets initially read as reducing imminent hike risk but not opening the door to cuts. Third, big bank earnings (JPM, Wells Fargo) delivered bumper results, with Dimon flagging resilience alongside "mounting risks." The equity market rallied on the CPI print — S&P at 7547 (↑1.6% vs 4W) — but yields simultaneously climbed, an unusual divergence that suggests the bond market doesn't trust the inflation cool as durable. The US-Iran geopolitical overhang is the wild card: the "brief peace deal" that helped June CPI could unravel, and the Guardian reports an Australian rate hike becomes likely if the conflict resumes within a week, suggesting global second-order effects are being priced.

Playbook ✓: Warsh maintaining hawkish rhetoric while holding rates is exactly what the transition playbook predicted — establish credibility first, act later. Bank earnings strong on NII from higher-for-longer.  |  ⚠️ Divergence: The divergence is equities rallying while yields rise. In a capital-flow framework, this only makes sense if foreign capital is flowing into US equities (risk-on) while simultaneously exiting Treasuries (duration rejection). A softening growth pulse and VIX at 16.47 (complacency) is a structurally unstable combination: US existing home sales unexpectedly fell in June with mortgage rates near 1-year highs, and China's growth is seen weakening to the bottom of its official target range, while vol stays complacent. This historically resolves violently in one direction. The Inc. headline about the "AI boom triggering a surprise rate hike" captures a growing undercurrent: AI capex ($700B in data center spend cited) is genuinely inflationary via electricity demand and labor competition, which could force Warsh's hand even if headline CPI cools.

🇪🇺 European Union  Mixed  → easing bias

Macro Stance: ECB in an easing cycle relative to the Fed, with the economy described as "a mess" by The Economist while equity markets are called "a steal." Fiscal fragmentation persists. The digital euro pilot advanced significantly with 36 payment providers selected, including Revolut and UniCredit.

New / Most Important: The digital euro pilot moving to a 36-provider live trial is structurally significant. This is the most concrete step toward a retail CBDC in any major economy. ECB's Cipollone explicitly framed it as a payments resilience tool — read: reducing dependence on US-controlled card networks (Visa/Mastercard) and dollar-denominated payment rails. For capital flow analysis, a functional digital euro would reduce euro liquidity leakage through dollar correspondent banking and give the ECB real-time visibility into cross-border flows within the eurozone. This is a medium-term negative for US payment network monopolies and a positive for European monetary sovereignty. The timeline to full deployment remains uncertain, but the pilot phase with major banks signals political commitment has survived the usual EU implementation friction.

Playbook ✓: European equities cheap on valuation, narrative of "ugly but investable" playing out as The Economist highlights.  |  ⚠️ Divergence: The digital euro acceleration is faster than consensus expected — most institutional investors still treat CBDC as a 2030+ story. It's moving now.

PMIs & Activity: The external engine is running hot. New / Most Important: June trade is the day's print and it confirms the two-speed economy rather than easing it. A record $412 billion of exports, growing at the fastest pace since 2021, sits next to crude imports at a near-decade low, so the 36% import jump is not a broad domestic-demand signal. Growth is being exported: car shipments above 1mn a month and AI hardware are carrying a quarter in which GDP is expected to slip to the bottom of the target range. A consumption plan, not a demand package, is Beijing's answer for now.

Playbook ✓: Export strength, weak oil demand, a property drag and targeted rather than broad support are all on the deflationary script.  |  ⚠️ Divergence: The consensus reads a record export month as a growth upgrade; the growth pulse reads it as a widening imbalance. With private credit contracting, developers in a fresh liquidity crunch and the PMI barely above 50, the surplus is the release valve for domestic weakness. The transmission is to China's trading partners rather than its suppliers. European exporters absorb the car and AI-hardware surge, which is why Berlin is now asking for a currency dialogue. Oil loses a buyer as Chinese crude imports fall to a near-decade low, and construction-linked demand for iron ore and AUD stays soft while property investment falls by double digits. EM Asia shares the AI-hardware upside, but the political risk of the surplus is rising faster than the volumes.

🇯🇵 Japan  Bearish (bonds) / Mixed (equities)  ↑ rate direction

Macro Stance: BOJ on a clear tightening trajectory — producer prices hit the fastest pace since 2023, growth forecasts may be revised up, and the institution is described as building momentum for its "first rate hike in 31 years." The Takaichi government is sending contradictory signals, creating bond/currency volatility.

New / Most Important: The Japan pension reallocation story is the most important capital-flow signal globally this week. Japan signaled a "massive pension shift to domestic assets," sparking a rally in both the yen and JGBs. This is structurally enormous. Japan's Government Pension Investment Fund (GPIF) and related pools are the largest allocators in the world — any meaningful shift from foreign to domestic assets means selling US Treasuries and repatriating yen. This is a structural headwind for USTs and a tailwind for the yen, reinforcing BOJ tightening. The "slow motion currency crisis" framing from Business Insider captures the pre-repatriation dynamic: USDJPY reportedly eyeing 175, which would make the pension pivot even more urgent as a stabilization tool. The yen carry trade unwind risk is now a two-headed threat — BOJ hiking AND domestic capital returning home. This is the single most important cross-border capital flow shift in the signal feed and should be monitored as a potential trigger for a UST sell-off if executed at scale.

Playbook ✓: BOJ normalization proceeding as telegraphed.  |  ⚠️ Divergence: The pension repatriation is NOT in the consensus playbook — most macro funds are positioned for gradual BOJ tightening, not a simultaneous pension-driven capital flow reversal. The combination of BOJ hikes + pension repatriation + USDJPY at extreme levels creates a reflexive feedback loop that could move much faster than orderly normalization.
