Macro Stance: The Fed remains in a holding pattern. Playbook ✓: BOJ hiking into yen weakness — textbook.  |  ⚠️ Divergence: The speed and breadth of tightening (hikes + QT + intervention threats simultaneously) exceeds consensus expectations. This is the carry-trade-unwind accelerant that global macro should be watching above all else.

🇦🇺 Australia  Bearish  ↑ rates (hike risk rising)

Macro Stance: The RBA has turned decisively hawkish. Chief economist Hunter warned of more frequent supply shocks and said the Board "will intervene as necessary" to bring inflation back to target. Economists are now calling for no rate cuts until 2027, with a fourth hike looming. Banks made a "shock move" on mortgage rates — likely repricing higher. This is the most hawkish RBA posture in the current cycle.

New / Most Important: The RBA's framing around supply shocks is structurally important and represents a philosophical shift. Hunter's speech explicitly addressed how the RBA should respond to supply-driven inflation — the traditional playbook says "look through it," but the RBA is signaling it won't. This means every oil spike, every supply-chain disruption, every climate-related agricultural shock now carries rate-hike risk in Australia. For a commodity-exposed, housing-levered economy, this is a dangerous feedback loop: supply shocks raise rates, which crush housing, which slows consumption, which requires easing — but you can't ease because the supply shock is still inflationary. The AUD strengthened on Hunter's hawkish remarks, but this is a bear trap for the currency — the domestic economy cannot sustain this tightening trajectory. Short AUD/long USD or short ASX banks is the play if the RBA actually follows through on another hike.

Playbook ✓: Hawkish RBA supporting AUD in the near term.  |  ⚠️ Divergence: Softer inflation data (per BNY) challenges the hawkish stance — there's a real risk the RBA is fighting the last war. The divergence between the data (softening) and the rhetoric (hawkish) is unusually wide.
