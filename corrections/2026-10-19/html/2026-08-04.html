🌍 Geographic Macro Overview

🇺🇸 United States  Mixed  → rates on hold

Macro Stance: The Warsh-led Fed held rates steady at its July 29 meeting, but the decision was remarkably contentious — three officials voted for a hike. Fiscal policy remains stimulative; headlines describe an "overstimulated" economy with bond markets pricing in sustained inflation risk. The 10Y yield at 4.63 (+10 bps vs 4W) and the 2Y at 4.25% (prior close, +12 bps vs 4W) confirm the market is not buying rate-cut hopes anytime soon. The 10Y-2Y spread at +45 bps (prior close, 10 bps steeper than four weeks ago) is positively sloped but still flat by historical standards — no recession signal — and the modest steepening itself reflects term-premium re-pricing as deficit concerns and inflation credibility doubts compound.

New / Most Important: The three-way dissent at the Fed was structurally new and important. This is the most hawkish FOMC split under Warsh's leadership to date, and it exposes a credibility problem: markets are now openly questioning whether the Fed has "all hat, no cattle" on inflation. Warsh stated the Fed "won't hesitate to stop inflation," but bond markets pushed yields higher in response, suggesting the statement was interpreted as too little, too late. The coordinated US-Japan yen intervention (with Bessent's involvement) signals that the administration is willing to use FX policy as a macro tool — something that hasn't been in the playbook since the 1990s. S&P 500 at 7734.82 (↑3.1% vs 4W) is flirting with record highs, driven by massive earnings delivery and US-Iran diplomacy optimism.

Playbook ✓: Equities grinding higher on earnings momentum, mega-cap tech leading (Palantir +30% post-earnings, Applied Digital surging on AI infrastructure revenue). Oil easing on Iran diplomacy provides a marginal inflation relief valve.  |  ⚠️ Divergence: The bond market is breaking from the equity market's sanguine view. With yields surging and the Fed's credibility openly questioned, we have a classic late-cycle divergence: equities celebrating earnings growth while bonds scream that the inflation/deficit mix is unsustainable. The three dissents for a hike — in a regime that was supposedly neutral-to-dovish — suggest the internal Fed debate has shifted structurally hawkish. If one more data print (JOLTs due today, ISM services this week) shows labor market heat, the September meeting becomes a genuine live hike possibility. This is not priced into equities at all. Gold holding near its highs confirms that the "smart money" macro hedge bid is still present underneath the equity euphoria.

🇪🇺 European Union  Bearish  ↑ rates likely rising

Macro Stance: The ECB faces a deteriorating inflation picture. Eurozone CPI rose to 2.9% in July, surprising to the upside, with energy prices identified as a key upside driver. Markets have shifted toward pricing a September ECB rate hike as increasingly likely. The euro is advancing on tightening expectations.

New / Most Important: The July inflation print at 2.9% was the catalyst — it reversed what had been a tentative disinflation narrative and reopened the hiking debate. ECB wage trackers show no pickup in wage pressures, which creates an awkward divergence: headline inflation is being driven by energy and administered prices, not domestically generated demand. This puts the ECB in an uncomfortable position — hiking into cost-push inflation risks choking an already fragile growth recovery.

Playbook ✓: Euro strengthening on hawkish ECB re-pricing fits the standard playbook of rate-differential-driven FX moves.  |  ⚠️ Divergence: The divergence between headline CPI (rising) and wage pressures (flat) is the key structural tension. If the ECB hikes into an energy-driven inflation shock rather than a demand-driven one, it repeats the 2011 Trichet mistake. The Hormuz supply disruption headlines in the feed suggest this energy-driven inflation may be stickier than expected, making the ECB's dilemma genuinely worse. This is a macro setup where being short European duration and long euro can coexist — unusual but internally consistent given the hiking re-pricing.

🇨🇳 China  Mixed  → accommodative but constrained

Macro Stance: Accommodative in intent, constrained in effect. The PBOC will enhance financial support for the real economy in H2 (State Council Information Office, [Aug 3]) after pledging timely adjustment of its policy tools (Reuters, [Aug 2]) and a moderately loose stance backed by incremental measures (Global Times, [Aug 2]); one read of the Politburo is that RRR and rate cuts are likely in Q3 (BigGo Finance, [Jul 30]). The overnight reverse repo rate stays at 1.25% (Reuters, [Jul 28]). The currency is the constraint: the yuan firmed despite a weaker PBOC fixing as the dollar sagged (Business Recorder, [Aug 3]), after a three-year high the central bank signalled discomfort over (The Edge Malaysia, [Jul 31]). June CPI rose only 1% (Global Times, [Jul 9]) while PPI climbed 4.1% (finance.biggo.com, [Jul 8]), a margin squeeze rather than reflation.

PMIs & Activity: Both July surveys now point the same way. The official manufacturing PMI dropped to 49.2 (The State Council Information Office, [Jul 31]) and non-manufacturing to 49 (Macau Business, [Jul 31]); the private manufacturing PMI eased to 50.9 (Fibre2Fashion, [Aug 3]), a four-month low (Reuters, [Aug 2]). Reconciled: the export-tilted private sample is still expanding but slowing, the broader official sample is contracting, and Nikkei Asia sees more signs of slowdown on weak demand ([Aug 3]), while Standard Chartered says growth momentum softened again (www.tmgm.com, [Aug 3]). SCMP's framing fits: AI boom, factory gloom, with tech soaring as traditional industries feel the chill ([Aug 3]). Q2 GDP grew 4.3%, below the target range (Bloomberg, [Jul 15]), and first-half industrial profits rose 18.7% as a tech boom masks the property slump (Caixin Global, [Jul 27]). Tesla's wholesale sales in China grew for a ninth consecutive month, a rare firm read on durables.

Credit Impulse: June new bank loans rose less than expected (The Star, [Jul 15]) and credit growth missed forecasts as the PBOC flagged a "new normal" (Caixin Global, [Jul 16]); household loans fell by 366.8 billion yuan in the first half (36Kr, [Jul 17]). Cash at Chinese banks has swelled to a record as borrowing slows (Nikkei Asia, [Jul 28]): liquidity is ample, credit demand is not, which is why "support for the real economy" pledges change little on their own. Local debt is still fragile, with one LGFV insisting it can repay even as a bond sale hit a snag (The Edge Malaysia, [Aug 3]). The impulse reads flat, with households negative.

Property: The first July read is weak: housing prices fell 0.5% and sales volume weakened (Investing.com, [Aug 3]), and real home prices have sunk to their lowest level in 21 years (the deep dive, [Aug 3]). June's official data had new home prices falling at a slower pace (Reuters, [Jul 14]), but property investment slumped 18 percent y/y in January–June (The Standard (HK), [Jul 15]). Households are walking away from homes and into portfolios (South China Morning Post, [Aug 1]) and July was the worst building month since Covid (Wood Central, [Aug 1]); the contrarian bid is in prime offices, where investors bet on a rebound (South China Morning Post, [Aug 2]).

Fiscal, Policy & Trade: The Politburo settled on incremental policy support (Caixin Global, [Jul 31]), which an SCMP editorial reads as cautious confidence ([Aug 3]); leaders pledged support without big new stimulus plans (Reuters, [Jul 30]), while Bloomberg reports officials will consider new stimulus after the slowdown ([Jul 30]). Reconciled: incremental now, more only if data worsen, and one economist argues the Iran war could still alter Beijing's calculus (South China Morning Post, [Aug 3]). On trade, China drew "red lines" around its economic model ahead of EU and US talks (Reuters, [Aug 2]) and, as The Economist puts it, won't apologise for overcapacity ([Aug 3]); the US urged China to "fully meet" its rare-earths pledge (Nikkei Asia, [Jul 31]) as a September summit takes shape (Firstpost, [Jul 31]).

Markets & Flows: Onshore stocks closed down as an AI selloff hammered the chip supply chain (marketscreener.com, [Aug 3]), while the Hang Seng regained 26,000 (The Standard (HK), [Aug 3]) as Alibaba rallied on its new AI model (CNBC, [Aug 3]). The social security fund is buying A-shares (South China Morning Post, [Aug 3]), and five-year government bond futures debuted in Hong Kong (Global Times, [Aug 3]). Apple is testing memory chips from a rising Chinese rival (Yahoo Finance, [Aug 3]), keeping the CXMT threat to Micron live.

New / Most Important: Property's July numbers confirm what the PMIs implied. A 0.5% fall in housing prices with weaker volumes, on top of construction in contraction, means the drag is deepening in the sector that matters most for household balance sheets. The PBOC's promise of more support for the real economy is the right signal, but with record cash already idle in banks, rate and RRR cuts address price, not demand.

Playbook ✓: Accommodative signalling, incremental fiscal support and a weak property market are on script.  |  ⚠️ Divergence: The rest of the world's factories are accelerating: the US ISM manufacturing PMI rose to 55.6 in July (FXStreet, [Aug 3]) and euro-zone factory output hit a near 4-1/2-year high (Reuters, [Aug 3]), while China's PMIs slide. The drag is Chinese domestic demand, so it transmits through China-specific channels: iron ore eased as faltering China demand offset stimulus hopes (Business Recorder, [Jul 28]), AUD and NZD stay exposed after NZD/USD slipped on weak China PMIs (VT Markets, [Jul 31]), and European exporters face a weaker China consumer. Oil easing to $76.16 trims the terms-of-trade tax but does not fix demand.

🇯🇵 Japan  Bearish (for bonds)  ↑ rates rising / intervention active

Macro Stance: The BOJ held rates at its July 31 meeting but explicitly nodded to the possibility of an early rate hike. The weak yen is now an active inflation threat, and the BOJ has committed to more rate hikes. The economy is described as "two-speed" — export sectors booming on yen weakness while domestic consumption suffers from imported inflation.

New / Most Important: The US-Japan coordinated yen intervention is the most structurally significant development globally this week. This is a joint operation involving both the US Treasury (Bessent) and Japanese authorities — the first coordinated FX intervention of this kind in decades. Bessent's CNBC remarks hinting at an imminent BOJ rate hike, combined with CNN coverage of the intervention mechanics, suggest this is a deliberate policy shift, not a one-off. The intervention drove yen gains that have since partially retraced. Traders are now "glued to one trade" — the yen carry unwind — which means positioning is extremely concentrated. If the BOJ follows through with a hike, the carry unwind could be violent and would propagate across global risk assets, because yen-funded carry trades have been a core source of global liquidity for the past several years. This is the single most important cross-border capital flow risk on the board right now.

Playbook ✓: BOJ hawkish signaling is consistent with yen weakness forcing their hand.  |  ⚠️ Divergence: The US co-signing this intervention is the divergence. It suggests Washington views yen weakness as destabilizing — either for trade balance reasons or because it's exporting deflation to US import prices via competitive Asian FX. Bessent's involvement transforms this from a Japan-only story into a global macro coordination event.

🇦🇺 Australia  Bullish (for bonds)  → hold, hike risk diminished

Macro Stance: Inflation undershot forecasts at 3.8%, materially reducing the probability of an RBA rate hike. Markets pared rate hike bets aggressively, and major banks have begun cutting home loan rates in a "rate cutting frenzy." The RBA's own survey found most Australians believe rate rises worsen inflation — a politically inconvenient finding.

New / Most Important: The inflation undershoot was the key event — it flipped the consensus from "August hike likely" to "August hike unlikely." Some economists warn the RBA is not out of the woods, and one headline notes the RBA has gained "ammunition" for an August hike, creating an unresolved tension in the outlook.

Playbook ✓: Softer inflation → hold rates → banks cut mortgage rates → housing relief. Standard transmission.  |  ⚠️ Divergence: The fact that major banks are cutting rates preemptively, before the RBA has moved, is unusual. It suggests banks see the credit cycle turning and are competing for market share, which could restimulate housing demand and ironically create the very inflation impulse the RBA is trying to avoid. Watch housing data closely.
