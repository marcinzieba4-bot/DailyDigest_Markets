🇺🇸 United States  Mixed  → Rates on hold, hawkish lean

New / Most Important: The key development this week was Fed Chair Warsh's hawkish turn reshaping market expectations, combined with multiple outlets flagging that the stock market and the real economy appear fundamentally out of sync. The Reuters headline about the packed upcoming week of earnings, CPI, and Iran headlines suggests the next catalyst cluster is imminent. The market is pricing perfection with VIX in the complacency regime while the Fed is "genuinely conflicted" — that gap is the trade.

Playbook ✓: Rising equities + rising yields + falling VIX = classic risk-on with the "bad news is ignored" phase intact. The equity issuance surge and AI-led concentration match the late-cycle playbook.  |  ⚠️ Divergence: The structural divergence is the Fed leaning hawkish — possibly toward hikes — while equities grind higher into all-time territory. Warsh's hawkish pivot is not a consensus-priced outcome; the bond market is beginning to internalize it (yields rising) but equity vol is pricing zero risk. This is the mirror image of 2023's "immaculate disinflation" trade — we may be entering a regime where the market must price "immaculate stagflation" risk: growth slowing (IMF at 3% global), inflation sticky enough to keep the Fed on hold or hiking, and equities levitating on AI flows alone. The equity issuance wave tells you corporate treasurers see this as a window, not a floor. Housing is barely moving — the "just crying for lower rates" headline confirms the real economy channel is impaired. When the VIX is at 15 and the Fed is talking about hikes, someone is wrong.

🇯🇵 Japan  Bearish (bonds) / Bullish (JPY)  ↑ Rates rising

Macro Stance: The BOJ is on a hiking path with market pricing pointing toward a policy rate around 2% by end of 2027. Producer prices hit their fastest pace since 2023, reinforcing the tightening case. The yen remains near 40-year lows, generating "slow motion currency crisis" headlines.

New / Most Important: This week's biggest structural signal: Japan signaled a massive pension shift to domestic assets, which sparked a rally in the yen and JGBs. This is structurally enormous. Japan's Government Pension Investment Fund (GPIF) and the broader pension ecosystem have been the world's largest exporters of capital for decades — their overweight in foreign bonds and equities has been a pillar of the global carry trade and a key suppressor of JGB yields. A repatriation of even a fraction of these assets would simultaneously strengthen the yen, drain liquidity from US Treasuries and European bonds, and compress JGB term premia. This is the mirror of what happened in 2022 when Japanese lifers started hedging — except this time it's a deliberate policy-driven reallocation, not a market-forced adjustment. The knock-on for UST is directly negative: one of the largest structural buyers of US duration is being told to come home. Combined with BOJ hiking and PPI accelerating, this is a regime change for Japanese capital flows that global macro portfolios must position around.

Playbook ✓: BOJ hiking into accelerating wholesale inflation while the yen stays weak is textbook.  |  ⚠️ Divergence: The pension repatriation signal is the divergence — the playbook assumed Japan would keep exporting capital even as BOJ hiked. If pension flows reverse, the yen could strengthen violently, unwinding carry trades globally. The Atlantic Council piece on the weak yen "spelling trouble at home and abroad" suggests policymakers are now actively worried, making intervention + pension reallocation a coordinated strategy.
