🇺🇸 United States  Mixed  ↑ rates — hike bias per FOMC minutes

Macro Stance: The Fed is in a hawkish hold with an active tightening bias. July FOMC minutes released this week revealed officials saw a "need for rate hike if inflation doesn't cool," with a September hike explicitly still on the table. Fiscal remains expansive and deficit concerns are acute — the 30-year yield topped 5.31% (highest in 19 years) on Aug 17 and El-Erian called the 5.27% level a "structural shift." The 10Y at 4.74 (+6 bps vs 4W) and 2Y at 4.24% (prior close, −9 bps) produce a +50 bps curve — positively sloped but still flat by historical standards, and 14 bps steeper than four weeks ago as the long end carries term premium while the front end edges lower. Treasury Secretary Bessent acted to "break bond market fever" via buyback operations, and crypto/gold markets explicitly rallied on the buyback announcement, confirming this was read as quasi-liquidity injection.

New / Most Important: This is the week the US bond market forced the administration's hand — again. Bessent's emergency buyback operation (reported Aug 19-20) was a direct response to alarming long-end yields. The FOMC minutes (Aug 19-20) simultaneously revealed a hawkish contingency that markets had not fully priced. This creates a structurally novel tension: the Treasury is injecting liquidity at the long end to suppress term premium while the Fed is signaling it may tighten further at the short end. This is effectively a policy conflict — fiscal dominance in real time. The equity market is shrugging it off (S&P 7674, ↑3.5%) because the buybacks are functionally acting like sterilized QE, improving collateral conditions and freeing dealer balance sheets. Gold at 4624 (↑13.7%) and Bitcoin at 77261 (↑21.7%) are the honest markets here — they are pricing the debasement channel, not the rate channel. Watch for this Treasury buyback mechanism to become a recurring feature — it's the new "not-QE."

Playbook ✓: Equities grinding higher on AI/mega-cap leadership (Nasdaq ↑4.2% vs Russell ↑3.0%, narrow market confirmed). VIX crushed to 15.1 in COMPLACENCY regime despite bond stress — classic late-cycle risk appetite. New Trump tariffs on Canada announced, keeping trade uncertainty alive.  |  ⚠️ Divergence: The massive divergence is that the bond market is screaming fiscal unsustainability while equities remain near highs. Historically a curve steepening on rising long-end yields (not Fed cuts) — even to a still-flat +50 bps — is a warning of sovereign credit deterioration, not a risk-on signal. Gold up 13.7% in four weeks while China's July bank loans posted a record contraction confirms this is not healthy reflation — it's defensive positioning masquerading as momentum. The fact that Treasury had to intervene with buybacks to calm the 30-year market is a fiscal dominance signal that structurally changes the playbook: the Fed's rate decisions are becoming secondary to Treasury's debt management operations as the primary driver of financial conditions.

🇯🇵 Japan  Bearish (Bonds) / Bullish (JPY)  ↑ rates — hike imminent

Macro Stance: The BoJ is converging toward its first rate hike in 31 years with accelerating momentum. Japanese inflation picked up further this week, backing the case for tightening. Multiple sources — Mizuho, Bloomberg, FT — all confirmed rising inflation data and strengthening hike expectations. The yen strengthened against the dollar as rate hike bets solidified.

New / Most Important: This is the most consequential central bank story globally right now and it is structurally NEW in a generational sense. Japan has not hiked rates since the early 1990s; the prospect of normalization threatens to unwind the world's largest carry trade. The yen carry trade unwind fears are colliding with easing oil prices, creating a complex cross-asset dynamic. Takaichi's BoJ appointments in 2027 loom as a political constraint on the pace of hikes, but the market is front-running the near-term move. Morgan Stanley flagged the "weak yen matters to investors" — this is the flip side: if the yen strengthens sharply on a hike, Japanese institutional repatriation of foreign bond holdings (particularly US Treasuries) would compound the US long-end problem. This is the most dangerous feedback loop in global macro right now: BoJ hikes → yen strengthens → Japanese investors sell US Treasuries → US yields spike further → Treasury forced into more buybacks → dollar weakens → yen strengthens further. Japanese corporate bond issuers are already flagging rate hikes as a "hurdle," suggesting credit conditions will tighten domestically.

Playbook ✓: Inflation data supporting normalization narrative. Yen appreciation on hawkish repricing.  |  ⚠️ Divergence: The political overlay (Takaichi's 2027 BoJ appointments) could cap the hiking cycle before it gets meaningful. The market may be pricing a more aggressive path than the BoJ can deliver given political constraints. But even a single hike could trigger the repatriation cascade described above.
