🇨🇳 China  Bearish  → easing bias

Macro Stance: The PBOC is easing through liquidity, not price. It boosted liquidity to smooth tax payments and debt issuance (Bloomberg, [Jul 14]) while telling some banks not to re-discount bills at rates below 0.5% (Reuters, [Jul 14]), a floor under a bill market that banks use to pad loan books when genuine demand is missing. Loan prime rates are expected to stay unchanged for a 14th month in July (Reuters, [Jul 17]). The PBOC signalled comfort with yuan levels and expects two-way moves (Bloomberg, [Jul 15]); the yuan touched a 4-week high (TradingView, [Jul 15]) before weakening slightly on the fixing (BusinessToday Malaysia, [Jul 17]). Prices are the wrong mix: June CPI rose only 1% (chinadailyhk, [Jul 8]) and slowed more than forecast (Bloomberg, [Jul 8]), while PPI climbed 4.1% (BigGo Finance, [Jul 8]) to a near four-year high that squeezes manufacturers (Reuters, [Jul 8]).

PMIs & Activity: The surveys and the hard data point in different directions. The June official manufacturing PMI rose to 50.3 and the non-manufacturing PMI to 50.2 (Reuters, [Jun 29]); the private gauge capped the strongest factory quarter since late 2020 (Reuters, [Jun 30]). Yet second-quarter GDP growth weakened to 4.3%, below the official target range (Bloomberg, [Jul 15]), the slowest since 2022 as investment slumped (CNBC, [Jul 14]). June industrial output rose 5.3% y/y and retail sales returned to growth (BusinessLine, [Jul 15]), but retail sales of consumer goods were up just 1.3% in the first half (Ecns.cn, [Jul 15]) and the investment slide deepened as infrastructure joined property (Caixin, [Jul 15]). The engine is external: June exports surged 27% y/y with imports up 36% (Reuters, [Jul 13]), and Capital Economics now calls AI the main driver of growth ([Jul 16]).

Credit Impulse: Credit is the weak link. June new loans missed forecasts (Bloomberg, [Jul 15]) and overall credit growth undershot as the PBOC flagged a "new normal" (Caixin, [Jul 16]). Household loans decreased by 366.8 billion yuan in the first half (36Kr, [Jul 17]), and record consumer defaults undermine the push to boost spending (Reuters, [Jul 15]). Read: with households repaying and private firms not borrowing, the impulse rests on state-directed financing, and the bill-rate floor shows banks scrambling for assets.

Property: Prices are stabilising at the top while investment keeps falling. June new home prices fell at a slower pace, though Reuters doubts a recovery ([Jul 14]); Bloomberg reads it as a sign the market is stabilising ([Jul 14]), and first-tier prices extended a 4-month rebound (SCMP, [Jul 15]). But property investment slumped 18% y/y in January–June (The Standard, [Jul 15]), developers face a fresh liquidity crunch (Nikkei Asia, [Jul 13]) and Beijing is seeking to curb new bond sales by indebted LGFVs (Bloomberg, [Jul 15]), so the land-finance drag on local governments persists.

Fiscal, Policy & Trade: Beijing's response so far is plans, not money. The State Council approved the first five-year plan to boost consumption (China Daily, [Jul 13]), with a 60 trillion yuan retail-sales target for 2030 (Caixin, [Jul 14]), but Reuters reports a bet on faster state-backed projects to avoid broad stimulus ([Jul 16]). The FT says leaders are zeroing in on the need for stimulus ([Jul 18]) as the July Politburo meeting on the economy is prepared (Sinocism, [Jul 13]). Trade friction is building on both flanks: the surplus with the EU hit a record (Bloomberg, [Jul 14]) and Brussels signalled emergency import curbs (SCMP, [Jul 14]), while the US Senate backed a 100% tariff bill targeting buyers of Russian oil, China included (The New Indian Express, [Jul 17]).

Markets & Flows: The week ended in an AI-driven liquidity squeeze. CXMT's $8.6 billion IPO stirred liquidity concerns (TradingView, [Jul 17]), ChiNext plunged over 7% (BigGo Finance, [Jul 16]) and investor fear over chip stocks hit a record (Bloomberg, [Jul 16]); Moonshot AI then added fuel to Wall Street's chip sell-off (WSJ, [Jul 17]). The Hang Seng reclaimed 25,000 (Moomoo, [Jul 16]) only to slump 446 points as tech selling deepened (BusinessToday Malaysia, [Jul 18]). Foreign investors marginally offloaded onshore bonds in June (The Economic Times, [Jul 17]) even as the 10-year yield fell on stimulus bets (TradingView, [Jul 14]).

New / Most Important: One of China's top investors says finance, not AI, is the country's biggest bottleneck (CNBC, [Jul 16]), and this week's numbers bear him out. Exports, AI hardware and factory output are running hot, but transmission into domestic demand is broken: loans miss, households repay debt, consumer defaults are at a record and property investment is still falling at a double-digit pace. The market rout shows the same constraint from the other side, as a single $8.6 billion listing was enough to drain liquidity from A-share tech. Until credit to households and private firms turns, AI capex alone cannot lift the domestic cycle, and the Politburo's choice is between more planning and actual money.

Playbook ✓: Weak domestic credit, a GDP miss, LPR on hold, liquidity-led easing and a managed yuan are all on the bear script.  |  ⚠️ Divergence: The surveys are not bearish: a 50.3 manufacturing PMI and 27% export growth describe an economy producing more while its own credit impulse is negative. That mismatch is what transmits abroad. Europe absorbs it first, with record Chinese surpluses and emergency curbs in preparation, a direct risk to European exporters' margins. The commodity channel is split: crude imports plunged to the lowest in nearly a decade (Bloomberg, [Jul 13]), a drag on oil demand, and with property investment down 18% construction demand is not recovering, a headwind for iron ore and AUD even after BHP won higher quarterly iron ore prices (Reuters, [Jul 16]). A Politburo stimulus signal would matter for these assets only if it reached household and private credit.

🇯🇵 Japan  Mixed  ↑ hawkish tightening cycle

Macro Stance: The BOJ remains on a hawkish trajectory with markets pricing the next rate hike conditional on inflation persistence. The yen is at a 40-year low, creating acute pain for nearly half of Japanese firms. BOJ bond-buying pauses have left the yen exposed to intervention risk. EUR/JPY reached the upper 185 range before Iran tensions and BOJ hike expectations capped gains.

New / Most Important: The Japan Times published a notable call for "a more aggressive Bank of Japan," while MUFG flagged a "powerful long-term tailwind" for the yen. The Takaichi government and BOJ are sending contradictory signals, rattling both bond and currency markets. This government-central bank tension is structurally new — Japan is attempting something unprecedented: tightening monetary policy while the yen is historically weak, the government wants fiscal expansion, and corporate Japan is being squeezed by both higher rates and a weak currency simultaneously. The BOJ's bond-buying pause is a stealth tightening that risks triggering JGB volatility if it accelerates. Japan should be able to "weather a US market crash" per SCMP, suggesting international investors are starting to view Japan as a relative safe haven — a remarkable shift from its historical beta-to-global-risk profile. Watch for the BOJ to become the surprise hawkish outlier among major CBs in H2.

Playbook ✓: Gradual tightening with yen weakness is consensus.  |  ⚠️ Divergence: The government-BOJ policy contradiction is the risk — if Takaichi pushes back hard against rate hikes while BOJ pushes forward, FX vol will spike and carry trades face a disorderly unwind.

The demand side confirms this is a supply-shock narrative, not demand-driven — China's crude imports fell to the lowest in nearly a decade in June. Retail energy bulls see a simple thesis: Strait of Hormuz risk + OPEC production jumps still can't keep pace with geopolitical disruption = crude to $100. The key risk is a sudden de-escalation or demand destruction from a slowing economy, which would expose the entire move as a geopolitical premium that evaporates overnight.
