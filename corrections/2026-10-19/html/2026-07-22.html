Macro Stance: Loan prime rates were held for a 14th consecutive month (The Business Times, [Jul 20]) despite the second-quarter growth miss (Sharecast, [Jul 20]), and the market now eyes a rate-cut window near the end of the third quarter (BigGo Finance, [Jul 21]). Prices are the wrong mix: June CPI rose only 1% (chinadailyhk, [Jul 8]) and slowed more than forecast (Bloomberg, [Jul 8]), while PPI climbed 4.1% (BigGo Finance, [Jul 8]) to a near four-year high that squeezes manufacturers (Reuters, [Jul 8]).

PMIs & Activity: The surveys and the hard data point in different directions. The June official manufacturing PMI rose to 50.3 and the non-manufacturing PMI to 50.2 (Reuters, [Jun 29]); the private gauge capped the strongest factory quarter since late 2020 (Reuters, [Jun 30]). Yet second-quarter GDP growth weakened to 4.3%, below the official target range (Bloomberg, [Jul 15]). June industrial output rose 5.3% y/y and retail sales returned to growth (BusinessLine, [Jul 15]), but retail sales of consumer goods were up just 1.3% in the first half (Ecns.cn, [Jul 15]), infrastructure investment turned negative (BigGo Finance, [Jul 19]) and the car market heads for its worst year since 2021 (CNBC, [Jul 20]). Even Shanghai's 5.6% beat comes with a two-speed recovery (SCMP, [Jul 21]); Trivium reads the miss as a deepening split between tech exports and domestic demand ([Jul 21]), with June exports up 27% y/y (Reuters, [Jul 13]).

Credit Impulse: Credit is the weak link. June new loans missed forecasts (Bloomberg, [Jul 15]) and overall credit growth undershot as the PBOC flagged a "new normal" (Caixin, [Jul 16]). Household loans decreased by 366.8 billion yuan in the first half (36Kr, [Jul 17]), and record consumer defaults undermine the push to boost spending (Reuters, [Jul 15]). Read: with households repaying and private firms not borrowing, the impulse rests on state-directed financing.

Property: Prices are stabilising at the top while capital keeps leaving. June new home prices fell at a slower pace, though Reuters doubts a recovery ([Jul 14]), and first-tier prices extended a 4-month rebound (SCMP, [Jul 15]). But property investment slumped 18% y/y in January–June (The Standard, [Jul 15]) and KKR and AEW seek to sell China property assets at steep losses (Bloomberg, [Jul 20]). Fiscal, Policy & Trade: Beijing vowed to achieve its annual growth target (Bloomberg, [Jul 20]) and the premier chaired a State Council executive meeting (Macau Business, [Jul 21]), but the toolkit is still plans and projects: the first five-year consumption plan (State Council Information Office, [Jul 15]) and faster state-backed projects to avoid broad stimulus (Reuters, [Jul 16]), even as the FT says leaders are zeroing in on the need for stimulus ([Jul 18]). At the margin support is being withdrawn, with a key tax break on lithium EV batteries ending (Caixin, [Jul 20]). On trade, EU lawmakers arrived for high-stakes talks (SCMP, [Jul 21]) and the foreign minister discussed the trade balance with them (Reuters, [Jul 21]) as the yuan draws scrutiny in Europe (Bloomberg, [Jul 21]) and Paris and Berlin present a united front (SCMP, [Jul 20]). A new US sanctions bill threatens a 100% tariff on buyers of Russian oil, China included (The Independent, [Jul 16]).

Markets & Flows: The rescue has broadened. New / Most Important: The market rescue has become AI industrial policy. Set beside the NVIDIA-China export restriction debate in the US, which Cramer frames as national security, both sides are now fencing AI supply chains. For China that concentrates state capital in the one sector that is growing, while household credit shrinks and property investment falls; it raises valuations in tech without raising the domestic demand the Q2 miss exposed.

Playbook ✓: Rate hold, managed yuan, state support for equities and stimulus by plan rather than by cash are on script.  |  ⚠️ Divergence: Emerging-market assets rallied on China's tech support (Bloomberg, [Jul 21]), so the equity channel to EM is real, but the macro channels have not changed. China keeps importing iron ore heavily (Mining.com.au, [Jul 21]) even with property investment down 18%, because surplus steel is exported: Indian steelmakers are pivoting home as China squeezes margins (Reuters, [Jul 21]). That supports iron ore and AUD for now while pressuring steel producers from India to Europe, and an export-led 50.3 PMI keeps European exporters exposed to the curbs Paris and Berlin are aligning on. A real lift to Asian and commodity demand still needs household and private credit to turn.

🇯🇵 Japan  Bearish (bonds) / Bullish (yen recovery)  ↑ rates — faster hikes signaled

Macro Stance: The BoJ is in active tightening mode with rates already at a 31-year high, and the yen has hit a 40-year low — creating a monetary policy paradox where even hiking hasn't arrested currency weakness. The BoJ has now signaled openness to *faster* rate hikes, a material hawkish escalation.

New / Most Important: This is structurally significant. Multiple sources confirmed today that BoJ officials view recent yen weakness as an upside inflation risk and are "open to raising rates faster" than previously communicated. Japan's Ministry of Finance stated readiness for "decisive currency action" — verbal intervention that historically precedes actual FX intervention. The 40-year yen low occurring simultaneously with 31-year high interest rates is an extraordinary divergence that reveals just how powerful the carry-trade structural short on the yen has become. The yen is being used as a global funding currency at such scale that domestic rate hikes alone cannot overcome the flow dynamics. If the BoJ accelerates hikes AND the MoF intervenes simultaneously, we could see a violent yen short-squeeze that reverberates through global risk assets — reminiscent of the August 2024 episode but potentially larger given the carry positions are more extended. This is the single most important cross-border capital flow risk on the board today. Global macro funds should be actively stress-testing their yen-funded carry book exposure.

Playbook ✓: BoJ tightening into yen weakness is consensus.  |  ⚠️ Divergence: The *pace* signal is the break — "faster hikes" was not in the market's base case. Combined with intervention threats, this creates a non-linear yen recovery risk.

Macro Stance: The RBA is now in a hawkish posture with rate *hike* probability reportedly doubling due to the US-Iran war driving fuel prices higher. The AUD has strengthened above 0.7000 on this hawkish stance. Inflation remains the top concern for Australians per a new RBA community survey.

New / Most Important: This is a significant regime shift. While most DM central banks are debating the pace of easing or holding, the RBA is now actively warned by analysts as potentially *hiking* — making Australia a hawkish outlier in the G10. The energy supply shock from US-Iran conflict is the catalyst, transmitting through fuel costs into an economy where inflation was already sticky. The RBA's new community survey revealing inflation as Australians' top concern gives the central bank political cover to tighten further. This divergence creates a long-AUD trade thesis against currencies where central banks are easing. The AUD above 0.70 reflects this, and energy prices plus China's still-heavy iron ore buying support the terms-of-trade channel, even though China's property investment fell 18% y/y in January–June. Watch for AUD/JPY as the expression of this divergence — long the hiker against the currency where hikes aren't working.

Playbook ✓: Commodity-linked currency strengthening with oil surging and China still buying iron ore is textbook.  |  ⚠️ Divergence: A potential rate *hike* cycle restarting in a DM economy in H2 2026 is deeply contrarian relative to global consensus of synchronized easing.
