Macro Stance: Loan prime rates were held for a 14th consecutive month (The Business Times, [Jul 20]) despite the second-quarter growth miss (Sharecast, [Jul 20]), and the market now eyes a rate-cut window near the end of the third quarter (BigGo Finance, [Jul 21]). PMIs & Activity: The surveys and the hard data point in different directions. The June official manufacturing PMI rose to 50.3 and the non-manufacturing PMI to 50.2 (Reuters, [Jun 29]); the private gauge capped the strongest factory quarter since late 2020 (Reuters, [Jun 30]). Yet second-quarter GDP growth weakened to 4.3%, below the official target range (Bloomberg, [Jul 15]). June industrial output rose 5.3% y/y and retail sales returned to growth (BusinessLine, [Jul 15]), but retail sales of consumer goods were up just 1.3% in the first half (Ecns.cn, [Jul 15]), infrastructure investment turned negative (BigGo Finance, [Jul 19]) and the car market heads for its worst year since 2021 (CNBC, [Jul 20]). Household loans decreased by 366.8 billion yuan in the first half (36Kr, [Jul 17]), and record consumer defaults undermine the push to boost spending (Reuters, [Jul 15]). Read: with households repaying and private firms not borrowing, the impulse rests on state-directed financing.

Property: Prices are stabilising at the top while capital keeps leaving. On trade, EU lawmakers arrived for high-stakes talks (SCMP, [Jul 21]) and the foreign minister discussed the trade balance with them (Reuters, [Jul 21]) as the yuan draws scrutiny in Europe (Bloomberg, [Jul 21]) and Paris and Berlin present a united front (SCMP, [Jul 20]). Playbook ✓: Rate hold, managed yuan, state support for equities and stimulus by plan rather than by cash are on script.  |  ⚠️ Divergence: Emerging-market assets rallied on China's tech support (Bloomberg, [Jul 21]), so the equity channel to EM is real, but the macro channels have not changed. 🇯🇵 Japan  Bearish (bonds) / Bullish (yen recovery)  ↑ rates — faster hikes signaled

Macro Stance: The BoJ is in active tightening mode with rates already at a 31-year high, and the yen has hit a 40-year low — creating a monetary policy paradox where even hiking hasn't arrested currency weakness. The BoJ has now signaled openness to *faster* rate hikes, a material hawkish escalation.

New / Most Important: This is structurally significant. Multiple sources confirmed today that BoJ officials view recent yen weakness as an upside inflation risk and are "open to raising rates faster" than previously communicated. Japan's Ministry of Finance stated readiness for "decisive currency action" — verbal intervention that historically precedes actual FX intervention. The 40-year yen low occurring simultaneously with 31-year high interest rates is an extraordinary divergence that reveals just how powerful the carry-trade structural short on the yen has become. The yen is being used as a global funding currency at such scale that domestic rate hikes alone cannot overcome the flow dynamics. If the BoJ accelerates hikes AND the MoF intervenes simultaneously, we could see a violent yen short-squeeze that reverberates through global risk assets — reminiscent of the August 2024 episode but potentially larger given the carry positions are more extended. This is the single most important cross-border capital flow risk on the board today. Global macro funds should be actively stress-testing their yen-funded carry book exposure.

Playbook ✓: BoJ tightening into yen weakness is consensus.  |  ⚠️ Divergence: The *pace* signal is the break — "faster hikes" was not in the market's base case. Combined with intervention threats, this creates a non-linear yen recovery risk.
