🇺🇸 United States  Mixed  → rates on hold (hawkish tilt)

Macro Stance: The Warsh-led Fed held rates steady at its July 29 meeting — the fifth consecutive hold this year — but the decision was fractured: three FOMC members dissented in favor of a hike. Fiscal stance remains expansionary. The yield curve has bear-steepened with the 10Y at 4.74% (+25 bps vs 4W) while the 2Y rose less, to 4.28% (prior close, +14 bps vs 4W), widening the 10Y-2Y spread to +47bps — positive but still flat by historical standards. GDP growth was characterized as sluggish with "stealthy strength" underneath, while mortgage rates hit the highest level in a year.

New / Most Important: The three-dissenter hawkish hold was the week's defining event. This is structurally significant: Warsh's public posture that the Fed "won't hesitate to stop inflation" is being met with bond market skepticism — Reuters described the market as "scratching its head." J.P. Morgan dropped what headlines called a "rate bombshell" around Warsh's inflation stance. The 10Y surging while VIX sits at a complacent 15.99 (↓1.0%) is a classic late-cycle divergence — equity vol is priced for perfection while the bond market prices for policy error. Oil's +23.3% surge in four weeks is an incoming inflation accelerant that makes the three dissenters look prescient. The S&P at 7489.72 (↑0.1%) is essentially flat — large-cap equities are absorbing the rate shock for now, but Nasdaq 100 at 28274.20 (↓3.6%) shows duration-sensitive growth is cracking. This sets up a scenario where the Fed may actually hike in Q4 if energy-driven inflation re-accelerates — a deeply non-consensus outcome the bond market is beginning to price.

Playbook ✓: Warsh holding rates while signaling hawkish bias is exactly what the "higher for longer" camp expected. Bear steepening on fiscal concerns is textbook.  |  ⚠️ Divergence: The VIX at 16.0 in COMPLACENCY regime while three Fed governors voted to hike is a glaring disconnect. Equity markets are treating this as a benign hold; the bond market is treating it as a prelude to tightening. The oil shock (+23.3% in 4 weeks) hasn't yet filtered into CPI prints but will hit headline inflation with a lag of 4-6 weeks, potentially forcing the Fed's hand. If Warsh's next meeting faces an energy-inflated CPI, those three dissenters become a majority. The Russell 2000 (↓2.2%) underperforming large caps on a rate-adjusted basis while the Russell/Nasdaq ratio actually improved (+1.5%) tells us small-caps are rotating from growth-adjacent tech into value — breadth is expanding in a way that's rate-insensitive but liquidity-sensitive.

🇪🇺 European Union  Bearish  ↑ rates — hike cycle restarting

Macro Stance: The ECB is now firmly on course to raise rates again in September after Eurozone inflation surprised to the upside, rising to 2.9% in July. Higher energy prices are the primary transmission mechanism. Bund yields held firm on strong data and hike expectations. The euro rallied on the back of this.

New / Most Important: This is a genuinely structural shift that markets were not positioned for six months ago. The ECB had been expected to be in a cutting cycle by mid-2026 — instead it is re-tightening. Eurozone inflation at 2.9% in July, driven by energy, puts the ECB in a bind: the wage tracker showed no pickup in wage pressures (per WSJ), meaning this is a supply-side inflation impulse that rate hikes can't easily address. Yet the ECB is signaling it will hike anyway, prioritizing credibility over growth optimization. This creates a deeply uncomfortable setup for European risk assets — tighter monetary policy into what is fundamentally a cost-push shock, not a demand-pull overheat. The euro's rally on hike expectations will further tighten financial conditions via the currency channel, compounding the drag. For global capital flows, this means European fixed income becomes a relative attractor for yield-seeking capital, but equity risk premiums must widen.

Playbook ✓: Energy-driven inflation forcing hawkish CB response is the classic European policy trap. Euro strength on rate differentials is textbook.  |  ⚠️ Divergence: The ECB hiking while the wage tracker shows NO pickup in wage pressures is a policy error signal. They're tightening against supply-side inflation with no demand-side confirmation — the playbook says this crushes growth without solving the inflation problem. Watch European credit spreads for the crack.

🇨🇳 China  Mixed  → easing bias

Macro Stance: Policy rates are on hold, the easing bias is explicit, and the currency has become the constraint. The PBOC set the overnight reverse repo rate steady at 1.25% (Reuters, [Jul 28]) and maintained liquidity support before month-end (TradingView, [Jul 28]); one read of the Politburo is that RRR and rate cuts are likely in Q3 (BigGo Finance, [Jul 30]). The yuan hit a three-year high despite the PBOC signalling discomfort over the gains (The Edge Malaysia, [Jul 31]), and the central bank is leaning against the rally with a stable daily fix (The Business Times, [Jul 31]). Bonds price the easing: the 10-year yield hit a one-month low (TradingView, [Jul 30]). June CPI rose only 1% (Global Times, [Jul 9]) while PPI climbed 4.1% (finance.biggo.com, [Jul 8]), a margin squeeze rather than demand-pull reflation.

PMIs & Activity: The full July set confirms the turn. The official manufacturing PMI dropped to 49.2 (The State Council Information Office, [Jul 31]) and the non-manufacturing PMI to 49 (Macau Business, [Jul 31]), as factories and construction slumped together (The Edge Malaysia, [Jul 31]) and services returned to contraction (TradingView, [Jul 31]). Against June's 50.3 (Reuters, [Jun 29]) this is a clean break, which CNBC ties to a demand slump and typhoons ([Jul 30]). Exports are no longer a reliable offset, with US-bound shipments falling in July after a brief recovery (CNBC, [Jul 30]). Q2 GDP grew 4.3%, below the target range (Bloomberg, [Jul 15]), and first-half industrial profits rose 18.7% as a tech boom masks the property slump (Caixin Global, [Jul 27]). The consumer is the weak leg: China Beige Book sees weakness on the consumer side (CNBC, [Jul 31]) and BMW's profit fell as the China slump deepened (Investing.com, [Jul 30]).

Credit Impulse: June new bank loans rose less than expected (The Star, [Jul 15]) and credit growth missed forecasts as the PBOC flagged a "new normal" (Caixin Global, [Jul 16]); household loans fell by 366.8 billion yuan in the first half (36Kr, [Jul 17]). Cash at Chinese banks has swelled to a record as borrowing slows (Nikkei Asia, [Jul 28]): liquidity is ample, credit demand is not. The biggest banks have launched their first repo-linked loans (Yahoo Finance, [Jul 27]), but a dip in the debt ratio may not bode well for the economy (South China Morning Post, [Jul 31]). The impulse reads flat, with households negative.

Property: June new home prices fell at a slower pace (Reuters, [Jul 14]) and first-tier prices extended a four-month rebound (South China Morning Post, [Jul 15]), but resale prices fell faster (Reuters, [Jun 30]) and property investment slumped 18 percent y/y in January–June (The Standard (HK), [Jul 15]). State-linked buyers still pay up for prime land, with Poly Developments and China Resources Land spending 23.2 billion yuan in a day in Shanghai (finance.biggo.com, [Jul 28]). Vanke completed a board reshuffle as Xu Enli takes the helm for a turnaround battle (finance.biggo.com, [Jul 31]), and in Hong Kong builder Parkview missed an interest payment on a loan (The Business Times, [Jul 30]).

Fiscal, Policy & Trade: The Politburo read has settled on incremental policy support (Caixin Global, [Jul 31]), a fiscal push without big stimulus (VT Markets, [Jul 31]). Leaders pledged to support the economy without big new stimulus plans (Reuters, [Jul 30]), while Bloomberg reports top officials will consider new stimulus after the slowdown ([Jul 30]). Reconciled: incremental measures now, a larger package only if the data worsen, and July's PMIs are the first test. On trade, the US urged China to "fully meet" its rare-earths pledge (Nikkei Asia, [Jul 31]), China voiced "serious concern" over new US curbs (South China Morning Post, [Jul 31]) and a Trump-Xi September summit is taking shape (Firstpost, [Jul 31]); China says the US pledged to cap replacement tariffs at 20% (Bloomberg, [Jul 27]).

Markets & Flows: Chinese stocks are on track for their worst month in a decade (Financial Times, [Jul 30]) as chip stocks slump on a rotation away from tech (Bloomberg, [Jul 30]); AI IPOs have failed to lift the market (International Business Times, [Jul 31]) even as firms ready the most buybacks since the trade war (The Edge Malaysia, [Jul 30]), and the Politburo bolstered hope the state will support stocks (South China Morning Post, [Jul 31]). The PBOC is set to further boost panda bond issuance (China Daily, [Jul 31]), and central banks led by China drove record Q2 gold demand (TipRanks, [Jul 30]).

New / Most Important: The full July PMI set widens the gap between data and policy. With manufacturing at 49.2 and non-manufacturing at 49, construction and services have joined factories in contraction, while leaders committed only to incremental support. The near-term lever is monetary, with RRR and rate cuts the base case, but a yuan at a three-year high tightens conditions for exporters just as US-bound shipments roll over. BYD's overseas push on flash-charging technology shows where the growth still is: abroad.

Playbook ✓: Incremental Politburo support, liquidity easing, weak property and a managed trade truce heading into a September summit are on script.  |  ⚠️ Divergence: The consensus treated the Politburo as the turning point; instead July's PMIs show contraction broadening to construction and services, with property investment down 18 percent. European carmakers absorb a deepening China slump, EM equity flows suffer as Chinese stocks head for their worst month in a decade, and oil at $84.67 stays a terms-of-trade tax on China's margins.

🇯🇵 Japan  Bearish  ↑ rates — hike bias intensifying

Macro Stance: The BOJ held rates at 1% on July 31 but warned core inflation is exceeding their 2% target and committed to further rate hikes. Suspected JPY intervention occurred ahead of the meeting. Governor Ueda's post-meeting speech discussed the policy outlook with a hawkish lean.

New / Most Important: The combination of an explicit commitment to more hikes plus suspected FX intervention is a significant escalation. The BOJ revised its inflation forecast down while keeping rates steady — a seemingly contradictory signal that actually reveals their hand: they're using a lower forecast to buy time before a hike they know is coming. The yen weakness forcing intervention ahead of a policy meeting where they held is the clearest sign of credibility strain. For global capital flows, BOJ tightening remains the most important single-variable risk in the world — any unwind of the yen carry trade reverberates across all risk assets. The weak yen being cited as "key in assessing inflation risk" means the currency itself has become a policy input, creating a reflexive loop: yen weakness → imported inflation → hike expectations → yen strengthening → unwinding carry → risk-off globally. This is the structural fault line that matters most for cross-border capital allocation in H2 2026.

Playbook ✓: Hold-then-hike signaling is exactly the BOJ's gradualist script.  |  ⚠️ Divergence: Intervention before a hold (not before a hike) is unusual and suggests the BOJ and MOF are not fully coordinated, or that the next hike timeline has been pulled forward relative to market expectations.
