🌍 Geographic Macro Overview

🇺🇸 United States  Mixed  → rates on hold / hawkish lean

Macro Stance: New Fed Chair Kevin Warsh appears firmly in a "prices are too high" posture, with markets pricing potential rate-hike risk rather than cuts. The 10Y yield has risen to 4.58% (+15 bps vs 4W ago), the 2Y at 4.13% (prior close, +6 bps), leaving a +42 bps curve that is positive but still flat by historical standards — a late-cycle reflationary signal. The Fed's Beige Book signaled an improving economy even as June CPI growth reportedly slowed, creating a "good data = no cuts" dynamic. Fiscal stance remains expansionary.

New / Most Important: The signal feed reports the largest US inflation decline in over six years — described as a "goose egg" moment on CNBC — yet this was immediately offset by renewed US-Iran tensions pushing mortgage rates higher and oil prices firmer. Retail sales came in below expectations, suggesting consumer fatigue beneath the headline disinflation. The Warsh Fed's July meeting will be his first rate decision as Chair, and the framing from multiple sources indicates markets now see the Fed as structurally more hawkish under his leadership than under the prior regime. This is a regime change worth emphasizing: Warsh is a "sound money" hawk who views asset-price inflation as a feature of his mandate, not a bug. The dollar surged post-decision, confirming the market digested this as a tightening signal even without an actual hike. Capital flows are responding — BTC is ripping (+7.4% vs 4W), functioning as a liquidity barometer that suggests the private sector is front-running eventual policy exhaustion even as the Fed leans hawkish.

Playbook ✓: S&P 500 at 7553 (↑0.6%) grinds higher on breadth rotation — Russell outperforming Nasdaq by 4.2% over 4W is textbook late-cycle broadening. VIX at 16.18 screams complacency.  |  ⚠️ Divergence: The divergence is the Nasdaq 100 declining 2.8% vs 4W ago even as the S&P makes highs. Chip stocks are getting pummeled — SOXX selloff, SK Hynix diving, TSMC falling despite record profits. The market is telling you AI capex has peaked in its ability to surprise to the upside. Mega-cap tech is now a source of funds, not a destination. This is the kind of rotation that historically precedes a 5-10% Nasdaq correction while the equal-weight index holds up. The capital flow is unmistakable: out of momentum tech, into value, smalls, and real economy plays. Watch the Russell/Nasdaq ratio — if it keeps climbing, this is a multi-quarter regime shift, not a one-week blip.

New / Most Important: June's data split cleanly. China is spending its income, not borrowing against the future, and the state is cleaning up local debt rather than levering it. Without a credit turn, the July Politburo meeting has to choose between a larger fiscal push and accepting growth at the bottom of the range.

Playbook ✓: Weak credit, a property-led investment drag, export strength and a stable, managed yuan are on script.  |  ⚠️ Divergence: Markets bought the consumer rebound; the growth pulse says the binding constraint is credit and investment. That matters for the transmission abroad. Mining shares led FTSE falls as China's growth slowed (Yahoo Finance UK, [Jul 15]), the clearest read-through to iron ore and AUD from an investment slump of this depth. European exporters face a record surplus and a machinery lobby now calling for duties, so the policy risk is rising for EU industrials. Oil loses a marginal buyer as Chinese crude imports sit at a near-decade low (Bloomberg, [Jul 13]). EM Asia keeps the AI-export tailwind, and a stable yuan limits contagion to regional currencies. A rebound in loan demand, not another liquidity operation, is what would change the read.

🇯🇵 Japan  Bearish  ↑ rates rising / hawkish

Macro Stance: The BOJ is in an active tightening cycle but sending contradictory signals alongside the Takaichi government, rattling both bond and currency markets. The yen has weakened to what headlines describe as a 40-year low, with USD/JPY eyeing levels that trigger intervention risk above 160. Nearly half of Japanese firms report being hurt by BOJ rate hikes.

New / Most Important: This is a structurally novel situation that deserves attention. The BOJ is hiking rates into a weak yen — the opposite of what conventional macro textbooks predict should happen. Normally a central bank hiking rates strengthens its currency; here, the yen keeps weakening because the rate differential with the US remains enormous and Japan's energy import bill is surging on Hormuz tensions. The BOJ paused bond buying, leaving the yen even more exposed to intervention risk. Meanwhile, the Takaichi government's fiscal expansionism is running directly counter to BOJ tightening, creating an incoherent policy mix that resembles early-1990s UK pre-ERM crisis dynamics. Japanese SMEs are getting crushed between rising domestic borrowing costs AND a weak currency inflating import costs — a genuine credit event risk if this persists through Q3. EUR/JPY pushing into the upper 185 range confirms the yen is weakening against everything, not just the dollar.

Playbook ✓: BOJ tightening into a structural current account deterioration driven by energy imports — textbook terms-of-trade trap.  |  ⚠️ Divergence: The fiscal-monetary contradiction between Takaichi and the BOJ is the divergence. If intervention comes and fails to hold, the next yen leg lower could be disorderly.
