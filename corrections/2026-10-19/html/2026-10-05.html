Macro Stance: The Fed held rates steady at its last meeting — the first decision since the Iran conflict spiked oil prices. Fiscal policy remains expansive, running structurally wide deficits that are now colliding with a term-premium repricing. The 10Y yield surged to 5.34% (+56 bps vs 4W ago), a level that headline data confirms is a 24-year high for the 30Y maturity, while the 2Y sits at 4.83% (prior close, +49 bps). The 10Y-2Y spread at +45 bps (prior close, little changed over four weeks) is positive but still flat by historical standards — this is no longer about inversion risk but about the whole curve repricing fiscal sustainability and persistent inflation.

New / Most Important: The Fed held rates last week despite oil-driven inflation pressures. Economists are now publicly divided on whether further hikes are coming this year. The 30-year yield hit a 24-year high. The jobs market was described as "still fine" by the FT. S&P 500 at 7769 (↑0.7% vs 4W) is edging toward records, but the Nasdaq 100 at 31005 (↑4.9%) is doing the heavy lifting — the Russell 2000 at 2852 (↓4.2%) is in outright retreat. This is a textbook narrow-breadth, liquidity-bifurcated market: mega-cap tech is absorbing capital while rate-sensitive small caps get crushed by the term-premium repricing.

Playbook ✓: AI investment continues to anchor growth and mega-cap outperformance; the Deloitte note confirmed AI keeps economic growth "in gear, for now." Equities near records with VIX at 15.52 (complacency regime) fits the late-cycle pattern of crowding into quality duration.  |  ⚠️ Divergence: The structural break is the 10Y at 5.34% co-existing with equities near all-time highs. This divergence is historically rare and unstable. The mechanism: fiscal dominance is forcing term premium wider, but AI-driven productivity expectations are suppressing equity risk premium for the mega-caps that dominate the index. This creates a two-tier economy — asset-heavy balance sheets (real estate, small business, regional banks) face a punitive cost of capital while tech balance sheets are net cash and self-funding. The Russell/Nasdaq ratio collapsing 8.7% in 4 weeks is the sharpest expression of this bifurcation. The danger is that the yield surge eventually breaks something in the rate-sensitive economy (housing, CRE, leveraged credit) while headline indices mask the stress. Watch credit spreads and regional bank earnings this month — if they confirm small-business lending contraction, the "soft landing" narrative is a mirage covering a two-speed economy.

Multiple former BOJ board members are now publicly calling the end of the reflation era and calling for rates to reach 1.5% by December. New / Most Important: This week's signal cluster is structurally important. Former board member Noguchi stated that "reflation policy has run its course" and sees a December rate hike. If the BOJ normalizes to 1.5% and Japanese institutional investors begin repatriating capital (selling foreign bonds to buy higher-yielding JGBs), the global liquidity consequences are enormous. Japanese life insurers and pension funds are the largest holders of US Treasuries outside the Fed — any marginal repatriation flow into a market already repricing term premium (UST 10Y at 5.34%) is an accelerant. The yen weakened post-NFP despite strong inflation data, suggesting FX markets haven't fully priced the hawkish shift. This creates a snap-back risk for yen shorts if the December hike materializes.

Playbook ✓: Yen weakness persisting as markets wait for actual policy action rather than jawboning.  |  ⚠️ Divergence: The speed and conviction of the hawkish rhetoric from former officials is ahead of market pricing. If the BOJ actually delivers 1.5% in December, the carry trade unwind could be the single largest cross-border capital flow event of 2026.
