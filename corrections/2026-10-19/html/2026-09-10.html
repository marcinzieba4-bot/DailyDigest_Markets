🇪🇺 European Union  Bearish  ↑ hiking

Macro Stance: The ECB hiked rates to 2.5% today, driven by energy-fueled inflation from the Iran conflict and oil above $100. The central bank warned of "longer-lasting" inflation and signaled this may be the start of a new rate-hike cycle rather than a one-off adjustment. Bond yields hit multi-year highs across the eurozone.

New / Most Important: This is a structurally pivotal moment. The ECB had been in an easing posture through much of 2025 and early 2026 — this hike represents a full U-turn in the policy cycle. The statement explicitly cited the Iran-related energy shock as fueling inflation while simultaneously warning of weaker growth. This is a stagflationary policy response: hiking into slowing growth because the central bank has lost confidence in the inflation anchor. European bond markets are repricing aggressively, with traders now bracing for a sustained hiking cycle. The cross-border capital flow implication is that European risk assets face a double squeeze — higher rates crushing multiples while energy costs crush margins. The euro may initially strengthen on rate differentials, but if growth deteriorates sharply, the currency won't hold. This is the worst macro setup for European equities since 2022.

Playbook ✓: Energy shock → inflation overshoot → forced tightening. Classic European vulnerability to exogenous supply shocks.  |  ⚠️ Divergence: The ECB hiking while acknowledging weaker growth is an explicit admission that it's choosing to fight inflation at the cost of recession. The last time this happened (2022-23), European equities massively underperformed. But the divergence this time is that the US may also hike simultaneously — there's no "dovish anchor" anywhere in the G4. Global liquidity is draining in unison, which is rare and dangerous.

🇯🇵 Japan  Bearish for JGBs / Bullish for JPY  ↑ hiking aggressively

Macro Stance: The BOJ is expected to raise rates to 1.25% this month and reach 1.75% faster than previously expected. BOJ policymakers are publicly warning of price risks that "may trigger rapid rate hikes." Japan spent $80B in foreign reserves defending the yen, and the yen is now near seven-month highs.

New / Most Important: This is a genuine regime change in global capital flows. The BOJ moving to 1.25% and signaling 1.75% ahead means the Japanese carry trade — the single largest source of leveraged cross-border liquidity for the past decade — is being systematically unwound. The $80B FX intervention signals desperation-level commitment to yen strength. The global flow implications are massive: Japanese institutional capital (GPIF, lifers, banks) will repatriate from US Treasuries and European bonds as domestic yields rise, removing a structural bid from global fixed income at precisely the moment everyone else is also selling bonds. This is the hidden accelerant behind the US 10Y surge to 4.92%. The yen carry unwind is not a one-day event — it's a multi-quarter structural flow reversal that drains liquidity from every risk asset that was funded in yen. Watch USD/JPY: if it breaks materially lower, it signals the carry unwind is entering its acute phase.

Playbook ✓: BOJ normalization → yen strength → carry unwind → global risk-off pressure. We've seen this movie before (July 2024).  |  ⚠️ Divergence: The speed of the BOJ's hawkish pivot is far faster than any model predicted. "Faster than expected" to 1.75% combined with US pressure suggests a coordinated approach to yen strengthening that has geopolitical dimensions beyond pure monetary policy.
