🇺🇸 United States  Mixed  ↑ rate hike risk rising

Macro Stance: The Fed is in a hawkish hold that is now tilting toward outright tightening. FOMC minutes released this past week showed officials saw a need for a rate hike if inflation doesn't cool. The 10Y yield is at 4.74% (+6 bps vs 4W ago) while the 30Y yield topped 5.31% — the highest in 19 years — signaling a structural repricing in term premium. The 2Y yield at 4.24% (prior close, −9 bps vs 4W ago) leaves a 10Y-2Y spread of +50 bps — positively sloped but still flat by historical standards — a configuration where the front end edges toward eventual cuts but the long end screams fiscal risk. Trump is publicly calling for lower rates even as inflation remains sticky. Kevin Warsh as Fed Chair is now firmly in the news cycle, and Trump reportedly made a multimillion-dollar bond bet whose outcome hinges on Warsh's appointment — a deeply unusual entanglement of personal financial interest and central bank governance.

New / Most Important: This week's FOMC minutes were the structural revelation: the Fed explicitly put a rate HIKE back on the table, a paradigm shift from the "higher for longer" framing that dominated H1 2026. Mohamed El-Erian flagged the 30Y at 5.27%+ as a structural shift making America permanently more expensive — this is the "Minsky Moment" thesis gaining institutional credibility (AEI published explicitly on this). The bond market forced the Trump administration to act again per PBS reporting, echoing the October 2023 dynamic. Meanwhile equities are shrugging it off: S&P at 7674 (↑3.5%), Nasdaq 100 at 29309 (↑4.2%), driven by narrow mega-cap leadership (Russell/Nasdaq ratio ↓1.1%). VIX at 15.13 (↓18.6%) is in complacency territory — the equity market and bond market are telling completely different stories.

Strong retail earnings buoyed markets mid-week after a 700-point Dow selloff.  |  ⚠️ Divergence: The structural divergence is the steepening yield curve combined with equity complacency — this is NOT normal. A steepening 10Y-2Y spread (+50 bps, 14 bps wider than four weeks ago) with the Fed threatening hikes typically precedes a credit event, not all-time equity highs. China's record July loan contraction and a 12.4% plunge in US housing starts confirm the bond market's message: real money is hedging tail risk even as VIX says nobody's hedging in equity-land. Gold at $4680 (↑15.1% in 4 weeks) is screaming that sovereign credit risk is being repriced globally, and yet the S&P grinds higher on liquidity flowing into a narrow set of names. This disconnect can persist — it did in 2007 for months — but when it breaks, it breaks violently. The US Treasury buyback program reportedly redirecting capital toward Asia adds another wrinkle: the government is trying to manage curve shape while simultaneously losing control of term premium.
