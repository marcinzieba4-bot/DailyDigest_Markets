🇯🇵 Japan  Bearish (bonds)  ↑ rates — September hike now base case

Macro Stance: The BOJ is in active tightening mode. Governor Ueda has signaled a September rate hike. The Summary of Opinions flagged upside price risks and a possible faster hiking pace. This is being actively supported by the US — Bessent's "nudge" firmed the case for a September move, and the US sold euros to prop up the yen.

New / Most Important: This is structurally one of the most important stories in global macro. The US is ACTIVELY SUPPORTING BOJ tightening by intervening in FX markets to strengthen the yen — selling euros, not dollars. This is a tripartite arrangement: the US provides yen floor support through euro sales, the BOJ hikes to attract carry back into yen, and Japan reciprocates by continuing to hold (or at least not aggressively sell) USTs. Rabobank warns a September hike may be insufficient to boost the yen, which means the coordinated approach may need to escalate. The yen is wobbling in thin holiday trading despite all this support, suggesting the structural carry-trade unwind has further to run. Rising JGB yields will continue to repatriate Japanese capital from global bond markets — this is the single largest marginal flow shift in the world and directly explains why US 10Y yields are rising even as risk appetite appears healthy.

Playbook ✓: BOJ normalization grinding forward as expected.  |  ⚠️ Divergence: The US selling euros (not dollars) to support the yen is an unprecedented policy construct — it means Washington views yen stability as a national security priority and is willing to sacrifice ECB policy space to achieve it.
