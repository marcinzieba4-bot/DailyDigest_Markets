Macro Stance: The BOJ held rates at 1% and upgraded its GDP forecast, but is signaling more rate hikes ahead as price pressures build. The yen is near 40-year lows against the dollar, creating a self-reinforcing inflation loop through import prices. The BOJ meeting on Friday is the next catalyst.

New / Most Important: This is a structurally important moment. The Nikkei tumbled 4.31% in a single session while the BOJ simultaneously signals more hikes — a classic policy tightening into equity weakness dynamic. The yen stuck near 160 despite hawkish BOJ signaling reveals that the US-Japan rate differential is still too wide for the yen to respond to domestic tightening alone. Early rate-rise bets are increasing per Nikkei Asia, and Japanese life insurers are rotating into yen-denominated products — this is a massive capital flow signal. When Japan's ~$4 trillion life insurance sector repatriates from foreign bonds into JGBs, it creates a structural bid for the yen and selling pressure on US Treasuries. This is one of the most important cross-border flow dynamics in global macro right now: BOJ hikes → life insurer repatriation → UST selling → higher US yields → stronger dollar → weaker yen → more BOJ pressure. A reflexive loop.

Playbook ✓: Weak yen + hawkish signaling is textbook for the BOJ normalization path.  |  ⚠️ Divergence: Nikkei down 4.31% suggests the equity market is no longer treating BOJ hikes as a positive normalization signal — it's becoming genuinely contractionary for risk assets. Friday's decision is binary risk.
