🌍 Geographic Macro Overview

🇺🇸 United States  Mixed  Fed on hold; bull steepening

Macro Stance: The first week of November flipped the US narrative from higher-for-longer to the Fed is done. The FOMC held rates and upgraded its assessment of growth [1] , while Powell hedged on whether tightening is over [2] and said rate cuts are not on the radar [3] . Labour data did the dovish work: payrolls rose by 150,000 in October, less than expected [4] , unemployment rose to 3.9% [5] , and the WSJ called it a sharp hiring slowdown [6] . This is a soft-landing relief trade, not an all-clear: the ISM manufacturing index sat at 46.7 [7] , so growth is softening just as the market stops fearing the Fed.

Rates & Curve: The 2Y closed at 4.93% and the 10Y at 4.67%, leaving the 10Y-2Y spread at -26 bps and the curve still inverted (US Treasury, prior close 2023-11-06). Over 20 observations the 2Y fell 15 bps from 5.08% and the 10Y 11 bps from 4.78%, a bull steepening with both down and the 2Y down more; over five observations the long end led, the 10Y down 21 bps against 10 bps for the 2Y. That is a term-premium unwind even though the Treasury said it would increase most auction sizes [8] , and it comes days after the WSJ was decoding an almost 5% 10-year yield [9] . The S&P 500 closed at 4,365.98 index points, up 4.78% over 5 sessions, and the VIX fell 24.61% to 14.89 index points (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2023-11-06); the DXY eased 0.85% over 5 sessions to 105.22 index points as traders bet the Fed was done [10] .

New / Most Important: The key development is the repricing of risk appetite. Bloomberg summarised the Street view that the Fed is done with rate hikes [11] , and the Los Angeles Times had Wall Street heading for its best week of 2023 [12] . The Russell 2000 gained 5.50% over 5 sessions and KRE 9.00%, which looks like a squeeze in the most rate-sensitive pockets (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2023-11-06). Yet the Monday tape faded: the Russell fell 1.29% and KRE 1.42% while the S&P 500 edged up 0.18%. Fortune reported that Stan Druckenmiller is really nervous about the economy and stocks [13] , whereas BlackRock's Rick Rieder warned against underestimating US resilience [14] .

Playbook ✓: Hold a moderate equity overweight tilted to quality duration (mega-cap software, utilities) rather than chasing small caps, and add Treasury duration on any back-up in the 2Y toward the 5.08% level of early October. Trigger to add risk: a second soft labour or inflation print that keeps the 2Y below 4.93% while the VIX stays below 17.70 index points, its level 20 sessions ago. What changes the call: a re-flattening led by the front end, with the 2Y back above 5.08%, or a VIX close above 17.70, either of which would mark the rally as a squeeze inside a tightening cycle.

🇪🇺 European Union  Bearish  ECB on hold; stagnation

Macro Stance: Europe is the clearest case of restrictive policy biting into growth. Euro-zone GDP was estimated to dip 0.1% in Q3 [15] , and the New York Times framed Europe's economy as faltering while the US grows strongly [16] . The ECB has stopped hiking: it kept rates on hold for the first time in over a year [17] , and Vujcic said the ECB is finished with rate hikes for now [18] . That is a supportive backdrop for duration but a poor one for cyclical earnings.

Activity & Inflation: Euro-zone inflation eased further than expected in October [19] , while the October PMI sat near a three-year low, stirring recession worries [20] , and banks tightened credit standards further [21] . Lagarde still says the ECB will get inflation down to its 2% target in 2025 [22] , which limits room for early cuts. Germany is the weak link: DW asks whether industry, the backbone of the economy, can be saved [23] , and Parkiet reported an unexpected fall in German retail sales [24] .

New / Most Important: Goldman Sachs warned that the Israel-Hamas war could have major implications for Europe's economy [25] . The equity market is looking through it: the Euro Stoxx 50 rose 3.24% over 5 sessions to 4,158.64 index points, yet it is up only 1.12% over 20 sessions and fell 0.38% on the last session (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2023-11-06). The UK shows the next stage of the cycle: the Bank of England held at 5.25% [26] while warning of recession risk [27] , and the UK manufacturing PMI showed an eighth straight month of decline [28] .

Playbook ✓: Underweight euro-area cyclicals against US quality; take European exposure through defensives and Bunds rather than the DAX, which is up just 0.05% over 20 sessions, while the CAC 40 is down 0.11%. Trigger to turn constructive: a PMI rebound from near three-year lows together with easier bank credit standards. What changes the call: a Middle East energy shock that revives inflation and forces the ECB back to a hawkish tone.

🇨🇳 China  Bearish  Targeted easing; credit strain

Macro Stance: China entered November with a demand problem that policy has not yet met at scale. Factory activity unexpectedly contracted in October as demand dropped [29] , and the Caixin survey confirmed the manufacturing slump [30] . The Economist's question, why firms are not going under when the economy is a mess [31] , captures the bind: forbearance keeps defaults low but also keeps capital trapped in weak balance sheets. Our stance is Bearish on the growth impulse, not on every Chinese asset, because the state is visibly centralising financial control: Vice Premier He Lifeng now heads the top financial policy bodies [32] .

PMIs & Activity: The October surveys undercut hopes of a durable recovery. The Independent tied the factory contraction to a faltering pandemic recovery [33] . Earlier data were less grim: September industrial profits extended gains helped by policy support [34] , and CGTN reported positive Q3 performance across the regions [35] . The economy is losing momentum rather than collapsing. Prices confirm the demand gap: consumer prices stalled and factory-gate deflation persisted in September [36] . Commodities feel it first; OilPrice.com noted China's economic woes weighing on oil [37] , consistent with Brent falling 3.37% over 20 sessions to 85.18 USD/bbl (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2023-11-06).

Credit Impulse: Credit transmission is the weakest link. Zee Business reported a spike in China's short-term money rates, with markets expecting more easing [38] , and Reuters found some Chinese institutions borrowing at a 50% rate as liquidity was squeezed [39] : funding is ample at the core and scarce at the edge. Banks will not fix that on their own; analysts quoted by the South China Morning Post see stable asset quality but persistent margin pressure [40] , which limits appetite to cut lending rates. The East Asia Forum's local government credit dilemma [41] is the structural constraint: the borrowers most willing to spend are least able to borrow. The Financial Times describes a breakdown of China's social contract [42] , and when households doubt that property and jobs will keep compounding, they save rather than borrow, so even cheaper credit meets weak demand.

Property: Property remains the drag. New home prices fell 0.3% in September [43] , Bloomberg said the decline accelerated in a blow to sentiment [44] , and a survey cited by Reuters had Golden Week home sales down 17% against 2022 [45] . Al Jazeera's reporting on crumbling, unfinished homes [46] explains why buyers stay away: presales fund construction, so every stalled project raises the perceived risk of the next purchase. Nippon.com asks whether China will repeat the Japanese experience of deflating asset bubbles [47] , the right question for anyone modelling a slow balance-sheet repair. The New York Times summed up the pivot as more semiconductors, less housing [48] . Beijing is steering credit toward industry rather than reflating real estate, which argues against a V-shaped recovery in steel and iron-ore demand.

Fiscal, Policy & Trade: Externally, Beijing is managing tension rather than escalating it. The White House says there is an agreement in principle for a Biden-Xi meeting [49] , aimed for November [50] , and Reuters reported China warming to US chipmaker Micron as tensions ease [51] . China still keeps leverage over supply chains after tightening exports of graphite, a key battery material [52] . On the capital account, Reuters reported that China lent $1.34 trln in 2000-2021, with the focus shifting from Belt and Road to rescue finance [53] , and Nikkei Asia examined the 40% drop in China's US Treasury holdings [54] . Bruegel's third-time-lucky look at the push to internationalise the renminbi [55] fits the same pattern: Beijing is building financial reach abroad while domestic demand stays weak.

Markets & Flows: Equities are trading the policy option rather than the data. The Hang Seng rose 1.71% on the last session and 3.22% over 5 sessions to 17,966.59 index points, while the Shanghai Composite gained 0.91% to 3,058.41 index points but remains down 1.24% over 20 sessions (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2023-11-06). Tencent added 3.30% on the session, yet Baidu's US ADR is down 18.85% over 20 sessions and ASHR 1.63%. Reuters described micro-caps shining in a flagging share market [56] , a sign of speculative rotation rather than broad buying, while lithium names Ganfeng and Tianqi suffered a sell-off amid a price crash [57] . A Chinese firm even launched stock indices to tap the rush into safe-haven gold [58] , and Moutai shares jumped after the first price increase for the spirit in six years [59] , proof that pricing power survives at the very top of the consumer market.

New / Most Important: The governance shift is the story to watch: He Lifeng's elevation [32] and an industrial-first plan [48] point to tighter central control of credit allocation. Corporate evidence is split. Xpeng and Li Auto delivered record numbers of cars in October [60] , and Qualcomm's results signalled an improving Chinese smartphone market [61] . But Yum China fell to a one-year low on disappointing results [62] , and the WSJ reported big Western brands being squeezed by Chinese belt-tightening [63] . Consumption is bifurcating toward value and toward national champions in autos and technology.

Playbook ✓: Stay underweight China beta (FXI, ASHR) and hold any exposure through Hong Kong-listed platforms with self-help, such as Tencent, rather than property-linked cyclicals. Trigger to upgrade: a sustained easing in interbank funding stress plus a Biden-Xi meeting that de-escalates chip controls. What changes the call to Bullish: an explicit central-government fiscal transfer to finish stalled housing. ⚠ Divergence: Hong Kong equities and EV deliveries are rising while factory surveys contract and home prices fall, so the market is front-running stimulus that has not been announced.

🇯🇵 Japan  Mixed  BOJ yield-curve control; weak yen

Macro Stance: Japan's policy mix remains unusually accommodative, and that is both its equity tailwind and its systemic risk. Minutes of the September BOJ meeting showed members saw no need for new yield-control tweaks [64] , yet the bank has already had to intervene as the 10-year JGB yield hit a fresh decade peak [65] . The Economist argues Japan poses a threat to the global financial system [66] , and BNP Paribas asks whether the yield curve is out of control [67] . The risk is a disorderly exit, not a lack of growth.

Activity & Currency: The weak yen is flowing straight into profits. Toyota posted record first-half profits and lifted its outlook on a weaker yen [68] , with Reuters crediting the weak yen and hybrid demand for the blowout [69] . Toyota rose 3.12% on the last session and 9.88% over 20 sessions to 2,846 JPY, and the Nikkei 225 gained 2.37% to 32,708.48 index points (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2023-11-06). Bloomberg reported that Japan stayed out of the currency market in October despite the yen's weakness [70] , while the East Asia Forum argues an undervalued yen poses problems for the BOJ [71] . Fiscal policy is leaning against the cost of living: the government compiled a $113 billion package to cushion inflation [72] , and MUFG raised its long-term deposit rate for the first time in 12 years [73] , a quiet sign that domestic rates are normalising from below.

Playbook ✓: Overweight Japanese exporters on a currency-hedged basis. EWJ is up 2.77% over 20 sessions against 5.26% for the Nikkei 225, a gap consistent with the currency drag on unhedged dollar investors, and Trustnet's to yen or not to yen [74] is exactly the hedging question. Trigger to trim: a BOJ signal that it will widen or abandon yield-curve control, which would lift the yen and compress exporter margins. What changes the call: another JGB sell-off that forces emergency intervention.

Fiscal policy is adding demand rather than restraining it, with millions of Australians eligible for $23b of November cost-of-living relief [83] . The ASX 200 rose 3.31% over 5 sessions to 6,997.40 index points, with CBA up 4.36% to 100.42 AUD (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2023-11-06).

Playbook ✓: Prefer deposit-rich banks such as CBA over housing-linked retailers until the decision is digested; do not chase iron ore, because Fortescue's 12.34% gain over 20 sessions already embeds China optimism despite the property downturn [84] . Trigger: a hike with hawkish guidance helps bank margins and hurts discretionary spending. What changes the call: a hold, signalling the RBA now prioritises mortgage stress over inflation, which would favour housing-linked equities and hurt bank margins.

🇵🇱 Poland  Bullish  Government handover; budget reset

Macro Stance: Poland is the strongest equity story among the six regions, but it is moving from the euphoria phase into the execution phase. The Conversation credits young, female voters with defeating the populists in the October 15 election [85] , and the market repriced governance risk at speed. The macro tailwind is real. Money.pl reported the GUS October inflation figures [86] , and Strefa Inwestorów described a strong drop in October CPI driven by base effects, with inflation expected to stay close to the current level until the end of 2023 [87] . Real wages keep rising; Bankier.pl called it the highest such growth since February 2022 [88] , which supports consumption into 2024. We stay Bullish, but the next leg depends on fiscal credibility rather than the election result.

Markets: The rerating has been violent. The iShares MSCI Poland ETF (EPOL) rose 17.94% over 20 sessions to 20.45 USD, and the Beta ETF WIG20TR tracker gained 16.69% to 39.58 PLN (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2023-11-06). Banks led, with mBank up 26.27%, Bank Pekao 21.07% and PKO BP 20.11% over 20 sessions, and state-controlled utilities joined in, Tauron up 21.14% and PGE 17.03%, consistent with investors pricing less political interference. Parkiet wrote that the WIG20 is again showing its better face [89] . On monetary policy the domestic debate has turned hawkish: Rzeczpospolita sees no road back to the world of cheap money [90] , and economist Marcin Mrowiec told the paper the inflation target is far on the horizon, sometimes seemingly beyond it [91] . NBP credibility is itself political: TVN24 quoted Glapiński speaking of success and apologising that it came now, before the elections [92] , and Rzeczpospolita's Krzysztof Adam Kowalczyk branded his early-October appearance an election broadcast [93] . A central bank seen as partisan has less room to ease into a fiscal expansion.

Fiscal & Politics: The fiscal inheritance is the biggest risk to the rally. Rzeczpospolita warns that the budget left by PiS may be even worse than thought [94] , and Money.pl reported economists calculating the real budget hole for 2024 at a sum it called staggering [95] . Interia flagged that PiS is leaving a surprise that could derail the new government's plans [96] , while a Tusk adviser discussed whether the opposition would freeze electricity prices in 2024 [97] . Politics adds delay: after the president's address, Money.pl wrote that Mateusz Morawiecki had been given time to concrete PiS into financial companies [98] , and Interia explained the constitutional procedure by which the new government will be chosen [99] . Big-ticket projects are now in question too; DW asks whether Donald Tusk will ground Poland's multibillion airport plans [100] . The finance minister said rating agencies kept Poland's ratings and outlooks stable [101] , the anchor that keeps this a fiscal debate rather than a funding crisis.

Corporate & Sectors: The micro picture is the most bullish part of the story. Business Insider Polska headlined over a billion zloty of profit at Dino Polska and said the company is not stopping [102] ; Dino will invest in three new distribution centres [103] and spend over 400 mln zł on projects that speed up store expansion [104] . The stock rose 23.69% over 20 sessions to 433.90 PLN (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2023-11-06). Competition is coming, as Wyborcza.biz reports the Eurocash group moving to compete with Dino and Żabka [105] . XTB, up 16.17% over 20 sessions, began paying interest on clients' uninvested cash [106] , a defensive move to retain balances. KGHM paid 1.3 billion zloty of corporate income tax to the state last year [107] , a reminder of what Warsaw stands to lose by squeezing the miner. Orlen, up 21.15% over 20 sessions to 66.15 PLN, faces scrutiny of what its pre-election fuel-price cut cost, which Subiektywnie o finansach has now put in numbers [108] . PKO BP will not take Poczta Polska's stake in Bank Pocztowy [109] , and the KNF said WIBOR will exist no longer than the end of 2027 [110] , a slow-burn benchmark reform that matters for mortgage-heavy lenders.

Geopolitics & Security: Security risk is rising in rhetoric rather than in market pricing. Putin ally Dmitry Medvedev called Poland a dangerous enemy and warned it could lose its statehood [111] , and a Russian court threatened Poland's Europol Gaz with a $1.57 bln fine over a Gazprom lawsuit [112] . The Economist describes Poland beefing up its armed forces [113] , Italian F-35s based at Malbork are guarding NATO's eastern flank [114] , and a Polish soldier shot a Syrian who had crossed from Belarus in what authorities called an unfortunate accident [115] . Interia asks whether Russia will resume nuclear tests after withdrawing from a key treaty [116] , a reminder that the threat on the eastern flank is not only verbal. Defence spending is fiscally sticky, one more reason the budget gap will not close quickly.

New / Most Important: The new tension is strong corporate delivery against an unresolved budget. Rzeczpospolita asks what the new government will do in the political game over the state budget [117] , and a heated TVN studio argument over Poland's finances [118] shows the issue has gone mainstream. Housing policy adds inflation risk: Bankier.pl quoted Buda saying money for the Safe Credit scheme will last until a 0% loan is introduced [119] , after Forbes reported that the 2% credit programme upended the housing market and pushed prices up [120] . Bank Pekao, meanwhile, will expand abroad on the EU single passport [121] . Subsidised credit and energy prices are the levers that will decide whether the rerating sticks.

Playbook ✓: Stay overweight Poland through EPOL or the WIG20TR tracker, but rotate from the biggest election winners (mBank, Pekao) toward self-funded growth such as Dino and LPP, which is up 14.43% over 20 sessions to 13,960 PLN (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2023-11-06). Trigger to add: a credible 2024 budget from the incoming coalition that addresses the hole economists have flagged [95] . What changes the call: a prolonged caretaker period under Morawiecki or an unfunded electricity-price freeze. ⚠ Divergence: equities are pricing a reform dividend while fiscal commentary points to a larger 2024 deficit; prices and public finances are telling opposite stories.
