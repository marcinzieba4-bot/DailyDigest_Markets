🇺🇸 United States  Bullish  Fed on hold; disinflation repricing

Macro Stance: October's inflation data moved the debate from how high rates go to how long they stay there. CNBC reported consumer prices were flat on the month with core CPI at a two-year low, and U.S. News put the annual rate at 3.2% as gasoline fell [1] [2] . Producer prices then slid 0.5%, the biggest drop since 2020, according to WNDU [3] . Reuters said the cooling boosted hopes the Fed is done hiking [4] . Demand is cooling rather than cracking: CFO Dive and El País reported October retail sales slipping after a hot third quarter, while Barron's argued the dip actually shows consumer resilience [5] [6] [7] . Falling inflation with positive but slower spending is the cleanest soft-landing mix of this cycle, and it lowers the discount rate on every risk asset. We are Bullish on U.S. risk.

Rates & Curve: The 2Y closed at 4.90% and the 10Y at 4.53%, leaving 10Y-2Y at -37 bps and 10Y-3M at -100 bps (US Treasury, prior close 2023-11-15). Over 20 observations the 2Y fell 29 bps from 5.19% and the 10Y fell 38 bps from 4.91%, a bull flattening that pushed the spread a further 9 bps deeper; the curve remains inverted. The five-observation tape is less friendly: the 10Y is up 4 bps since 2023-11-08 while the 2Y is down 3 bps, as the market digests a 30-year auction that Barron's said broke badly and Moody's shift of the U.S. credit outlook to negative [8] [9] . Volatility has collapsed: VIX closed at 14.18, down 26.22% over 20 sessions, and the dollar index at 104.39 is 2.05% lower (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2023-11-15), consistent with Reuters reporting the dollar sank on the inflation print [10] .

New / Most Important: The shutdown tail is shrinking: the BBC reported the House passed a stopgap with Democratic support, and ABC7 said the bill now goes to the Senate [11] [12] . That leaves data as the sole driver, and the risk is that disinflation mutates into a growth scare. The Economist warned a famous recession rule may soon trigger, after October payroll growth slowed and unemployment rose to 3.9% [13] [14] . Small caps are the tell: the Russell 2000 gained 5.08% over five sessions to 1,801.22 versus 2.74% for the S&P 500 at 4,502.88 (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2023-11-15), a move Yahoo Finance dissected under the headline that the index has been soaring [15] .

Playbook ✓: Action: overweight U.S. equities with a tilt to rate-sensitive cyclicals, banks and small caps, funded from energy. Trigger to add: another benign inflation print with the 10Y holding below its 4.91% level of 2023-10-18. What changes the call: a 2Y back above 5.19%, which would signal re-accelerating inflation, or a jump in unemployment that turns bull flattening into recession pricing. Fundstrat's Tom Lee argues the 'face-ripper' rally continues, and Michael Burry closed his winning market short before turning his fire on chip stocks — the best-known bear has stopped betting on broad downside [16] [17] .

🇨🇳 China  Mixed  Targeted easing; property drag persists

Macro Stance: China delivered a better October than feared, but not a turning point. CNBC reported retail sales and industrial output grew faster than expected, and Reuters added that property remained a drag [30] [31] . Consumer prices slipped back into decline, Reuters reported, a reminder that supply still exceeds demand [32] . Beijing's response is incremental and targeted — more central-bank liquidity and a housing-funding scheme under discussion — rather than a bazooka. We are Mixed: activity has stabilised enough to support cyclical exposure on dips, but deflation and housing leave no room for a durable re-rating story.

PMIs & Activity: The survey picture going into the hard data was weak: Capital Brief said the Caixin PMI confirmed an October manufacturing slump [33] . Trade shows the same split: the Macau Daily Times reported imports rising in October while exports fell for a sixth straight month [34] . The beat in factory output and consumption therefore looks domestically driven rather than a revival of external demand [31] . Singles Day underlined the consumer's hesitation: Reuters Breakingviews described a buyers' 'commitment issue', even as Statista showed the event dwarfing U.S. Cyber Week in scale [35] [36] . The SCMP's take on prices — that under the hood the situation is not as disastrous as headline deflation implies — is the constructive read we lean toward [37] .

Credit Impulse: Credit is being steered rather than unleashed. Reuters reported the PBOC boosted its liquidity injection through the medium-term policy loan while holding the rate unchanged — easing by quantity, not price [38] . The fastest credit growth is in the wrong place: Reuters said high-tech manufacturing loans are raising fears of a wave of cheap exports, and the WSJ reported trading partners worry China is making too much stuff [39] [40] . Loans to factories rather than households keep producer prices under pressure and push deflation abroad. Capital is also leaking, with the BBC reporting businesses pulling billions in profits out of China [41] . A genuine credit impulse needs household borrowing, and only a housing stabilisation can unlock it.

Property: Property remains the binding constraint. Macau Business reported property investment decreased in the first ten months, and CNBC estimated China's unfinished projects at 20 times the size of Country Garden [42] [43] . Country Garden's own sales remain at deflated levels amid collapsed demand, according to the SCMP [44] . The policy response is taking shape: Reuters, citing Bloomberg News, said Beijing is eyeing $137 billion of new funding to boost the housing market, and the SCMP laid out the 'three major projects' at the heart of the real-estate overhaul [45] [46] . Funding on that scale supports construction and completions, which is the right medicine for confidence, but it works slowly and does nothing for prices in the near term.

Fiscal, Policy & Trade: Fiscal policy is drifting looser. Bloomberg reported the 2024 budget deficit is seen above 3.5% in a break with past practice, and the World Economic Forum noted the IMF raised its China growth forecasts [47] [48] . On trade, the Biden–Xi meeting in San Francisco opened with both leaders pledging to reduce tensions, and Xi said the Earth is 'big enough' for both countries to succeed [49] [50] . Bloomberg reported Biden saying the U.S. wants better ties to help China's economy [51] . Détente lowers tail risk but leaves the export-control regime intact: CNBC reported Nvidia will sell new China chips that still meet U.S. rules, and Tencent said it holds an Nvidia chip stockpile while seeking domestic supply [52] [53] .

Markets & Flows: Hong Kong did the heavy lifting: the Hang Seng rose 3.92% to 18,079.00 in the last session, while the Shanghai Composite added only 0.55% to 3,072.83 (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2023-11-15). Tencent gained 4.81% to 322.60 HKD, Alibaba 5.05% to 83.20 HKD, and the KWEB internet ETF is up 8.17% over 20 sessions. Foreign money has not followed: the SCMP reported Bridgewater sold Xpeng, Yum and TSMC last quarter as hedge funds cut China bets, and a Reuters commentary said the Wall Street tortoise has overtaken the Chinese hare [54] [55] . The SMH even reported wealthy Chinese using total strangers to sneak cash out [56] . That positioning gap is precisely why short-covering rallies in Hong Kong internet names can be violent.

New / Most Important: Tencent's quarter is the key micro signal for the consumer-tech complex. Reuters reported robust revenue growth as games and advertising sales shone, and The Business of Fashion said profit beat estimates in defiance of the downturn [57] [58] . Advertising strength is a direct read on merchant confidence, which looks healthier than retail sales alone suggest. The caution comes from Baidu's CEO, who warned that China's rush to develop AI models risks wasting resources — a sign the domestic AI capex race is due for consolidation [59] . Tencent Music also beat revenue estimates despite a gambling-related hit, Reuters reported, so the platform beat is not a one-company story [60] .

Playbook ✓: Action: own Hong Kong-listed platforms (Tencent, Alibaba) and China-exposed iron ore producers tactically, funded from onshore A-shares, where the ASHR tracker has gained only 2.04% over 20 sessions (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2023-11-15). Trigger to add: confirmation that the $137 billion housing-funding plan is approved, alongside further policy-loan injections [61] . What changes the call: another month of falling consumer prices with weaker credit, or a breakdown of the post-summit détente. ⚠ Divergence: activity beat forecasts and Hong Kong equities rallied, yet property investment is still shrinking and prices are still falling — the market is pricing stimulus that Beijing has discussed but not delivered.
