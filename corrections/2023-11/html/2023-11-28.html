🇺🇸 United States  Mixed  Fed on hold; 2024 cuts priced early

Macro Stance: The US goes into the last days of November priced for a finished Fed. Reuters reports the dollar weakening as the rate-cut view weighs [1] , and risk assets have taken the hint: the S&P 500 closed at 4,550.43, up 10.52% over 20 sessions, the Nasdaq 100 at 15,961.98, up 12.56%, and VIX sits at 12.69 after a 40.34% 20-session collapse (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2023-11-27). We hold a Mixed stance rather than a Bullish one because the rally is running ahead of the data. The S&P 500 slipped 0.20% in the last session, a pause rather than a reversal, and housing is still frozen, with home sales at a 13-year low in October as the market's freeze deepened [2] . A market that has priced the end of tightening now needs growth to hold, not more rate relief.

Rates & Curve: The curve has done the Fed's easing for it. The 2Y closed at 4.84% and the 10Y at 4.39%, leaving 10Y−2Y at −45 bps (US Treasury, prior close 2023-11-27). Over 20 observations the 2Y fell 15 bps and the 10Y 45 bps, a bull flattening that deepened the inversion, while the 30Y dropped 50 bps and the 3M only 10 bps because the front end stays tied to a policy rate the latest Fed minutes say must stay high [3] . Momentum is fading: over five observations the declines shrank to 4 bps at the 2Y and 5 bps at the 10Y. Bank of America now argues cuts are coming next year [4] , but $7.6 trillion of US government debt maturing within a year is a supply wall that can rebuild term premium [5] .

New / Most Important: The holiday weekend delivered volume bought at a discount. CNBC reports Black Friday shoppers spent a record $9.8 billion in US online sales, up 7.5% from last year [6] , yet Reuters described a 'lot quieter' Black Friday that brought out discount hunters [7] . Pipeline prices point the same way: producer prices slid 0.5% in October, the biggest drop since 2020 [8] . Disinflation with resilient nominal spending is exactly the soft-landing mix the market is paying for; the bill arrives in fourth-quarter retail gross margins, where markdowns absorb the volume, and in the inflation data that will tell the Fed whether goods deflation is offsetting sticky services.

Playbook ✓: Stay long duration in the belly and add quality equities on pullbacks, but do not chase beta with VIX at 12.69 (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2023-11-27). Trigger to add: a 10Y close below the 4.39% prior close while unemployment holds near October's 3.9% [9] , which would confirm cooling without a break. What changes the call: a front-end backup that lifts the 2Y back toward the 4.99% it yielded 20 observations ago, which would mean cuts were over-discounted (US Treasury, prior close 2023-11-27). Hedge equity length with index volatility while it is cheap; Goldman's finding that hedge-fund bets on US stocks are the most crowded ever makes an unwind the asymmetric risk [10] .

🇪🇺 European Union  Bearish  ECB on hold; German fiscal brake bites

Macro Stance: Europe is the weak link in the developed-market soft landing. Reuters reports Germany's economy shrank slightly in the third quarter [11] , and Berlin froze government spending as its budget crisis deepened [12] . Equities have rallied anyway on global duration: the Euro Stoxx 50 closed at 4,354.41, up 8.09% over 20 sessions, and the DAX at 15,966.37, up 8.49%, while the FTSE 100 managed only 1.82% (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2023-11-27). We are Bearish because that rally rests on lower yields and a handful of quality compounders such as SAP, up 12.74%, rather than on broad earnings upgrades.

Activity & Inflation: Output is contracting while the ECB refuses to declare victory. Euro-area GDP dipped 0.1% in Q3 [13] , yet Lagarde warned against a premature inflation celebration [14] . The transmission channel to watch is credit: the ECB warns eurozone banks are starting to show stress as loan defaults rise [15] . Frozen German fiscal support plus unchanged policy rates is a stagnation mix, negative for cyclicals and supportive of core bonds, and it leaves the ECB reacting to weakness rather than pre-empting it.

New / Most Important: The most important new datapoint is corporate. Reuters reports Volkswagen will reduce headcount at a core VW brand it calls 'no longer competitive' [16] , Germany's cost problem compressed into one sentence. The steel sector warns that the German budget hole puts industry at risk [17] . Beneath the index, autos lag: the STOXX autos tracker fell 1.04% over five sessions while the DAX gained 0.41% (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2023-11-27), and LVMH's 2.84% five-session drop shows luxury no longer offsets industrial weakness.

Playbook ✓: Underweight European cyclicals and autos against US quality, and own core European duration as the hedge for an ECB that eventually capitulates. Trigger to cover: a German budget fix that unfreezes investment, confirmed by the Euro Stoxx 50 holding above 4,354.41 while autos stop underperforming. What changes the call: a services-inflation rebound that pushes Bund yields higher despite contraction, which would hurt the duration leg and the equity rally at the same time.

🇨🇳 China  Mixed  PBOC easing bias; targeted property and market support

Macro Stance: China is in a policy-supported stall. Beijing is using administrative and balance-sheet tools to stabilise markets and developers, but profits and prices still show demand too weak to sustain itself. Reuters reports industrial profits growth slowed, keeping stimulus calls alive [18] , and the People's Bank governor pledged to support growth while urging reforms [19] . Prices reflect the stalemate: the Shanghai Composite closed at 3,031.70, down 1.19% over five sessions and up only 0.34% over 20, and the Hang Seng at 17,525.06, up 0.68% over 20 (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2023-11-27). Mixed is the right stance: the policy floor is real, the demand catalyst is not yet visible.

PMIs & Activity: The October trade and price data set the tone. Exports fell for a sixth straight month even as imports rose [20] , and consumer prices slipped back into decline as the recovery wobbled [21] . Rising imports with falling exports is more consistent with commodity restocking than with a pickup in external demand, and negative consumer inflation erodes the nominal growth that services corporate and local-government debt. Profits are the cleaner read on activity, and their growth slowed in the latest month [18] , which matters more for capex intentions than the level. The next official manufacturing PMI is the test of whether factories are stabilising or whether the profit squeeze feeds into fewer orders and weaker hiring.

Credit Impulse: Credit is being steered rather than broadly expanded. The governor's pledge [19] points to more liquidity, but liquidity is not the binding constraint when private borrowers do not want to borrow. The shadow side is cracking: The Guardian reports a Chinese shadow bank admitting a £30bn shortfall after 'management ran wild' [22] , losses that land on wealthy savers and on the trust funding developers relied on. That is a negative credit impulse from outside the banking system which official lending has to offset before it adds anything. Copper's two-month peak on China demand hopes [23] has already faded, with the front-month contract at 3.76 USD/lb and down 1.31% over five sessions (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2023-11-27), so metals are not front-running a credit turn.

Property: Property remains the binding constraint, but the response is escalating. The Business Times reports Beijing weighing unprecedented builder support with first-ever unsecured loans [24] , and Shenzhen cut down payments on second homes as cities try to revive demand [25] . The two measures attack different problems. Down-payment cuts address buyers, but buyers hesitate when presales for unfinished homes carry completion risk; unsecured lending addresses that completion risk by funding developers on trust rather than collateral, a regime shift for Chinese banks. Until money moves, property stress keeps transmitting to households, to local governments that depend on land sales, and to the steel and copper demand that Australian miners price off. Watch whether the first allocations reach private developers or only state-backed ones.

Fiscal, Policy & Trade: The external climate is softening at the margin, and the currency is the clearest evidence. The yuan traded near a four-month high on firmer fixings and steadier China-US ties [26] , which gives the PBOC room to ease without importing a depreciation scare. Friction has moved into technology: Nvidia shares closed down on a report it delayed a China AI chip designed to comply with US export rules [27] , so the supply of advanced compute to Chinese platforms remains politically capped. On fiscal policy, the profit squeeze argues for central-government transfers rather than more local infrastructure, because local governments are themselves constrained by weak land revenue. A firmer yuan, lower US yields and a weaker dollar together loosen the external constraint on easing.

Markets & Flows: Parts of the equity market are now administered. Reuters reports the Beijing bourse told 'major shareholders' to refrain from selling [28] , a supply-side measure that removes one source of selling without creating buyers. Offshore wrappers still slipped in the latest session: ASHR fell 1.07%, FXI 1.29% and KWEB 1.89%, although KWEB kept a 5.78% 20-session gain (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2023-11-27). Leadership is idiosyncratic and earnings-led. Baidu's US line is up 14.71% over five sessions after its third-quarter call [29] , while Alibaba's Hong Kong shares are down 7.03% over 20 sessions and CNN reports Jack Ma backed off plans to sell shares after the stock plunge [30] . Tencent, at 322.80 HKD and up 9.28% over 20 sessions, remains the quality anchor, while Xiaomi's 6.43% five-session drop shows hardware is not sharing the rebound.

New / Most Important: The single most important change is the shift from verbal to balance-sheet support for developers, paired with explicit guidance to large shareholders not to sell [28] . Together they cap downside at the margin, but neither repairs household demand, which is why consumer prices keep falling. The test for investors is whether support reaches household balance sheets, through transfers or lower mortgage costs, rather than stopping at lenders and listed companies. Until it does, rallies in China beta are trading opportunities, not a new cycle.

Playbook ✓: Stay Mixed: own China through quality platforms such as Tencent rather than broad beta, and keep metals exposure light until property credit actually flows. Trigger to add: a confirmed unsecured-loan programme for builders [24] together with a Shanghai Composite close above 3,031.70 and a positive five-session change. What changes the call: another month of export contraction and consumer-price deflation, which would show policy chasing a weakening economy. ⚠ Divergence: administrative support and a firmer yuan [26] are lifting the floor, while consumer prices keep sliding [21] ; markets are pricing policy while the economy is still pricing deflation.

🇯🇵 Japan  Bullish  BOJ ultra-loose under an inflation comeback

Macro Stance: Japan is the one major market where inflation is a tailwind for equities rather than a threat. Reuters reports Japan's inflation comeback is prompting investors to tear up old playbooks [31] , and Oxford Economics expects 2024 wage rises to be as high as this year's [32] . Wage-led inflation lifts nominal sales for domestic companies after decades of deflation, while the Bank of Japan has shown little urgency to tighten: September minutes showed members saw no need for new yield-control tweaks [33] . The Nikkei 225 closed at 33,447.67, up 9.30% over 20 sessions but down 0.41% over five (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2023-11-27), which reads as consolidation rather than reversal.

Activity & Currency: The currency is the swing factor. LeapRate reports the dollar-yen pair traded sideways despite upbeat Japanese inflation data [34] , and MarketWatch describes the previously invincible US dollar sliding against the yen and the yuan [35] . A firmer yen shows up in exporter performance: Toyota fell 4.24% over five sessions to 2,770 JPY and Sony 2.10%, while chip-equipment maker Tokyo Electron is up 23.81% over 20 sessions and Advantest 13.55% (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2023-11-27), although Advantest's 4.09% five-session pullback shows even the winners consolidating. The split is the trade: global AI capex is lifting equipment makers whatever the yen does, while autos depend on a weak currency that the US rate cycle is starting to undermine.

Playbook ✓: Bullish with a domestic tilt: prefer chip equipment and beneficiaries of wage-driven demand over yen-sensitive exporters such as Toyota. TSMC weighing a next-generation chip fab in Japan [36] reinforces the semiconductor-capex leg. Trigger to add: a Nikkei 225 close back above 33,447.67 with the five-session change turning positive. What changes the call: a fast yen appreciation that forces exporter guidance cuts, or a yield-control adjustment that lifts JGB yields abruptly and drains the domestic reflation trade.

🇦🇺 Australia  Bearish  RBA hawkish after hike to 4.35%

Macro Stance: Australia is the developed market where the central bank is still tightening into a slowing consumer. The Reserve Bank lifted rates to a 12-year high of 4.35% [37] , and Governor Bullock now warns that inflation is 'homegrown' [38] . The ASX 200 closed at 6,987.60, down 1.00% over five sessions and 0.76% in the last (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2023-11-27), lagging the global rally. Banks dominate the index, and Commonwealth Bank's 6.64% 20-session gain looks stretched for a lender whose borrowers face higher repayments. We stay Bearish until domestic services inflation turns.

Demand & Financing: Demand is splitting. CommBank data show consumers cutting discretionary spending while leaving room for travel and entertainment [39] , the pattern of households protecting experiences while mortgage repayments rise. The RBA's own reasoning points at services: hairdressers, dentists and dining out were the price rises that told it to raise rates again [40] , and those prices respond to wages and rents, not to the exchange rate. Institutions are changing too, as Australia appointed the Bank of England's Andrew Hauser as deputy RBA governor [41] . Commonwealth Bank fell 1.04% in the last session to 102.61 AUD, and CSL's 11.11% 20-session gain shows the market hiding in global earners.

Playbook ✓: Underweight Australian banks and discretionary retail against the miners and global earners; mortgage stress plus sticky services inflation leaves little room for multiple expansion. Trigger to cover: a services-inflation print that lets the RBA signal a peak. What changes the call: Chinese property support moving from down-payment cuts [25] to developer credit, which would favour BHP and Fortescue, the latter down 3.12% over five sessions, over domestic cyclicals.

🇵🇱 Poland  Bullish  NBP on hold; handover to a Tusk coalition

Macro Stance: Poland combines the strongest cyclical momentum in the region with the loudest political noise. Bankier reports real wages rising at their fastest pace in years with nominal pay at a record [42] , and Super Biznes reports the first increase in consumption in nine months [43] . That is a classic real-income recovery: inflation falling faster than wage growth hands households purchasing power, and they have started to spend it. The blemish is inflation itself, as GUS revised October CPI slightly higher than first estimated [44] , which makes Governor Glapiński's declaration that Poland no longer has high inflation [45] look premature. A central bank that declares victory early while wages run at records is the main macro risk to an otherwise constructive picture. We are Bullish on Polish assets, selectively.

Markets: The market priced the political change early. The USD-listed EPOL ETF closed at 21.77 USD, up 12.80% over 20 sessions, while the PLN-denominated Beta ETF WIG20TR tracker closed at 40.02 PLN, up 5.01% over 20 sessions but down 1.84% over five (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2023-11-27). The gap between the two is the currency: Business Insider Polska writes that the zloty is shining globally and that forecasts give it more room [46] . Not everyone is convinced, as Bankier's currency forecast says analysts have not bought into an 'era of a strong zloty' [47] . For foreign investors the currency has delivered much of the return, which raises the bar for adding now. Domestically, banks led the latest pullback, with PKO BP down 4.19%, mBank 4.30% and Pekao 2.12% over five sessions, while utilities PGE and Tauron jumped 13.19% and 10.15%, a rotation from the winners of the election rally into names with policy optionality.

Fiscal & Politics: Monday's swearing-in of Mateusz Morawiecki's new cabinet was a constitutional formality. Notes From Poland calls it a likely temporary government [48] , and OKO.press says it was sworn in for two weeks and will fall after 14 days [49] . Gazeta Prawna reports the budget will be submitted by Donald Tusk's government [50] , which means next year's fiscal framework will be written quickly by the incoming coalition, and Business Insider Polska spells out how Tusk's flagship promise would raise pay [51] . The upside is EU money: OKO.press reports €5 billion from the recovery plan will flow despite unmet milestones [52] , cash that funds investment without adding to domestic borrowing. For bonds and the zloty, recovery-fund inflows offset the deficit risk of new spending promises; for equities, they support the construction, energy-transition and bank-lending pipeline those funds finance.

Corporate & Sectors: State-company governance is the equity story. PGE's nuclear venture with ZE PAK received a decision-in-principle for a KHNP plant in Wielkopolska [53] , and PGE rose 13.19% over five sessions to 8.15 PLN (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2023-11-27), a move that suggests investors now value utilities on investment plans rather than on political control. Orlen chartered a supertanker to buy Venezuelan crude [54] , diversifying supply, yet PKN is down 3.05% over 20 sessions, one of the few large caps lower over the month. The coal miner JSW lost 19.06% over five sessions to 41.20 PLN, a slide that dwarfs the WIG20TR tracker's 1.84% five-session dip. In banks, a Supreme Court ruling that a loan agreement is void because it relied on the bank's own FX tables [55] keeps the foreign-currency mortgage overhang alive for lenders such as mBank. Consumers lead instead: LPP is up 16.63%, Pepco 24.17% and Dino 11.38% over 20 sessions, while KGHM's 3.40% five-session drop tracks softer copper and Allegro, down 1.02% over five, has yet to join the consumer rally.

Geopolitics & Security: Security spending is structural. Rzeczpospolita reports Poland is building a 54 billion zł air shield that will protect, among other assets, its Patriot batteries [56] . Air defence is a priority any government is likely to keep, so the programme should survive the change of government and gives domestic industrials and engineering contractors a multi-year order floor. The near-term friction sits at the border: Reuters reports Polish truckers started a round-the-clock blockade of a fourth Ukrainian border crossing [57] , a dispute that hits logistics revenue and relations with Kyiv. The incoming government inherits both, and the blockade is its first test of resolving a trade dispute without alienating the domestic constituency behind it.

New / Most Important: The central bank has become a political story. Rzeczpospolita reports the NBP pays millions to Governor Glapiński's advisers and will not disclose their names [58] , and Business Insider Polska reports PZU chief executive Beata Kozłowska-Chyła is set to move to the NBP [59] . Appointments flowing from state companies into the central bank during a government handover raise the odds of an institutional confrontation with the incoming coalition. For markets, NBP independence anchors the zloty and bond yields; a public clash would be the one channel that could reverse the currency's strength, while PZU, down 3.66% over five sessions, now also faces a leadership change. A central bank seen as partisan has to keep rates higher for longer to buy the same currency stability, a cost borne by borrowers and bank valuations.

Playbook ✓: Overweight Poland through EPOL and domestic consumers such as LPP and Dino, plus utilities with policy tailwinds; stay underweight banks until foreign-currency mortgage rulings stop multiplying. Trigger to add: the WIG20TR tracker recovering its five-session loss from 40.02 PLN once a Tusk government wins its confidence vote. What changes the call: an open clash between the new government and the NBP that weakens the zloty, the risk Bankier's sceptics already flag [47] . ⚠ Divergence: the economy is accelerating, with record real wages [42] and rising consumption [43] , yet the banks that should monetise it are falling, with PKO BP down 4.19% over five sessions, and the central bank's credibility is under fire [58] .
