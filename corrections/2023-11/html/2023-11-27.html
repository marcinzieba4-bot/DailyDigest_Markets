🌍 Geographic Macro Overview

🇺🇸 United States  Mixed  Fed on hold, market pricing cuts

Macro Stance: Mixed. November has been a bond-led melt-up rather than an earnings upgrade: the S&P 500 closed at 4,559.34 index points, up 10.20% over 20 sessions and 1.13% over five, the Nasdaq 100 rose 13.27% and the VIX sank to 12.46, down 39.75% (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2023-11-24). The fuel is disinflation, with headline CPI cooling to 3.2% in October [1] . The cost is demand: Business Insider argues that 'the consumer slowdown has begun' [2] . That combination is ideal for bonds and tolerable for multiples but thin for earnings, so the rally's durability rests on yields staying down rather than on profits accelerating. Breadth is the encouraging tell, with the Russell 2000 up 9.08% over 20 sessions, close to the large-cap pace.

Rates & Curve: The 2Y closed at 4.92% and the 10Y at 4.47%, leaving 10Y-2Y at -45 bps and 10Y-3M at -107 bps (US Treasury, prior close 2023-11-24). Over 20 observations the 2Y fell 10 bps from 5.02% and the 10Y fell 39 bps from 4.86%, so the spread moved -29 bps: a bull flattening of an inverted curve, with the 30Y down 41 bps. The last five observations clawed part of that back, 2Y +9 bps and 10Y +2 bps, in a window that included minutes showing a Fed consensus that rates need to stay high [3] . Supply is the counterweight: $7.6 trillion of US government debt matures within a year, adding pressure on rates [4] . Long-end relief eases financial conditions, which is exactly what a 'higher for longer' Fed is inclined to lean against.

New / Most Important: The dollar is where the cut narrative is expressed most cleanly. InvestmentNews reports the dollar down further on rates speculation [5] , and DXY ended at 103.40 index points, down 3.00% over 20 sessions and 0.93% over five (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2023-11-24). Positioning is the risk multiplier: Goldman says hedge-fund bets on US stocks are the most crowded ever [6] . Black Friday sent a useful signal, with online sales at a record $9.8 billion, up 7.5% from last year [7] . Volume bought with heavy discounting supports disinflation and squeezes retail margins at the same time, which is the bond market's ideal outcome and only half of the equity market's.

Playbook ✓: Own duration and quality growth, funded from crowded cyclical beta rather than from new gross exposure. The trigger to add is a quiet front end: if the 2Y stops extending its +9 bps five-observation rebound while DXY holds below 103.40, the easing impulse is intact. What changes the call is a 10Y retracing toward the 4.86% it held 20 observations ago, the signal that the market front-ran a Fed that has not blessed cuts. With the VIX at 12.46, index puts are the cheapest insurance available against a crowded long book, and they pay in exactly the scenario where a hot inflation print forces bonds and equities to reprice together.

🇪🇺 European Union  Bearish  ECB on hold, German fiscal brake

Macro Stance: Bearish on the economy, neutral on the index. Germany's economy shrank slightly in Q3 [8] and Berlin froze government spending as its budget crisis deepened [9] , yet the DAX closed at 16,029.49 index points, up 9.14% over 20 sessions, and the Euro Stoxx 50 at 4,372.10, up 8.91% (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2023-11-24). European equities are trading the global rates rally, not domestic growth: the STOXX 600 rose 7.08% to 459.98 index points while BP fell 10.18% as oil slid, and the FTSE 100's 2.70% gain shows how little the commodity-heavy indices are participating.

Activity & Inflation: Activity is weak and broad: euro-zone GDP dipped 0.1% in Q3 [10] , and the German spending freeze removes the one engine that could have offset it. Fiscal space elsewhere is thin as well: Rzeczpospolita reports the IMF judging Italy, France and Spain too indebted [11] , so no other large economy can carry Germany. Lagarde nonetheless warned against premature inflation celebration [12] . Contracting output alongside a central bank warning against premature celebration is the classic setup for margin pressure: firms lose pricing power before they get cheaper money, and the adjustment falls on employment and capex in 2024.

New / Most Important: The ECB warns euro-zone banks are starting to show 'stress' as loan defaults rise [13] . Credit quality is the transmission channel from a fiscal squeeze to equities, and it is now visible in the central bank's own assessment rather than only in private surveys.

Playbook ✓: Underweight euro-zone cyclicals and domestic banks; prefer global earners such as SAP, up 12.85% over 20 sessions to 141.08 EUR, and Siemens, up 23.23% to 150.86 EUR (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2023-11-24). Trigger to move neutral: an ECB signal that cuts are discussable. A further rise in loan defaults, or a German budget fix that relies on tax increases, would deepen the underweight.

🇨🇳 China  Bearish  Incremental easing, no bazooka

Macro Stance: Bearish, with policy acting at the margin rather than at scale. The Shanghai Composite closed at 3,040.97 index points, up just 0.77% over 20 sessions, and the Hang Seng at 17,559.42, up 0.92% after a 1.96% drop in the last session (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2023-11-24), a fraction of the S&P 500's 10.20% over the same window. Global yield relief has not transmitted to China because the binding constraint is domestic balance sheets, not the dollar. When households and developers are deleveraging, lower US yields help the currency at the margin but do not create borrowers, and the equity market is pricing exactly that. Even the consumer-tech winners are being sold into strength, with Xiaomi down 5.90% over five sessions to 15.00 HKD.

PMIs & Activity: The Caixin survey confirmed an October manufacturing slump [14] , exports fell for a sixth straight month even as imports rose [15] , and consumer prices slipped back into decline [16] . Read together, these describe a demand problem rather than a supply problem: rising imports alongside falling exports say restocking is not the issue, while negative CPI says pricing power has gone. Falling prices raise real debt burdens for households and developers, which is why each easing step has bought a smaller equity response, and why investors treat every new measure as a floor-setting exercise rather than a turn.

Credit Impulse: Credit is flowing, but to the wrong end of the economy for a cyclical rebound. Reuters reports high-tech manufacturing loans are raising fears of a wave of cheap exports [17] : supply-side credit that exports deflation instead of creating domestic demand. The shadow system is cracking at the same time, with Zhongzhi admitting a £30bn shortfall after 'management ran wild' [18] . The combination matters beyond China's borders: Beijing is lending to factories while wealth-management savers take losses, so the impulse adds goods supply abroad and subtracts consumption at home. Until household and developer credit turn, the signal is neutral-to-negative for metals and commodity currencies.

Property: The direction is clear; the scale is not. China is weighing unprecedented builder support with first-ever unsecured loans [19] , and Shenzhen cut down payments on second homes as cities try to revive demand [20] . Yet more Chinese cities recorded falling home prices in October [21] . Unsecured lending matters because it moves banks from collateral-based to policy-based credit, effectively a quasi-fiscal transfer to developers. The test is whether the money reaches private builders; if it stays with state-linked names, land sales will not recover, local-government revenue stays squeezed, and falling prices keep feeding household caution. City-by-city demand easing has so far moved sentiment for weeks, not prices for quarters.

Fiscal, Policy & Trade: The technology front stays hostile: CNBC reports Nvidia delayed a China AI chip designed to comply with US export rules [22] . That delay is a two-sided signal. Export controls are binding on the most valuable compute, which pushes Chinese buyers toward domestic substitutes, and it leaves US chipmakers' China revenue at the mercy of licensing rather than demand. Fiscal policy, meanwhile, is being delivered through the banking system rather than the central budget, which is why the unsecured builder-loan proposal [19] matters more than any headline deficit number: Beijing prefers quasi-fiscal credit that does not show up as central government debt. When credit builds factories rather than homes, the excess capacity surfaces as exports and trade friction, so export-control and trade-defence disputes are likely to intensify rather than fade.

Markets & Flows: Offshore internet is decoupling from the onshore tape. KWEB closed at 28.56 USD, up 8.59% over 20 sessions, and Tencent at 321.20 HKD, up 9.70%, while Alibaba's Hong Kong line fell 7.52% to 76.20 HKD and the ASHR onshore tracker gained only 2.73% (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2023-11-24). Alibaba's slide was steep enough that Jack Ma backed off plans to sell shares [23] , a founder signal that caps near-term stock supply but does not repair the restructuring narrative. The yuan traded near a four-month high on a firmer fixing and steadier China-US ties [24] , which flatters foreign holders' returns but tightens conditions for exporters already facing falling shipments. PDD's ADR rose 10.34% over 20 sessions to 118.70 USD and JD's 10.96% to 28.76 USD, so value-oriented platforms are winning the domestic trade-down much as off-price retailers are in the US. Platform cash flows, not policy beta, are what foreign money is buying.

New / Most Important: The builder-loan proposal is the one item that could change the property feedback loop [19] ; everything else is incremental. Autos are the bright exception, with BYD selling more than 300,000 electric cars in a month [25] . Zeekr revealed a revenue surge in updated financials ahead of a US IPO [26] , so Chinese EV makers now seek dollar capital as well as foreign customers. China's growth is increasingly exported through vehicles, which is a problem for European autos rather than a solution for Chinese domestic demand.

Playbook ✓: Stay underweight onshore A-shares and property-linked credit; keep selective exposure to platform internet through KWEB and Tencent, whose cash flows do not depend on land sales. Upgrade if unsecured builder loans are actually extended to private developers and monthly home prices stabilise. Downgrade further if another shadow lender follows Zhongzhi into a public shortfall. ⚠ Divergence: the yuan near a four-month high [24] and copper at a two-month peak on China demand hopes [27] are pricing a recovery that the PMI, CPI and home-price data do not yet show.

🇯🇵 Japan  Mixed  BOJ normalisation on watch

Macro Stance: Mixed. The Nikkei 225 closed at 33,625.53 index points, up 7.53% over 20 sessions (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2023-11-24), and inflation is behaving like a regime change: Reuters reports Japan's inflation comeback is prompting investors to tear up old playbooks [28] , and the latest national print was described as upbeat even though the yen barely moved [29] . That non-reaction is the key observation. It says currency traders do not yet believe the BOJ will translate higher inflation into higher rates, which leaves a large policy surprise priced close to zero.

Activity & Currency: Pimco is buying yen to prepare for tighter BOJ policy [30] , an early institutional bet against a consensus that still treats the yen as a funding currency. Fiscal policy is cushioning households with a $113 billion package [31] , which supports consumption but adds to the inflation impulse the BOJ is reluctant to acknowledge. Exporters have priced the weak yen: Toyota closed at 2,803.00 JPY, up 6.17% over 20 sessions, while Sony gained only 1.98% to 12,850.00 JPY and SoftBank fell 1.25% (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2023-11-24). The dispersion says the market is paying for currency translation and chips, not for broad domestic demand, and even the chip names are volatile: Advantest fell 3.78% over five sessions to 4,532.00 JPY in a week when Nvidia's shares also slipped.

Playbook ✓: Prefer semiconductor equipment and domestic reflation over pure yen-sensitive exporters. Tokyo Electron, up 17.87% over 20 sessions to 24,005.00 JPY (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2023-11-24), benefits from TSMC weighing a next-generation fab in Japan [32] whatever the currency does. Trigger: BOJ language that loosens yield-curve control would favour banks and domestic demand and argue for trimming Toyota after its run; a renewed dollar bid would extend the exporter trade. What changes the call is an inflation print that rolls over, which would push normalisation back and revive the weak-yen exporter trade.

🇦🇺 Australia  Mixed  RBA tightening bias

Macro Stance: Mixed: a hawkish central bank against a softening consumer. The RBA hiked again this month [33] , and Governor Bullock has since called inflation 'increasingly homegrown', suggesting rates will stay high [34] . The ASX 200 closed at 7,040.80 index points, up 3.13% over 20 sessions but down 0.12% over five (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2023-11-24), a clear laggard to global equities. Domestic tightening is doing what global easing cannot undo: the local discount rate is rising while offshore yields fall.

Demand & Financing: CommBank data show consumers cutting discretionary spending while leaving room for travel and entertainment [35] , a pattern of services resilience that keeps the 'homegrown' inflation argument alive. Treasurer Chalmers is moving to axe his power to override RBA rate calls [36] , which removes a tail risk of political interference in the rate path. Fortescue's 12.49% 20-session gain to 25.03 AUD and Rio Tinto's 7.70% contrast with a 7.02% fall at Lynas, so investors are paying for Chinese steel demand rather than for the critical-minerals theme.

Playbook ✓: Favour resources over rate-sensitive domestic cyclicals; CBA's 6.02% 20-session gain to 103.69 AUD (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2023-11-24) already discounts a soft landing. Trigger to turn constructive on domestic demand: Bullock retreating from the 'homegrown' framing. Risk: China's builder support stalls and removes the iron-ore leg that is propping up the index. EWA, up 8.93% over 20 sessions to 22.45 USD (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2023-11-24), shows dollar-based holders captured more of the move than local investors, with the currency doing part of the work.

🇵🇱 Poland  Bullish  Power handover, EU funds unlocking

Macro Stance: Bullish. Poland is the rare economy where growth, real incomes and external funding are turning up together. GDP grew for the first time in two quarters, an economy 'bouncing off the bottom' in 300Gospodarka's phrase [38] ; Bankier.pl reports real wages rising at the fastest pace in years with nominal pay at a record [39] ; and Super Biznes notes the first rise in consumption in nine months [40] . Inflation is the wrinkle: GUS revised October CPI slightly higher than its flash estimate [41] , so the NBP has less room than the political debate assumes. Real-income growth plus EU funds is a consumption-and-capex mix that domestic equities are only partly pricing, and it favours banks and utilities over exporters facing a strong currency.

Markets: Foreign money has voted first. EPOL closed at 21.78 USD, up 11.75% over 20 sessions, while the Beta ETF WIG20TR tracker rose 5.04% to 40.35 PLN (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2023-11-24); much of the gap reflects the stronger złoty in dollar terms. Business Insider Polska writes that the Polish currency is shining globally and that forecasts leave the złoty room to run further [42] . Banks led the domestic re-rating, with PKO BP at 46.52 PLN, up 9.07% over 20 sessions, Pekao at 137.70 PLN, up 7.92%, and mBank at 555.20 PLN, up 6.97%, though PKO slipped 2.43% over five sessions as the political handover approached. KGHM, at 116.30 PLN and up 3.93%, has lagged the banks despite copper's firmer tone, a sign that the strong złoty is taxing exporters' translated earnings. The market is paying for domestic rate-sensitive franchises, not for commodity exposure.

Fiscal & Politics: The transition is messy but investor-friendly in direction. Gazeta Prawna reports the Morawiecki government will be sworn in on Monday, while the budget will already be tabled by a Tusk government [43] . A cabinet built to last two weeks cannot change the fiscal path, so the first real budget signal will come from the incoming coalition. Money is moving ahead of the politics: the European Commission accepted the amended recovery plan (KPO), with energy the biggest winner [44] , and OKO.press reports €5 billion will flow despite unmet milestones [45] . EU transfers arriving while wage growth is strong is a classic setup for domestic demand and a firmer currency, provided the new government does not answer it with a large unfunded spending programme. Sequencing matters for equities: EU money reaches energy and grid projects first, which is why the utilities have moved before the consumer names.

Corporate & Sectors: Power utilities are the clearest winners of the KPO shift and of management change at state firms. PGE jumped 6.36% in the last session to 7.69 PLN and Tauron 5.66% to 3.94 PLN (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2023-11-24), after Business Insider Polska reported a change at the top of PGE, with a Ziobro ally departing [46] , and PGE PAK received a decision-in-principle for its nuclear plant [47] . The market is pricing de-politicised management and EU-funded grid investment together. The flip side of state ownership is JSW, down 17.42% over five sessions to 41.10 PLN, matching Business Insider Polska's report of a state company's share-price collapse amid big wage rises and an extra tax [48] ; Orlen, up 3.12% over five sessions to 64.20 PLN, sits between the two. Among consumer names, LPP is up 17.83% over 20 sessions to 15,000.00 PLN, and Pepco rose 15.23% over five sessions although brokers cut its target price by more than half [49] , a move that looks driven by positioning rather than by a fundamental re-rating. Dino, up 11.00% to 442.10 PLN, and Allegro, up 2.21% to 29.64 PLN, split the consumer trade between physical grocery and e-commerce, while Orange Polska, at 7.84 PLN and up 1.71% over 20 sessions, shows the defensive telecom left behind by the cyclical rotation.

Geopolitics & Security: Security risk is elevated but priced as background noise. Poland charged 16 people after dismantling a Russian spy ring [50] , and BBC Monitoring says the network sought to disrupt arms supplies to Ukraine [51] , a reminder that Poland's role in that supply chain makes it a target. For markets the channels are twofold: defence spending, which supports domestic industrial and construction activity, and the risk premium on the złoty, which a hybrid incident on the eastern border could widen quickly. Neither channel is under stress today; the currency's run says investors treat security as a fiscal cost rather than a tail risk. European defence equities price the same theme, with Rheinmetall at 283.40 EUR, up 6.50% over 20 sessions.

New / Most Important: The NBP-government conflict is the key new risk. TVN's Fakty reports Glapiński could face the State Tribunal over bond purchases [52] . A fight over central-bank independence is the one development that could reverse the złoty's run, because it would attach a political premium to the very rate path that is currently supporting the currency and the banks. Tolerance so far rests on the dispute being legal and political rather than monetary; nothing in the NBP's conduct has yet changed the rate outlook. If the conflict begins to influence monetary decisions, foreign holders of Polish bonds and bank shares would demand a premium quickly.

Playbook ✓: Overweight Polish equities in dollar terms via EPOL, tilted to banks and power utilities; avoid state-controlled names exposed to wage settlements and extra taxes, such as JSW. Trigger to add: the first Tusk budget confirming KPO-funded energy investment. What changes the call: formal State Tribunal proceedings against the NBP governor, or KPO payments slipping. ⚠ Divergence: the złoty is pricing a clean institutional reset [42] , while the NBP-government confrontation and the Supreme Court's ruling voiding a loan contract over a bank's FX tables [53] say legal and institutional risk for banks is rising, not falling.
