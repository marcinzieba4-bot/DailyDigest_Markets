🌍 Geographic Macro Overview

🇺🇸 United States  Mixed  Fed on hold; disinflation priced, consumer cracking

Macro Stance: The United States reaches Friday with its cleanest disinflation signal of the cycle and the first visible crack in the consumer, a mix that favours bonds over cyclicals. October consumer prices were flat on the month and core inflation hit a two-year low [1] , while producer prices slid 0.5% in October, the biggest drop since 2020 [2] . Reuters framed the release as boosting hopes that the Fed is done with rate hikes [3] . Demand is softening in step: retail sales slipped in October as consumers pulled back after summer splurges [4] , and Walmart sounded a note of caution on consumers ahead of the holidays [5] . That is the soft-landing script, but it is now priced with little margin for a growth scare.

Rates & Curve: The 2Y yields 4.83% and the 10Y 4.45%, leaving the 10Y-2Y spread at -38 bps (US Treasury, prior close 2023-11-16). Across 20 observations the 2Y has fallen 31 bps from 5.14% and the 10Y 53 bps from 4.98%; the curve remains inverted and the 20-observation move is a bull flattening, with both yields down and the 10Y down more. Inside that, the last five observations tilted the other way: the 2Y fell 20 bps against 17 bps for the 10Y, the first sign that the front end is pricing an end to hikes rather than only a term-premium unwind. The 10Y-3M spread at -106 bps keeps the classic recession signal deep, and the Economist notes a famous rule that says America may soon be in recession [6] .

New / Most Important: Equities have already banked the pivot. The S&P 500 closed at 4,508.24 points, +3.70% over five sessions and +5.38% over 20 sessions, the VIX sits at 14.32 points after a 33.08% drop over 20 sessions, and the DXY at 104.37 points is down 1.45% over five sessions (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2023-11-16). Reuters reported the dollar sank after data showed US inflation cooling [7] , before it steadied as later data muddied Fed expectations [8] . Small caps carry the tell: the Russell 2000 fell 1.52% on the session after a 5.13% five-session burst, and FOREX.com describes it as having second thoughts on Fed rate cuts [9] . Fiscal tail risk eased only partly, as the Senate passed a stopgap funding bill to avert a shutdown [10] after Moody's changed the US credit-rating outlook to negative [11] .

Playbook ✓: Action: keep duration overweight in the belly, where the 5Y yields 4.43%, and favour quality large caps over the Russell 2000 while the consumer softens; IEF has gained 3.96% over 20 sessions (US Treasury, prior close 2023-11-16; Yahoo Finance, prior close 2023-11-16). Add on a consumer print weak enough to extend the 2Y decline without lifting the 10Y-3M spread out of deep inversion. Change the call if the 2Y climbs back toward 5.14%, its level 20 observations ago, or if the VIX returns to 21.40 points, where it stood 20 sessions back; either would mean the market has stopped trusting the disinflation path.

🇪🇺 European Union  Bearish  ECB on hold; German fiscal shock

Macro Stance: Europe is the weak link in the soft-landing story. Brussels cut its EU growth forecast again to just 0.6% for this year [12] , and euro area employment rose even as GDP shrank [13] , a labour-hoarding stagnation that keeps services costs sticky while output contracts. We are Bearish on euro-area domestic cyclicals, and the new fiscal shock in Germany reinforces that call.

Activity & Inflation: Eurozone GDP decreased by 0.1% q/q in Q3 [14] , the October PMI sat near a three-year low and stirred recession worries [15] , and euro-zone banks tightened credit standards further [16] . Inflation is easing more slowly: Eurostat says energy price rises are slowing [17] , while Lagarde argues that sustaining the ECB rate at 4% should help on prices [18] , a message that rules out early relief for borrowers. The periphery is outrunning the core: Brussels says Spain will be the fastest-growing economy of the EU's big four over the next two years [19] .

New / Most Important: The shock of the week is fiscal. Germany's constitutional court dealt a 60 billion euro budget blow to the Scholz government [20] by annulling the move to repurpose COVID funding [21] , just as German housebuilding is collapsing [22] . Markets are discriminating rather than panicking: the Euro Stoxx 50 closed at 4,302.45 points, -0.30% on the session but +5.19% over 20 sessions, the DAX added 0.24%, and the FTSE 100 lost 1.01% on the session and 1.18% over 20 sessions (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2023-11-16). Siemens jumped 5.70% after beating earnings forecasts while warning that sales growth will slow in 2024 [23] , while Burberry joined LVMH and Gucci in the luxury slump [24] .

Playbook ✓: Action: underweight euro-area domestic cyclicals and fund global industrial quality, where order books rather than German demand drive earnings. Trigger to cover: a credible plan to replace the climate funds that the court ruling forces Berlin to reshuffle [25] . Change the call if the PMI rebounds from its near three-year low while bank lending standards stop tightening, which would signal that the stagnation is ending rather than deepening.

🇨🇳 China  Mixed  PBoC liquidity via policy loans, rates unchanged

Macro Stance: China's macro data surprised to the upside just as its equity market was hit by a policy shock from Washington, and that split defines our Mixed stance. Retail sales and industrial output grew faster than expected in October [26] , yet Reuters stresses that property is still a drag on the economy [27] . NPR's read of the summit is that China's weakened economy shaped Beijing's posture in the talks with Biden [28] . Activity is stabilising at a low level; it is not yet an upturn that earns a re-rating while the property and chip-access discounts persist.

PMIs & Activity: Survey data are weaker than the hard data. The Caixin PMI confirmed October's manufacturing slump [29] , and exports fell for a sixth straight month even as imports rose [30] . Consumer prices are back in decline as the recovery wobbles [31] , which tells us demand, not supply, is the binding constraint. Singles Day reinforced the point: Breakingviews saw a buyers' commitment issue [32] even though Statista says the event still blows away US Cyber Week [33] . Better retail sales are volume bought with discounts, and the pricing power that drives margins is missing; even Japanese beauty stocks report China-slowdown and boycott woes [34] .

Credit Impulse: The PBoC chose quantity over price: it boosted its liquidity injection through a policy loan and left the rate unchanged [35] . Where credit flows matters more than how much. Reuters reports that high-tech manufacturing loans raise fears of a wave of cheap exports [36] , and the WSJ describes China as making too much stuff while other countries worry [37] . Credit aimed at capacity rather than households supports industrial output and depresses prices, consistent with the October data and with FreightWaves' view that stimulus is missing in action while risks rise [38] . Our test for a genuine impulse is household borrowing, not policy-bank lending.

Property: Property is still subtracting. Property investment decreased in the first 10 months [39] , more cities recorded falling home prices in October [40] , and CNBC reports that unfinished projects are 20 times the size of Country Garden [41] , whose own sales remain at a deflated level [42] . Credit stress keeps spreading: Moody's downgraded Longfor to below investment grade [43] . The policy answer is getting larger but remains a proposal: Beijing is eyeing $137 bln of new funding to boost the housing market [44] , tied to the 'three major projects' of its real estate overhaul [45] . Even constructive observers hedge, with the Macau Daily Times seeing sparks of life despite persisting weakness in troubled real estate [46] .

Fiscal, Policy & Trade: The San Francisco summit lowered the temperature without removing the technology wall. Reuters reports that blunt talks yielded deals on military and fentanyl issues [47] , and Washington removed sanctions on a Chinese institute in a push for fentanyl help [48] , but Biden then called Xi a dictator [49] and Beijing called the rhetoric extremely wrong [50] . Corporate America still wants access, with Tim Cook, Elon Musk and Steve Schwarzman spotted at Xi's dinner with US CEOs [51] . At home, China pledged to attract more foreign investment and boost consumption [52] , yet the BBC reports that businesses are pulling billions in profits out of the country [53] . On trade, Beijing keeps leverage over the battery chain, having tightened exports of graphite, where it is the top producer [54] .

Markets & Flows: Price action treats the summit as a disappointment for Chinese technology. The Shanghai Composite closed at 3,050.93 points, -0.71% on the session, and the Hang Seng at 17,832.82 points, -1.36%; in New York, FXI fell 4.08%, KWEB 4.07%, the Alibaba ADR 9.14% and NIO 6.80%, Xiaomi lost 6.55% in Hong Kong, and only JD.com is still +9.05% over five sessions (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2023-11-16). NextBigFuture tied the slide in China technology and electric-car stocks to the Biden-Xi meeting [55] . Positioning was already light: Bridgewater sold Xpeng, Yum and TSMC last quarter as hedge funds cut China bets [56] . The exception is Tencent, +5.87% over five sessions, after robust revenue growth as games and advertising sales shone [57] .

New / Most Important: The most important headline is Alibaba scrapping its cloud business spin-off, citing the US chip export ban [58] . That turns export controls from a sector risk into a corporate-structure risk: the sum-of-the-parts unlock behind the bull case is gone, and CNN tied the share drop directly to the chip war [59] . The ADR's 9.14% drop contrasts with the Hong Kong line's 81.35 HKD close, -2.22% (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2023-11-16), a gap the Asian session still has to resolve. Tencent says it holds an Nvidia AI chip stockpile but is seeking to buy domestic [60] , and TrendForce sees China advancing in-house AI chip development despite US controls [61] ; SMIC's 80% drop in third-quarter profit [62] shows that substitution is costly, and Baidu's CEO warns that the rush to develop AI models risks wasting resources [63] .

Playbook ✓: Action: stay underweight China internet platforms that depend on US silicon and keep exposure through cash-generative domestic leaders such as Tencent, whose Hong Kong shares closed at 324.80 HKD (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2023-11-16). Trigger to add: the $137 billion housing package moving from consideration to disbursement [64] , which would validate iron ore's resilience on property hopes [65] . Change the call if exports fall again while the PBoC keeps easing only through quantity. ⚠ Divergence: hard activity data beat forecasts and industrial output is rising, yet consumer prices are falling and offshore equities sold off hard; the data say stabilisation, while prices say the policy and technology discount is widening.

🇯🇵 Japan  Mixed  BOJ easy; intervention watch

Macro Stance: Japan is a currency story wrapped around a weak domestic economy. CNBC reports the economy shrank far more than expected in the third quarter [66] , and Inshorts notes it was the largest contraction in 2 years after GDP had expanded 4.8% in April-June 2023 [67] . External demand is the offset, as exports grew better than expected while auto shipments climbed [68] . Labour scarcity is the structural theme, as taxi operators suffer from a lack of drivers [69] and Uber's CEO sees huge potential in Japanese ride-sharing [70] . We are Mixed: earnings are being carried by a weak yen and global capex, not by households.

Activity & Currency: The yen's slide to multi-decade lows keeps markets on intervention alert [71] , and CNBC reported the dollar slipped after hitting a one-year high against the yen [72] , the first sign that falling US yields can do what the BOJ has not. Policy is still passive: September minutes showed BOJ members saw no need for new yield-control tweaks [73] , while Tokyo compiled a $113 billion package to cushion inflation [74] . Deutsche Bank calls Japan's government debt a $20 trln carry trade [75] , which is why any exit from easy policy will be slow. Equities mirror the export mix: the Nikkei 225 closed at 33,424.41 points, -0.28% on the session and +4.31% over 20 sessions, Tokyo Electron is +13.77% over 20 sessions and Advantest +10.54% over five, while SoftBank Group is down 5.24% over 20 sessions (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2023-11-16) after another Vision Fund loss [76] .

Playbook ✓: Action: hold semiconductor-equipment and auto exporters, with Toyota +7.87% over 20 sessions (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2023-11-16), but hedge the currency leg, because the pivot narrative in US rates is the likeliest trigger for yen strength. Trigger: confirmed intervention or a BOJ signal on yield control would rotate us toward domestic banks. Change the call if export momentum fades while the yen strengthens, which would remove both supports at once.

Trigger: a soft next spending print that removes the hike bias would favour banks again. Within miners, watch capital allocation: Fortescue is setting up an investment platform to fund green energy projects [86] , which could dilute the iron-ore cash return. Change the call if China's housing funding disappoints, because iron ore's resilience is what is holding the index together.

Labour is tight, with Czechia and Poland posting the lowest unemployment levels [92] , which keeps wage-driven consumption intact. We are Bullish on Polish assets, with the NBP as the main source of volatility.

Markets: The zloty is doing the heavy lifting. Fiscal & Politics: The transfer of power is now procedural rather than uncertain. Prime Minister Mateusz Morawiecki submitted his government's resignation [100] , the opposition parties signed a coalition agreement [101] , and Donald Tusk says his list of ministers is ready [102] . The fiscal inheritance is the debate: Bankier examines the 'Morawiecki hole' in public debt and asks whether it is time to panic [103] , while the IFP public-finance institute alleges the outgoing government planned shocking spending cuts and calls it budget fraud [104] . Grzegorz Kołodko calls the 2024 budget the coalition's first test [105] , and Money.pl says the new government has a different idea for investing PPK pension savings [106] , a potential source of domestic equity demand. The upside lever is Brussels: Business Insider Polska reports the EU will unblock KPO recovery funds for Tusk as an encouragement, with a catch [107] . The constraint is social spending, after a Constitutional Tribunal ruling that is good news for pensioners and hard for a Tusk government [108] .

Corporate & Sectors: Corporate news is mixed beneath the bank-led rally. Orlen has a problem with Americans not executing a contract [109] , and a fire at its Płock site temporarily halted asphalt production [110] , although it is pursuing a nuclear reactor investment with support from the US Phoenix programme [111] ; the stock is down 5.79% over 20 sessions at 62.60 PLN. CD Projekt's co-founder sold part of his shares and the price fell [112] , but the studio is already preparing a Cyberpunk sequel [113] , and the stock is +7.85% over 20 sessions. Dino plans to sell fuel through its own station network [114] , a margin extension that sits alongside its 14.11% 20-session gain. Allegro is the outlier, down 9.28% on the session, while LPP leads consumer names at +18.46% over 20 sessions (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2023-11-16). Retail participation is rising, with brokerage account numbers growing and XTB finally on the podium [115] , and Pekao and Poczta Polska have started a cash-deposit cooperation for entrepreneurs [116] . Housing credit is the boom-bust risk: the banks' association counts over 33 thousand subsidised 'Safe Credit' loans [117] .

Geopolitics & Security: Security spending is becoming an industrial story. Reuters reports Northrop Grumman is exploring ammunition co-production in Poland [118] , a US official says major Ukrainian F-16 maintenance will take place in Poland [119] , and Poland has deployed a new tank battalion near the Belarus border [120] . The friction is commercial: a blockade of border crossings with Poland threatens Ukraine's entire economy, according to GMK Center [121] , and Finland accuses Russia of aiding illegal migrant crossings [122] . Warsaw is also positioning for reconstruction finance, with a Polish representative elected to the board of the register of damage caused by Russian aggression against Ukraine [123] . For markets, the build-out supports defence-linked industry and logistics, while the border dispute tests how the new government balances Ukraine support with domestic hauliers.

New / Most Important: The central-bank fight is the most important new risk. Governor Adam Glapiński says whoever attacks the central bank attacks Poland [124] , and argues that he and the NBP are attacked because critics want to adopt the euro [125] . The prospect of a State Tribunal for the NBP head is being raised publicly [126] , while Ludwik Kotecki argues rates should be held for longer [127] and says the governor is breaking the law [128] . A strong zloty gives the NBP room to stay on hold; the risk is that institutional conflict, not inflation, becomes the driver of Polish rates.

Playbook ✓: Action: overweight Polish banks and the WIG20 through the Beta ETF WIG20TR or EPOL, funded from euro-area cyclicals; it is a currency-plus-earnings trade that an ever stronger zloty supports [129] . Trigger to add: the formal swearing-in of the new government and a timetable for KPO disbursement. Change the call if the NBP conflict escalates into legal action against the governor, or if October's higher-than-estimated inflation [130] becomes a trend. ⚠ Divergence: the zloty is at its strongest in three years and banks are rallying on a friendlier political outlook, yet inflation came in above estimates and the central bank is in open conflict with the incoming majority; the currency is pricing an institutional normalisation that has not happened yet.
