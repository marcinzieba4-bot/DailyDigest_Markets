🇺🇸 United States  Mixed  Fed on hold; minutes say high for longer while the curve prices the peak

Macro Stance: The US goes into the holiday priced for a soft landing that the data have only half delivered. October's flat headline CPI, with core at a two-year low [1] , is what turned the bond market: the debate moved from how high rates go to how soon they come down. The Fed has not followed. Barron's read the latest FOMC minutes as a consensus that rates need to stay high [2] . Underneath, the rate shock is finally reaching activity: existing-home sales hit a 13-year low in October [3] , and Fannie Mae expects weaker consumer spending to tip the economy into a mild recession in 2024 [4] . We stay Mixed. Disinflation plus fading growth suits bonds, but equities price only the benign half.

Rates & Curve: The 10Y closed at 4.42% and the 2Y at 4.89%, leaving 10Y-2Y at -47 bps and 10Y-3M at -112 bps (US Treasury, prior close 2023-11-22). Over 20 observations the 10Y fell 53 bps from 4.95% and the 2Y 19 bps from 5.08%, a -34 bps spread move: an inverted curve in a bull flattening, with the long end doing the work as term premium bled out of the curve. The last five observations show -11 bps on the 10Y but only -1 bps on the 2Y, so the front end has stopped pricing hikes without yet committing to cuts. Three-month bills at 5.54% still out-yield every other point on the curve, which keeps cash a genuine competitor to duration, and the 30Y at 4.55% pays only 13 bps over the 10Y. Further long-end gains therefore need either weaker growth data or a Fed that validates cuts.

New / Most Important: The signal of the week was how little the market paid for good news. Nvidia's revenue tripled [5] , yet the stock fell 2.46% to 487.16 USD (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2023-11-22). Goldman says hedge-fund bets on US stocks are the most crowded ever [6] , and the VIX at 12.85 index points, down 36.35% over 20 sessions, leaves almost no insurance in the system. The S&P 500 closed at 4,556.62 index points, up 8.83% over 20 sessions, while Seeking Alpha counts 471 days without a record close [7] . Crowded books now either chase or de-risk, and holiday-thinned liquidity exaggerates whichever comes first.

Playbook ✓: Action: hold duration in the belly, where the 5Y at 4.44% avoids the supply risk of the long end, and keep equity beta neutral rather than adding at the highs. Trigger to add risk: the 2Y breaking below 4.89% faster than the 10Y, a bull steepening that would mean cuts are priced for reasons equities can survive. Trigger to cut risk: a VIX move back toward 20.19, its level 20 sessions ago, or a rebound in the DXY from 103.92 index points after its 2.45% 20-session fall. What changes the call: an upside inflation surprise that validates the higher-for-longer message and pushes the curve into a bear flattening.

Activity & Inflation: The ECB is leaning against the market's relief: Lagarde warned against a premature celebration on inflation [10] . That gap between a hawkish message and falling input costs leaves room for euro yields to follow Treasuries lower. Credit is the transmission risk: the ECB warns euro-zone banks are starting to show stress as loan defaults rise [12] , which tightens lending standards even without another hike and does the ECB's work for it.

New / Most Important: The new risk is fiscal tightening by accident. Siemens has rallied 23.47% over 20 sessions to 149.64 EUR (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2023-11-23), which shifts the burden of proof from rates to order books.

Playbook ✓: Action: prefer euro duration and quality exporters with dollar revenue over domestic German cyclicals, and keep European autos underweight. Trigger: a German budget fix that restores investment funds would justify adding cyclicals; another bank-stress warning would argue for trimming lenders. What changes the call: ECB speakers moving from 'premature' to data-dependent language on cuts. Until then, prefer the Euro Stoxx 50's global exporters to domestic banks and real estate, which need the budget resolved first.

🇨🇳 China  Mixed  Targeted property support; benchmark lending rates on hold

Macro Stance: China is easing by increments, not by bazooka, and markets are pricing exactly that. The Shanghai Composite closed at 3,061.86 index points, up 2.46% over 20 sessions, and the Hang Seng at 17,910.84, up 5.08% (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2023-11-23), a recovery led by platform names while the property complex keeps leaking. Reuters reported benchmark lending rates left unchanged, as expected [14] , even as consumer prices slipped back into decline [15] . That combination is the core problem: with prices falling, an unchanged nominal lending rate is a rising real rate, so every month of patience tightens conditions for indebted developers and households. Mixed: support is real but rationed, and deflation blunts each dose.

PMIs & Activity: The activity pulse is weak at the margin. Caixin's survey confirmed an October manufacturing slump [16] , so the private, export-heavy end of industry has not yet felt the support measures. The mix matters more than the level: state-directed investment can hold up headline output while smaller private manufacturers face thin orders and falling selling prices. Consumer prices back in decline [15] tell the same story from the demand side, because capacity is outrunning spending. Until prices turn, any survey bounce is better read as restocking than as a durable recovery, and earnings for cyclical A-shares will lag the policy headlines rather than lead them.

Credit Impulse: Credit is being steered rather than expanded. Reuters reports that high-tech manufacturing loans are raising fears of a wave of cheap exports [17] , which shows where the marginal yuan of bank lending goes: to capacity, not to households. That is an impulse that adds supply and deflation rather than demand. The shadow system is the other side of the ledger: a Chinese shadow bank admitted a £30bn shortfall after management 'ran wild' [18] , a reminder that trust and wealth products are the channel through which property losses reach household savings. When those products impair, savers hoard deposits, consumption weakens and banks lean even harder on directed corporate lending. A broad impulse needs household borrowing to restart, and nothing in the current mix delivers that.

Property: Policy is moving from demand tweaks toward direct developer support. The Business Times reports Beijing weighing unprecedented builder support with first-ever unsecured loans [19] , while chinadailyhk reports home prices falling in more cities in October [20] . Unsecured loans would address completion and developer liquidity, but falling prices keep buyers waiting for a bottom. Demand-side easing such as smaller deposits is the weaker tool, because expectations rather than affordability are the binding constraint: a buyer who expects lower prices next year does not move for a smaller deposit. Unsecured lending matters more because it moves the state from refinancing projects to underwriting developers themselves, socialising part of the sector's credit risk onto bank balance sheets.

Fiscal, Policy & Trade: External policy is in a thaw. Beijing approved the Broadcom-VMware merger with conditions, which the South China Morning Post read as a sign of thaw with the US [21] . Friction has migrated to products: hybrid sales are surging at home, posing a fresh threat to foreign automakers [22] , the commercial face of the same capacity push that directed lending finances. Technology controls remain the hard edge, and the US criminal probe into Applied Materials' shipments to SMIC [23] shows Washington enforcing at the toolmaker level even as merger approvals flow. For investors the trade-off is clear: approval thaws help global deal-makers and multinationals, while export enforcement caps China's own chip build-out and keeps semiconductor equipment revenue from China at risk.

Markets & Flows: The equity split is about governance more than macro. Tencent closed at 329.20 HKD, up 14.46% over 20 sessions, and Xiaomi gained 15.57%, while Alibaba fell 5.04% over five sessions to 77.25 HKD (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2023-11-23). Reuters says the cloud spin-off U-turn lopped $20 billion off Alibaba's market value [24] , analysts cut targets by up to 25% [25] , and Jack Ma reversed a plan to sell shares after the slide [26] . Investors are paying for clean capital returns and punishing restructuring uncertainty. Offshore wrappers lag the local tape: FXI fell 3.38% and ASHR 1.37% over five sessions (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2023-11-22), while the Shanghai Composite rose 0.36% over five, so offshore-listed China exposure has not joined the local rally.

New / Most Important: The unsecured-loan proposal is the most important new fact because it changes who carries developer risk. If state banks lend to builders without collateral, completion risk falls, but losses migrate to bank capital, and the shadow-bank channel exposed by that £30bn shortfall [18] keeps the system's weakest savers exposed. Metals have priced the support first: the SCMP reports China demand hopes drove copper to a two-month peak [27] , with copper at 3.78 USD/lb, up 5.90% over 20 sessions (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2023-11-23). That is a bet on construction completions rather than on household demand, which is why consumer prices can keep falling while copper rallies.

Playbook ✓: Action: own Hong Kong-listed platform leaders with cash returns, Tencent first, and keep metals exposure through copper miners, but stay out of developers and out of Alibaba until the restructuring path is credible. Trigger to add: formal adoption of unsecured builder loans alongside a cut in benchmark lending rates. Trigger to cut: another shadow-bank failure or a fresh leg down in home prices. ⚠ Divergence: policy headlines are turning more supportive, yet offshore China ETFs fell over five sessions and consumer prices are back in decline, so the rally in metals and Hong Kong tech is front-running a demand recovery the price data do not yet show.

Playbook ✓: Action: favour domestic demand, banks and service exporters over large-cap manufacturers, and hedge less of the yen exposure than in prior quarters. Trigger: sticky core inflation reinforces BOJ exit pricing and the yen bid; a soft print revives the carry trade and flatters exporters again. 🇵🇱 Poland  Bullish  NBP on pause; power passes to a Tusk coalition

Macro Stance: Poland is the cleanest regime-change trade in Europe: a pro-EU government is assembling, EU money is unlocking and, as Gazeta.pl put it, the zloty is the strongest in years [36] . The macro base is turning up. Bankier reports Polish industry finally back in positive territory [37] and real wages rising at the fastest pace in years, with nominal pay at a record [38] . Real-wage growth is the engine: it converts disinflation into spending power and feeds bank volumes. The catch is persistence. TVN24 notes October inflation came in higher than first estimated [39] , and Rzeczpospolita quotes Kotecki on Poland running the second-highest deficit in the EU, with a reaction from the Commission to follow [40] . Bullish, with fiscal discipline as the variable to watch.

Markets: Polish assets have re-rated hard as power passes to a Tusk-led coalition [41] . EPOL closed at 21.78 USD, up 13.14% over 20 sessions (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2023-11-22), and the Beta WIG20TR tracker at 40.33 PLN, up 6.42% (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2023-11-23). Part of the gap is currency: the dollar-based ETF also captures a zloty that Business Insider Polska says 'shines worldwide', with forecasts giving it plenty more room [42] . A stronger zloty lowers imported inflation and gives the NBP room, while foreign holders earn both the equity re-rating and the currency. Banks led: PKO BP rose 11.21% over 20 sessions to 46.74 PLN, Pekao 9.13% and mBank 8.73%, while Orlen fell 4.25% and Tauron 3.74% (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2023-11-23). Consumer names joined: LPP is up 17.11% and Dino 16.26%. The latest session was a pause, with the WIG20TR tracker down 0.52%, consistent with profit-taking rather than a change of story.

Fiscal & Politics: The handover is now mechanical. Gazeta Prawna reports that Morawiecki's government will be sworn in on Monday but that the budget will be submitted by a Tusk government [41] , and Polsat reports Tusk pointing to St Lucy's day in mid-December for his cabinet [43] . The money is the market story: OKO.press says it is official that €5 billion from the KPO recovery plan will flow despite unmet milestones [44] , and Interia says the first large transfers arrive in January [45] . EU funds matter twice for markets, through public investment that lifts construction and energy capex and through capital inflows that support the zloty. The cost side is just as concrete: Business Insider Polska tabulates how Tusk's flagship pledge would raise salaries [46] , and DW's press review stresses that the EU billions are not unconditional [47] . Pay rises on top of a large deficit are what keep the RPP cautious, because a wage-led fiscal impulse lands in services, where inflation tends to be stickiest.

Corporate & Sectors: The corporate tape maps the political transition. Business Insider Polska reports a collapse in a state company's share price against a backdrop of big wage rises and an extra tax [48] ; coking-coal miner JSW fell 12.17% in its latest session to 42.00 PLN (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2023-11-23), consistent with that description. State-controlled names now carry change-of-control risk in both directions: new boards may cut politically driven spending, but windfall taxes and wage deals land first. Orlen chartered a supertanker to carry Venezuelan crude to China [49] , a trading move that does little for its 4.25% 20-session decline. In private names, Dino warned that a large transaction may not close [50] yet is up 16.26% over 20 sessions to 441.90 PLN, suggesting the market values its store roll-out over deal-making. CD Projekt fell 3.03% to 110.40 PLN despite announcing a Cyberpunk 2077 Ultimate Edition for this year [51] , and KGHM gained 4.69% over 20 sessions to 116.00 PLN alongside firmer copper.

Geopolitics & Security: Security risk is rising but contained. Al Jazeera reports Poland charged 16 people after dismantling a Russian spy ring [52] , a reminder that the country's role as a transit hub makes it a target for hybrid pressure. For markets the relevance is the logistics corridor: Poland is a key land route for Western aid to Ukraine, so sabotage risk falls on rail, ports and energy infrastructure rather than on financial assets directly. A pro-EU government should strengthen allied coordination, and the defence-spending trajectory is unlikely to change with the handover. The premium shows up in the currency only if an incident hits physical infrastructure, and so far the zloty's strength says investors treat the war as a fixed cost rather than a rising one.

New / Most Important: The NBP is the new fault line. Fakty TVN reports Governor Glapiński could face the State Tribunal over the bank's bond purchases [53] , the NBP has written to the ECB to complain [54] , and Business Insider Polska reports PZU chief Beata Kozłowska-Chyła is headed to the NBP [55] . For markets the question is the reaction function, not the legal outcome. A governor under pressure from an incoming government may resist cuts to demonstrate independence, which supports the zloty but delays relief for floating-rate borrowers and keeps bank margins fat. That is bullish for bank earnings in the near term and bearish for the political risk premium if the clash escalates into a constitutional fight.

Playbook ✓: Action: stay overweight Polish banks and the zloty through EPOL and the WIG20TR tracker, and avoid state-controlled energy and coal until new boards and the tax regime are known. Trigger to add: the Tusk government's confidence vote and the first KPO disbursement. Trigger to trim: a State Tribunal motion that escalates into open conflict with the NBP, or a deficit path that draws formal Commission action. Banks carry their own legal tail as law firms 'go after WIBOR', although court rulings have so far disappointed them [56] . ⚠ Divergence: the zloty and EPOL price a smooth transition and EU money, but the NBP-government clash and a pay-heavy budget point to more fiscal and institutional noise, and JSW's 12.17% drop shows how fast state-company equity can be re-priced.

Pattern: The market pays for profit quality and forward visibility, not for top-line beats. Where attention is low, as in European capital goods and US small caps, the reaction function is the opposite: modest guidance holds can re-rate stocks that the market has stopped watching.

Playbook: Action: buy guidance-raisers with margin levers on post-earnings weakness, and avoid cyclicals guiding down into 2024.
