🇺🇸 United States  Bullish  Fed on hold, peak-rate relief

Macro Stance: Bullish, on a short leash. The Federal Reserve held rates for a second straight meeting [1] and upgraded its assessment of growth [2] , while Powell hedged on whether the tightening campaign is over [3] and said rate cuts are not on the radar [4] . Markets chose to hear the first half: Wall Street rallied on hopes that the hikes are done [5] . The S&P 500 closed at 4,317.78 index points, up 1.89% on the session and 4.36% over five, while the VIX fell 7.17% to 15.66 (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2023-11-02). The growth mix makes a peak believable without a recession: the third quarter was a blockbuster [6] , yet the ISM manufacturing index stood at 46.7 [7] and ADP counted only 113,000 private hires in October, fewer than expected [8] .

Rates & Curve: The 2Y closed at 4.98% and the 10Y at 4.67%, leaving the 10Y-2Y spread at -31 bps and 10Y-3M at -87 bps (US Treasury, prior close 2023-11-02). Over 20 observations the 2Y is down 7 bps from 5.05% and the 10Y down 6 bps from 4.73%: a bull steepening, but of a curve that remains inverted. The five-observation move is where the drama sits — the 10Y fell 19 bps against 4 bps for the 2Y, so the long end did the work as term-premium anxiety eased. The Treasury increased the size of most of its auctions [9] , yet the long end rallied across the window that contained the announcement; only days earlier the WSJ was decoding an almost-5% 10-year [10] . TLT gained 2.73% over five sessions while SHY slipped 0.06% (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2023-11-02): relief is being priced in duration, not in cash.

New / Most Important: The decisive change is the reaction function, not the decision. Axios highlighted Powell's supply-side optimism [11] — the argument that better labour supply and productivity can cool inflation without crushing demand — and the American Enterprise Institute declared the Fed's hikes done [12] . BlackRock's Rick Rieder counters that investors should not underestimate the economy's resilience [13] ; the next payrolls report has to arbitrate between those readings. The dollar is already leaning toward the doves: Bloomberg argued the greenback is near its peak [14] , and DXY fell 0.71% to 106.12 index points (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2023-11-02). The tell was KRE, up 5.67% in one session, and the Russell 2000, up 2.67%.

Playbook ✓: Action: overweight quality duration (IEF over SHY) and the rate-sensitive equity that is still under-owned — regional banks, utilities, listed real estate — sized as a trade rather than a regime call. Trigger to add: a payrolls print that confirms the softness ADP flagged in private hiring [8] while the 10Y holds below the 4.73% level of 20 observations ago. What changes the call: a hot hiring or wage number that drags the 2Y back above 5.05%, or weak demand at the enlarged coupon auctions [9] . Hedge cheaply: with the VIX at 15.66 and down 24.27% over five sessions, index puts are insurance that has just become cheaper.

Macro Stance: Bearish on growth, constructive on duration. The euro-zone economy shrank 0.1% in Q3 [15] , Europe's biggest economy shrank in the third quarter [16] , and the New York Times describes Europe faltering while the US keeps growing [17] . Equities looked through it for a day: the Euro Stoxx 50 rose 1.90% to 4,169.62 index points and the DAX 1.48%, but the DAX is up only 0.49% over 20 sessions (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2023-11-02). The bounce is a rates trade imported from Washington, not a European growth call.

Activity & Inflation: October inflation eased further than expected [18] , which gives the ECB cover: it kept rates on hold for the first time in more than a year [19] , and Vujčić says it is finished with hikes 'for now' [20] . Activity is the problem. The October PMI sat near a three-year low, stirring recession worries [21] , and banks tightened credit standards further [22] , so past hikes are still working their way through lending. Falling inflation, contracting output and tighter credit form a bond-market setup, not an equity one.

New / Most Important: The Bank of England held at 5.25% [23] , leaving rates at a 15-year high [24] , and warned of recession risk in the run-up to an expected election [25] ; MoneyWeek argues it cannot afford to hike again [26] . The FTSE 100 is down 0.07% over 20 sessions, the laggard among the major European benchmarks (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2023-11-02). With London and Frankfurt both pausing into weakness, European policy is now following the Fed rather than leading it.

Playbook ✓: Action: underweight euro-area cyclicals against quality compounders with pricing power — SAP is up 6.53% over 20 sessions and Novo Nordisk 11.59% (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2023-11-02) — and prefer core duration to equity beta. Trigger to cover: a PMI rebound strong enough to show that credit tightening has peaked. What changes the call: a reacceleration in core inflation that revives hike talk in Frankfurt, which would hit both bonds and the expensive defensives at once.

🇨🇳 China  Bearish  State credit push, demand relapse

Macro Stance: Bearish. October disappointed: factory activity unexpectedly contracted as demand dropped [27] , and the Caixin survey confirmed the slump a day later [28] . The Economist's question — an economy that is 'a mess', so why are firms not going under? [29] — captures the policy choice. Forbearance and state credit keep weak firms alive rather than forcing a clean-out, which protects employment but caps the return on capital, and that is why equity investors keep demanding a discount. The Shanghai Composite closed at 3,009.41 index points, down 3.15% over 20 sessions (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2023-11-02).

PMIs & Activity: The relapse is demand-led. CNN's account puts falling demand at the centre of the October contraction [27] , and OilPrice links softer crude directly to China's economic woes [30] . There are offsets: September industrial profits extended their gains with policy support [31] , and CGTN reported positive Q3 performance across the regions [32] . Prices are the weak link — consumer prices stalled and factory-gate deflation persisted in the last print [33] , so nominal growth trails real growth and corporate revenue suffers. Import demand is concentrated in staples: wheat imports could set a record this year [34] , as could soybean imports [35] .

Credit Impulse: The credit pulse is being generated by the state, not by households. Bloomberg reports government debt supply surged in October to a 2023 high [36] , the fiscal channel substituting for weak private borrowing. That issuance drained the interbank market: short-term money rates spiked and markets now expect more easing [37] , while Reuters reports some institutions borrowing at a 50% rate as liquidity was squeezed [38] . The banks pay for it in margins — ICBC posted scant profit growth on shrinking margins [39] , and analysts expect margin pressure to persist even though asset quality is stable [40] . A squeeze of this kind usually ends with a central-bank liquidity injection; the question is whether the injection reaches the private sector or merely refinances the state.

Property: Property remains the drag on household wealth and confidence. New home prices fell 0.3% in September [41] , a faster pace of decline in Bloomberg's account [42] , and Golden Week home sales fell 17% against 2022 [43] . Evergrande's winding-up hearing was adjourned as it seeks a new debt deal [44] — a reprieve, not a resolution. Al Jazeera's reporting on crumbling, unfinished homes [45] explains why presales, the sector's funding model, stay broken: buyers will not prepay for flats that may never be delivered. Nippon.com is already asking whether China will repeat Japan's post-bubble experience [46] , and even an iron-ore bull like Fortescue frames its optimism as 'despite' the property downturn [47] .

Fiscal, Policy & Trade: Beijing is tightening its grip on supply while easing demand mainly through the budget. It is weighing copper curbs to cut overcapacity and emissions [48] , has imposed growth limits on oil refining [49] , and tightened exports of graphite, a key battery material [50] . He Lifeng's appointment to a key party commission concentrates economic authority [51] . On trade, the White House says there is an agreement in principle for a Biden–Xi meeting [52] , aimed at San Francisco in November [53] , though SCMP warns that differing objectives will limit the outcomes [54] . Reuters reports China warming to Micron as tensions with Washington ease [55] — small, but concrete.

Markets & Flows: Hong Kong is outperforming the mainland on a buyback bid. The Hang Seng rose 0.75% to 17,230.59 index points and is up 0.20% over 20 sessions, while the ASHR CSI 300 tracker is down 2.55% (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2023-11-02). SCMP tied an earlier Hong Kong rally to more Chinese companies joining buyback plans [56] , and Reuters notes micro-caps shining in a flagging A-share market [57] — speculative, liquidity-driven pockets rather than broad conviction. New supply is struggling: J&T Express fell in a lacklustre Hong Kong debut [58] , and lithium names Ganfeng and Tianqi sold off on a price crash [59] . Dispersion is extreme: Xiaomi is up 27.15% over 20 sessions while Baidu's US line is down 17.72% (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2023-11-02). Alibaba upgraded its Tongyi Qianwen AI model and released industry-specific versions [60] , yet its Hong Kong line is down 1.53% over 20 sessions (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2023-11-02): AI announcements are not enough to re-rate platforms that still depend on the domestic consumer.

New / Most Important: The new information is the collision between state issuance and a cash-short money market. When government borrowing at a 2023 high [36] meets weak loan demand, the interbank curve becomes the transmission channel, and a 50% borrowing rate for some institutions [38] is a plumbing warning rather than a growth signal. Corporate evidence is bifurcating around winners: BYD's third-quarter profit soared to a record [61] , Xpeng and Li Auto delivered record numbers of cars in October [62] , and Moutai raised prices for the first time in six years [63] . Meanwhile Canada Goose cut its sales forecast on a rocky China recovery [64] and Yum China fell to a one-year low on disappointing results [65] : pricing power at the top, strain in the mass market.

Playbook ✓: Action: stay underweight mainland cyclicals and property-linked credit, and express any China exposure through Hong Kong-listed platforms with buybacks and pricing power rather than the A-share index. Trigger to turn constructive: a manufacturing PMI back above 50 alongside a visible easing in money-market rates [37] . What makes the call more bearish: another failed restructuring step at Evergrande or a renewed spike in funding costs. ⚠ Divergence: fiscal issuance at a 2023 high [36] and an agreed-in-principle Biden–Xi meeting argue that policy and diplomacy are turning supportive just as activity relapses — the risk to an underweight is a policy-driven squeeze, not a data-driven one.

Activity & Currency: The policy mix is pulling in opposite directions. Chip equipment led the tape, with Advantest up 10.00% on the session and Tokyo Electron up 8.89% over five, while Toyota is up 11.40% over 20 sessions (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2023-11-02).

Playbook ✓: Action: own exporters and semiconductor-equipment names, but hedge the currency leg rather than treating yen weakness as permanent; EWJ rose 6.30% over five sessions (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2023-11-02). Trigger to cut: a further BoJ step or official intervention that turns the yen, since an undervalued currency is now a policy problem for the central bank [74] . What changes the call: a decisive yen rally that unwinds the translation windfall now embedded in raised earnings guidance, which would hit autos harder than chip equipment.

🇵🇱 Poland  Bullish  Post-election rerating meets fiscal audit

Macro Stance: Bullish, but the case rests on politics and disinflation rather than on growth. After the October election, the victorious democratic camp is preparing for a tense coexistence with President Duda [85] , and investors have re-rated Polish risk hard: the dollar-denominated EPOL ETF is up 20.46% over 20 sessions and the PLN WIG20TR tracker 14.91% (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2023-11-02). Disinflation supports the bid. Strefa Inwestorów reads October's sharp CPI drop as a base effect, with inflation seen near the current level through year-end [86] , and Wyborcza stresses that prices are still rising, just more slowly [87] . Economist Marcin Mrowiec warns the inflation target is far away on the horizon, at times seemingly beyond it [88] , which limits the RPP's room to ease. The NBP itself has become a political variable: in early October Glapiński spoke of a success he apologised for delivering 'now, before the elections' [89] , a performance Rzeczpospolita called an election broadcast [90] . Under a new majority, central-bank governance moves onto the risk map.

Markets: Warsaw was closed on November 1, so the latest Polish closes, dated 2023-11-02, compare with 2023-10-31. Parkiet says the WIG20 is once again showing its better side [91] , and the banks lead: mBank is up 32.32%, Pekao 27.72% and PKO BP 25.08% over 20 sessions, with PZU up 16.27% and Orlen 15.07% (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2023-11-02). The rerating is a bet that a new government lowers state-interference risk in listed companies; the cost is that rerated banks now carry less margin for the legal bill on mortgages. The currency is part of the story — commentators are already asking how strong the zloty can get in 2024 [92] , and part of the gap between the dollar EPOL and the local WIG20TR tracker reflects that translation. Domestic savers feel the disinflation directly: after the election, the coupon on inflation-linked savings bonds is falling [93] .

Fiscal & Politics: The incoming coalition inherits a fiscal audit, not a windfall. Rzeczpospolita writes that the budget left by PiS may be even worse than thought [94] ; Money.pl reports economists calculating a staggering real budget hole for 2024 [95] ; TVN24 says a letter to Brussels revealed a record deficit [96] ; and Rzeczpospolita has tallied the debt swept under the carpet [97] . ING frames the question as how much fiscal space remains after the elections [98] and how the new majority turns its platform into a viable economic strategy [99] , while the finance minister insists rating agencies are keeping ratings and outlooks stable [100] . Coalition partners are dividing posts and say they will reach agreement [101] , but Interia warns PiS is leaving a surprise that could derail the new government's plans [102] . A Tusk adviser has commented on freezing electricity prices in 2024 [103] — a direct fiscal and utility-earnings question.

Corporate & Sectors: Banks face a second legal front: the KNF says WIBOR will exist no longer than the end of 2027 [104] , and TVN24 reports a court that, in a case going beyond WIBOR, fully released borrowers from paying instalments [105] . On foreign-currency loans, Bank Millennium aims to conclude 1,000 settlements a quarter [106] , and a state-owned giant has changed its mind, so Bank Pocztowy will again not pass to PKO BP [107] . In resources, KGHM paid PLN 1.3 billion of corporate income tax to the state last year [108] , and an analyst raised the price target for JSW [109] , whose shares are up 15.03% over five sessions. Orlen now runs more parcel lockers than Allegro [110] — a competitive threat to Allegro, which trades at 30.79 PLN, up 7.02% over five sessions. Utilities rallied too — Tauron is up 14.28% and PGE 8.09% over 20 sessions (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2023-11-02) — so any electricity-price freeze becomes a direct earnings question. CD Projekt is at a turning point, with leadership change and new games on a horizon of uncertain distance [111] ; the stock is down 14.38% over 20 sessions (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2023-11-02).

Geopolitics & Security: Security risk is rising in Russian rhetoric. A Putin ally warned 'enemy' Poland that it risks losing its statehood [112] , Dmitry Medvedev said Poland could lead to a third world war [113] , and a Russian court threatened Europol Gaz with a $1.57 billion fine over a Gazprom lawsuit [114] . Interia reports that Russia has withdrawn from a key nuclear-test treaty [115] . Warsaw is answering with capacity: The Economist details the beefing-up of Poland's armed forces [116] , and Poland signed an agreement to launch its first satellite constellation [117] . Friction with Kyiv persists — Ukraine has sent Poland a note over the blocking of the border [118] — a logistics risk for exporters and transport firms that the defence build-out does not offset.

New / Most Important: The most important development is the widening gap between market euphoria and fiscal disclosure. Prime Minister Morawiecki still speaks of a 'very big chance' on the year-end deficit [119] , while Professor Orłowski asks whether Poland is on a Greek path [120] and Krzysztof Bosak calls the scale at which public finances were moved outside parliamentary control unprecedented [121] . Pekao's work on public-debt servicing costs [122] shows why this matters for bond investors: a higher deficit financed off-budget still has to be refinanced in the market. Housing is the pressure point: flat prices are again rising faster than incomes [123] , a legacy of the subsidised 2% mortgage scheme that Forbes says upended the property market [124] . Business Insider Polska reports that Poles are saving on food but not on clothes [125] — consumption is selective rather than collapsing.

Playbook ✓: Action: stay overweight Polish equities through banks, insurers and the WIG20TR tracker, but trim into strength where 20-session gains exceed 25%, as at PKO BP and Pekao (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2023-11-02). Trigger to add: a coalition agreement that names a finance minister with a credible deficit path and a stable rating message [100] . What changes the call: a wave of WIBOR lawsuits [126] or a budget hole large enough to tempt the new government into heavier bank levies. ⚠ Divergence: equities are pricing a governance dividend while the fiscal arithmetic deteriorates — the record-deficit disclosure [96] sits uneasily with a bank rally that implicitly assumes the hole will not be closed at shareholders' expense.
