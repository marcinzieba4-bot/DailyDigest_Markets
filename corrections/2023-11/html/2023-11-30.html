🇺🇸 United States  Bullish  Fed on hold; cut debate opens

Macro Stance: The bond market has declared the hiking cycle over, and the data are not arguing back hard enough to stop it. Q3 GDP was revised up to a 5.2% annualised pace, hotter than first estimated [1] , yet Treasuries kept rallying because investors treat the quarter as the peak of a stimulus rush rather than a new trend; Jamie Dimon's warning that the economy is on a 'sugar high' [2] is the same reading from the sceptic's side. Fed Governor Waller's confidence that inflation is heading back to 2% gave the rally its policy anchor [3] , and the debate has moved from how high to how soon to cut [4] . We are Bullish on US duration and quality equities, with one caveat: households are spending through discounts, and Black Friday week sales gains were the softest in years [5] .

Rates & Curve: The 2Y closed at 4.64% and the 10Y at 4.27%, leaving 10Y-2Y at -37 bps, while 3M bills still yield 5.45% and the 10Y-3M gap sits at -118 bps (US Treasury, prior close 2023-11-29). Over 20 observations the 2Y fell 43 bps from 5.07% and the 10Y 61 bps from 4.88%: a bull flattening of an inverted curve, with the 10Y-2Y spread a further 18 bps deeper. The last five observations alone took 22 bps off the 2Y and 14 bps off the 10Y, with Waller's remarks landing inside that window. A front end pricing cuts while the curve stays inverted says markets expect easing because inflation is fading, not because growth is breaking, the friendliest mix for equities. Supply is the counterweight: $7.6 trillion of government debt matures within a year [6] , so every auction tests how much easing the long end can absorb.

New / Most Important: Equities are digesting a powerful month rather than extending it. The S&P 500 closed at 4,550.58, -0.09% on the day but +8.51% over 20 sessions; the Nasdaq 100 sits at 15,987.60 (+10.95%) and the Russell 2000 has matched the S&P at +8.51% as lower yields reach small caps (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2023-11-29). The VIX at 12.98 is 5.16 points below its level 20 sessions ago, and DXY at 102.77 is down 3.65% over the same window, on pace for its worst month of the year as bets on a finished Fed pile up [7] . The Street's 2024 calls are unusually dispersed: JPMorgan's S&P 500 outlook is the grimmest on Wall Street [8] , while Deutsche Bank sees a record 5,100 [9] .

Playbook ✓: Action: hold duration through the belly (IEF +4.78% over 20 sessions) and keep equities tilted to quality growth, but buy index protection while the VIX is under 13 (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2023-11-29). Trigger to add: a benign inflation print that keeps the cut debate alive [4] . What changes the call: a 2Y back toward the 5.07% it held 20 observations ago (US Treasury, prior close 2023-11-29), or data that turn the 5.2% GDP revision into a trend rather than a peak [1] .

🇨🇳 China  Bearish  PBOC easing bias; targeted property support

Macro Stance: China is the weak link in an otherwise reflating world, and markets are saying so before the data do. The Hang Seng fell 2.08% to 16,993.44 on the day and 4.18% over five sessions, the Shanghai Composite slipped 0.55% to 3,021.69, and FXI has lost 4.20% in five sessions (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2023-11-29); Hong Kong led Asian losses as the mainland closed at a one-month low [19] . The policy message is supportive but incremental, with the central bank's head pledging to support growth while urging reforms [20] . Incrementalism is exactly the mix that fails to re-rate cyclicals when global investors have just earned 8.51% in a month from the S&P 500, so we are Bearish on China beta.

PMIs & Activity: The official November PMI is the next test, and the Reuters poll expects factory activity to have contracted for a second month [21] . The October backdrop explains the pessimism: industrial profit growth slowed, keeping calls for stimulus alive [22] , and consumer prices slipped back into decline [23] . Falling prices with slowing profits is a margin squeeze, and it is reaching consumer companies too, with the CEO of KFC China's owner, Joey Wat, publicly explaining a consumer spending slump [24] . That matters because consumer names were supposed to be the defensive leg of any recovery; if they too are reporting a spending slump, the 2024 earnings base is still falling, and a contracting PMI would confirm that manufacturing offers no offset.

Credit Impulse: Credit is being steered rather than expanded. Reuters reports that high-tech manufacturing loans are raising fears of a wave of cheap exports [25] : credit pushed into supply rather than demand exports deflation and invites trade friction. Beijing is weighing first-ever unsecured loans to developers [26] , a quasi-fiscal backstop rather than a broad impulse, while the shadow system is still deleveraging painfully, with Zhongzhi admitting a £30bn shortfall after 'management ran wild' [27] . Until household borrowing turns, an impulse that favours factories over homes keeps the growth mix lopsided and the export channel deflationary, and it leaves the yuan dependent on policy defence rather than on capital inflows.

Property: Property remains the drag that policy keeps nibbling at. Shenzhen cut down payments on second homes as cities try to revive demand [28] , but October brought more cities recording falling home prices [29] . Down-payment cuts work only where buyers believe prices have bottomed; with declines spreading, households rationally wait, which is why easing city by city has produced marginal transactions rather than a turn. The binding constraint is developer funding, so the unsecured-loan proposal [26] is the one property headline that could change the cycle if it arrives with state-bank participation and scale.

Fiscal, Policy & Trade: Fiscal support is being rationed, and the trade channel is absorbing the slack. Cheap-export credit pushes surplus production abroad, which raises the odds of tariff responses from trading partners rather than a domestic demand recovery. Iron ore is the one market openly betting on a bigger push, with analysts seeing $150 in 2024 on Chinese stimulus [30] ; that forecast needs exactly the central-government spending Beijing has so far rationed, and it leaves Australian miners exposed to a policy disappointment. For Europe and emerging Asia the same mix means imported disinflation; for China's own listed exporters it means volume without margin.

Markets & Flows: The equity tape is splitting between platforms winning share and incumbents losing it. Alibaba fell 2.22% in Hong Kong and 2.70% in New York, leaving the ADR -9.53% over 20 sessions, while PDD is +39.75% (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2023-11-29); Jack Ma praised rival PDD in a rare memo urging Alibaba staff to change [31] as PDD moved to the verge of surpassing Alibaba [32] . Tencent at 317.00 HKD is still +10.45% over 20 sessions and KWEB +6.72%, so this is stock selection rather than a wholesale internet exit; FXI, the large-cap benchmark, is -1.80% over 20 sessions. State support shows up in market plumbing: the Beijing bourse told major shareholders to refrain from selling [33] , and Meituan launched a $1 billion buyback [34] . Supply suppression props up prices, but it does not create the foreign demand that would re-rate the market.

New / Most Important: The most important development is the absence of a bazooka. Industrial profits are slowing [22] while investors sell Hong Kong, and the policy response remains a sequence of targeted measures. ASHR at 24.83 USD is -0.28% over 20 sessions, flat in a month when the S&P 500 gained 8.51% (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2023-11-29); that is the opportunity cost of waiting for Beijing, and it compounds every month that global duration keeps rallying without China. Foreign allocators who were waiting for a policy pivot are instead being paid to wait elsewhere.

Playbook ✓: Action: stay underweight China beta (FXI, ASHR) and express views through relative value, long PDD against BABA, rather than outright longs. Trigger to cover: a PMI that beats the contraction consensus plus a formal bank-backed developer lending programme. What changes the call: evidence that the unsecured-loan plan is broad rather than symbolic. ⚠ Divergence: metal markets are trading as if a stimulus is coming that China equities refuse to price; COPX is +4.72% over 20 sessions and iron-ore forecasts point to $150 [30] , while the Hang Seng is -0.63% over the same window (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2023-11-29). One side is wrong, and we think equities are reading Beijing's restraint more accurately than the metal bulls.

🇵🇱 Poland  Mixed  NBP versus incoming coalition

Macro Stance: Poland enters December with strong cyclical tailwinds and a deteriorating governance premium. Against that, October inflation was revised higher than first estimated [47] , while Governor Glapiński had already declared that Poland no longer has high inflation [48] . Real wage growth plus a central bank that has declared victory over inflation is good for consumer and bank earnings and bad for the credibility premium in Polish bonds and the złoty. We are Mixed: long the consumer and the banks, short the state companies being asked to fund policy, starting with Orlen.

Markets: The Warsaw tape split violently on the day. Orlen collapsed 7.90% to 59.60 PLN, leaving it -10.38% over 20 sessions, while PKO BP gained 1.79% to 47.75 PLN and Pekao rose 0.94% to 140.15 PLN. That gap between local and dollar returns plausibly reflects the złoty, though the two funds also differ in composition. Bankier's currency forecast shows analysts still unconvinced by an 'era of the strong złoty' [49] , and on the day pb.pl reported the dollar firming after a sharp fall while the euro slipped against the złoty [50] , a pause rather than a reversal in a month when DXY lost 3.65%. Banks are the domestic winners because a stronger currency and resilient wages both support loan quality; mBank is +8.35% over 20 sessions, while XTB, the retail broker, is +3.61%.

Fiscal & Politics: The fiscal story is now about who pays for the transition. WysokieNapiecie reports that Orlen will put up 15 billion złoty to fund the freeze of electricity, gas and heating prices [51] , a quasi-fiscal levy on minority shareholders; XTB asked whether a multi-billion 'special contribution' was behind Orlen's 6% slide [52] , and Analizy.pl summed it up bluntly: 'Orlen is bleeding on the exchange, because of politicians' [53] . Politically, the new Morawiecki government was sworn in with OKO.press predicting it will fall after 14 days [54] and Gazeta Prawna calling it a cabinet without a chance of a confidence vote and without a budget [55] ; next year's budget will be tabled by Donald Tusk's government [56] , and Tusk has made pay rises a flagship promise [57] . The risk for bondholders is that energy relief and wage promises get financed off balance sheet through state companies, where they escape the deficit headline but not the cost of capital.

Corporate & Sectors: Inside the index the political discount is being repriced name by name. Utilities are the mirror image of Orlen: PGE is +15.18% and Tauron +12.64% over five sessions, consistent with the price-freeze bill landing on the refiner rather than the generators, and PGE also secured a decision-in-principle for its nuclear project with ZE PAK [58] . CD Projekt earned more than 200 million złoty net in Q3 [59] , yet the stock fell 3.47% to 104.20 PLN and is -8.48% over five sessions; investors are trading the Witcher pipeline, on which almost half the studio's developers now work [60] , rather than the quarter. Retail is two-speed: Dino is +12.92% over 20 sessions to 450.00 PLN while Allegro is -3.85% and LPP slipped 2.47% on the day. PZU, another state-controlled name, is -3.93% over 20 sessions, while KGHM is +4.45% and Asseco Poland gained 1.60% on the day (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2023-11-29). The common thread is policy exposure: companies asked to pay for the transition are de-rated, while those it protects or leaves alone are bid.

Geopolitics & Security: Security risk is rising on both eastern borders. The Kremlin warned of tensions if Poland sends troops to the Finnish-Russian border [61] , and Polish truckers extended a round-the-clock blockade to a fourth Ukrainian border crossing [62] , a trade irritant that complicates the next government's Ukraine policy. Poland is building a 54 billion złoty air-defence shield that will protect Patriot batteries among other assets [63] . Kremlin rhetoric about the Finnish border adds a northern dimension that keeps NATO's eastern flank, and Poland's role in it, at the centre of European security planning. For investors this is mainly a spending story: defence outlays support domestic industry and widen the deficit at the same time, which is one more reason the curve needs a credible budget from the next government.

New / Most Important: The single most important development is the Orlen precedent. A state-controlled champion funding a 15 billion złoty price freeze [51] tells foreign holders that dividends and capex at state companies can be subordinated to energy-price politics during the handover. The second is the escalating fight over the central bank: a constitutional expert told Gazeta Prawna that suspending the NBP president would breach the constitution [64] , while the bank keeps hanging banners in its own defence, the latest calling Glapiński 'the best' [65] . A drawn-out legal war over the NBP is a złoty risk that local assets have not priced.

Playbook ✓: Action: stay long Polish banks (PKO BP, Pekao) and domestic consumer winners (Dino), via EPOL or the WIG20 total-return ETF, but underweight Orlen until the price-freeze funding is legislated and capped. Trigger to add: a confidence vote that installs Tusk's cabinet and a budget that funds energy relief on balance sheet. What changes the call: a move to suspend Glapiński that triggers złoty weakness, or 'special contributions' spreading to banks. ⚠ Divergence: dollar investors are being paid to own Poland, with EPOL +10.83% over 20 sessions, while the local WIG20 total-return tracker is only +3.31% and Orlen -10.38% (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2023-11-29); currency translation is probably doing much of the work that domestic earnings and governance are not.
