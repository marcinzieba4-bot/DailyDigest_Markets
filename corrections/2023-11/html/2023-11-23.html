🇺🇸 United States  Mixed  Fed on hold; curve bull-flattening

Macro Stance: Wall Street went into the Thanksgiving break on a firm note. The S&P 500 closed at 4,556.62 index points, up 0.41% on the session and 8.83% over 20 sessions, while the Nasdaq 100 finished at 16,001.39, up 11.26% (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2023-11-22). The policy message has not moved with it: Barron's read the Fed minutes as a consensus that rates need to stay high [1] . Equities are therefore discounting falling inflation and resilient growth at the same time, a combination the bond market is only half-endorsing. We stay Mixed: the trend is friendly, but the margin for disappointment has narrowed sharply.

Rates & Curve: Treasuries carry the cleanest signal. The 2Y closed at 4.89% and the 10Y at 4.42%, leaving 10Y-2Y at -47 bps and 10Y-3M at -112 bps (US Treasury, prior close 2023-11-22). Over 20 observations the 2Y fell 19 bps from 5.08% while the 10Y fell 53 bps from 4.95%, a bull flattening that pushed the 10Y-2Y spread a further 34 bps into inversion; the curve shape remains inverted. Over five observations the 2Y is down just 1 bps against 11 bps for the 10Y, so the long end is doing the work while the front end still respects the higher-for-longer message. Reuters noted gold slipping below $2,000 as the dollar gained and US yields pared losses [2] , a sign the bond rally paused into the holiday rather than accelerating, yet the VIX at 12.85 index points, 7.34 points lower over 20 sessions, prices little of that risk (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2023-11-22).

New / Most Important: Housing and inflation explain why the long end rallied. CNN reported US home sales on pace for the worst year since 1993 [3] , and J.P. Morgan summarised the October CPI report as inflation falling to 3.2% [4] . Together they hand the bond market both reasons to own duration: weaker volumes and cooler prices. Forecasters are moving in the same direction, with CFO Dive relaying Fannie Mae's call that weaker consumer spending will trigger a mild recession in 2024 [5] . The nuance is that a mild recession is good for bonds but not obviously for an S&P 500 already up 8.83% in 20 sessions; earnings, not multiples, would have to absorb the slowdown.

Playbook ✓: Action: keep duration long in the 5Y-10Y sector and fund it from cash rather than from equities, because the bull flattening says investors are buying growth insurance, not betting on imminent cuts. Trigger to add: a 10Y close below 4.42% with the 2Y anchored near 4.89% would confirm the long-end bid, and equity exposure should then tilt toward rate-sensitive breadth such as XLRE and IWM rather than the most crowded mega-caps. What changes the call: a 2Y back above 5.08%, the level of 20 observations ago, would mean the minutes' hawkish consensus is being re-priced, and we would cut duration.

Activity & Inflation: Activity is the weak leg. New / Most Important: The fresh risk is fiscal and financial rather than monetary. The Guardian reported the ECB warning that euro-zone banks are starting to show 'stress' as loan defaults rise [9] , and the New York Times reported Germany freezing government spending as its budget crisis deepens [10] . The two interact: a fiscal squeeze in the largest economy lands just as bank asset quality turns, which is how a mild contraction becomes a credit problem. That combination argues against chasing the European rally through domestic lenders.

Playbook ✓: Action: own European quality cyclicals with pricing power, such as Siemens, up 17.88% over 20 sessions, rather than domestic banks, where the stress warning caps the margin story; HSBC slipped 1.08% on the session (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2023-11-22). Trigger: a German fiscal fix that unfreezes spending would broaden the rally into DAX cyclicals. What changes the call: rising loan defaults alongside sticky core inflation would turn the ECB's no-cut stance into a growth headwind.

🇨🇳 China  Mixed  Targeted easing, property drag

Macro Stance: China is easing at the margin but not enough to change the earnings arithmetic. The Shanghai Composite closed at 3,043.61 index points, down 0.79% on the session and 0.95% over five sessions, while the Hang Seng was flat at 17,734.60 and 1.90% lower over five sessions (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2023-11-22). FreightWaves captured investor frustration with 'why stimulus is MIA and risks are rising' [11] . The gap matters because Chinese earnings revisions follow nominal growth, which falling prices and a shrinking property sector hold down. We hold a Mixed stance: policy is supportive enough to prevent a slide, not enough to trigger a re-rating.

PMIs & Activity: The activity data remain uneven. Capital Brief reported the Caixin PMI confirming October's manufacturing slump [12] , and macau daily times noted imports rising in October while exports fell for a sixth straight month [13] . That pairing is more constructive than it looks: rising imports point to domestic restocking and commodity demand, while falling exports reflect weak Western goods demand rather than a domestic collapse. Prices are the quieter worry, with Reuters reporting consumer prices back in decline as the recovery wobbles [14] . Falling prices keep household income growth soft, so a consumption-led recovery needs longer, which is why exports and investment still dominate the growth mix.

Credit Impulse: Credit is flowing to the wrong places for a consumer-led recovery. Reuters reported China kept its lending benchmark rates unchanged, as expected [15] , so the price of money is not falling for households or developers. Instead, Reuters flagged high-tech manufacturing loans raising fears of a wave of cheap exports [16] , which channels credit into capacity rather than demand and exports deflation abroad. The market mechanism matters: credit steered to factories supports industrial-metal volumes but compresses margins, a mix that favours commodity suppliers over Chinese equity holders.

Property: Property remains the binding constraint. chinadailyhk reported more Chinese cities recording falling home prices in October [17] , and SCMP reported Moody's downgrading Longfor's credit rating to below investment grade [18] , notable because Longfor had been treated as one of the stronger private developers. When the better-run names lose investment-grade status, the problem has moved from leverage to demand: buyers expecting lower prices postpone purchases, which starves developers of presales, their main funding source. Reuters quoted analysts saying China needs to pull multiple levers for a property turnaround [19] , an implicit admission that piecemeal local easing has run its course. Until price declines stop spreading, developer credit stays a distressed asset class.

Fiscal, Policy & Trade: External policy is turning more constructive. SCMP called China's conditional approval of the Broadcom-VMware merger a sign of a thaw with the US [20] , a concrete regulatory dividend rather than rhetoric. On currency internationalisation, Bloomberg reported a $7 billion swap between the Chinese and Saudi central banks [21] , part of a slow effort to settle commodity trade outside the dollar. The fiscal side is the gap: no large central-government demand package accompanies these moves, so the policy mix remains diplomacy plus targeted credit. For investors the thaw lowers tail risk on US-listed Chinese names and chip supply chains without changing the domestic earnings outlook, which is why we prefer to own it through global semiconductor and commodity names.

Markets & Flows: Equity flows are punishing China's platform champions for corporate missteps rather than macro. Alibaba's US ADR closed at 78.96 USD, down 9.31% over five sessions, and the Hong Kong line fell 7.63% (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2023-11-22) after Reuters reported the company scrapped its cloud spin-off citing US chip export bans [22] . Business Insider then reported Jack Ma reversed a plan to sell shares because the stock had dropped so much [23] . Tencent is the counterpoint, up 12.72% over 20 sessions to 324.40 HKD, and Baidu's ADR jumped 5.91% on the session. Xiaomi dropped 10.00% over five sessions and the ASHR A-share ETF fell 1.18%, so dispersion, not direction, is what China equity investors are paid for.

New / Most Important: The newest positive is a firmer currency backdrop. Business Recorder reported the yuan near a four-month high on a firmer fixing and steadier China-US ties [24] , and SCMP reported China demand hopes driving copper prices to a two-month peak [25] . A firmer yuan gives the central bank room to cut rates without triggering capital-flight fears, because the depreciation pressure that constrained it has eased with the dollar index's 2.45% 20-session decline. That makes the next lending-rate decision the variable to watch: a cut alongside a stable currency would be a cleaner easing signal than anything delivered so far.

Playbook ✓: Action: stay underweight China beta through FXI and ASHR, and express the policy put through suppliers instead: copper producers such as Freeport, up 9.01% over 20 sessions, and Tencent over Alibaba within the platforms (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2023-11-22). Trigger to upgrade: a cut in the lending benchmarks that Reuters reported were held unchanged [15] , or evidence that home-price declines stop spreading. What changes the call: a Shanghai Composite close below its 20-session-ago level of 2,974.11 index points would signal that easing is losing the race with property deflation. ⚠ Divergence: the yuan is near a four-month high [24] and copper touched a two-month peak on China demand hopes [25] , yet the Shanghai Composite fell 0.95% over five sessions and Alibaba's ADR dropped 9.31%, so currency and commodity markets are pricing a recovery that domestic equity holders still refuse to underwrite.

Corporate & Sectors: Corporate news is mixed beneath a strong tape. Orlen fell 1.62% on the session to 63.01 PLN and is down 3.96% over 20 sessions (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2023-11-22), in a session when Brent also fell; TVN24 reported Orlen chartering a supertanker to buy Venezuelan crude [50] , a supply-diversification move that matters for refining margins but not for the political overhang on the stock. Geopolitics & Security: Security risk is rising at the border and inside the country. Al Jazeera reported Poland charging 16 people after dismantling a Russian spy ring [54] , a reminder that hybrid pressure is a standing feature rather than an episodic shock. The Ukrainian border is a trade risk: the BBC reported Polish truck protests leaving Ukrainian drivers stranded [55] , a dispute that can disrupt transit logistics and strain relations with a key neighbour. Defence is also a domestic growth engine: Money.pl reported state group PGZ will build a 'superradar' for the Patriot system [56] , part of a procurement cycle that supports local industrial output. For markets the security premium shows up in defence and logistics names rather than in the zloty, which has strengthened regardless.

New / Most Important: The NBP itself has become the political flashpoint. Fakty TVN reported Governor Glapiński could face the State Tribunal over the bank's bond purchases ('Może mu grozić Trybunał Stanu za skupowanie obligacji') [57] , and Business Insider Polska carried the NBP's first deputy president calling those reasons 'inflated and political' [58] . Meanwhile Money.pl reported the NBP enlarging its gold reserves [59] , a diversification policy that continues regardless of the dispute. For investors the risk is less the legal outcome than a period in which monetary communication is read through a political lens, which can add a premium to Polish bonds even while the zloty strengthens. The incoming majority has an incentive to resolve the conflict quickly, because a contested central bank would undercut the credibility gain that drove the rally.

Playbook ✓: Action: stay overweight Polish equities via EPOL and the banks, with Pekao up 12.79% over 20 sessions to 139.30 PLN (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2023-11-22), funded from Orlen and the state utilities, where fuel and power-price politics weigh. Trigger to add: the first KPO transfers that Interia expects in January [47] and a sworn-in Tusk government. What changes the call: a formal Commission response to the deficit, which Kotecki says is coming [44] , or a State Tribunal motion against the NBP governor that pushes the zloty lower. ⚠ Divergence: the zloty and bank stocks are pricing a smooth institutional reset, yet the deficit is the EU's second-highest and the central bank is in open conflict with the incoming majority [57] , so fiscal and governance risk premia are barely priced.
