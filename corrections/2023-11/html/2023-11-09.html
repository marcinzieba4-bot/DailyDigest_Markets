🇺🇸 United States  Mixed  Fed on hold; long-end rally

Macro Stance: The US has swung in a week from a term-premium scare to a soft-landing trade, and the macro evidence supports the shift without yet proving it. The Fed kept rates unchanged and Powell hedged on whether the tightening campaign is over [1] , even as the committee upgraded its assessment of growth [2] after a blockbuster third quarter [3] . Then October payrolls rose by only 150,000, less than expected [4] , unemployment ticked up to 3.9% [5] and the ISM manufacturing PMI read 46.7 index points [6] . Our stance is Mixed: slowing growth with easing inflation is ideal for duration and quality growth, but a rising jobless rate after a strong quarter is how late-cycle slowdowns usually begin, and card delinquencies keep climbing [7] .

Rates & Curve: The curve remains inverted: the 2Y is at 4.93%, the 10Y at 4.49%, the 10Y-2Y spread at -44 bps and 10Y-3M at -105 bps (US Treasury, prior close 2023-11-08). Over 20 observations the 2Y is down 6 bps and the 10Y down 9 bps, a bull flattening; over five observations the 10Y fell 28 bps against just 2 bps for the 2Y, so the long end did nearly all the work. Reuters commentary now treats October as a bad bond dream [8] , and Hoisington's Hunt says the bond rally is just getting started [9] . The mechanism matters: a long-end rally that compresses term premium eases financial conditions without any Fed cut, which is why the VIX is at 14.45 points, -14.34% over five sessions, and the S&P 500 is at 4,382.78 points, +3.42% over five sessions (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2023-11-08).

New / Most Important: The fresh risk is fiscal and structural rather than monetary. Speaker Johnson is nearing a choice on how to avoid a government shutdown [10] , BNY Mellon flags Treasury market functioning risks as a key reform looms [11] , and hedge funds are feuding with Gary Gensler over the Treasury market [12] . On the other side, BofA's Moynihan says the economy is headed for a soft landing [13] , and Powell warned that forecasts must stay flexible in a dynamic economy [14] . Equity breadth shows who investors believe: the Nasdaq 100 is +0.47% over 20 sessions while the Russell 2000 is -3.34% (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2023-11-08), so money is buying balance-sheet quality, not the cycle.

Playbook ✓: Own 5Y-10Y Treasuries and add on any back-up toward 4.58%, where the 10Y stood 20 observations ago; pair that with large-cap quality growth rather than small caps. The trigger to extend is a 2Y break below the current 4.93%, which would show the front end starting to price cuts rather than the long end merely shedding term premium. What changes the call: a firm next CPI print after September's 3.7% [15] , or a shutdown fight that revives term premium. With DXY at 105.59 points, -1.21% over five sessions (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2023-11-08), the softer dollar also supports EM duration while payroll momentum cools.

🇨🇳 China  Bearish  Property triage, deflation risk

Macro Stance: China is in a slow-burn, deflation-tinged slowdown that policy is managing rather than reversing. The IMF upgraded its 2023 and 2024 growth forecasts [31] , and a central bank adviser says the economy is still in a medium-speed growth stage [32] , but prices tell a harder story: September consumer prices stalled while factory-gate deflation persisted [33] , and Reuters' Morning Bid frames the next price data as a test of inflation versus disinflation [34] . The WSJ's judgment that China is becoming a problem for investors [35] captures the equity mood. Bearish: activity is stabilising at a lower altitude while property keeps draining household confidence and local-government balance sheets.

PMIs & Activity: The Caixin survey confirmed an October manufacturing slump [36] . Trade was mixed: imports rose in October while exports fell for a sixth straight month [37] . Profits look better than volumes, with September industrial profits extending gains on policy support [38] , and Reuters reports car sales are seen 20% higher in November as makers chase annual goals [39] . That combination fits a domestic, policy-led stabilisation with weak external demand, but it is a volume push into a price war rather than a recovery in pricing power. Foreign brands feel it first: the WSJ reports big Western brands are being squeezed by Chinese belt-tightening [40] , and Yum China fell to a one-year low as quarterly results disappointed [41] .

Credit Impulse: The credit channel remains impaired. Analysts describe Chinese banks' asset quality as stable but expect margin pressure to persist [42] , and East Asia Forum highlights the local-government credit dilemma [43] : the vehicles that once turned easing into infrastructure are themselves balance-sheet constrained. Abroad, China now looks like a creditor under strain, labelled the world's biggest debt collector as poorer nations struggle with its loans [44] , with developing countries owing it at least $1.1 trillion [45] . Nikkei Asia examines a 40% drop in China's US Treasury holdings [46] . Without household borrowing, rate cuts transmit weakly, so we treat easing as a floor rather than a catalyst.

Property: Property has moved into triage. Reuters reports authorities have asked Ping An to take a controlling stake in Country Garden [47] , alongside a factbox on both groups [48] and a list of the measures taken to shore up the indebted sector [49] . The fundamentals justify the urgency: home prices fell at a faster pace in September [50] , new-home prices dropped 0.3% that month [51] , and Golden Week home sales fell 17% against 2022 [52] . A state-directed insurer rescue socialises developer losses onto an insurance balance sheet; it limits contagion but moves risk to Ping An shareholders and does nothing for new-home demand. Nippon.com asks whether China will repeat Japan's deflating-bubble experience [53] , the right question for anyone pricing a quick rebound.

Fiscal, Policy & Trade: Economic policymaking is consolidating around Vice Premier He Lifeng, who now heads the top financial policy bodies [54] , and the NYT summarises the new plan as more semiconductors, less housing [55] . Trade tools are being used as leverage: China tightened exports of graphite, a key battery material [56] , and the Atlantic Council dissects its latest critical-mineral restrictions [57] . Diplomacy is the swing factor, with Biden and Xi set to steal the APEC spotlight with talks to steady ties [58] and the White House aiming for a meeting in San Francisco in November [59] . ChinaFile argues that renminbi settlement of China-Saudi oil trade will insulate that flow from US sanctions [60] . A constructive summit would cut the export-control premium on Chinese tech; a failure would deepen decoupling.

Markets & Flows: Onshore equities are drifting: the Shanghai Composite is at 3,052.37 points, -0.86% over 20 sessions, and ASHR, the CSI 300 tracker, is -2.55% over 20 sessions, while the Hang Seng is at 17,568.46 points after a +2.73% five-session bounce (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2023-11-08). Internet platforms led that bounce, with KWEB +5.47% and Tencent +7.46% over five sessions, yet Baidu's US line is -19.91% and JD -12.82% over 20 sessions: investors pay only for cash generation. Xiaomi's +31.43% over 20 sessions is the outlier, and PDD's +6.53% over five sessions fits the same preference for cash generators. Speculation lives at the fringe, where Bloomberg describes a meme-stock craze built on the dragon zodiac [61] , while some long-only institutions step back, with Vanguard leaving a China partnership while keeping billions invested [62] .

New / Most Important: Two developments matter most. First, the Ping An request [47] is the clearest sign that Beijing prefers engineered rescues to disorderly defaults for systemically large developers. Second, AI-chip substitution is accelerating: Baidu placed an AI chip order with Huawei in a shift away from Nvidia [63] , and Wccftech lists Huawei, Tencent, Baidu, Birentech and Moore Threads as domestic alternatives [64] . For US chip designers that is a slow leak of China revenue; for Chinese platforms it is a capex cost and a capability risk. Alibaba's business split and leadership reshuffle [65] show that restructuring, not growth, is the equity story.

Playbook ✓: Stay underweight China beta through FXI and ASHR, and keep any exposure in cash-generative internet leaders such as Tencent, whose five-session bounce shows how quickly buyers return on better policy news. The trigger to add is a Biden-Xi meeting that delivers concrete easing on export controls plus evidence that the Ping An route stabilises developer funding. What changes the call to Bullish: a turn in producer prices from deflation to stability. Avoid iron-ore-sensitive materials while China's state-backed iron-ore buyer says prices are too high [66] . ⚠ Divergence: the IMF upgrade [31] , rising October imports [37] and the November car-sales outlook argue for a cyclical floor that a Bearish stance could underrate.

Geopolitics & Security: Security cuts both ways. Net effect: defence spending supports domestic industry and weighs on the deficit, while the geopolitical risk premium stays visible but contained.

New / Most Important: The RPP hold is the market-moving fact, and the debate it opens is about how long real rates stay high. Rzeczpospolita argues there is no road back to the world of cheap money [129] , the NBP's projections for 2024 inflation and GDP [130] frame that debate for the incoming government, and economist Marcin Mrowiec warns the inflation target is far on the horizon, at times seemingly beyond it [131] . A hawkish pause plus a strong zloty supports bank net interest income but tightens conditions for exporters: Polish exports are rising, yet the most export-oriented sectors carry more than 3.2 billion zloty in overdue debts [132] .

Playbook ✓: Stay long Polish banks (PKO BP, Pekao, mBank) and Dino through EPOL or the WIG20TR tracker, funded against Orlen and KGHM, where oil and copper are headwinds.
