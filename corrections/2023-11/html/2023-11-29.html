🇺🇸 United States  Mixed  Fed on hold, cuts being priced

Macro Stance: Mixed: disinflation is winning the argument, but markets have already paid for most of the good news. Fed Governor Waller said he is confident inflation is headed back to 2 percent [1] , while Bank of America says interest-rate cuts are coming next year [2] . When a sitting governor sounds that confident, the market stops debating one more hike and starts debating the date of the first cut, which is exactly the shift visible at the front of the curve. Demand is cooling at the margin rather than breaking: Black Friday online spending set a record $9.8 billion, up 7.5% [3] , yet Reuters cited a report showing the softest Black Friday-week sales gain in years even as discounts drew the crowds [4] . Record dollars bought with markdowns is the profile of a consumer still spending but trading down, which supports bonds more cleanly than higher equity multiples.

Rates & Curve: The 2Y Treasury closed at 4.73% and the 10Y at 4.34% (US Treasury, prior close 2023-11-28), leaving 10Y-2Y at -39 bps and 10Y-3M at -113 bps, so the curve shape remains inverted. Over 20 observations the 2Y fell -30 bps from 5.03% and the 10Y fell -54 bps from 4.88%, a bull flattening in which the 10Y-2Y spread moved a further -24 bps. The last five observations look different at the front: the 2Y dropped -16 bps against -8 bps for the 10Y, the policy-sensitive end repricing cut odds rather than reacting to growth. The 3M bill at 5.47% shows the funds rate has not moved, so every basis point of easing is still an expectation. Supply is the counterweight: $7.6 trillion of US government debt matures within a year [5] , and Semafor's 'nobody wants U.S. Treasury bonds' framing [6] is the risk to the duration trade if auctions start to tail.

New / Most Important: The dollar is on pace for its worst month of 2023 on bets the Fed is done raising rates [7] ; DXY closed at 102.75, -3.18% over 20 sessions from 106.12 (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2023-11-28). Easier financial conditions show up everywhere, yet index momentum has stalled: the S&P 500 sits at 4,554.89, +9.31% over 20 sessions but only +0.17% over five, VIX has collapsed to 12.69, down 7.06 points in 20 sessions, and the Russell 2000 is -0.79% over five sessions at 1,792.81. Sell-side optimism is escalating anyway, with Deutsche Bank now predicting a record 5,100 for the S&P 500 in 2024 [8] .

Playbook ✓: Own intermediate duration through IEF (93.38 USD, +4.01% over 20 sessions) rather than the long bond, because the rally is anchored in policy expectations at the front and belly. Keep S&P 500 exposure at benchmark and use VIX at 12.69 to buy cheap downside protection instead of adding beta into a stalled tape. Trigger to extend duration: a 2Y break below 4.73% on a softer core inflation print. What changes the call: a 10Y retracing toward 4.88%, the level 20 observations ago, or a dollar rebound that undoes the DXY's -3.18% month.

🇪🇺 European Union  Mixed  ECB on hold, euro tightening

Macro Stance: Mixed. The euro area is stagnating while its equity markets ride lower global yields. Germany's economy shrank slightly in the third quarter [9] , and its economic challenges are complicating resolution of the budget crisis despite the chancellor's reassurances [10] . The region's largest economy is therefore contracting at the moment its fiscal room is being questioned, which removes the natural stabiliser for a weak 2024 and leaves the ECB as the only macro lever, and the ECB is still talking tough.

Activity & Inflation: Activity data stay soft: the euro zone's October PMI sat near a three-year low, stirring recession worries [11] . The ECB warns that banks are starting to show stress as loan defaults rise [12] , while Governor De Cos calls it premature to talk about rate cuts [13] . That is a central bank holding the line in words while credit quality erodes underneath. It usually ends with policy following the data, and in the meantime European duration benefits from every further fall in US yields.

New / Most Important: The currency is doing some of the tightening: the euro has hit three-month highs while the dollar faces its weakest month in a year [14] . Corporate Europe is restructuring into it, with Volkswagen cutting headcount at a core brand it calls 'no longer competitive' [15] . The Euro Stoxx 50 closed at 4,348.02, +7.06% over 20 sessions, and the DAX at 15,992.67, +7.98% (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2023-11-28), while the dollar-priced FEZ is +11.37%, a reminder that much of the euro-area rally seen from abroad is currency translation.

Playbook ✓: Hold European equity exposure through quality industrial and software names with pricing power, such as Siemens (+21.17% over 20 sessions) and SAP (+12.47%), and stay light on credit-sensitive domestic lenders while the ECB's default warnings build. Trigger to add: PMIs turning up from the October trough. What changes the call: a further euro surge that squeezes exporters' margins, or a German budget fix that restores fiscal impulse.

🇨🇳 China  Bearish  Targeted easing, no bazooka

Macro Stance: Bearish. Beijing is signalling support without the scale that would change the growth trajectory. The PBOC governor pledged to support growth while urging reforms [16] , a pairing that usually signals targeted relending and structural tools rather than broad household transfers. Industrial profit growth slowed, keeping stimulus calls alive [17] , so the private sector is asking for more than the central bank is offering. The policy mix is supply-side: credit to factories, piecemeal property easing and administrative support for share prices. That combination lifts output and exports before it lifts domestic prices or margins, which is why we stay underweight broad China beta and pick single names instead.

PMIs & Activity: A Reuters poll expects November factory activity to have contracted for a second month [18] , so the pending official PMI is expected to confirm rather than reverse the October slump. Trade data tell the same story from the outside: exports fell for a sixth straight month in October while imports rose [19] . Rising imports alongside falling exports is more consistent with input stockpiling than with a consumer recovery. The pattern is a producer economy running ahead of its customers: volumes hold, prices give. For equity investors that means volume-led earnings with little pricing power, a poor mix for cyclical margins and a reason to expect further downgrades in materials and industrial exporters before upgrades arrive.

Credit Impulse: Credit is flowing, but to the wrong end of the economy for a demand recovery. Reuters reports that a surge in high-tech manufacturing loans is raising fears of a wave of cheap exports [20] , a supply impulse that pressures global goods prices rather than reflating China. At the household end, shadow finance is cracking: a Chinese shadow bank admitted a £30bn shortfall after 'management ran wild' [21] , the kind of loss on savings products that makes households more cautious, not less. Directed lending to factories plus losses in savings products is a combination that raises the saving rate at exactly the wrong moment. Until credit shows up in household borrowing and mortgage demand, the impulse supports industrial volumes more than consumer equities.

Property: Property policy is loosening city by city. Shenzhen cut down payments on second homes as cities try to revive demand [22] , and China is weighing unprecedented builder support through first-ever unsecured loans [23] . Buyers have not responded: more Chinese cities recorded falling home prices in October [24] . Unsecured developer lending keeps projects alive and protects suppliers, but it socialises losses onto bank balance sheets rather than restoring buyer confidence, which is the variable that matters for sales. The signal to watch is second-home easing spreading to the other tier-one cities; until then, falling prices keep households in wait-and-see mode and the property drag on growth persists into 2024.

Fiscal, Policy & Trade: Technology controls still bite even as the diplomatic tone softens. Nvidia shares closed lower on a report it delayed a China AI chip designed to comply with US export rules [25] , which means Chinese cloud buyers face both delay and capability limits on imported accelerators. That pushes spending toward domestic suppliers and away from US vendors, a slow structural loss for the latter. Western capital is recalibrating as well: Goldman Sachs is paring back its 'growth at all costs' China strategy [26] , a sign that the investment-banking fee pool from Chinese listings and cross-border deals is not expected to rebuild quickly. For markets, the trade and technology channel matters more right now than any fiscal headline.

Markets & Flows: Onshore equities are being propped rather than bid. Reuters reports the Beijing bourse told 'major shareholders' to refrain from selling [27] , administrative support that limits downside but rarely creates sustained upside. The Shanghai Composite closed at 3,038.55, +0.66% over 20 sessions, while the Hang Seng ended at 17,354.14, -2.14% over five sessions, and FXI lost -3.67% over the same five (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2023-11-28). Dispersion is extreme: PDD jumped +18.08% in a single session and Tencent is +10.51% over 20 sessions, but Alibaba in Hong Kong is -7.12% over 20 sessions and Baidu's ADR fell -3.52% on Tuesday. Offshore money is picking winners, not buying the index, and the onshore market is being held up by regulators rather than by buyers.

New / Most Important: Two capital-markets signals stand out. Shein has filed for a US IPO in a major test of investor appetite [28] , and Meituan launched a $1 bln share buyback [29] ; Chinese consumer platforms are monetising through US listings and buybacks rather than waiting for a domestic re-rating. Huawei is pushing its technology into the EV world with new cars and automaker partnerships [30] , a competitive threat that matters as much to foreign carmakers as to Chinese rivals. Corporate China is adapting to slow growth with capital returns and export ambition, which helps selected stocks but does not fix the macro.

Playbook ✓: Stay underweight broad China beta through FXI and the Hang Seng; express any long only through cash-returning platforms such as Tencent and PDD, or through iron-ore exposure listed outside China. Trigger to cover the underweight: an official PMI back above 50 together with tier-one property easing and evidence of household credit growth. What deepens it: renewed home-price declines or a failed listing window for the Shein deal. ⚠ Divergence: policy is visibly easing, through PBOC pledges, Shenzhen's down-payment cut and exchange guidance against selling, while the data keep deteriorating, with factory activity expected to contract again and home prices still falling; the market is trading the policy, not the data.

🇯🇵 Japan  Mixed  Reflation, BOJ patient

Macro Stance: Mixed. Japan finally has the inflation it spent decades wanting, and Reuters reports investors are tearing up their old playbooks around it [31] . Japanese inflation data were upbeat even as the dollar-yen pair traded sideways [32] , and Oxford Economics expects 2024 wage rises to look as high as this year's [33] , the wage-price loop that would let the BOJ normalise. The policy debate is about timing, not direction. The market has not yet forced it: the Nikkei 225 closed at 33,408.39, +7.80% over 20 sessions but just +0.06% over five (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2023-11-28), and EWJ is +7.38% over 20 sessions in dollar terms.

Activity & Currency: The weak yen is the transmission channel. Singapore travellers are splurging in Japan amid a weakening yen [34] , which flatters tourism and exporters' translated profits while squeezing household real incomes through import prices. Governance is the stronger equity story: Toyota group companies plan a $4.7 billion sale of a Denso stake [35] , the kind of cross-shareholding unwind that can release capital for buybacks and lift return on equity. That gives foreign investors a reason to own Japan without a strong currency view, since buybacks raise per-share earnings whatever the yen does. In semiconductors, Tokyo Electron is +21.83% over 20 sessions and Advantest +12.66%, while Toyota trails at 2,755.00 JPY, +4.42%, and Sony sits at 12,825.00 JPY, +5.82%.

Playbook ✓: Stay long Japan through governance and chip-equipment winners such as Tokyo Electron and Advantest rather than export-heavy autos, because yen weakness is already in prices and any BOJ normalisation would hit currency-driven earnings first. Trigger to add: wage settlements consistent with the Oxford Economics view and the Nikkei 225 holding above 33,408.39. Hedge part of the currency exposure, since a stronger yen would erode dollar returns on unhedged holdings. What changes the call: a sharp yen rebound on a policy shift, which would favour domestic-demand names over exporters.

🇦🇺 Australia  Mixed  RBA hawkish hold, homegrown inflation

Macro Stance: Mixed, leaning hawkish. RBA Governor Bullock says inflation is increasingly homegrown, suggesting rates will remain high [36] , and argues households are coping even as Lowe issues a warning [37] . Consumers are waiting for discounts: October retail sales slipped as shoppers held off for Black Friday [38] , a timing effect that could flatter the next print and complicates the RBA's read. A central bank that sees inflation as homegrown has little reason to ease just because goods prices cool, so the hold is likely to be long even if the next hike never comes.

Demand & Financing: Housing is where demand and financing collide. Experts warn a temporary pull-back in migration might be necessary to relieve rental stress [39] , because population growth is outrunning supply and rents feed directly into homegrown inflation. The external side offers an offset: analysts see iron ore at $150 in 2024 on Chinese stimulus [40] , and the Aussie dollar has hit a three-month high [41] . The ASX 200 closed at 7,015.20, +3.46% over 20 sessions, lagging global peers because banks and miners dominate the index and neither benefits much from falling US yields, with BHP at 46.43 AUD and Commonwealth Bank at 103.34 AUD, +7.02% (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2023-11-28).

Playbook ✓: Prefer Australian banks over miners while homegrown inflation keeps the cash rate high, since net interest margins benefit from a hawkish hold. Trigger to rotate into BHP and Fortescue: a Chinese PMI back above 50 with iron ore confirming the stimulus call. What changes the call: a domestic inflation undershoot that revives cut expectations and compresses bank margins.

🇵🇱 Poland  Bullish  Recovery meets caretaker politics

Macro Stance: Bullish. Poland is the rare European economy where real incomes, credit and confidence are all turning up together. GDP is growing again for the first time in two quarters as the economy 'bounces off the bottom' [42] , and Super Biznes reports the first rise in consumption in 9 months [43] . Wages are rising 'like yeast', which Analizy.pl expects to strengthen consumption [44] : with nominal pay outpacing prices, households regain purchasing power and the recovery becomes consumption-led rather than export-led, which suits domestic banks and retailers. Ipsos finds consumer confidence back near pre-pandemic levels [45] , so households are willing to spend the gains rather than save them. The constraint is that October inflation came in higher than first estimated [46] , which limits the room for further monetary easing and keeps real rates from falling much further.

Markets: Polish equities have been one of the month's strongest trades. EPOL closed at 22.32 USD, +2.53% on the day and +13.13% over 20 sessions, while the Beta ETF WIG20TR tracker is +5.23% over 20 sessions at 40.47 PLN (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2023-11-28); much of the gap between the dollar-priced fund and the zloty-priced tracker reflects the currency contribution. Banks led Tuesday's session, with mBank +3.28%, PKO Bank Polski +2.00% and Pekao +1.76%, while KGHM rose +2.13% to 117.40 PLN, tracking the global metals bid rather than domestic politics. Business Insider Polska writes that the Polish currency 'shines' globally and forecasts still give the zloty room [47] , yet Bankier's currency survey finds analysts unconvinced by an 'era of a strong zloty' [48] . Credit is re-accelerating: mortgage sales are sharply higher on 'government steroids', a quarter 'like the good old days' [49] , which feeds bank volumes but also housing demand.

Fiscal & Politics: Politics is in an awkward interregnum that markets are choosing to look through. OKO.press calls the newly sworn Morawiecki cabinet a government for two weeks that will fall after 14 days [50] , and Gazeta Prawna says it has no chance of a confidence vote and no budget [51] . The 2024 budget that matters will instead be submitted by Donald Tusk's government [52] , so the fiscal plan investors must price is the new coalition's, whose full cabinet list is already circulating [53] . The fiscal prize is EU money: the European Commission accepted Poland's revised KPO recovery plan, with energy the biggest winner [54] . Tusk's flagship pledge to raise pay [55] is the spending risk bond investors will price once the new team takes office; a pay rise funded by EU inflows is benign, one funded by extra issuance is not.

Corporate & Sectors: The KPO approval, with energy named the biggest winner, coincides with a sharp utility rally: PGE is +16.90% over five sessions at 8.45 PLN and Tauron +12.65% at 4.23 PLN. PGE also adds a nuclear leg, as PGE PAK Energia Jądrowa received the decision-in-principle for a KHNP plant in Wielkopolska [56] . CD Projekt earned over 200 mln zł of net profit in Q3 [57] , yet the stock closed -1.51% at 107.95 PLN and is -4.64% over five sessions, so the print lands on a share that has been de-rating into it. Budimex won a Czech D11 motorway contract worth 2 mld zł [58] , a sign that Polish contractors can export capacity while domestic tenders wait for EU money. Retail is split, with Pepco +30.36% over 20 sessions and Dino +13.79% at 444.00 PLN while Allegro is -2.20%, so the consumer recovery is being priced in discount and food retail rather than in e-commerce. JSW is the casualty at -18.27% over five sessions, with Wyborcza reporting that prosecutors slowed a probe into money siphoned out of the coal miner [59] .

Geopolitics & Security: Security spending is structural, not cyclical. Rzeczpospolita reports Poland is building an air shield worth 54 mld zł that will also protect its Patriot batteries [60] . The threat is concrete: Poland charged 16 people after dismantling a Russian spy ring [61] , evidence that the war's spillover risk now extends inside Poland's borders. The Ukrainian relationship also carries economic friction, as Polish truckers started a round-the-clock blockade of a fourth border crossing [62] . For investors the blockade is a logistics and trade-flow risk for exporters and transport names, while defence outlays are a multi-year commitment that a change of government is unlikely to reverse.

New / Most Important: The NBP has become a political battleground. A new NBP banner no longer argues that inflation is gone; it now says Governor Glapiński is 'the best' [63] . Rzeczpospolita reports the NBP pays millions to Glapiński's advisers and will not disclose their names [64] , the kind of disclosure fight that invites scrutiny from the incoming coalition. The bank is also publicly rebutting accusations that its bond purchases were unconstitutional [65] , a defence suggesting the bank expects scrutiny under a new government. For markets the risk is credibility rather than the policy rate: a central bank in open conflict with the incoming government can widen the risk premium on Polish bonds and the currency even as the fundamentals improve.

Playbook ✓: Stay long Polish equities through EPOL and the WIG20TR tracker, overweight banks (PKO, Pekao, mBank) and KPO-linked utilities (PGE, Tauron), sized for headline volatility around the NBP and for legal risk after the Supreme Court voided a loan agreement over a bank's FX tables [66] . Trigger to add: a clean confidence vote for the Tusk cabinet and a 2024 budget that funds the pay pledge without a jump in issuance. What changes the call: a confrontation that threatens NBP independence or a stall in KPO disbursement. ⚠ Divergence: the macro and currency signals are unambiguously positive, but the institutions are in open conflict, with a caretaker cabinet lacking a majority and a central bank campaigning for its own governor, so the political risk premium could rise just as growth improves.
