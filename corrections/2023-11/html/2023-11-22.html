🇺🇸 United States  Mixed  Fed on hold, minutes lean against cuts

Macro Stance: The US has priced a soft landing faster than the Fed is willing to validate it. October CPI was flat on the month with core inflation at a two-year low, which turns disinflation from a forecast into a reported fact [1] . Tuesday's Fed minutes cut across that optimism: they show a consensus that rates need to stay high, and Wall Street ended lower while gold jumped on the cautious tone [2] [3] . The real economy is cooling unevenly. Existing-home sales fell to a 13-year low in October as the housing freeze deepened, the clearest casualty of the rate shock [4] . Our stance is Mixed: the slowdown is orderly and inflation is behaving, but policy relief is being rationed.

Rates & Curve: The bond market has already done much of the easing. The 10Y Treasury closed at 4.41% and the 2Y at 4.86%, leaving the 10Y-2Y spread at -45 bps; over 20 observations the 2Y fell 16 bps from 5.02% and the 10Y fell 42 bps from 4.83%, a bull flattening that deepened the inverted curve by 26 bps (US Treasury, prior close 2023-11-21). The latest five observations carry the warning: the 2Y rose 6 bps while the 10Y slipped 3 bps, the front end resisting the cut bets that softer jobless-claims data had encouraged [5] . The 3M bill at 5.53% still pays more than any longer tenor, so cash competes hard with duration. Risk assets have relaxed in step, with VIX at 13.35, down 29.63% over 20 sessions, and DXY at 103.59 index points, down 2.52% (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2023-11-21).

New / Most Important: Three items reset the tape. First, the minutes framed the debate as how long rates stay restrictive, not how high they go [2] . Second, Nvidia reported that revenue tripled as the AI chip boom continued; its shares last closed at 499.44 USD, up 14.39% over 20 sessions, so the bar was already high (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2023-11-21) [6] . Third, Lowe's missed sales estimates and cut its outlook, a warning for big-ticket home spending [7] . Positioning amplifies all three, with Goldman saying hedge-fund bets on US stocks are the most crowded ever [8] . The S&P 500 closed at 4,538.19, down 0.20% on the session but up 6.84% over 20 sessions, while the Russell 2000 fell 1.32%, a sign that breadth is thinning (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2023-11-21).

Playbook ✓: Action: keep a core long in 7-10Y Treasuries through IEF, up 3.03% over 20 sessions, funded from front-end cash rather than added at any price, and pair it with cheap S&P 500 protection while VIX sits at 13.35 (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2023-11-21). Trigger to add duration: further weakness in housing and claims that pulls the 2Y back below its level of five observations ago. What changes the call: a 2Y back above 5.02%, which would mean the market is re-pricing the hawkish minutes as renewed hiking risk, or a VIX move through its 18.97 reading of 20 sessions ago, a sign of the crowded long unwinding.

🇪🇺 European Union  Bearish  ECB on hold, fiscal brakes tighten

Macro Stance: Europe is stagnating under a central bank that will not yet bless the disinflation. Brussels cut its EU growth forecast again, to just 0.6% for this year, and Christine Lagarde warned against a premature celebration on inflation [9] [10] . Weak activity, restrictive policy and now fiscal retrenchment make this a Bearish macro mix, even though equity indices are behaving as if the cycle has already turned.

Activity & Inflation: Tight policy is still transmitting through credit: euro-zone banks tightened lending standards further in the ECB's survey, which keeps investment and housing under pressure even as headline inflation eases [11] . The fiscal impulse has flipped to a drag. Germany froze government spending as its budget crisis deepened, and the Commission warned France over breaches of its fiscal guidance [12] [13] . Equities have looked through all of it: the Euro Stoxx 50 closed at 4,331.90, up 6.56% over 20 sessions, and the DAX at 15,900.53, up 6.86% (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2023-11-21). It has arrived alongside a 42 bps fall in the US 10Y over 20 observations, a falling-rates tailwind rather than an earnings-led one.

New / Most Important: The budget shock is the new fact: after the court's 60 billion euro blow to the Scholz government, Berlin loses a counter-cyclical support at the moment private demand is weakest, and investment plans that relied on that money are now in limbo [14] . A second political test is pending in the Netherlands, where Bloomberg's investor guide flags tech curbs and a bank tax as the market-relevant planks [15] .

Playbook ✓: Action: underweight German domestic cyclicals and construction-linked names, and prefer euro-area duration to equities for the next leg. Trigger: further spending freezes or a failed budget workaround in Berlin. What changes the call: a credible German fix alongside evidence that bank lending standards are easing, which would turn a rates-driven equity rally into an earnings-backed one. Until then, European equity outperformance is borrowed from US bonds.

🇨🇳 China  Mixed  LPR hold, supply-side credit, property drag

Macro Stance: China is being managed rather than rescued. Benchmark lending rates were left unchanged, as expected, so the price of credit is unchanged even as growth stays soft [16] . The outspoken economist Yu Yongding is flagging stagflation risks while insisting it is not too late to act, which captures the problem: growth is soft, prices are falling and the response is incremental [17] . We hold a Mixed stance. The external backdrop has improved, with the yuan near a four-month high on a firmer fixing and steadier China-US ties, but the domestic demand floor that would justify a Bullish call has not been laid [18] .

PMIs & Activity: The cycle is soft rather than collapsing. The Caixin survey confirmed an October manufacturing slump, exports fell for a sixth straight month even as imports rose, and consumer prices slipped back into decline as the recovery wobbled [19] [20] [21] . The rising-imports, falling-exports combination is the more constructive detail, because it says domestic demand for inputs is holding up while external orders fade. Falling consumer prices are the less constructive one: deflation raises real borrowing costs for exactly the indebted developers and local governments that need relief, and it erodes the nominal revenue from which their debts are serviced. Until consumer prices stop falling, any improvement in activity data will be read as volume bought with margin.

Credit Impulse: Credit is flowing, but to the supply side. Reuters reports that loans to high-tech manufacturing are raising fears of a wave of cheap exports, an impulse that builds capacity rather than household spending power [22] . A cheap yuan has catapulted China to the second-biggest trade-funding currency, so offshore use of renminbi credit is rising alongside onshore lending [23] . What is missing is a demand-side impulse. An unchanged loan prime rate leaves mortgage costs where they were, and mortgage demand depends on a confidence that a rate cut alone could not restore [16] . Until credit reaches households, the impulse lifts output and exports rather than prices, which is why China is exporting deflation to its trading partners instead of curing it at home.

Property: Property remains the binding constraint. The WSJ reports that China's problem with unfinished homes keeps getting bigger, and property investment kept falling through the first ten months of the year [24] [25] . Moody's cut Longfor to below investment grade; a cut across that threshold means stress is migrating up the quality curve toward developers that were still considered investable [26] . Unfinished homes are the core of the problem because buyers in China typically pay before completion, so every stalled project destroys trust in presales, the sector's main funding source. Policy that finances completions would address that directly; policy that simply trims rates would not.

Fiscal, Policy & Trade: The diplomatic temperature has dropped, which matters more for risk premia than for growth. The Biden-Xi talks were blunt but yielded deals on military communication and fentanyl, lowering the odds of an accident-driven escalation that would hit Hong Kong-listed technology hardest [27] . Trade friction has not gone away. Directed lending is building high-tech manufacturing capacity faster than domestic demand can absorb it, and the overflow goes abroad [22] . A rising export push combined with subsidised capacity is the classic recipe for tariff retaliation, so Chinese exporters' margins face a political ceiling even as their volumes grow. Fiscal policy is the missing lever: without central-government spending aimed at households, the burden stays on credit tools that are running into diminishing returns.

Markets & Flows: Onshore equities are drifting while offshore tech is re-rated name by name. The Shanghai Composite closed at 3,067.93, up 3.57% over 20 sessions, the Hang Seng at 17,733.89, up 4.37%, and the CSI 300 tracker ASHR is up 3.04% (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2023-11-21). Dispersion is the trade: Tencent is up 14.77% over 20 sessions after robust revenue growth from games and advertising, while Alibaba's US shares are down 5.88% over five sessions after it scrapped its cloud spin-off citing the US chip export ban (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2023-11-21) [28] [29] . JD.com is up 11.21% over 20 sessions and PDD 5.71% over five, so investors are still paying for platforms with clean execution. KWEB lost 2.51% and FXI 1.99% in the last session, which says index-level enthusiasm is thin and money is selective.

New / Most Important: The newest signal is that policy is steadying the currency and commodity demand rather than housing. Copper reached a two-month peak on China demand hopes, and the yuan's four-month high lowers the cost of imported inputs for Chinese manufacturers [30] [18] . These are confidence votes, not proof of recovery. Alibaba's setback is the counterweight: analysts cut its price targets by up to 25% after the restructuring snag, showing that US technology controls now cap the valuation of China's platform champions [31] .

Playbook ✓: Action: own China through the cash-generative, policy-favoured channel, Tencent over Alibaba, and keep developers and banks underweight; express any reflation view through industrial metals and the yuan rather than real estate. Trigger to add: a demand-side housing measure or a cut in benchmark lending rates. What changes the call: another downgrade of a higher-quality developer, or the yuan surrendering its four-month high as US-China tension returns. ⚠ Divergence: markets are pricing a turn, with the yuan near a four-month high and Tencent up 14.77% over 20 sessions, yet property investment is still falling and consumer prices are back in deflation [25] [21] .

🇯🇵 Japan  Mixed  BOJ exit debate, yen turning

Growth, however, just contracted by the most in two years after expanding 4.8% in April-June, so the BOJ faces a stagflation-shaped choice rather than a clean exit [34] . We hold a Mixed stance: corporate earnings power is intact, but the currency tailwind that inflated exporter profits is turning, and a central bank that tightens into a contraction would weigh on domestic cyclicals.

Activity & Currency: MarketWatch notes the once-invincible dollar is now sliding against both the yen and the yuan [35] . Exporters feel that first: Toyota closed at 2,735 JPY, down 3.82% over five sessions, even though Japan's exports grew better than expected as auto shipments climbed (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2023-11-21) [36] . Chip-equipment makers are the offset, with Tokyo Electron up 20.46% and Advantest up 15.61% over 20 sessions as they ride the AI capital-spending cycle, while the Nikkei 225 closed at 33,354.14, up 7.60% (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2023-11-21). Sony, up 3.66% over 20 sessions, sits between the two camps, with entertainment revenue likely less sensitive to the exchange rate than car exports.

Playbook ✓: Action: hold Japan through chip-equipment leaders and domestic financials rather than yen-sensitive autos, and hedge the currency only partially, since a stronger yen is the trade Pimco is already positioning for. Trigger: any BOJ move beyond the yield-control settings its members saw no need to tweak in September would favour banks and pressure exporters [37] . What changes the call: a renewed dollar rally that reverses the yen's gains, or a second weak GDP print that forces the BOJ to wait.

🇵🇱 Poland  Bullish  NBP under political fire, złoty strong

Macro Stance: Poland is the strongest macro story in our coverage. Bankier reports real wages rising at the fastest pace in years with nominal pay at a record, and Polish industry has finally swung back into positive territory [44] [45] . The economy avoided a technical recession in the third quarter, even if, in Bankier's words, the GDP statistics went a little haywire [46] . Inflation is the residual risk: the statistics office GUS recalculated October inflation, and it came in slightly higher than first reported [47] . Combine rising real incomes, recovery-fund money that Brussels is now releasing and a strong złoty and the result is a consumption-led recovery that also lifts bank earnings through loan volumes and deposit margins [48] . We are Bullish on Polish assets, with a political premium priced around the central bank.

Markets: The złoty is the market's verdict on the election. Rzeczpospolita says the currency is impressing with its strength, with the euro the cheapest since September 2020 [49] . A strong currency is doing part of the central bank's work by lowering import prices, the cleanest route to disinflation without further rate cuts. In equities, the WIG20 total-return ETF closed at 40.60 PLN, up 10.93% over 20 sessions, and the MSCI Poland ETF (EPOL) at 21.79 USD, up 14.26%, the wider dollar gain partly reflecting the currency, though EPOL fell 1.13% in the last session (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2023-11-21). Banks led: PKO BP is up 17.78%, mBank 17.49% and Pekao 16.86% over 20 sessions. The consumer trade is not uniform, with Allegro down 6.22% over five sessions and LPP down 2.08% in the last session. Treasury bonds also had a boom month in October, a sign of firm domestic demand for high nominal yields [50] .

Fiscal & Politics: The fiscal story has flipped from blockade to inflow. OKO.press reports it is official that 5 billion euros from the recovery plan will flow to Poland despite unmet milestones, a political signal from Brussels as power changes hands [48] . The handover itself is messy. Mateusz Morawiecki has named a date to present his own cabinet, while Newsweek is already sketching how a Tusk government would split the ministries [51] [52] . The bill is large: in Rzeczpospolita, Kotecki says Poland has the second-highest deficit in the EU and the European Commission will react [53] . A new coalition therefore inherits both the inflow and the deficit, and its first budget will show whether EU money substitutes for domestic borrowing or simply adds to spending. Bond investors will judge it on that distinction.

Corporate & Sectors: Corporate news points to a less state-directed Poland. Wyborcza reports that Orlen will not take over the Puławy nitrogen works, a rebuff for Obajtek [54] . Orlen closed at 64.05 PLN, down 1.55% over 20 sessions, the only large cap in the group to lose ground, just as the Puławy rebuff closes one route for expansion (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2023-11-21). KGHM hosted analysts on November 20 and 21 and is up 5.56% over five sessions at 118.65 PLN, alongside a 3.58% five-session gain in copper [55] . CD Projekt announced Cyberpunk 2077: Ultimate Edition for release before year-end, a low-cost way to monetise an existing franchise [56] . In retail, brokers cut Pepco's target price by more than half even as the stock rose 9.89% over five sessions, while Dino, up 18.45% over 20 sessions, is the cleaner play on rising real wages [57] . PZU, up only 4.54% over 20 sessions, lags the banks, a reminder that the rally is concentrated in rate-sensitive lenders.

Geopolitics & Security: Security is a structural tailwind for defence spending and a tail risk for sentiment. Interia cites a report warning that NATO has five years to be ready, a timeline that justifies multi-year procurement rather than one-off purchases [58] . The friction point is the Ukrainian border, where truck protests have left Ukrainian drivers stranded [59] . That dispute is an early test of how an incoming government balances domestic lobbies against its pro-Ukraine security line, and it carries a cost for logistics and transport businesses exposed to the corridor. For investors, the security premium shows up less in the złoty than in the valuation of defence-adjacent industrials and in the budget, where higher military spending competes with social promises for the same fiscal space. Large procurement signed by the outgoing government could also be reviewed by its successor, and any cancellation would hit the domestic defence industry first.

New / Most Important: The central bank has become the political front line. Fakty TVN reports that NBP president Adam Glapiński could face the State Tribunal over the bank's bond purchases, and Marta Kightley argues such charges would be an assault on the bank's independence [60] [61] . The NBP meanwhile keeps adding gold to its reserves [62] . For investors the issue is credibility: a politicised fight over the governor can cap the złoty's rally even as fundamentals improve, and it muddies any easing path at a moment when October inflation was revised up [47] . A central bank under legal threat tends to err toward caution, which supports the currency in the short run but raises the risk premium on Polish bonds.

Playbook ✓: Action: stay overweight Polish banks and domestic consumer names such as Dino and LPP on the wage and EU-funds tailwind, funded from Orlen and state-energy exposure. Trigger to add: a sworn-in government and the first recovery-fund transfer. What changes the call: the State Tribunal push turning into a parliamentary vote, or a Commission deficit procedure that forces front-loaded tightening. Banks carry one legal tail: law firms are going after WIBOR, though court decisions have so far disappointed them [63] . ⚠ Divergence: the złoty and bank stocks are pricing a clean institutional reset, yet the second-highest deficit in the EU and the fight over the central bank's president point to a noisy and costly transition [53] .
