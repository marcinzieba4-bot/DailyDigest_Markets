🌍 Geographic Macro Overview

🇺🇸 United States  Bullish  Fed on hold; disinflation confirmed

Macro Stance: The disinflation case has moved from hope to data. October CPI was flat on the month with core at a two-year low, per CNBC, and J.P. Morgan puts headline inflation at 3.2% [1] [2] . Producer prices slid 0.5%, the biggest drop since 2020, and retail sales fell, adding to signs the economy is cooling after hot Q3 growth [3] [4] . With the Fed holding rates on November 1 and Powell hedging on whether the tightening campaign is over, the policy baseline is a long hold rather than another hike [5] . We are Bullish on duration and a broadening rally, because a cooling consumer is what lets the Fed stop. The risk is that the same data morph into a recession narrative, which The Economist already raises through a famous rule [6] .

Rates & Curve: The Treasury curve has bull-flattened. The 2Y sits at 4.88% and the 10Y at 4.44%, leaving 10Y-2Y at -44 bps and the curve still inverted (US Treasury, prior close 2023-11-17). Over 20 observations the 2Y fell 19 bps and the 10Y fell 49 bps, so the spread moved -30 bps; over five observations the 2Y dropped 16 bps and the 10Y 17 bps. The 3M bill at 5.50% keeps 10Y-3M at -106 bps, a reminder that cash still out-yields duration. Reuters says slowing inflation has boosted hopes the Fed is done with rate hikes [7] , and Business Insider reports investors have not been this bullish on bonds since the depths of the Great Financial Crisis [8] . The conflicting signal is supply: the WSJ asks where the foreign buyers of Treasury debt have gone [9] , so the long end could re-steepen if domestic demand tires.

New / Most Important: Equities have already front-run the pause. The S&P 500 closed at 4,514.02, up 2.24% over five sessions and 6.86% over 20, the Russell 2000 gained 5.42% in five sessions, and the VIX sank to 13.80, down 36.43% over 20 sessions (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2023-11-17). The dollar index fell 1.93% over five sessions to 103.82, a slide Reuters tied directly to cooling inflation [10] . Biden signed a stopgap bill averting a shutdown, removing one tail risk [11] . The fresh shock is corporate: OpenAI ousted chief executive Sam Altman [12] , and Microsoft fell 1.68% on the session as the news broke, with options activity jumping [13] .

Playbook ✓: Action: stay long intermediate Treasuries through IEF, up 3.42% over 20 sessions, and rotate equity risk toward rate-sensitive breadth such as small caps and regional banks rather than chasing megacaps. Trigger to add: another soft inflation print that keeps the 2Y well below the 5.07% it held on 2023-10-20. What changes the call: a re-acceleration that pushes the 10Y back toward 4.93%, or a growth scare fast enough to vindicate the strategist who told Business Insider the economy is at the very onset of a recession [14] . With the VIX at 13.80, index puts are cheap insurance for the growth-scare branch, not a reason to sell the rally.

Macro Stance: Europe is a stagnation-with-relief story. The ECB kept rates on hold in October for the first time in over a year [15] , and Bloomberg quotes Vujcic saying the ECB is finished with hikes for now [16] . CNBC warns German housebuilding is collapsing and could drag the wider economy down [21] . The offset is labour: employment rose even as GDP shrank [22] , which keeps services inflation sticky and argues against early cuts, while slowing energy price rises help the headline rate [23] . Across the Channel, The Guardian says a fall in retail sales in Great Britain signals a high-street recession [24] , so the consumer squeeze is pan-European.

New / Most Important: The new shock is fiscal. Germany's top court dealt a 60 billion euro budget blow to the Scholz government by annulling the repurposing of COVID funds [25] [26] , forcing a reshuffle of climate policy money [27] . Corporate news is firmer: Siemens beat earnings forecasts while warning sales growth will slow in 2024 [28] , and its shares jumped 13.50% over five sessions. Lagarde used the week to call for a single European market watchdog to push capital markets union [29] .

Playbook ✓: Action: hold Europe at neutral, expressed through quality industrials and electrification exposure rather than banks or autos. Trigger to upgrade: evidence that Berlin replaces the annulled fund through a budget workaround while credit standards stop tightening. What changes the call: a renewed PMI slide or an ECB that pushes back against easing hopes, either of which would cap the 7.85% rerating already in the Euro Stoxx 50.

🇨🇳 China  Mixed  Targeted easing; property drags

Macro Stance: China is a two-speed economy and markets are pricing the slow lane. October factory output and consumption beat forecasts, but Reuters stresses that property is still a drag [30] , consumer prices are back in decline [31] , and the WSJ argues Xi can no longer brag about the economy [32] . Beijing's response remains targeted rather than a bazooka. We stay Mixed: activity has stabilised enough to remove the hard-landing tail, yet the confidence channel, visible in the Alibaba sell-off and spreading developer downgrades, is still broken. The investable edge is in policy-supported onshore exposure, not offshore platforms.

PMIs & Activity: The surveys lag the hard data. Caixin's PMI confirmed an October manufacturing slump [33] , and exports fell for a sixth straight month even as imports rose [34] . Yet CNBC reports October retail sales and industrial output grew faster than expected [35] , and September industrial profits extended gains helped by policy support [36] . The mix points to domestic demand stabilising off a low base while external demand fades. Deflation is the warning: the SCMP quotes analysts saying the situation under the hood is not as disastrous as headline CPI suggests [37] , but falling prices still raise real debt burdens for developers and local governments. The Shanghai Composite's 2.39% 20-session gain to 3,054.37 index points is consistent with stabilisation, not acceleration (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2023-11-17).

Credit Impulse: Credit is being steered, not unleashed. The central bank boosted its liquidity injection through a policy loan while leaving the rate unchanged [38] , a sign it prefers quantity support to price cuts. Reuters, citing Bloomberg News, reports Beijing is eyeing $137 bln of new funding to boost the housing market [39] , money that would flow through policy channels rather than household borrowing. Where credit grows fastest it goes to factories: Reuters warns high-tech manufacturing loans raise fears of a wave of cheap exports [40] . That composition matters, because supply-side credit exports deflation abroad and does little for consumer confidence at home. The impulse is therefore positive for capacity and metals volumes but weak for margins.

Property: Property remains the binding constraint. Property investment kept decreasing through the first 10 months [41] , more cities recorded falling home prices in October [42] , and CNBC reckons unfinished projects are 20 times the size of Country Garden [43] . The WSJ says the unfinished-homes problem keeps getting bigger [44] , and Moody's downgraded Longfor below investment grade [45] , extending stress to developers once seen as survivors. Policy is shifting toward urban redevelopment, which the New York Times describes as bulldozing old neighbourhoods to revive the economy [46] , and analysts polled by Reuters say Beijing must pull multiple levers for a turnaround [47] . Until funding completes presold homes, buyer confidence will not return.

Fiscal, Policy & Trade: Diplomacy bought a pause, not a reset. Biden and Xi's blunt talks yielded deals on military contacts and fentanyl [48] , but Biden called Xi a dictator afterwards [49] . China pledged to attract more foreign investment and boost consumption [50] , a response to the BBC's finding that businesses are pulling billions in profits out of the country [51] . Trade frictions cut both ways: Beijing tightened exports of graphite, a key battery material [52] , and Reuters reports Trump vows to kill the Asia trade deal Biden is pursuing if elected [53] . On the positive side, Asia Financial says the yuan has passed the euro to become the second top trade currency [54] .

Markets & Flows: Offshore flows are voting against the platforms. Alibaba scrapped its cloud spin-off citing the US chip export ban [55] , and its Hong Kong shares dropped 9.96% on the session to 73.25 HKD, now down 7.45% over 20 sessions. Tencent fell 2.96% the same day despite logging robust revenue growth [56] , and the Hang Seng lost 2.12% to 17,454.19 index points (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2023-11-17). Onshore and broader proxies held up better: ASHR rose 3.95% and KWEB 7.88% over 20 sessions. Institutional positioning is light; the SCMP reports Bridgewater sold Xpeng, Yum and TSMC as hedge funds cut China bets [57] . That underweight is fuel for any policy surprise, but also the reason rallies fade quickly.

New / Most Important: The Alibaba U-turn is the signal of the week. Reuters says it lopped $20 billion off the group's market value [58] , as Jack Ma's family trust prepared to sell 10 mln shares [59] , although Ma's office says he is positive on Alibaba and will continue to hold [60] . Tencent says it holds an Nvidia AI chip stockpile but is seeking to buy domestic [61] , confirming that export controls now shape corporate strategy. Meanwhile The Economist expects EVs to make China the world's biggest car exporter [62] , the clearest growth engine that does not depend on property.

Playbook ✓: Action: hold onshore A-share exposure through ASHR and policy-sensitive materials rather than offshore internet, and keep Alibaba underweight until management replaces the abandoned cloud listing with a clear capital-return plan. Trigger to upgrade: confirmation that the $137 bln housing fund is deployed to finish stalled projects [39] , alongside a second month of retail-sales beats. What changes the call: renewed developer defaults or a further cut to the US chip channel. ⚠ Divergence: iron ore is holding a two-year high according to GMK Center [63] , so bulk commodities are pricing a construction recovery, while consumer prices fall and Moody's downgrades keep moving through developers such as Longfor [45] ; metals are discounting a recovery that household confidence has not yet confirmed.

Playbook ✓: Action: stay long Japanese equities through EWJ, up 7.26% over 20 sessions, with an overweight in semiconductor equipment, and hedge part of the currency exposure. Trigger to add: a pullback in Tokyo Electron toward the 20,180 JPY it held on 2023-10-19 while AI-related tool demand holds. What changes the call: a disorderly yen rebound if US easing hopes accelerate, which would hit exporters such as Toyota, or a BOJ exit from yield control that pulls domestic money home. SoftBank, down 4.89% over 20 sessions, shows leverage is still punished.

🇵🇱 Poland  Bullish  NBP on hold; coalition handover

The labour market is tight, with PropertyNews.pl placing Poland on the EU podium for the lowest unemployment [89] . Stance: Bullish on domestic assets, because the political change unlocks EU money and compresses the risk premium faster than inflation can disappoint.

Markets: Prices are already moving. The złoty is the macro confirmation: Rzeczpospolita says the currency impresses with its strength, with the euro at its cheapest against it since September 2020 [91] , and Next Gazeta reports the złoty at its strongest in years, another barrier broken [92] . Households are locking in yields as well, with Business Insider Polska describing an October boom in treasury savings bonds [93] , and retail equity participation is widening as XTB rolls out a passive product that Bankier.pl calls good news for ETF investors [94] .

Fiscal & Politics: The transition is messy but directionally positive. Mateusz Morawiecki submitted his government's resignation, using his Sejm speech to warn against the EU [95] , and is now trying to assemble a cabinet: he promises a government within 7-8 days and an offer to opposition MPs [96] , naming Third Way and Confederation as partners [97] . Money.pl carries the line 'I don't know whether anyone has received a proposal' [98] , so markets treat this as a procedural detour before a Tusk-led coalition. The fiscal inheritance is the real issue. The Institute of Public Finance says the outgoing government planned shocking spending cuts it calls budget fraud [99] , MPC member Ludwik Kotecki says Poland has the second-highest deficit in the EU and expects a European Commission reaction [100] , and Grzegorz Kołodko calls the 2024 budget the coalition's first test [101] . The upside: Business Insider Polska reports the EU will unblock the KPO recovery funds for Tusk as an encouragement, though with a catch [102] .

Corporate & Sectors: Corporate news splits into domestic winners and policy-exposed laggards. Allegro plans to enter more markets after a good third quarter [103] , yet the stock fell 5.46% over five sessions to 29.20 PLN, a gap that rewards patient buyers. Dino has opened its first fuel station [104] , and Portal spożywczy says its shelf prices are shaming the discounters [105] ; the shares are up 17.76% over 20 sessions. Orlen is the laggard, down 5.65% over 20 sessions to 62.26 PLN; Money.pl reports that Americans are not fulfilling a contract with the group [106] , and a fire temporarily halted asphalt output at Płock [107] . CD Projekt co-founder Michał Kiciński sold part of his stake and pb.pl noted the share price fell [108] , but the studio says a Cyberpunk sequel is already in preparation [109] . Banks' legal overhang is easing: law firms are going after WIBOR, but court rulings so far disappoint them [110] , while PKO's pay-later service has passed 150 thousand activated clients [111] . LPP is up 17.58% over 20 sessions and KGHM 9.30% over five on firmer copper.

Geopolitics & Security: Security spending is a structural support for industry, while the border is a near-term irritant. Polish truck protests have left Ukrainian drivers stranded [112] , and GMK Center warns the blockade of crossings threatens Ukraine's entire economy [113] . Defence cooperation is deepening: PGZ will build a super-radar for the Patriot system [114] , Northrop Grumman is exploring ammunition co-production in Poland [115] , and a US official says major Ukrainian F-16 maintenance will take place in Poland [116] . Poland has deployed a new tank battalion near the Belarus border [117] , Interia relays a report that NATO has five years to be ready [118] , and Polsat flags Russian propaganda claiming Ukraine is preparing an attack on Poland [119] . Russia's Duma has adopted a budget with record defence spending [120] .

New / Most Important: The most market-relevant development is the fight around the central bank. Rzeczpospolita asks whether the NBP has reached a ceasefire or the end of its internal war [121] , Kotecki accuses Glapiński of breaking the law [122] , and Demagog examines whether the governor could face the State Tribunal [123] . MPC member Łukasz Hardt calls communication the NBP's weak side [124] . For investors, a politicised central bank is the main threat to the złoty's run, because a dovish surprise against sticky inflation would undercut the currency strength that is currently lowering import prices.

Playbook ✓: Action: stay overweight Polish banks and domestic consumer names (PKO, Pekao, Dino, LPP) through EPOL or the WIG20TR tracker, and use Allegro weakness to build a position. Trigger to add: a failed confidence vote for Morawiecki's new cabinet that hands power to the Tusk coalition, followed by a formal KPO unblocking. What changes the call: a dovish NBP move that reverses the złoty, or an EU deficit procedure that forces tightening into a weak year; wnp.pl quotes a well-known economist predicting that 2024 will be a lost year [125] . ⚠ Divergence: equities and the złoty price an orderly handover and fresh EU cash, while inflation came in higher than estimated [126] , the deficit is the second-highest in the EU [100] , and the central bank is in open conflict with the incoming majority.
