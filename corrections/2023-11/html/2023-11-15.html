🇺🇸 United States  Bullish  Fed on hold; disinflation repricing

Macro Stance: October's consumer-price report did what weeks of Fed caution could not: it gave markets permission to treat the hiking cycle as finished. Prices were flat on the month and core inflation hit a two-year low, per CNBC [1] , while the annual rate cooled to 3.2% as gasoline fell, per U.S. News [2] . Reuters framed the print as boosting hopes the Fed is done [3] , days after Powell leaned hawkish in IMF remarks [4] and said the Fed 'will not hesitate' to tighten further [5] . We side with the data over the rhetoric. The S&P 500 closed at 4,495.70, up 1.91% on the session and 2.80% over 20 sessions, while the Russell 2000 jumped 5.44% to 1,798.32 (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2023-11-14). Small caps beating the Nasdaq 100's 2.13% gain is the signature of a rates trade, not an AI trade; a 5.49% jump in IWM suggests investors were underweight the assets most exposed to funding costs.

Rates & Curve: The 2Y closed at 4.80% and the 10Y at 4.44% (US Treasury, prior close 2023-11-14), leaving 10Y-2Y at -36 bps and 10Y-3M at -108 bps; the curve shape remains inverted. Over 20 observations the 2Y and the 10Y both fell 39 bps, a parallel rally with the spread unchanged, and over five observations the 2Y is down 11 bps and the 10Y down 14 bps. The 30Y, at 4.61%, has dropped 33 bps. That symmetry matters: markets are pricing a lower policy path and a smaller term premium at once, unwinding what Reuters now calls October's bad bond dream [6] . The dollar index fell 1.47% to 104.11 points and VIX dropped to 14.16 (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2023-11-14), as Reuters reported the dollar sinking on the data [7] . Moody's negative outlook on US credit [8] and a 30-year auction that broke bad [9] are reminders that supply, not only inflation, set the long end in October.

New / Most Important: Beyond CPI, the House passed short-term funding legislation to avoid a shutdown and sent the bill to the Senate [10] , shrinking an immediate fiscal tail. Fundstrat's Tom Lee argues the 'face-ripper' rally will continue because inflation may have hit a wall [11] , and Michael Burry closed his bets against the S&P 500 and Nasdaq [12] . The counterweights are The Economist's warning that a famous rule points to recession [13] and MarketWatch's question whether record credit-card debt is a red flag [14] .

Playbook ✓: Action: overweight rate-sensitive US equities (IWM, KRE, XLRE) against a neutral mega-cap weight, and hold intermediate Treasuries. Trigger to add: the 10Y holding below 4.83%, its level 20 observations ago, with VIX below 17.88. What changes the call: a 2Y back toward 5.19%, or a hot next CPI print that revives Powell's hiking bias, which would hit small caps hardest.

🇨🇳 China  Mixed  Targeted easing vs property drag

Macro Stance: China is easing at the margin while the private sector keeps voting with its feet. The policy direction is clear: Beijing is eyeing $137 bln of new funding to boost the housing market, per Bloomberg News via Reuters [28] , and Bloomberg reports a 2024 budget deficit seen above 3.5% in a break with past practice [29] . The private signal runs the other way: the BBC documents businesses pulling billions in profits out of China [30] , and the WSJ argues Xi can no longer brag about the economy [31] . Our stance is Mixed. Policy is turning, but it is chasing a deflationary property and household balance-sheet problem rather than leading a new cycle.

PMIs & Activity: The cyclical data are soft. Caixin's survey confirmed October's manufacturing slump [32] , exports fell for a sixth straight month even as imports rose [33] , and consumer prices are back in decline as the recovery wobbles [34] , renewing deflation fears per the New York Times [35] . The South China Morning Post argues that under the hood the inflation picture is less disastrous [36] , and September industrial profits extended gains on policy support [37] . Consumption is patchy: Reuters Breakingviews sees a Singles Day commitment issue among buyers [38] , while car sales are seen 20% higher in November as makers chase sales goals [39] . The IMF has nonetheless raised its China GDP forecasts [40] , and a central bank advisor tells Reuters the economy is still in a medium-speed growth stage [41] .

Credit Impulse: Credit is flowing, but to the wrong end of the economy for reflation. Reuters reports that loans to high-tech manufacturing are raising fears of a wave of cheap exports [42] , echoing the WSJ's verdict that China is making too much stuff [43] and its account of green-energy spending causing a global glut [44] . Supply-side credit adds capacity and exports deflation; it does not lift household demand. The proposed $137 billion housing package [45] is the first credit aimed at the demand-side bottleneck. Capital is also leaking: the Sydney Morning Herald reports wealthy Chinese using strangers to sneak cash out of the country [46] . Until lending reaches household balance sheets, the impulse stays supply-heavy and disinflationary for the rest of the world.

Property: Property remains the binding constraint. Reuters reported that authorities asked Ping An to take a controlling stake in Country Garden [47] , while the developer's sales remain at deflated levels amid collapsed demand [48] . CNBC puts China's unfinished projects at 20 times the size of Country Garden [49] , which frames the scale of the completion problem. New home prices fell 0.3% in September [50] , and the 'three major projects' are Beijing's designated overhaul of real estate development [51] . Iron ore is the market's tell: a Reuters column says it defies commodity gloom amid property hopes [52] . A rescue routed through a large insurer transfers risk rather than reviving buyers, so we treat it as stabilization, not recovery.

Fiscal, Policy & Trade: Fiscal policy is where the regime may shift, because a deficit above 3.5% would break an informal ceiling [29] . Diplomacy is the near-term catalyst: Xi is in the US for a high-stakes summit with Biden at APEC [53] , and Bloomberg quotes Biden saying the US wants better ties to help China's economy [54] . Friction persists underneath. Wingtech is selling a British wafer factory under UK pressure [55] , Nvidia will reportedly sell new chips to China that still meet US rules [56] , Beijing has tightened exports of graphite [57] , and The Economist expects EVs to make China the world's biggest car exporter [58] , a magnet for trade defense in Europe and the US.

Markets & Flows: Onshore equities are drifting: the Shanghai Composite closed at 3,056.07, down 0.89% over 20 sessions, and the Hang Seng at 17,396.86, down 1.55% over five sessions (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2023-11-14). US-listed proxies did better on the weaker dollar, with FXI up 2.15% and KWEB up 2.10% on the session. Institutional appetite looks thin, with the WSJ saying China is becoming a problem for investors [59] . Reuters Breakingviews sums it up as the Wall Street tortoise overtaking the Chinese hare [60] . Alibaba's Hong Kong line fell 4.75% over five sessions while Xiaomi has gained 26.48% over 20, a market paying for product cycles rather than macro. In New York, the JD.com ADR rose 3.73% and Alibaba's 1.96%, while Baidu is down 8.53% over 20 sessions.

New / Most Important: The housing funding report [28] and the Biden-Xi meeting are the two developments that can redirect flows. Time describes both leaders as seeking to turn back the clock at the San Francisco summit [61] ; any easing in chip-control or tariff rhetoric would relieve the discount on Chinese tech. Platform earnings are not the problem: Tencent Music beat revenue estimates despite a China gambling hit [62] .

Playbook ✓: Action: stay underweight China equity, but tilt the mix toward policy beneficiaries such as iron-ore-linked materials and property-completion supply chains, and away from consumption platforms until household credit turns. Trigger to upgrade: evidence that the housing funding is being deployed alongside a deficit above 3.5% [29] , with FXI holding its session gain. What changes the call: a summit failure or tighter chip rules, which would push the Hang Seng lower again. ⚠ Divergence: iron ore and US-listed China ETFs are pricing a property turn while consumer prices are back in decline and Country Garden sales remain depressed; one of these signals is wrong, and the deflation data have the longer track record.

New / Most Important: The GDP turn and the fate of the NBP conflict will decide the next leg. Playbook ✓: Action: overweight Polish equities through EPOL and the WIG20TR tracker, with PKO BP and Pekao as the core and the consumer names that rose near 20% over 20 sessions trimmed.
