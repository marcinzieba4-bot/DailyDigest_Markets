🌍 Geographic Macro Overview

🇺🇸 United States  Mixed  Fed on hold, hedging

Macro Stance: The first week of November flipped the narrative from higher-for-longer to peak rates. The Fed held for a second straight meeting while upgrading its read on growth [1] , and Powell hedged on whether the tightening campaign is over [2] . Then the labour data cracked: payrolls rose 150,000 in October, less than expected [3] , unemployment rose to 3.9% [4] , and the WSJ called it a sharp hiring slowdown [5] . Manufacturing stayed in contraction, with the ISM gauge at 46.7 index points [6] . The S&P 500 closed at 4,358.34 index points, up 5.85% over five sessions, and the VIX fell to 14.91 index points, down 29.90% over the same span (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2023-11-03). Our stance is Mixed: the rally is a rate trade, and we are not yet convinced it is a growth trade.

Rates & Curve: The 2Y closed at 4.83% and the 10Y at 4.57%, leaving 10Y-2Y at -26 bps (US Treasury, prior close 2023-11-03). Over five observations the 10Y fell 27 bps and the 2Y 16 bps; over twenty observations the 2Y is down 20 bps and the 10Y down 15 bps, a bull steepening of an inverted curve. The 10Y-3M spread sits at -96 bps, so bills still out-yield the long end. Supply is the cross-current: Treasury is increasing the size of most of its debt auctions [7] to manage a growing debt load [8] , and the WSJ was decoding an almost 5% 10-year yield only days earlier [9] . The dollar index fell 1.45% over five sessions to 105.02 index points (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2023-11-03), which is the first transmission of lower yields into global financial conditions.

New / Most Important: Bloomberg summed up the Street reaction as the Fed being done with rate hikes [10] , and the LA Times had Wall Street leaping toward its best week of 2023 [11] . Powell, however, said the question of rate cuts is not on the radar [12] . The economy behind the data is not weak: Q3 growth was a blockbuster [13] , and BlackRock's Rick Rieder warned against underestimating US resilience [14] . Housing is where restrictive policy bites: mortgage purchase applications fell 26.5% over eighteen weeks to the lowest since 1995 [15] . The WSJ noted that the buy-the-dip mantra lives on in the bond market [16] , and that reflex has now been rewarded.

Playbook ✓: Keep duration exposure, but stop chasing it. A 10Y back above 4.83%, the current 2Y level, would say the market priced the peak too early; a 10Y holding below 4.57% with unemployment drifting higher confirms the pivot narrative. Prefer quality growth and rate-sensitive laggards over deep cyclicals until ISM manufacturing climbs back toward expansion. What changes the call: a re-acceleration in payrolls, which would revive the hike debate Powell left open. In equities, the cleanest expression is the curve itself: banks and small caps gain most from a bull steepening, while a VIX at 14.91 means hedges are cheap enough to keep tail protection on into the next CPI print.

🇪🇺 European Union  Bearish  ECB pause, stagnation

Macro Stance: Europe is the weak link. Euro-area GDP dipped 0.1% in Q3 2023 [17] , Germany shrank in the third quarter [18] , and the New York Times framed the region as faltering and diverging from US strength [19] . The ECB kept rates on hold for the first time in over a year [20] , and policymaker Vujcic said it is finished with hikes for now [21] . That combination, a central bank stepping back while output contracts, is what a late-cycle stall looks like; the risk is that the pause comes after the damage to credit is already done. Stance Bearish on growth, constructive on duration.

Activity & Inflation: Inflation is finally cooperating: euro-zone inflation eased further than expected [22] , and Lagarde says the ECB will bring it to the 2% target in 2025 [23] . Activity is not: the October PMI sat near a three-year low [24] and banks tightened credit standards further [25] . The Stoxx 600 closed at 444.24 index points, up 3.41% over five sessions but down 0.16% over twenty (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2023-11-03), so the bounce came from global rates rather than domestic earnings.

New / Most Important: The Bank of England held at 5.25% [26] and warned of recession risk [27] , while UK manufacturing PMI declined for an eighth straight month [28] . Goldman Sachs flagged that the Israel-Hamas war could carry major implications for Europe's economy [29] . Europe is more exposed than the US to any imported-price shock, and that is the asymmetry: disinflation lets the ECB wait, but a fresh supply shock would land on an economy with no growth cushion.

Playbook ✓: Own euro-area duration over euro-area cyclicals; the growth gap with the US argues against chasing the DAX rebound. Trigger: a PMI turn back toward expansion would make us more constructive. What changes the call: an energy shock from the Middle East, which would reverse the disinflation that justifies the ECB pause.

🇨🇳 China  Bearish  Piecemeal easing, weak demand

Macro Stance: China entered November with the recovery stalling again. Factory activity unexpectedly contracted in October as demand dropped [30] , and the private Caixin survey confirmed the slump rather than contradicting it [31] . The Economist asked why, if the economy is such a mess, firms are not going under [32] ; the likely answer is that forbearance keeps the losses inside the banking system, which is exactly why credit stays cheap but unproductive. The Shanghai Composite closed at 3,030.80 index points, down 2.56% over twenty sessions (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2023-11-03). Our stance is Bearish on the domestic cycle until policy moves from targeted relief to demand support.

PMIs & Activity: The October contraction matters because it followed a few months of tentative stabilisation: The Independent described the pandemic recovery as faltering [33] . September industrial profits extended their gains, helped by policy support [34] , but that is margin repair on lower input costs, not volume growth. Prices tell the same story: consumer prices stalled and factory deflation persisted in September [35] . State media point to positive Q3 performance across the regions [36] , yet the PMI break says momentum into the fourth quarter has faded. Weak domestic orders push manufacturers toward exports, which is the channel that keeps China a disinflationary force for the rest of the world.

Credit Impulse: The liquidity picture deteriorated at month-end. China's short-term money rates spiked, and markets now expect more policy easing [37] ; Reuters reported some institutions borrowing at 50% as liquidity was squeezed [38] . A funding squeeze of that size is a plumbing problem, not a credit boom, and it tends to precede liquidity injections rather than a genuine rise in private borrowing. Bank balance sheets are stable on asset quality, but analysts expect margin pressure to persist [39] , which limits how aggressively lenders can cut loan pricing. Local government finances remain the binding constraint, a credit dilemma laid out by East Asia Forum [40] . Until household and private-sector loan demand turns, the credit impulse stays weak however much liquidity is supplied.

Property: Property is still the drag. Home prices fell at a faster pace in September, a blow to sentiment [41] , and new home prices fell 0.3% in September [42] . Golden Week home sales fell 17% from 2022, according to a survey [43] . Evergrande's winding-up hearing was adjourned again as it seeks a new debt deal [44] , keeping the developer overhang unresolved, and Al Jazeera documented the human cost in unfinished homes [45] . The mechanism is self-reinforcing: falling prices delay purchases, presales dry up, and developers cannot finish projects, which further erodes buyer confidence. Nippon.com asked whether China will repeat the Japanese experience of deflating asset bubbles [46] , the right question for long-duration investors.

Fiscal, Policy & Trade: Policy is centralising. Economy tsar He Lifeng was made director of a key party commission [47] , a sign that financial decisions are moving closer to the top. On trade, the White House is aiming for a Biden-Xi meeting in San Francisco in November [48] , although the South China Morning Post argues differing objectives will limit the outcomes [49] . The tone is thawing at the margin: China is warming to US chipmaker Micron as tensions ease [50] . Resource policy is moving the other way, with Beijing mulling copper curbs to reduce overcapacity [51] after tightening exports of graphite, a key battery material [52] . Nikkei Asia examined what lies behind the 40% drop in China's Treasury holdings [53] .

Markets & Flows: Hong Kong is trading the summit and the easing hopes, not the data. The Hang Seng closed at 17,664.12 index points, up 2.52% on the session, and Tencent rose 4.85% to 302.60 HKD (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2023-11-03). NBC reported Hong Kong leading gains in Asia as key regional data came out [54] . Onshore, money is hiding in corners: micro-caps are shining in a flagging share market [55] , and a Chinese firm launched stock indices to tap the rush into safe-haven gold [56] . Moutai shares jumped after its first price increase in six years [57] , a reminder that pricing power still commands a premium. The dispersion is stark: Baidu's US ADR is down 18.63% over twenty sessions while Xiaomi is up 30.48% (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2023-11-03).

New / Most Important: The CNN-reported agreement in principle for a Biden-Xi meeting [58] is the most important swing factor for sentiment. On the real economy, consumer weakness is spilling over to Western brands squeezed by Chinese belt-tightening [59] , and Canada Goose cut its sales forecast on a rocky China recovery [60] . The exception is electric vehicles: Xpeng and Li Auto delivered record numbers of cars in October [61] , and BYD plans its first European plant in Hungary [62] , a strategy that takes the price war abroad. The casualties are showing too: Nio plans to cut its workforce by a tenth [63] . Meanwhile Alibaba launched an upgraded AI model to challenge Microsoft and Amazon [64] , a reminder that platform spending has shifted toward AI.

Playbook ✓: Stay underweight China beta: the A-share ETF ASHR closed at 25.21 USD, down 2.14% over twenty sessions (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2023-11-03), and nothing in the credit or property data argues for a durable turn. Express any summit optimism tactically through Hong Kong internet leaders rather than the broad market, and take profits into the meeting. The call changes on a PMI return to expansion together with an announced household-demand stimulus. ⚠ Divergence: Hong Kong equities and EV deliveries are rallying while factory surveys contract and interbank rates spike, so the market is pricing policy rescue before the data show any.

🇯🇵 Japan  Mixed  BOJ loosens yield grip slowly

The Nikkei 225 closed at 31,949.89 index points, up 4.41% over five sessions (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2023-11-02). Stance Mixed: earnings are strong, but the policy exit is the tail risk.

Activity & Currency: The currency is doing the heavy lifting for exporters. Toyota posted record first-half profits and lifted its outlook on the weaker yen [68] , and Japan's biggest firms are set for a $9.3 billion bonanza from the weak currency [69] . Japan stayed out of the currency market in October despite the yen's weakness [71] , and The Economist warned of how Japan poses a threat to the global financial system [72] . The channel to watch is capital repatriation: if domestic yields rise enough to keep Japanese savings at home, the selling pressure lands on foreign bond markets, including US Treasuries.

Playbook ✓: Own Japanese exporters and chip equipment, with Tokyo Electron up 8.89% over five sessions (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2023-11-02), but hedge the currency leg. The trigger to cut is a BOJ signal on ending negative rates, which would strengthen the yen and squeeze exporter margins at once. Lower US yields cut both ways here: they narrow the rate gap that drives the yen weaker, which pays the currency hedge but trims exporters' translation gains. The domestic pressure for normalisation is building: the central bank has said Japan's property market shows signs of overheating [73] .

For investors, defence is likely to be the hardest budget line for any coalition to cut, which narrows the room for the social promises elsewhere.

New / Most Important: The central bank's credibility is part of the post-election repricing. Big infrastructure is also up for review, with DW asking whether Tusk will ground Poland's multibillion airport plans [118] .

Prefer Dino and the banks over state-controlled energy and fuel names, where tariff freezes and price caps are the obvious fiscal tools.
