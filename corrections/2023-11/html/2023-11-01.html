🌍 Geographic Macro Overview

🇺🇸 United States  Mixed  Fed hold, term-premium test

Macro Stance: The US goes into the Fed's decision with growth still running hot and the bond market doing the tightening. GDP grew at a 4.9% annual pace in the third quarter, better than expected, CNBC reported [1] , yet Pimco's Browne called it the 'last gasp' of strong US data [2] . Equities have already conceded part of the argument: the S&P 500 closed at 4,193.80 index points (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2023-10-31), up 0.65% on the session but down 0.84% over 20 sessions, after the Los Angeles Times reported a slide that left the index 10% below its July peak [3] . Our stance is Mixed: nominal growth supports earnings, but the discount rate is now setting prices, and Bloomberg reported Wall Street cutting S&P 500 expectations as geopolitical risk rises [4] .

Rates & Curve: The Treasury curve is inverted but steepening from the long end, the configuration that hurts balanced portfolios on both legs. The 2Y closed at 5.07% and the 10Y at 4.88% (US Treasury, prior close 2023-10-31), leaving 10Y-2Y at -19 bps; over 20 observations the 2Y fell 5 bps while the 10Y rose 19 bps and the 30Y 23 bps, a 24 bps steepening of the spread. The 3M bill at 5.59% still sits 71 bps above the 10Y. WSJ's 'Decoding the (Almost) 5% 10-Year Treasury Yield' frames the debate [5] , and the Guardian's report that bond vigilantes return as deficits rise supplies the motive [6] : supply and term premium, not a more hawkish Fed path, are lifting the long end. Bloomberg explained why the Treasury's quarterly borrowing plan has the market's attention [7] , and TLT is down 1.74% over 20 sessions.

New / Most Important: The policy announcement lands Wednesday, and CNBC's preview sets out what to expect [8] ; CBS tied the decision directly to mortgage rates [9] . That is the channel to watch, because Wells Fargo warned that 8% mortgage rates could drag the housing market into a 1980s-style recession [10] . Risk appetite recovered into month-end, with Reuters reporting Wall Street ending sharply higher on earnings momentum as the Fed was eyed [11] , and the VIX fell 8.15% on the session to 18.14 index points (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2023-10-31). The DXY rose 0.51% to 106.66 index points on the day but is down 0.32% over 20 sessions, so the dollar is not amplifying the rate shock.

Playbook ✓: Action: stay neutral on S&P 500 beta and take duration only at the front end, where the 2Y at 5.07% pays more than the 10Y with far less price risk. Trigger to add equity risk: a statement that treats the rise in long yields as tightening that substitutes for further hikes, letting the 10Y stall without a growth scare. What changes the call: a renewed rise in the 10Y with the 2Y climbing alongside it, which would mean the market is re-pricing the Fed rather than term premium. In that case cut cyclicals first; Caterpillar's 6.65% one-session fall after an earnings beat shows how little support good numbers now provide [12] .

Credit is the transmission belt: euro-area banks tightened credit standards further in the ECB's survey [18] . Lagarde's FT interview, headlined 'I should have been bolder' [22] , adds a note of self-criticism from the top even as the hiking debate closes. With the hiking cycle read as over, the debate shifts to how long rates stay restrictive into a contraction, and bank equities are the pressure point: Reuters reported investors punishing Europe's big banks as results underwhelmed [23] .

Playbook ✓: Action: underweight European cyclicals and autos against defensives, and prefer core bonds to euro equities. Trigger: a second negative GDP quarter or a PMI that fails to recover would cement the call. What changes it: a rebound in credit demand or a China stimulus impulse large enough to lift German exporters. The Euro Stoxx 50 rose 0.81% on the session to 4,061.12 index points (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2023-10-31), a month-end bounce rather than a trend change.

🇨🇳 China  Bearish  Fiscal pivot, PMI relapse

Macro Stance: China's recovery has slipped back into contraction just as Beijing shifts from monetary drip-feed to fiscal support, and the market is treating the pivot as too small and too slow. Factory activity unexpectedly contracted in October as demand dropped, CNN reported [24] , while The Economist described Xi Jinping stepping up his attempt to rescue the economy [25] . The Shanghai Composite closed at 3,018.77 index points (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2023-10-31), down 3.11% over 20 sessions. Stance: Bearish on broad China beta, with selective exposure only where earnings are visibly decoupling from the macro.

PMIs & Activity: The October PMI miss matters because it reverses the late-summer stabilisation story; The Independent called it a faltering pandemic recovery [26] . The industrial side is mixed rather than collapsing: Reuters reported September industrial profits extended gains helped by policy support [27] , but September consumer prices stalled and factory deflation persisted [28] . Profits recovering on cheaper inputs while prices stay weak is a margin story, not a demand story. OilPrice.com reported China's economic woes weighing on crude [29] , a channel visible in Brent's 3.86% fall over 20 sessions to 87.41 USD/bbl (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2023-10-31). For the next print, the test is whether new orders recover once fiscal money reaches projects.

Credit Impulse: Credit is being forced through the central government's balance sheet. Bloomberg reported China's government debt supply surged in October to a 2023 high [30] , while CNBC argued the 1 trillion yuan debt plan 'isn't necessarily such a big deal' [31] . At the same time Reuters reported the cabinet curbing debt growth in twelve 'high risk' regions [32] , so the local-government channels that normally amplify stimulus are being restrained. The plumbing is tight: some Chinese institutions borrowed at a 50% rate as liquidity was squeezed, Reuters said [33] , and Zee Business reported short-term money rates spiking with markets expecting more policy easing [34] . Bank profitability caps credit growth: ICBC posted a scant profit gain on shrinking margins [35] .

Property: Property remains the drag that policy has not reached. New home prices fell 0.3% in September, WSJ reported [36] , and Bloomberg said the decline accelerated in a blow to sentiment [37] ; Golden Week home sales fell 17% against 2022 in a survey cited by Reuters [38] . The Evergrande winding-up hearing was adjourned as the developer seeks a new debt deal [39] , and WSJ reported that Xi is looking for someone to blame for the property bust [40] , a sign that accountability rather than a bailout is the political priority. Unfinished homes, the subject of Al Jazeera's report [41] , are the confidence channel: buyers who fear non-delivery stop pre-paying, which starves developers of the pre-sale cash that once funded construction.

Fiscal, Policy & Trade: The policy architecture is consolidating around fewer, trusted hands: Reuters reported He Lifeng, China's economy tsar, was made director of a key party commission [42] . Supply-side discipline is arriving with it. Bloomberg said Beijing is mulling copper curbs to reduce overcapacity and emissions [43] , and Reuters explained new growth limits on the vast oil-refining industry [44] . Trade policy is both lever and threat, after China tightened exports of graphite, a key battery material [45] . The diplomatic track offers a de-escalation catalyst, with Caixin reporting China and the US 'working together' on a Biden-Xi meeting at APEC in November [46] . A softer US front end would also give the PBOC room to ease without pressuring the currency.

Markets & Flows: Hong Kong is the pressure valve. The Hang Seng closed at 17,112.48 index points (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2023-10-31), down 1.69% on the session and 3.91% over 20 sessions; the SCMP reported the market revisiting an 11-month low [47] and then rising by the most in four weeks as more Chinese companies joined buyback plans [48] . Domestic money is rotating rather than leaving: China's biggest fund manager raised bets on liquor distillers and sold tech giants [49] , and Reuters said micro-caps shine in a flagging market [50] . Tencent fell 2.10% to 289.20 HKD and is down 5.55% over 20 sessions, Alibaba's Hong Kong line is down 6.48% and Xiaomi is up 13.61%: dispersion, not direction. ASHR, the CSI 300 tracker, is down 2.51% over 20 sessions.

New / Most Important: Corporate earnings are diverging from the macro. BYD's third-quarter profit soared to a record after beating Tesla in EV deliveries and capping costs, the SCMP reported [51] , and PetroChina posted a record $6.3 billion quarterly income on higher output and fuel sales [52] . Alibaba launched an upgraded AI model to challenge Microsoft and Amazon [53] . Against that, Yum China fell to a one-year low on disappointing results [54] , confirming the consumer as the weak link, and Berkshire Hathaway sold $25.8 million of BYD shares [55] , a reminder that long-term foreign holders are still trimming.

Playbook ✓: Action: underweight broad China beta through FXI and ASHR and avoid property-linked Hong Kong names; express upside only through companies whose earnings are independent of housing, such as EV exporters and selected platforms, in small size. Trigger to upgrade: visible acceleration in the government-funded spending implied by October's debt-supply surge [30] together with the easing the money market is already demanding [34] . What changes the call: a Biden-Xi meeting that delivers tariff or export-control relief would lift multiples faster than the data. ⚠ Divergence: record profits at BYD and PetroChina and buyback-driven bounces in Hong Kong sit against a contracting PMI, falling home prices and an interbank squeeze, so equity flows are pricing policy support that activity has not yet confirmed.

Stance: Bullish on equities, with an explicit fiscal hedge.

Markets: The equity move has been large and concentrated in banks. Fiscal & Politics: The new majority inherits a budget worse than advertised. Prof. Orłowski asked in Bankier whether Poland is on a 'Greek path' [83] , and ING analysed Poland's fiscal space after the October elections [84] . The handover is itself a risk: President Duda set the date of the new parliament but delayed naming a prime minister [85] , Le Monde described a tense coalition with the president [86] , and Rzeczpospolita warned that the outgoing government can still act and obstruct its successors [87] . The upside lever is Brussels: Donald Tusk vowed to unlock the recovery funds [88] , and Rzeczpospolita quoted Marzena Chmielewska saying Poland has a chance of a 5 billion euro KPO advance this year [89] .

Corporate & Sectors: Orlen is the political bellwether. Subiektywnie o finansach tallied what the pre-election fuel-price cut cost Orlen [90] , Agro Profil reported Orlen began raising pump prices even as crude got cheaper [91] , and Parkiet said it picked a new auditor from outside the Big Four [92] ; its third-quarter results sit on the domestic docket alongside the flash CPI and PMI in Strefa Inwestorów's weekly preview [93] . Orlen closed at 66.50 PLN, up 14.97% over 20 sessions. Competition is coming from retail: Money.pl ranked Tomasz Biernacki's Dino among Poland's biggest companies [94] , Forbes profiled Biernacki as a potential challenger to Orlen [95] , and Dino, up 15.94% over 20 sessions to 398.50 PLN, is reportedly planning its own fuel stations [96] . Bank Millennium aims to conclude 1,000 FX-loan settlements a quarter [97] , a reminder that legal costs remain a tax on bank earnings. JSW rose 6.28% on the session as pb.pl reported an analyst lifting its target price [98] , while CD Projekt, down 18.67% over 20 sessions, is 'at a bend' with a change at the helm and new games on a distant horizon, per Forbes [99] .

Geopolitics & Security: Security spending is a structural support for domestic demand and a standing source of headline risk. Poland joined Estonia, Finland, Latvia, Lithuania, Norway and Ukraine in a joint statement on strengthening regional security [100] , and Interia reported that Polish weapons are wreaking havoc at the front, leaving Russian forces no time to react [101] . The border remains a filter as well as a front line: Notes From Poland reported the detention of a Norwegian 'neo-Nazi extremist' trying to enter Ukraine to join the war [102] , and the Warsaw Institute catalogued Russia's disinformation war against Poland [103] . Industry is being pulled into the effort, with Budimex's construction robot, presented in Krakow [104] , set to help build a military hospital, per Business Insider Polska [105] , and Poland signing an agreement to launch its first satellite constellation [106] .

New / Most Important: The decisive variable is the speed of government formation relative to the fiscal reveal. Coalition partners are dividing posts and insist they will 'certainly reach agreement', Newsweek reported [107] , while Interia said PiS is leaving the opposition a 'surprise' that could thwart the new government's plans [108] . Tusk's adviser commented on whether the opposition will freeze electricity prices in 2024 [109] , a measure that would widen the hole further. For markets, the question is whether bank-led gains already discount the KPO money while ignoring the cost of financing the deficit.

Playbook ✓: Action: stay overweight Polish equities through the WIG20TR tracker or EPOL, tilted to banks and insurers such as PKO BP, Pekao and PZU, and fund part of it from Orlen and other state-controlled names exposed to political pricing. Trigger to add: a nominated prime minister and a concrete KPO timetable from Brussels. What changes the call: a 2024 budget that confirms the 'real hole' estimates without credible financing, or an RPP easing pace faster than the inflation path justifies. ⚠ Divergence: bank shares price a benign handover and EU inflows, while the fiscal data point to heavier bond supply, and falling savings-bond rates after the election signal a lower rate path [110] that compresses the net interest margins driving the rally.
