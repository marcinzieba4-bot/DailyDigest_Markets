🇺🇸 United States  Mixed  hold / tariff vigilance

Macro Stance: Stay invested in earnings visibility while buying protection against another tariff disappointment. The Wall Street Journal's economist survey found lower recession risk and stronger expected job growth, a useful counterweight to the assumption that every tariff announcement immediately ends the expansion. [1] But Reuters reported a renewed threat of tariffs on the EU and Mexico, and the transmission runs through import costs, sourcing decisions and corporate margins before it reaches aggregate growth. [2] Resilience justifies selective equity exposure; it does not justify assuming the Federal Reserve can neutralize an adverse supply shock. The investment question is whether firms can maintain sales without surrendering the margin that supports their valuations. A consumer who buys only after a discount can sustain revenue while still making the earnings outcome difficult. Keep that distinction central as the reporting season begins.

New / Most Important: June inflation and retail sales, alongside big-bank earnings, are the pending tests identified by Investopedia's week-ahead preview. [3] Morningstar's inflation preview expected a rise as tariff effects emerged; that is an expectation to test, not an outcome to trade as settled. [4] The political pressure on Powell adds a separate term-premium question. CNBC reported that the Fed responded to attacks over its renovation project, while NPR described criticism of its failure to cut quickly. [5] [6] Investors should distinguish an eventual reduction in policy rates from confidence in the institution setting them. Those can move in opposite directions, particularly if the long bond interprets political pressure as a threat to inflation discipline.

Playbook ✓: Hold profitable technology and firms demonstrating demand through guidance, hedge import-sensitive cyclicals, and keep reserve capital in shorter maturities. Add broad equity risk if inflation allows easier policy while retail sales and bank commentary preserve the growth case. Reduce duration-sensitive equity exposure if inflation surprises higher and long yields rise alongside it. A tariff pause would improve the tactical setup, but rebuilding positions should still require companies to confirm that orders survived the uncertainty. Do not pay for a perfect macro landing and perfect earnings execution in the same trade.

[7] The earnings risk is immediate even before implementation because customers can defer commitments and suppliers must decide who bears the prospective charge. Credit & Inflation: Reuters reported that the ECB's Schnabel set a very high bar for another rate cut as the economy held up. [8] That leaves investors with less reason to expect monetary policy to offset every trade shock. [9] [10] Neither picture supports a single indiscriminate regional reflation trade. Credit selection should distinguish businesses with pricing discretion from borrowers that need both cheaper financing and stronger final demand to repair their balance sheets.

New / Most Important: Macron announced a plan to accelerate French military spending, per Reuters. [11] That helps separate a funded strategic demand channel from the tariff-exposed manufacturing cycle. At the same time, Reuters reported Scope's forecast of rising German debt, reminding investors that fiscal support brings financing needs as well as orders. [12] European defense suppliers can benefit from the former without making long sovereign duration an automatic beneficiary of the same policy. Look for executable procurement and delivery capacity; a spending announcement becomes earnings only when suppliers can convert it.

Playbook ✓: Favor defense and selective domestic infrastructure over autos and broad exporter baskets. Add exporters only after a credible tariff accommodation or company guidance demonstrating that pricing and production changes protect profit. Hedge euro exposure when the investment thesis depends on local equity returns rather than currency appreciation. The bearish regional stance would soften if trade negotiations remove the incremental burden while the ECB retains room to support demand; a temporary relief rally alone would not establish that combination.
