🇺🇸 United States  Mixed  hold / resilient demand

Macro Stance: The economy is giving the Federal Reserve permission to wait. June retail sales rebounded after May's decline, while Reuters reported an improvement in July consumer sentiment. That combination supports corporate revenue and makes an immediate demand-led easing argument harder to sustain. It also means portfolios built around an imminent consumer collapse need a more demanding trigger than expensive financing alone. Keep the distinction between slower growth and falling activity: businesses can face narrower margins while household expenditure still expands. [1] [2] The constraint is inflation. Reuters described June consumer prices rising as tariff pass-through began. A supply-driven price increase is uncomfortable for a central bank because cutting rates cannot produce cheaper imported goods; it can instead help companies pass those costs through. The useful equity exposure is therefore to businesses with visible demand and control over costs, rather than a blanket bet that lower policy rates will rescue weak operating models. [3]

Rates & Curve: The 2Y Treasury yield was 3.88% and the 10Y 4.44%, leaving the 10Y−2Y spread at 56 bps. Over 20 observations the 2Y fell 6 bps, the 10Y rose 6 bps and the spread widened 12 bps. The curve is positively sloped; its move is steepening (10Y up, 2Y down/flat). That is a distinction with practical consequences: expected policy relief at the front end is not delivering cheaper long-term capital. The S&P 500 closed at 6,296.79, up 5.28% over 20 sessions, while VIX stood at 16.41 and DXY at 98.48. Equity resilience is substantial, but it sits alongside a long-end financing hurdle that remains real. (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2025-07-18; US Treasury, prior close 2025-07-18)

New / Most Important: The most consequential political risk is the credibility of monetary policy. Reuters said the rate-cut delay remained intact as inflation fears outweighed pressure from President Trump. The Wall Street Journal reported on Treasury Secretary Bessent's case against firing Jerome Powell. Neither story establishes a change of chair or a change of policy; together they make institutional independence a variable investors must price. An intervention that lowers expected short rates while lifting long yields would hurt long-duration equities even if the first reaction celebrated easier money. [4] [5]

Playbook ✓: Maintain profitable growth and selected industrial exposure, with short-duration bonds as the reserve for volatility. Add cyclical risk when earnings guidance confirms demand and longer yields stop rising. Reduce it if tariff-related price increases broaden while management teams cut volume expectations. Hedge the political risk through position size and duration discipline: a cheaper front end does not justify unlimited exposure to an expensive long end. The invalidation of this selective stance would be broad earnings upgrades alongside calmer inflation, which would support more participation beyond the established winners.

Playbook ✓: Prefer contracted industrial demand, power infrastructure and firms able to demonstrate stable margins. Keep broad European equity exposure measured until the ECB's pending decision and trade developments clarify the financing and export outlook. Buy an auto recovery only when guidance stabilizes; use a lower share price as an invitation to investigate, not as proof that estimates have bottomed. If manufacturers retain orders and the pause arrives without a hawkish shift, broaden the position gradually. If trade risk reaches bank asset quality, preserve liquidity rather than averaging across the whole region.

🇨🇳 China  Mixed  policy support / uneven demand

Macro Stance: China's growth headline is stronger than the domestic-demand story underneath it. Reuters reported Q2 GDP growth of 5.2% year on year, above the market forecast. Bloomberg's assessment was that the GDP beat masked fragile demand. Both can be true: output can outperform expectations while the private sector remains reluctant to spend, hire or raise prices. That supports selective exposure to policy beneficiaries and competitive businesses, while arguing against an indiscriminate domestic reflation trade. The test is whether stronger production becomes durable household income and corporate pricing power. [11] [12] A positive surprise relative to forecasts reduces immediate crisis risk; it does not by itself establish the end of disinflation or a self-sustaining property recovery. Position for a policy-supported expansion with weak links, and require those links to improve before increasing broad cyclical beta.

PMIs & Activity: The June manufacturing and services readings pointed in different directions. Reuters reported that factory activity returned to growth in the Caixin survey, while services activity growth slowed to a nine-month low. Manufacturing stabilization is useful, but a softer services pulse raises questions about the breadth of domestic spending. Avoid treating the factory improvement as a universal demand signal, especially when trade timing can influence orders. The next confirmation would be continued manufacturing expansion accompanied by firmer services activity, rather than a rotation in which exports support output while household-facing businesses lose momentum. [13] [14] Companies that can grow without aggressive discounting deserve a premium over those relying on volume alone. A rise in activity with continued price pressure can leave equity earnings behind the macro headline.

Credit Impulse: Credit availability and credit appetite remain different trades. TradingView described a 4.2 trillion yuan lending spike and explicitly questioned whether it represented stimulus firepower or an economic warning. Reuters' earlier account of banks stumbling on Beijing's consumer-lending push provides the reason for caution: expanding the supply of loans does not guarantee willing, creditworthy borrowers. The investment signal would improve if financing helped households make purchases and private businesses commit to expansion, rather than simply shifting liabilities around. [15] [16] Financial equities can benefit from stability without enjoying a clean margin or loan-demand acceleration. For now, treat stronger lending as a potential bridge to demand, and judge the bridge by what appears on the other side. An easing policy impulse that produces more deposits and bond buying than spending would support duration more directly than consumer profits.

Property: Housing remains the clearest obstacle to a broad confidence recovery. Bloomberg reported home prices falling at a faster pace and mounting calls for stimulus. Reuters described Beijing's commitment to sustainable urbanisation after decades of rapid growth, suggesting the policy conversation is also about the model of development, not merely a near-term sales rebound. Falling home values can restrain household risk-taking even when employment and aggregate output hold up. [17] [18] Prefer businesses whose cash flows are not dependent on a quick restoration of speculative housing demand. A credible property turn would require firmer prices and transactions that survive without ever-larger incentives. Until then, a policy announcement should be valued for the financing stress it removes, while a lasting earnings recovery requires evidence that customers are returning on commercially viable terms.

Fiscal, Policy & Trade: The most investable policy tension sits between de-escalation and strategic control. More exports ease immediate supply concerns for downstream users, but the quota framework preserves policy influence over a critical input. Neither headline implies unrestricted trade. [19] [20] On technology, Reuters reported production obstacles to Nvidia's China restart, and a senior Republican objection to resumed H20 shipments. Commercial access can improve while supply and political constraints still obstruct revenue conversion. [21] [22] Use confirmed delivery and customer demand as the next checkpoints. Strong shipments around that rush should not automatically be extrapolated into a steady future run rate. [23]

New / Most Important: Price competition is now a policy and profit issue across consumer platforms. The South China Morning Post reported the market regulator summoning Alibaba, Meituan and JD amid the delivery war. Intervention may eventually reduce destructive competition, but an official meeting is not evidence that subsidies or discounting have ended. The useful inflection would be healthier economics with customers retained. [24]

Playbook ✓: Keep selective internet and industrial exposure, favoring companies that show resilient demand without sacrificing unit economics. Add broad China exposure when services activity, housing and private credit demand improve together; reduce it when sales growth relies on escalating discounts. Express a technology-access recovery through staged positions because licenses, production and deliveries can diverge. Avoid chasing the index solely on a GDP surprise. ⚠ Divergence: the equity recovery is constructive, but faster home-price declines and aggressive price competition still challenge a durable domestic earnings recovery.
