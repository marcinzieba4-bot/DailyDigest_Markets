🇺🇸 United States  Mixed  hold / tariff dependent

[1] [2] That supports profitable cyclicals more directly than it supports a fresh rush into the most rate-sensitive balance sheets. The S&P 500 closed at 6,279.35, up 5.17% over 20 sessions, while VIX stood at 16.38 and DXY at 97.18. Stocks can absorb firmer yields when earnings improve, but that combination leaves expensive promises with less room for disappointment. (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2025-07-03; US Treasury, prior close 2025-07-03)

New / Most Important: The approaching tariff deadline now matters more than another celebration of the jobs number. [6] The Vietnam agreement is a useful warning against treating a deal headline as a fully settled commercial arrangement: Reuters reported that it still left questions for retailers. [7] A business can welcome lower uncertainty while remaining unable to price its next shipment confidently. That distinction will determine whether the next earnings commentary validates the rally or merely avoids the worst case.

Playbook ✓: Keep a core allocation to profitable US equities and add cyclical exposure only when trade clarity improves alongside company demand commentary. Fund it by trimming the most extended speculative positions, rather than by assuming bond yields must fall. Hold short-duration liquidity for a tariff-driven air pocket; use defined-risk index protection if exposure cannot be reduced. Upgrade the stance when an agreement clarifies actual operating costs and firms preserve margins. Turn defensive if trade restrictions broaden while private hiring and order commentary soften together. A lower equity index alone is a better entry price, not proof that the earnings risk has passed.

🇨🇳 China  Mixed  policy repair / uneven demand

Macro Stance: China is investable as a selective repair trade, with the strongest opportunities where policy removes a specific obstacle. It is harder to own as a broad domestic-demand acceleration. Reuters reported that factory activity returned to growth in June in the Caixin survey, while its services coverage described the slowest growth in nine months. [13] [14] Those releases can both be true. A manufacturing rebound can reflect renewed orders and trade relief before households or service employers feel confident enough to accelerate. The investment question is whether that first improvement spreads into pricing, private borrowing and property transactions. Until it does, a higher factory index deserves a narrower equity expression than an economy-wide reflation basket. Prefer businesses with an observable order or financing catalyst over those requiring every policy channel to work at once.

PMIs & Activity: The June factory improvement follows a much less flattering profit baseline. Reuters reported a sharp decline in May industrial profits, and CNBC put that fall at 9.1%. [15] [16] Production is not the same as profit: suppliers can increase output while discounting their way through excess capacity. June services slowing makes the distinction more important because it limits the evidence for a simultaneous consumer recovery. May retail sales had grown at their fastest pace since December 2023, with subsidies helping consumption, according to CNBC. [17] That points to a policy-supported pocket of demand, not an automatic recovery in discretionary spending everywhere. Watch whether stronger unit sales begin to support margins without requiring larger incentives. A rebound built on discounts and purchase timing is useful for suppliers but less durable for the wider earnings cycle.

Credit Impulse: Credit repair should be judged through the borrower and the use of funds. Nikkei Asia reported that a Chinese rural bank received a state rescue while warning of losses. [18] That is evidence of stress being addressed, not evidence that healthy private borrowers are suddenly expanding. A bank can become better protected while remaining cautious about lending. SCMP also reported calls for a large bond issuance to tackle local debt. [19] A call for action is still a proposal, and a refinancing program would first relieve balance-sheet pressure before creating fresh demand. The constructive sequence would be cleaner bank balance sheets, fewer refinancing disruptions and then stronger private investment. Buying the last stage before the first is visible risks paying for a transmission mechanism that remains clogged. Separate solvency support from a genuine increase in profitable credit creation.

Property: Housing remains the brake on an otherwise tempting stabilization story. Reuters reported that May home prices fell again, extending the long slump, while SCMP described new-home prices declining for a further month. [20] [21] Nikkei Asia reported a push toward sales of finished homes as the property downturn continued. [22] The distinction matters for confidence: completion and delivery can lower the perceived risk of a purchase, yet they do not guarantee that a household expects the asset to retain value. Goldman Sachs' warning, reported by SCMP, that the slump could continue further is a forecast rather than an outcome. [23] A durable turn would require transactions and prices to reinforce one another. Prefer consumption or infrastructure exposures with their own catalysts rather than assuming a developer rally proves household balance sheets have healed.

Fiscal, Policy & Trade: Trade policy offers real relief alongside a more complicated enforcement problem. Reuters reported that the US lifted restrictions on chip-design software and ethane while the trade truce held. [24] That removes specific frictions and can improve operating visibility for the affected supply chains. It does not abolish the wider tariff dispute. The Financial Times reported Chinese exports being rerouted through southeast Asia, and Reuters' Breakingviews warned that the Vietnam tariff arrangement could be unfavorable for China. [25] [26] Investors should distinguish legal market access from an assumed ability to bypass restrictions indefinitely. At home, Nikkei Asia reported planned action against forced excessive online discounts. [27] Better pricing discipline could help profits, but less aggressive discounting may also test how much demand is organic. The policy objective and the earnings result need separate verification.

New / Most Important: The fight against destructive competition is becoming central to the stock-selection debate. BNN Bloomberg reported signs that China was addressing price wars taking a toll on the EV industry. [28] That captures the problem neatly: sales competition can support output while leaving the supplier with a tougher return calculation. Alibaba's planned exchangeable-bond financing for cloud and commerce, reported by Reuters, adds another capital-allocation test. [29] Financing access is helpful, but investors still need to ask which investment earns a return and which simply defends market share. A broad technology label hides very different demands on capital.

Playbook ✓: Maintain a selective China allocation through liquid baskets and businesses with specific policy or demand catalysts. Add mainland exposure if factory improvement broadens into services and profit commentary; add internet exposure if competition becomes less destructive without a collapse in demand. Keep property-heavy and weak-bank exposures restrained until balance-sheet repair produces better transactions and lending behavior. Hedge trade-sensitive positions when access restrictions widen or supply-chain routing comes under greater scrutiny. Do not chase a rally based solely on a proposed debt solution. The upgrade requires an operating improvement that survives after the initial policy headline fades. ⚠ Divergence: manufacturing's return to growth conflicts with slower services and continuing housing-price weakness, so the broad recovery remains less convincing than the selective repair trade.

Pattern: The common denominator is visibility after uncertainty. Playbook: Own a smaller position before uncertain results and add when the operating bridge improves, even if the entry price is higher. Trim when a beat depends on costs that cannot be cut repeatedly or when management withdraws the visibility needed for valuation. Keep turnaround positions distinct from structural-growth holdings: they need different proof.
