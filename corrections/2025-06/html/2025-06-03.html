🇺🇸 United States  Bullish  earnings resilience / manufacturing caution

Macro Stance: Remain constructive on cash-generative equities while becoming more demanding about the economic bridge beneath the recovery. The new manufacturing evidence is a warning against equating higher share prices with an improving industrial cycle. ISM's May manufacturing report put the index at 48.5, and Reuters described tariff gloom weighing on factories while delivery times lengthened. That is a combination of constrained activity and supply friction, rather than a clean demand acceleration. [1] [2] The equity case therefore rests on differentiated earnings and investment demand. Companies that can serve a funded growth program may outperform even while a broader manufacturer struggles to price, source or plan with confidence.

Rates & Curve: The Treasury 2Y closed at 3.94% and the 10Y at 4.46%, leaving a 52 bps spread. Over 20 observations the 2Y rose 11 bps and the 10Y rose 13 bps: the curve was positively sloped, with bear steepening (both up, 10Y up more). Higher long-term financing costs make the cash timing of a growth plan more important. The S&P 500 closed at 5,935.94, up 0.41% in the session and 4.38% over 20 sessions. The Nasdaq 100 rose 0.71% in the session, while VIX was 18.36 and DXY 98.70. A calmer equity-volatility price does not remove the cost of financing uncertainty. (US Treasury, prior close 2025-06-02; Yahoo Finance, prior close 2025-06-02)

New / Most Important: There is still real capital being committed to selected opportunities. Reuters reported Applied Digital and CoreWeave signing a long-term lease, which moves part of the AI-infrastructure story from a general demand forecast into a specific commercial agreement. The agreement still requires delivery and payment over time. [3] At the same time, Reuters reported Dimon warning that US debt could produce bond turmoil. A large corporate contract and concern about the sovereign financing environment can both be valid; investors need to assess how the project survives the discount rate used to value it. [4] The useful distinction is between a business with an executable investment opportunity and a share price that needs benign conditions everywhere.

Playbook ✓: Favor profitable technology, selected infrastructure suppliers and businesses with flexible sourcing. Add exposure when a new commitment improves the quality of future cash generation without imposing disproportionate financing demands. Keep general industrial beta smaller until customer orders and delivery conditions improve. Do not fund a long-duration equity position with an expectation that weak factory data automatically forces an accommodating policy response. Trim if bond-market pressure and operating-cost uncertainty begin to overwhelm the earnings bridge; increase risk when the commercial improvement broadens beyond a narrow set of investment programs. A constructive stance should remain compatible with a liquid reserve and a clear company-level invalidation.

🧮 Earnings Pattern — What the Market Pays For

Separate a good result from a financeable next chapter

Rewarded: The strongest earnings evidence connects a customer budget to an economically useful result. Reuters reported Monzo's profit rising sharply, while CNBC reported Nvidia beating earnings and revenue expectations on strong data-center sales. Those are different businesses benefiting through different commercial channels. [42] [63] The common investment question is whether the next growth increment can preserve that profitability. A growing industry alone is not the evidence; the company must show how it converts customer activity into an attractive return after the costs of serving it.
