🌍 Geographic Macro Overview

🇺🇸 United States  Bearish  hold / uneven demand

Macro Stance: The equity rally is doing a better job of celebrating resilient technology earnings than of resolving the demand problem. Reuters reported that Microsoft and Meta lifted the market. [1] The April manufacturing ISM reading was 48.7, according to the official PR Newswire release. [2] That leaves the industrial economy contracting while a small group of scalable businesses provides the strongest earnings reassurance. The distinction should govern allocation: buy the earnings stream that has proved resilient, rather than borrowing its credibility for every economically sensitive stock. The first-quarter contraction remains a warning about spending distorted by tariffs, not a complete description of every company’s customer base. [3] A technology-led rally can coexist with a weakening consumer and cautious manufacturers for some time, but it becomes fragile if the leaders are asked to finance confidence in the whole market.

Rates & Curve: The 2Y yield closed at 3.70%, down 21 bps over 20 observations, and the 10Y at 4.25%, up 5 bps. The 10Y−2Y spread was 55 bps. The curve was positively sloped, with steepening (10Y up, 2Y down/flat). Both the 2Y and 10Y fell 7 bps over five observations (US Treasury, prior close 2025-05-01). The wider-window split between short and long yields still argues against assuming every duration asset benefits equally from a slowdown. The S&P 500 was 5,604.14, up 0.63% in the prior session but down 1.18% over 20 sessions; VIX was 24.60 and DXY 100.25, up 0.78% in the prior session (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2025-05-01). A stronger dollar and firm long yields can make a narrow equity rally harder to broaden.

New / Most Important: The new earnings evidence adds friction to the recovery story. Reuters reported disappointing Amazon cloud revenue and an income forecast that fell short, while CNBC said Apple faced difficulty predicting tariff costs beyond June. [4] [5] Neither development erases Microsoft’s strength. Together, however, they show why investors should resist turning one company’s investment cycle into a universal demand forecast. The next jobs report remains pending; CNBC identified Friday’s release as an important test of the economy’s direction. [6] Watch whether labor demand can support spending as firms adjust costs and sourcing.

Playbook ✓: Keep a defensive broad-market stance with a selective technology allocation. Favor cash generation and visible customer demand, retain short-maturity bonds for flexibility, and use rallies in weak-visibility consumer businesses to improve portfolio quality. Add cyclical risk if the jobs picture and company demand commentary stabilize without a renewed inflation or long-yield problem. Reduce if earnings warnings spread into previously resilient segments. The bearish stance should change with evidence of durable demand, rather than with the market’s understandable desire for an easier policy answer.

Activity & Inflation: German inflation slowed less than expected to 2.2% in April, CNBC reported, and Reuters described Germany avoiding recession while unemployment increased. [8] [9] This gives policymakers room for neither complacency nor a simple one-variable response. Lower inflation can help financing conditions, while employment softness can limit how quickly consumers respond. For investors, the stronger setup is a business that can defend margins under modest growth, not one that needs a full manufacturing revival to meet expectations.

New / Most Important: CNN reported the EU trade commissioner describing progress toward a US trade agreement. Playbook ✓: Maintain selective domestic and quality exposure, with exporter hedges in place. Add when actual trade terms or company orders make the earnings bridge clearer. Trim if unemployment weakness broadens or negotiations stall while firms continue to remove guidance. The right trade is a conditional recovery position with room to increase, rather than a large valuation bet that assumes policy uncertainty has already disappeared.

🇨🇳 China  Bearish  support / export disruption

Macro Stance: China’s policy response is active, but the equity case still has to pass a demand test. CNBC reported employment support and suggestions of further stimulus. [11] Support can reduce the probability of a disorderly adjustment without immediately restoring company profits. The most exposed businesses need new customers or better pricing, not only access to financing. Maintain an underweight to the broad export and property complex while keeping selective room for technology businesses with a genuine domestic product advantage. The market can rally on a change in policy expectations before the economy improves, but the durability of that rally will depend on how quickly support reaches spending and profitable orders.

PMIs & Activity: The Guardian described plunging manufacturing activity in the tariff war, and Reuters reported a sharp fall in April factory activity. [12] [13] These outcomes place the earlier first-quarter growth beat in context. Reuters’ GDP report had already warned of the tariff shock ahead. [14] For positioning, the important comparison is between demand pulled forward before the shock and demand that can survive after it. A factory can maintain output by building inventory or taking lower-margin business; neither is as useful to shareholders as stable orders with acceptable pricing. Watch orders and employment together. A narrower decline would be a first stabilization signal, while a broad recovery would require evidence that domestic demand is replacing lost external business.

Credit Impulse: March bank lending exceeded expectations, Reuters reported, creating a stronger financing baseline before the latest trade damage. [15] That helps explain why a stimulus narrative can remain credible even when the near-term activity news is poor. The missing link for investors is profitable credit use. New loans that fund productive equipment or working capital against secure orders are different from loans that cover losses. Reuters’ account of thinner margins at the Big Five banks shows the cost of support to the financial system’s shareholders. [16] Favor evidence of healthier borrowers and sustainable loan demand over a simple assumption that a larger credit quantity must produce a stronger bank stock. The financing impulse deserves attention, but it needs an economic destination.

Property: Property remains the least convincing channel for a quick demand rebound. Reuters reported deeper losses at Vanke and a proposed A-share sale. [17] A capital raising can extend a company’s room to maneuver while diluting existing shareholders; it is not equivalent to stronger homebuyer demand. The South China Morning Post described luxury-goods owners facing a difficult China environment amid the property slump and trade war. [18] That connects the housing problem to confidence and discretionary spending. Require an improvement in sales quality and cash collection before treating the sector as an engine of recovery. A policy-supported share rally can occur earlier, but investors should size it as a financing and sentiment trade until operating evidence catches up.

Fiscal, Policy & Trade: Targeted exemptions provide a pragmatic counterweight to confrontation. Reuters reported exemptions for some US goods and the creation of a broader exemption list. [19] [20] These actions can protect important inputs without settling the wider conflict. They make supply-chain selection more useful than a general declaration that tensions are easing. The South China Morning Post reported the US trade chief saying no China talks were underway, even as other agreements were described as close. [21] Treat that distinction seriously. Concessions elsewhere may change market expectations for China, but they do not establish a bilateral process. Companies can continue shifting capacity and relationships because uncertainty itself has a cost, even before a new tariff becomes effective.

New / Most Important: Technology policy is the main counterweight to the weak macro picture. Reuters reported Nvidia raising concerns with US lawmakers about Huawei’s AI capabilities, while CNBC described Alibaba’s new open-source models. [22] [23] These are signs of competitive pressure and continuing innovation, not a completed solution to weak household demand or export disruption. The investment implication is to separate technology capability from shareholder economics. Intense competition can accelerate adoption while limiting the profit any single platform retains. Prefer a clear monetization path and a strong balance sheet; avoid buying a broad internet basket solely because an impressive model announcement makes the national technology story more compelling.

Playbook ✓: Stay underweight broad export exposure and require tighter entry discipline in internet platforms. Add selectively if order deterioration stabilizes and policy support becomes visible in profitable demand. Use ASHR for a measured mainland allocation; use individual technology names when the product thesis can be evaluated on its own merits. Reduce if property financing stress deepens or tariff uncertainty keeps customers from committing orders. ⚠ Divergence: AI capability and targeted trade exemptions can support a sharp technology rally even while manufacturing remains weak, so preserve the ability to change exposure when company economics improve rather than tying every decision to the national growth headline.
