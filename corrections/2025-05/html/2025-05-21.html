🇺🇸 United States  Mixed  funding constraint / selective growth

Macro Stance: The recovery has reached the point where the cost of funding matters as much as relief over tariffs. Reuters reported Moody's downgrade rippling through bonds and causing concern for stocks. The Fed's latest decision kept rates unchanged while acknowledging uncertainty and stagflation risk. That combination makes a simple recession-versus-recovery framework inadequate: companies can maintain activity while investors demand a higher return on the capital used to finance it. Stay invested in businesses with credible cash generation, but do not treat a powerful equity rebound as evidence that the policy constraint has vanished. [1] [2]

New / Most Important: The new corporate distinction is visible in Home Depot's decision to maintain prices and reaffirm its full-year forecast, reported by CNBC. Walmart had warned that tariff-related price rises could arrive later in the month. Different retailers are choosing different responses to the same cost shock, which means the next stage of the market should reward operating flexibility rather than a single consumer narrative. April annual inflation of 2.3%, per CNBC, provides a favorable recent baseline, but it does not settle future pass-through. Playbook ✓: Hold profitable technology, selected industrial investment and essential-service cash flow. Add broad beta when long yields stabilize and earnings revisions support the rebound. If price increases weaken spending while the funding hurdle remains high, reduce discretionary exposure and keep hedges on expensive duration. The positive trigger is revenue resilience with manageable capital needs; the negative trigger is a widening gap between confident valuation and the cash businesses can actually retain.

🇨🇳 China  Mixed  lending relief / transmission test

Macro Stance: China has delivered another financing step, and the market now needs evidence that borrowers use it. Reuters reported cuts to key rates as the trade war continued to simmer. This changes the immediate policy setting without resolving the distinction between willingness to lend and willingness to invest or spend. The constructive case is that cheaper credit, lower household financing costs and a calmer trade environment reinforce one another. The cautious case is that uncertainty keeps private demand restrained even as official support increases. Hold exposure to companies with identifiable revenue engines, while keeping the broad domestic-recovery call conditional on credit transmission and household confidence. [12]

PMIs & Activity: April activity remains uneven. Reuters described industrial output and retail-sales growth slowing; CNBC's coverage emphasized disappointing retail spending alongside factory output resisting tariffs. The April manufacturing contraction remains an important warning about confidence and orders. These indicators should be read together: resilient production can reflect existing commitments, inventory management or substitution, while household spending responds to income security and asset prices. Look for the next survey to show improving demand rather than only a mechanical output rebound. For stocks, prefer earnings supported by product competitiveness or monetization over those requiring a sudden, uniform consumption acceleration. A production surprise does not automatically increase every company's pricing power. [13] [14] [15]

Credit Impulse: The lending-rate cuts and major banks' lower deposit rates create an attempt to improve the economics of borrowing while containing pressure on lenders. The weak recent loan backdrop still matters: TradingView reported new yuan lending at a 20-year low. Lower rates can reduce the burden on existing borrowers without generating a proportionate volume of new productive credit. Watch private borrowing appetite, loan use and the quality of activity financed. For banks, earnings depend on the interaction between asset yields, deposit costs and credit losses. More lending is attractive only if the new business earns an acceptable return. [16] [17] [18]
