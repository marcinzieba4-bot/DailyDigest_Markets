🌍 Geographic Macro Overview

🇺🇸 United States  Mixed  hold / labor resilience

Macro Stance: Labor resilience has bought the US economy time. AP reported employers adding 177,000 jobs while unemployment remained at 4.2%. [1] That supports household income and weakens the case for treating an imminent demand collapse as inevitable. It does not eliminate the problem of tariff costs or the uncertainty facing businesses deciding when to invest. The allocation implication is to move from a blanket defensive instinct toward selective risk taking. Favor firms whose demand has held up and whose financing does not require immediate policy relief. The distinction between a resilient labor market and an untroubled economy remains essential: employment can cushion spending while margins and confidence still deteriorate at the businesses serving that spending.

Rates & Curve: The Treasury curve is imposing a cost on the improved growth interpretation. The 2Y yield was 3.83%, up 12 bps over 20 observations, and the 10Y was 4.33%, up 27 bps. The 10Y−2Y spread was 50 bps. The curve was positively sloped, with bear steepening (both up, 10Y up more). The five-observation changes were increases of 9 bps in the 2Y and 4 bps in the 10Y (US Treasury, prior close 2025-05-02). The S&P 500 finished at 5,686.67, up 5.38% over 20 sessions, while the Nasdaq 100 was 20,102.61, up 8.54%. VIX stood at 22.68 and DXY at 100.00, down 2.03% over 20 sessions (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2025-05-02). Better risk appetite has arrived alongside a higher hurdle for financing and valuation.

New / Most Important: The next policy test is the Fed’s outlook. Reuters’ week-ahead preview placed it at the center of attention as the stock rally gathered pace. [2] A patient Fed would be consistent with labor resilience and tariff uncertainty; it would also challenge positions built on prompt easing. Company capital returns provide another signal worth watching. Reuters reported Apple’s shares falling as tariff concerns and a reduced buyback unsettled investors. [3] A strong franchise can still face competing demands on cash. Investors should ask which firms can fund investment and shareholder returns through the current environment without relying on a quick change in policy.

Playbook ✓: Hold a balanced risk budget with a preference for profitable growth, selected infrastructure and strong balance sheets. Add cyclical exposure if the labor cushion extends into durable customer demand and credit remains supportive. Reduce if higher long yields begin to undermine financing or tariff costs force broader guidance cuts. Keep short-maturity bonds as flexibility rather than making a large duration bet on growth weakness alone. A constructive view on the economy’s ability to avoid an immediate collapse can coexist with caution about the price already paid for that resilience.

🇨🇳 China  Bearish  talks optionality / price pressure

Macro Stance: China’s relief trade now has a clearer diplomatic possibility, but the operating backdrop remains difficult. NPR reported China considering US trade talks while demanding cancellation of tariffs. [7] That changes the distribution of possible outcomes without establishing an agreement or even a completed negotiating process. The equity market can respond positively because the worst case becomes less certain. For a durable overweight, however, investors need to see customers return and firms defend profitability. Maintain a cautious broad stance while allowing selective participation in companies whose earnings can survive a prolonged adjustment. Negotiation optionality is valuable; the price paid for it should still recognize that talks can stall and commercial relationships can shift before policymakers compromise.

PMIs & Activity: The April factory deterioration is still the activity baseline. Reuters reported a sharp fall as tariffs took effect, while the WSJ described plunging export orders. [8] [9] The first-quarter growth beat had preceded much of the adjustment, with Reuters warning of the shock ahead. [10] Do not let a relief rally erase that sequence. The practical test is whether orders stabilize without companies sacrificing so much price that additional volume fails to support earnings. A factory can find an alternative destination for output and still earn less. Watch the combination of new demand, employment and pricing rather than a single production headline. The stronger confirmation would be businesses receiving orders they can fulfill profitably, not merely keeping capacity busy.

Credit Impulse: March lending exceeded expectations and additional stimulus was expected, per Reuters. [11] That is a useful financing baseline for a government trying to limit the shock. It should be read alongside the purpose of borrowing. Credit supporting productive capacity with secure demand offers a different return path from credit financing unsold inventory or covering an operating loss. The next improvement needs to show up in private spending and borrower cash generation. Cheap money is less effective when firms hesitate because they cannot estimate the future market for their products. Prefer strong balance sheets and visible customers while the credit impulse works its way through the economy. A stronger aggregate financing number is encouraging only when investors can identify the activity it sustains.

Property: The South China Morning Post reported a policy-led property recovery heating up in core cities. [12] That is a constructive counter-signal to the broader weak-demand view. It suggests that support can influence activity where demand and financing align. Keep the geography precise: stronger core-city conditions do not establish a uniform national property recovery. Reuters’ account of deeper Vanke losses and a proposed share sale remains a reminder of the financing burden within the sector. [13] For a property allocation, require improving sales quality and cash collection alongside better sentiment. A developer can benefit from a more active market while existing shareholders still face dilution or a difficult balance-sheet repair. The recovery trade is therefore selective, with a high bar for capital discipline.

Fiscal, Policy & Trade: Policy is trying to protect employment and useful business capacity. CNBC reported employment support and hints of more stimulus. [14] The South China Morning Post examined a new law intended to protect the private sector. [15] These are important elements of confidence, but confidence must ultimately become investment and consumption. On trade, the more constructive signal is China evaluating outreach, as Al Jazeera reported. [16] Treat it as an option on de-escalation, not a finalized change in tariff costs. A company planning production must still decide where to locate capacity under uncertainty. Even if rates are reduced, firms may retain diversified sourcing because the cost of another disruption has become part of ordinary business planning.

Markets & Flows: Mainland and offshore pricing reflect different completed sessions. Shanghai’s latest close was 3,279.03, down 2.07% over 20 sessions (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2025-04-30). They do not establish mainland fund flows or a completed change in earnings. Choose the vehicle according to the intended exposure: mainland policy, offshore platform profits and the currency each contribute differently.

New / Most Important: Adjusting to lost export markets can create pressure at home. If goods are redirected into the domestic market, more supply could compete for the same spending, squeezing prices and margins. That mechanism can make output resilience less valuable to shareholders. The South China Morning Post’s account of exporters experiencing life after the US at the Canton Fair shows the search for alternative demand. [17] New customers can reduce dependence, but replacing a market takes commercial work. Investors should favor firms that can diversify while protecting economics, rather than assuming a new destination is automatically equivalent in profitability.

Playbook ✓: Underweight the broad export and weak-property complex, with small selective technology and core-city recovery positions where cash generation supports them. Add if talks become a credible process and order deterioration stabilizes without a fresh margin squeeze. Reduce if redirected supply intensifies price competition faster than policy supports demand. Keep relief trades smaller than investments supported by operating evidence. ⚠ Divergence: improving core-city property and a credible opening to talks can sustain a sharp equity recovery even while national price pressure worsens, so change the allocation when company economics improve rather than remaining fixed on a single bearish macro label.
