The Fed held rates steady at its May meeting while flagging rising uncertainty and stagflation risk. Own the companies that can live with that stance rather than the ones that require its reversal. Playbook ✓: Prefer domestic investment suppliers and quality lenders over a concentrated auto-export bet. Macro Stance: China's policy response is tangible, but its transmission into household confidence remains incomplete. Reuters reported cuts to key rates as the trade war continued, and Bloomberg described banks lowering benchmark lending rates after PBOC easing. The investment opportunity lies in distinguishing companies that can use cheaper money to earn a return from those using it to carry excess capacity or unfinished property. PMIs & Activity: April's factory activity fell sharply as tariffs bit, according to Reuters. The later April activity picture was uneven rather than uniformly weak: factory output resisted tariff pressure while retail sales disappointed. The distinction is more useful than a single growth label. Producers can maintain throughput while households remain cautious, leaving inventories, prices and margins to absorb the difference. CNBC also reported accelerating April industrial-profit growth as policy measures cushioned the tariff impact. That is encouraging for selected manufacturers, but the confirmation needed is broader domestic purchasing power, not output alone. Watch whether the next factory survey and subsequent retail release tell the same story; a production rebound that depends on price concessions would be a weaker equity signal than improving orders accompanied by firm margins. [18] [19] [20]

Credit Impulse: The April lending signal deserves more attention than the availability of policy support. TradingView described new yuan loans slumping to a long-term low. Read that as a warning about the willingness to borrow, rather than proof that every part of the financial system is starved of liquidity. Lower benchmark rates can reduce servicing costs and support refinancing, but a household worried about its home or income may still decline a new loan. For investors, the useful credit impulse is financing that becomes spending, investment and supplier receipts. Prefer businesses with self-funded expansion and avoid assuming that a lower interest bill will repair an unprofitable project. A sustained improvement in new lending alongside firmer domestic demand would justify broadening from selected policy beneficiaries into a more conventional cyclical allocation. [21]
