🇺🇸 United States  Mixed  hold / tariff tension

Macro Stance: The economy is still expanding, but the expansion is becoming harder to insure. Reuters reported that policymakers were expected to keep rates steady as tariffs roiled the outlook. [3] Treat that as a pending policy test. A patient central bank would be consistent with the present tension; an easing narrative needs evidence that demand weakness is winning over inflation pressure.

Equities have recovered forcefully, while the cost of carrying duration still demands respect. (Yahoo Finance, prior close 2025-05-05; US Treasury, prior close 2025-05-05)

New / Most Important: The most useful new distinction is between real business resilience and relief from an extreme risk discount. Reuters called the labor market resilient ahead of tariff turbulence. [4] Yet Ford withdrew its outlook and warned of a tariff hit, putting a concrete corporate face on that turbulence. [5] The jobs description supports maintaining equity exposure; the corporate warning argues for selecting the exposure carefully. A company with recurring demand, pricing control and a manageable funding requirement can outperform even when broad growth loses speed. A company relying on consumers absorbing every cost increase has a less reliable bridge from sales to earnings.

Playbook ✓: Keep profitable technology and cash-generating services as the core, with cash available to add after policy-driven volatility. Avoid funding that position with an unhedged long-bond bet: growth worries and tariff worries can move equities and duration in different directions. Add broad market risk if the next policy communication preserves inflation credibility while businesses continue to describe resilient orders. Reduce cyclical exposure if withdrawal of guidance spreads beyond the most directly tariff-exposed manufacturers. The invalidation is a cleaner combination of cooling prices and stable demand, which would make a wider equity allocation more attractive than this selective stance.

🇨🇳 China  Mixed  policy cushion / export shock

Macro Stance: China is a contest between domestic stabilization and an export shock already pressing on company decisions. Reuters reported that first-quarter GDP growth topped expectations, while warning that the US tariff shock loomed large. [9] That is the right distinction for positioning. The opening quarter demonstrates capacity to grow; it does not settle how businesses adjust when access to an important end market changes. The investable question is whether policy and domestic spending can replace enough lost external demand to protect earnings. Buy the businesses with credible domestic franchises and room to adapt. Avoid paying for a broad recovery before activity and profitability move together. Cheap assets can stay cheap when the route from demand to cash generation remains contested.

PMIs & Activity: April manufacturing activity weakened amid the tariff war, according to the Guardian's account of the factory downturn. [10] The Wall Street Journal separately described plunging export orders. [11] These are more relevant to the next earnings turn than extrapolating the earlier GDP release. An exporter can keep a factory busy briefly through existing orders while new business deteriorates; that makes the order pipeline an earlier warning than reported production. S&P Global's April manufacturing review also identified worsening global trade, confidence and inflation trends. [12] The confirmation to demand is a stabilization in new business accompanied by fewer reports of diverted goods. A recovery led only by front-loaded shipments would be fragile, because it borrows activity from a later period rather than creating a durable buyer.

Credit Impulse: March bank lending beat expectations and Reuters reported that more stimulus was expected. [13] The useful interpretation is a policy cushion, with the next test being whether easier credit becomes productive private demand. Lending can finance working capital and prevent a temporary sales shock from becoming a liquidity crisis. It cannot by itself guarantee that the borrower has a profitable market for additional output. China Development Bank increased infrastructure loans in the opening quarter, according to China Daily's Hong Kong edition. [14] That supports the public investment channel. Prefer exposure where financing can unlock an identifiable project or customer order; be more cautious where additional borrowing merely postpones recognition of a weak asset. A transaction recovery bought through discounts may help liquidity while doing less for household wealth and developer margins than the first celebratory headline suggests.
